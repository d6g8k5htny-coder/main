HOW OFTEN? · HOW LONG?

From a rare bar to its lifetime

Occurrence. Select finite bars whose peak has at least one rejected nearby candidate saddle in the prescribed distance and positive-gap bands. Count each selected bar once. As the distance scale shrinks, the chance that a landscape contains any selected bar has a leading fifth-power law. Given that one occurs, the chance of exactly one tends to one.

Lifetime density. For that same selected family, a later proof gives densities for the actual lifetimes rescaled by the cube of the distance scale. The scaled mean density converges over the whole positive lifetime axis, with no convergence rate supplied. This is a density of the selected bars, not of every bar in the landscape.

Population, precise limits and reviews

Fix the planar periodized Gaussian law, finite torus, positive-width compact birth and positive-gap intervals, and a nondegenerate distance band c_0 h ≤ distance ≤ C_0 h. These are ordinary finite superlevel H0 bars; the essential class is excluded. A bar's actual global elder partner need not lie inside the candidate cutoff.

If N_h counts those bars, occurrence gives P(N_h > 0) = L² C_U(1) h^5 + o(h^5) and conditional concentration of N_h at one. Its joint mark conclusion is weak convergence for bounded continuous tests, not joint-mark total variation or a Poisson law.

The density q_h is the expected once-counted lifetime measure per unit area, for lifetime divided by h³. The density proof gives ||h^-5 q_h − q_0||_L1(0,∞) → 0 and ∫q_0 = C_U(1). Its separate field-first corollary additionally uses the occurrence theorem: condition a landscape on N_h > 0, then choose one of its selected bars uniformly. That scalar lifetime density converges to q_0/C_U(1) in L1. It supplies no joint continuous-mark total variation, varying-band limit or rate.

The once-counted intensity, root stability and seven interfaces P, CAP, E1, E2, REC, CUB and ELDER remain hypotheses. Read the conditional occurrence review and the conditional density review. WHICH SADDLE?

A nearby saddle may not end the bar

Designated-pair failure. Fix a peak and one candidate saddle in advance. In the specified determinant-weighted Gaussian law conditioned at those points, ask whether that saddle is the peak's actual global elder death partner. Failure of this one pairing is a different observable from either the occurrence or lifetime density of selected bars.

For fixed compact birth heights and positive gaps, the failure probability has a leading cubic law in the pair's separation, with a controlled remainder. A pair with two witnesses to failure is still one failed pair. The coefficient therefore counts failures once; it does not count witnesses or once-counted replacement bars.

Weighted event, rate and source boundaries

At separation r, condition the original fixed-torus planar field on the designated critical points and height gap k r³. Use the full filtered Hessian-determinant weight and its full normalizer. With p_r the designated-pair success probability, the stated limit is 1 − p_r = r³(α₁ + α₂) + O(r^(13/4)), uniformly on the fixed compact birth/gap/frame parameters. The coefficient is α₁ + α₂, not α₁ + 2α₂. An essential maximum has no finite elder endpoint and counts as a failure here; it was excluded from the selected-bar population in the other card.

The proof's O(r^(1/4)) variation bound concerns its scaled raw-jet failure measure. It is not a lifetime-density rate, joint real-mark total variation or a field-law limit. The original correlated Gaussian law, compact-gap regression, corrected geometric selection, global elder identification and endpoint-tail inputs remain explicit hypotheses. There is no gap-to-zero, growing-volume, higher-dimensional or all-bar conclusion.

LANDED CONDITIONAL RESULT · MATH #233

Count each actual bar once

Several rejected nearby maximum–saddle candidates can describe the same persistence bar. Dividing each candidate’s contribution by its eligible multiplicity counts that bar once, using its actual global elder partner.

For a fixed admissible planar periodized Gaussian field on a fixed torus, positive-width compact birth and positive-gap intervals, and a fixed nondegenerate shrinking distance band, the result is I_h(1) = C_U(1) h^5 + o(h^5), with a bounded continuous marked weak limit. The population contains only ordinary finite superlevel H0 bars represented by eligible rejected candidates; it excludes the essential class. The actual partner need not be inside the candidate cutoff.

Conditional inputs remain: P, CAP, E1, E2, REC, CUB and ELDER. These seven analytic interfaces are retained hypotheses, not newly proved by this packet. The result does not supply a density, a rate, finite-h moment convergence, all-mark exhaustion, higher dimensions or an infinite-volume limit.
