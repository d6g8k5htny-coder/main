LANDED CUSTODY PACKET · MATH #390

One more term for short lifetimes

How often does a feature have a very short lifetime ℓ? Earlier packets expand that density for nearby peak–saddle candidate pairs through an ℓ^(1/3) term. The elder density keeps only pairs whose saddle is the peak’s actual elder partner; the rejected density keeps the others.

Note LU bounds the remainders of these expansions by O(ℓ^(2/3)) for candidate pairs, with an extra logarithm for elder and rejected pairs. Note C3 then identifies the candidate density’s next term:

ν_cand(ℓ) = cℓ^(−1/3) + B_(d,L) + I^cand ℓ^(1/4) + c₂ℓ^(1/3) + c₃ℓ^(2/3) + o(ℓ^(2/3)).

The coefficient c₃ is an explicit integral. It is positive for the Gaussian-kernel model, and for the torus field once the side L passes an unquantified threshold; its sign at L = 24 is not proved. No rate or certified number is claimed, and the sharpness of note LU’s ℓ^(2/3) remainder is claimed only above that threshold, not at L = 24. Conjecture 7 remains open.

GRID TO CONTINUUM · CONDITIONAL EXPECTATION RESULTS

Can a grid recover the shortest bars?

Reading cut
4 October 2026 · 06:20 UTC

A finer grid can resolve smaller features, but the lifetime bins must shrink slowly enough compared with the sampling error. These two landed arguments compare the expected number of short persistence bars in grid samples with the continuum count. They concern a fixed side-24 planar torus and its unconditioned normalized Gaussian field, under retained source hypotheses.

C131 · EXACT VERTEX SAMPLES

Start with exact field values

Sample the full field exactly at periodic grid vertices, with spacing h = 24/n. Count finite ordinary superlevel H0 bars, with multiplicity, in deterministic positive lifetime bins [λτ, μτ), where 0 < λ < μ are fixed and τ tends to zero. The essential class is excluded; no longest finite bar is discarded.

If h²√log(1/h) = o(τ_h), the expected absolute count discrepancy, divided by the continuum’s expected bin count, tends to zero. The sampled-to-continuum expectation ratio tends to one. This retains the M4–M5 density assumptions and the proof’s marked Kac–Rice interfaces.

C132 · CONDITIONAL SPECTRAL EXTENSION

Then account for how values are supplied

The extension uses spectral cutoffs coupled to the full field through the same Gaussian coordinates, controlled normalization and a supplied certificate for errors at grid vertices. Merely matching seed labels does not establish this coupling.

It gives the same expectation conclusion when the combined lifetime-displacement error is small compared with the bin scale and exceptional events have a sufficiently small expected count contribution. A small failure probability alone is insufficient. Fixed normalization or precision errors cannot silently be ignored as the bins shrink.

Scope: these are ensemble expectation results, not a certificate for the current numerical sampler. They do not establish FFT or roundoff accuracy, persistence-library arithmetic, a finite-grid starting threshold, confidence intervals, post hoc bin selection, zero-endpoint cumulative bins, evaluated constants, Lean alignment, a global theorem or a change in scientific status.

Exact conditions, complete proofs and scoped reviews

The grid observable is the exact mathematical vertex-cubical barcode: each cell enters at its minimum corner value. C132 imports D, the positive M4–M5 lifetime density; C, the unconditioned total-critical-count second moment; and A, the cubical bottleneck matching bound. It retains that source’s author-side disposition when consuming Fourier C6 Theorem F’s whole-field count moment. C131 does not consume C6, and neither result transfers a determinant-tilted Palm window count.

For C132, supplied nodal values have error at most η_n on a Borel event with failure probability at most q_n. Its lifetime-displacement allowance d_n includes spatial interpolation, the spectral tail, normalization and nodal error. Require both d_n = o(τ_n) and n²b_n + √b_n = o(τ_n^(2/3)), with b_n = min(1, n^-8 + q_n). The exceptional expected-count bound is (n² − 1)b_n + K_C√b_n; no independence between the field and certificate event is assumed.

One sufficient schedule uses M_n ≥ m_n, allowing M_n = ∞, and both [h_n² + exp(−βm_n²)]√log n + η_n = o(τ_n) and n²q_n + √q_n = o(τ_n^(2/3)), where β = π²/24². This is a conditional mathematical sampling contract, not a construction or certification of a Gaussian sampler. The full proof also states the finite bound’s range conditions and retains its inherited constants.

ONE FIXED PHYSICAL GAP

A cubic failure law with a sharper remainder

Fix the planar torus and field covariance, compact birth heights and all orthonormal frames. At separation r, the prescribed height gap is exactly r³: physical k = 1. Use the original continuously conditioned Gaussian law, full filtered Hessian-determinant weight and full normalizer.

With p_r the probability that the specified saddle is the peak's finite global elder death partner, the result is

The leading coefficient is unchanged: a pair that has two failure witnesses is still one failed pair. The remainder bounds the asymptotic approximation under the retained hypotheses. Its constants are analytically finite, not evaluated numerical error bars; no optimality claim is made.

HOW OFTEN? · HOW LONG?

From a rare bar to its lifetime

Occurrence. Select finite bars whose peak has at least one rejected nearby candidate saddle in the prescribed distance and positive-gap bands. Count each selected bar once. As the distance scale shrinks, the chance that a landscape contains any selected bar has a leading fifth-power law. Given that one occurs, the chance of exactly one tends to one.

Lifetime density. For that same selected family, a later proof gives densities for the actual lifetimes rescaled by the cube of the distance scale. The scaled mean density converges over the whole positive lifetime axis, with no convergence rate supplied. This is a density of the selected bars, not of every bar in the landscape.

Read the occurrence proof · Read the selected-bar density proof

Population, precise limits and reviews

Fix the planar periodized Gaussian law, finite torus, positive-width compact birth and positive-gap intervals, and a nondegenerate distance band c_0 h ≤ distance ≤ C_0 h. These are ordinary finite superlevel H0 bars; the essential class is excluded. A bar's actual global elder partner need not lie inside the candidate cutoff.

If N_h counts those bars, occurrence gives P(N_h > 0) = L² C_U(1) h^5 + o(h^5) and conditional concentration of N_h at one. Its joint mark conclusion is weak convergence for bounded continuous tests, not joint-mark total variation or a Poisson law.

The density q_h is the expected once-counted lifetime measure per unit area, for lifetime divided by h³. The density proof gives ||h^-5 q_h − q_0||_L1(0,∞) → 0 and ∫q_0 = C_U(1). Its separate field-first corollary additionally uses the occurrence theorem: condition a landscape on N_h > 0, then choose one of its selected bars uniformly. That scalar lifetime density converges to q_0/C_U(1) in L1. It supplies no joint continuous-mark total variation, varying-band limit or rate.

WHICH SADDLE?

A nearby saddle may not end the bar

Designated-pair failure. Fix a peak and one candidate saddle in advance. In the specified determinant-weighted Gaussian law conditioned at those points, ask whether that saddle is the peak's actual global elder death partner. Failure of this one pairing is a different observable from either the occurrence or lifetime density of selected bars.

For fixed compact birth heights and positive gaps, the failure probability has a leading cubic law in the pair's separation, with a controlled remainder. A pair with two witnesses to failure is still one failed pair. The coefficient therefore counts failures once; it does not count witnesses or once-counted replacement bars.

LANDED CONDITIONAL RESULT · MATH #233

Count each actual bar once

Several rejected nearby maximum–saddle candidates can describe the same persistence bar. Dividing each candidate’s contribution by its eligible multiplicity counts that bar once, using its actual global elder partner.

For a fixed admissible planar periodized Gaussian field on a fixed torus, positive-width compact birth and positive-gap intervals, and a fixed nondegenerate shrinking distance band, the result is I_h(1) = C_U(1) h^5 + o(h^5), with a bounded continuous marked weak limit. The population contains only ordinary finite superlevel H0 bars represented by eligible rejected candidates; it excludes the essential class. The actual partner need not be inside the candidate cutoff.

Conditional inputs remain: P, CAP, E1, E2, REC, CUB and ELDER. These seven analytic interfaces are retained hypotheses, not newly proved by this packet. The result does not supply a density, a rate, finite-h moment convergence, all-mark exhaustion, higher dimensions or an infinite-volume limit.
