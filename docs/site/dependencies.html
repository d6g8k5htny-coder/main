Start here: the mathematical ideas

A landscape, a threshold, a lifetime

Imagine a landscape whose height varies from place to place. Move a horizontal waterline through it and watch regions appear and join. This picture is a starting point for reading the research; each source specifies its own mathematical setting.

Random landscape
A function assigns a value, like height, to each location. In a random landscape the function is chosen according to a probability model. A picture of one landscape is one example, not a statement about every possible landscape.
Threshold and component
A threshold selects locations above or below a chosen value. A connected component is a piece joined together within that selected region. Read the source to see which direction its threshold moves.
Persistence and lifetime
Persistence tracks features as the threshold changes. A component can appear and later merge with another; the two threshold values describe its birth and death in the source's convention. Its lifetime measures their separation. This is about threshold values, which need not be elapsed time.
Hypotheses and scope
Hypotheses are the conditions a claim assumes. Scope says which objects or situations it covers. Read these before applying a result to another landscape or scale.

Read a claim in three steps

  1. Choose a claim and read its recorded scope and source.
  2. Follow its recorded relationships. An arrow points from the claim that depends on something to its dependency. Required and context relationships keep their recorded meanings; position and distance do not measure proof strength.
  3. Inspect evidence separately: source acquisition, mathematical review, kernel checking, statement alignment and scientific acceptance answer different questions. Missing evidence remains unknown.
