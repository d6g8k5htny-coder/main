### 1.5 CI could not fail on six of its own checkers

The step pattern `test -f X && python X || echo "skipped"` — introduced by me
this morning for the review, recovery and collision checkers and copied by the
runner agent for the carrier, lane and receipt checkers — swallows a **real
failure**: if the tool exists and exits nonzero, the `||` branch runs and the
step passes. Confirmed by execution: a deliberately failing command under that
guard exits 0. For those six steps "what CI enforces" was, until fixed, only
"what CI runs". The fix is `if [ -f X ]; then python X; else echo skipped; fi`,
which skips only when the tool is absent. Sixth defect the harness has found in
this repository's own work today; applied to all six steps once the runner
agent released `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, and proved by execution — a failing
tool under the new guard exits 1.

### 2.4 The A1 machinery, and what building it showed

`python3 -m research.bands.ladder`; `python3 -m pytest -q tests/test_bands.py`
(72 tests, 20 negative controls). `research/bands/` reproduces §2.3's numbers
from its own Fractions — spread `836263/10000`, `12.9092%`; `C ≥ 5.1818` and
`C ≥ 23.1709`; ratio `4.4716` — with the assumption attached to every returned
object and printed by every formatter.

Three things the build established beyond §2.3:

- **`ratio(κ)` has a closed form and is strictly decreasing** —
  `(|Δ_wide|/|Δ_narrow|)·(δ_narrow/δ_wide)^κ` — with certified values 4.6696 at
  κ = 0, 4.4716 at 1/8, 3.3017 at 1, 1.1672 at 4, 0.8253 at 5. It crosses 1
  only between κ = 4 and κ = 5, which is where the two forced *minima*
  coincide — not where reconciliation becomes possible. **The first draft of
  this bullet said "no exponent below 4 reconciles the two adjacent bands under
  one constant". That was false**, and the adversarial pass caught it: a
  modulus is an inequality, so two bands that force different minimal
  constants are satisfied together by the larger one.
  `ladder.common_admissible_constant(κ)` now exhibits that constant — max over
  bands of `constant.hi`, re-checked against each band with both roundings
  chosen against the conclusion — and returns `all_satisfied` at every κ in the
  sweep (`C = 23.1709` at κ = 1/8), with a control that fails if the maximum is
  replaced by the minimum. The 4.47× ratio is a statement about *tightness*:
  the two bands differ in the minimum each forces, not in whether one constant
  serves both. (Another defect the harness found in its own work today — and
  the first in a mathematical sentence rather than in a checker or a port.)
- **A decay envelope is a premise no finite computation here can check, and
  the first draft let it default to "certified".** The attack pass fed
  `band_enclosure` an envelope with `B = 7` against a kernel that decays at
  `B = 1/2` and received `certified = True` with a tail of 8.69e−26 against an
  honest 2.13e−1 — wrong by 2.45e24, exactly computed. `DecayEnvelope` now
  carries its own `certified` flag defaulting to `False`, `PlaneKernel.certified`
  defaults to `False`, and a `BandEnclosure` is certified only as the
  conjunction of the two, with the missing flag named in a NON-CERTIFYING
  caveat. The number is unchanged and still wrong by 2.45e24 — the control
  asserts that explicitly (`tail < honest / 10²⁰`) so the relabelling cannot be
  read as a repair of the bound. The falsifier's printed report had the same
  shape of hazard: two rows rendering identical decimals (`width=0.001
  modulus=0.001`) with verdicts PASS and FALSIFIED and no marker; every row now
  carries an `exact:` line with the Fractions, and the decimal columns are
  labelled as renderings.
- **The tail bound is uniform in the displacement, and that is the whole
  point.** For a kernel with a certified decay envelope, the omitted-lattice
  tail over an interval box depends on the displacement only through the box
  radius — proved by the reverse triangle inequality on shells, with no second
  truncation on the Gaussian branch and an integral comparison on the power
  branch. The frozen engine's `tail_bound` hard-codes a point separation
  (`rho = m*_LT − 17`). At the program's own period the proved Gaussian tail is
  ~1e−498 against a band width of 1.6e−3, which is exactly why the engine's
  "< 1e−60" side-assertion reads as sufficient and why the defect is easy to
  miss: the missing thing is not the tail's size but that it is never carried,
  and never uniform.
- **Two testing defects in its own first draft, found by running the controls
  against broken copies and fixed before shipping.** Wide-box containment tests
  passed even with the tail bound deleted, because band variation swamps a
  5.6e−3 tail — a point-band test was added. And a 4× shell undercount was
  uncatchable by domination on the Gaussian, because the honest bound is already
  ~13× the true omitted mass — two independent closed-form reimplementations
  were added and asserted faithful. The measured looseness (12.7× point, 13.5×
  box on the Gaussian; ~2× on the inverse-power kernel) is reported, not hidden.
  The README's mutation table is dated: the `a = L·M + R` row read 8 in the
  first draft, 9 on the auditor's recount against that file, and 14 against the
  current file, because the whole-box sweep and three near-edge parametrisations
  added since also catch it. Measured mutation counts move when tests are added;
  anyone adding tests must re-measure rather than trust the printed numbers.

What it does not do: compute a band bound for any jet of this program. The
kernels exercised are reference kernels. The central missing piece is a
certified decay envelope for the program's `kplane` at every order the 24-jet

### 2.5 The A5 cover driver — the one the lane records as unwritten

`python3 -m pytest -q tests/test_cover.py` (37 tests, 20 negative controls).
`research/cover/` is the annulus Riemann-sum driver RN5's next exact action
describes and `LANE_RN_UNIF.md` records as unwritten: a region-generic adaptive
cover whose accept / refine / reject / pending ledger is the first-class
output. Partition exactness is decided structurally over `Fraction` by
coordinate compression, never by area comparison — the sharpest control in the
package is a cover whose cell areas sum exactly to the domain's while carrying
both a gap and an overlap, which any area-based validator accepts.
`total()` raises `PendingCellsError` while any cell is pending and there is no
bypass flag; at max depth cells are left pending, not accepted. The RN5
annulus `0.1 ≤ |y| ≤ 5` and the T4 polar region `d ∈ [5, 17]` are instantiated
separately and never summed.

What building it established, as exact arithmetic rather than concern:

- **The acceptance rule's area factor cancels.** `width(|C|·rng) ≤ tol·|C|/|D|`
  reduces to `width(rng) ≤ tol/|D|`: a zeroth-order sup cover imposes a
  *uniform* bound on the integrand's oscillation per cell regardless of where
  the cell sits. That is the near-axis refinement cost RN5 says must be faced,
  in one line.
- **The polar anisotropy is 50×, exactly.** At a target Cartesian cell
  diameter of 1/10 the annulus needs 98 radial × 629 angular = **61,642**
  uniform cells; inner arc extent ≈ 9.99e−4 against outer ≈ 4.99e−2, the ratio
  being `r_hi/r_lo = 50` exactly. No fit, no sampling.
- **θ-halving alone cannot tighten the T4 cover past its shell resolution.**
  For a purely radial integrand, halving θ leaves a cell's range enclosure
  unchanged, so any tolerance below the shell's radial floor is unreachable at
  any depth. Demonstrated by running it, not asserted — and it bears directly
  on the freeze step `LANE_RN_UNIF.md` names as "polar cover d ∈ [5, 17] with
  theta-halving".
- **Choosing polar cells moves the boundary difficulty rather than removing
  it, and the module says where to.** Radii 1/10 and 5 are rational, so the
  annulus boundary is exact and the rejected boundary area is exactly 0; in
  exchange every cell area carries π as a certified enclosure rather than an
  exact rational. The Cartesian bracket of the same region rejects straddling
  cells with retained boundary-area bounds and residual enclosures instead.
- **Its reject path had no containment control, and two mutations proved
  it.** The first draft's controls caught a dropped interior-gap branch and a
  dropped boundary residual, and said so. The adversarial pass then found two
  mutations — `MX3`, quartering the rational upper bound on a rejected cell's
  area, and `MX4`, which leaves the bookkeeping untouched — that each left all
  29 tests green while returning `certified = True`, `covers_region = True`,
  no caveats, and an enclosure of `[71.09, 74.22]` (respectively
  `[71.09, 71.11]`) for an integral whose true value is 78.508. Seventeen of
  the 29 test docstrings named no mutation at all. The repair is a *reference
  integrand whose integral is known from geometry alone*: `f ≡ 1` on the
  annulus integrates to `π·(25 − 1/100) = 78.5084…`, computed from the region's
  exact radii and the certified `π` independently of any cover run. Control 22
  asserts the bracket's accounted and rejected areas sum to exactly 100 (catches
  `MX3`); control 23 asserts the certified total contains the geometric value
  (the only control that catches `MX4`); control 24 builds an all-OUTSIDE
  classification fault by hand — every flag reads clean and the bookkeeping
  still balances, so containment is the only thing that refuses it. A control
  that could only catch what its author imagined was the hole; a value known
  independently of the code is the patch. The test docstrings and README now say
  that completeness is *not* established: nothing shows a third such mutation
  does not exist.
- **`Total.certified_enclosure()` is the accessor a publisher must use.** The
  ledger's `enclosure` field is an enclosure of the *accounted* part; whether it
  is an enclosure of the region's integral is what `certified` and
  `covers_region` say. `engine/run.py`'s A5 task read the raw field and stamped
  it `certified_interval` regardless — latent on the polar region, which rejects
  no cells, and live the moment the lane is pointed at the Cartesian bracket.
  The runner now calls the accessor and records the refusal as a result when
  it fires; a control drives the task on a hand-built ledger over the bracket
  domain whose `Total` does not cover the region and asserts that no certified
  enclosure is published, and it fails when the raw-field read is restored.

What it does not do: certify any cell of the program's actual cover. Every
integrand exercised is a labelled reference; a float probe is included only to
prove the NON-CERTIFYING label propagates to the total and the receipt. The
constant-per-cell bound costs O(tol⁻²) cells, so a tight cover of the 78.5-area
annulus with a real integrand needs a higher-order cell bound behind the same
protocol. Both Pieces of `D3-LEMMA-RN-UNIF` remain OPEN; the lane receipts still
carry `lemma_closed: false`.
