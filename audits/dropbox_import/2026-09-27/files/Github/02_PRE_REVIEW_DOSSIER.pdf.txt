2. Physics-oriented orientation

2.1 Bargmann–Fock geometry
The Euclidean Bargmann–Fock field is a canonical smooth stationary isotropic Gaussian field. Its 
covariance is Gaussian in separation, so all field derivatives exist and the local jet has particularly 
explicit covariance. Periodization replaces the infinite domain by a compact torus while retaining 
stationarity. Side 24 is so large relative to the unit correlation length that the first image correction 
is proportional to e−24

2 / 2=e−288.

The normalization by Z24 is important: it makes K 24 (0 )=1. Any Fourier representation must use the 
corresponding Poisson prefactor consistently. The main manuscript includes that prefactor; one 
display in the archival technical appendix omits it. Positivity arguments survive the omission, but an 
exact equality must be corrected in a journal version.



2.2 Why short bars come from a local fold
A very short H 0 bar has a creating maximum and a death saddle at nearly equal heights. The local 
mechanism is a fold in the direction joining them. If r is their separation, the height gap is 
generically cubic in r:

f (M )−f (S )= κ r

The two endpoint Hessians each have one soft eigenvalue of order r, while their transverse blocks 
remain nondegenerate. Kac–Rice weights each critical point by an absolute Hessian determinant. 
The pair therefore contributes two powers of r from the endpoint determinants.

The value and gradient constraints become singular as the endpoints collide. A corrected divided-
difference coordinate system isolates this singularity. In dimension three its linear Jacobian 
contributes r−6. The remarkable cancellation in the table above leaves a universal r d r radial 
measure before lifetime pushforward.

2.3 Why topology is not automatic
Kac–Rice can count all nearby typed maximum–index-two-saddle pairs. Persistence counts only 
pairs selected by the global elder rule. A local pair might fail to be selected because of another 
critical point, because the outward branch returns to the same component, or because the two 
upper sectors already belong to the same component.

The proof therefore requires more than a local Gaussian calculation. It needs a topological and 
dynamical selection theorem showing that failure is rare under the determinant-weighted contact 
Palm law. That is the most delicate part of the project and the part most in need of independent 
review.

15. Literature position
The closest located literature falls into three broad groups:

16. Exact scope and exclusions
The candidate theorem concerns only:

• ambient dimension 3;

• torus side 24;

• the normalized periodized Bargmann–Fock covariance (1.1);

• superlevel-set filtration;

• finite nonessential H 0 bars;

• first-moment lifetime density per unit ordinary volume;

• the fixed-side limit ℓ ↓0;

• the ordered maximum–index-two-saddle and directed-sphere conventions stated here.



It does not claim:

• any other side length;

• an arbitrary stationary or isotropic Gaussian field;

• arbitrary dimension;

• H k for k >0;

• a probability law for an individual randomly selected lifetime;

• a central limit theorem or concentration theorem;

• an infinite-volume persistence process;

• interchange of L→∞ and ℓ ↓0;

• a machine-certified nonlinear quadrature;

• publication readiness or community acceptance.
