1. The “topological second law” has supporting ingredients but

no direct test

The prediction that ΔJ = J_peaks − J_troughs becomes increasingly negative with cosmic

time rests on the asymmetry gravitational collapse imprints on the density field. Several

studies confirm the physical picture without testing the specific entropy diagnostic.

Wilding et al. (2021, MNRAS 507) is the single most relevant paper. They tracked Betti

curves and persistence diagrams of ΛCDM N-body simulations across multiple cosmic

epochs Oxford Academic  using superlevel set filtrations of DTFE density fields. arXiv +2

Their persistence diagrams show clear evolution from nearly Gaussian topology at high

redshift toward strongly hierarchical, non-Gaussian topology at late times. Oxford Academic

They noted “self-similar character” in the diagram apex evolution tied to hierarchical

structure formation. Scite  ResearchGate  Critically, they computed only superlevel

persistence — no sublevel counterpart.

Abedi, Jalali Kanafi & Movahed (2024, arXiv:2410.01751) provide the closest available

dataset. They computed superlevel Betti curves and persistent entropy on Quijote matter

density fields at z = 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 across Gaussian smoothing scales R = 5–30 Mpc/h.

arXiv  They found Betti curve amplitudes are surprisingly insensitive to redshift for z ∈ [0,

3] at fixed smoothing, but persistent entropy varies with both smoothing and redshift-space



distortions. arXiv  Again, sublevel filtration was not computed.

The classical genus curve asymmetry between overdense clusters and underdense voids

(James et al. 2009, 2dFGRS) confirms the underlying physics: gravitational collapse creates

sharp, concentrated peaks while voids expand and become diffuse. No paper has

quantified this asymmetry using persistence entropy or any persistence-diagram

summary at multiple redshifts. The concept of ΔJ as a monotonic tracker of gravitational

evolution is untested. The field has overwhelmingly used superlevel filtrations only, making

the superlevel-vs-sublevel comparison a genuine gap.

2. No prior art exists for a canonical ensemble on persistence

lifetimes

Extensive searching confirms that the thermodynamic formalism — defining Z(β) = Ʃᵢ
exp(β·ℓᵢ) on persistence lifetimes and deriving free energy, entropy, and heat capacity
— has no precedent in the TDA literature.

The closest related work falls into three categories:

Persistence entropy (Chintakunta et al. 2015; Rucco et al. 2016; Atienza et al. 2020)

computes Shannon entropy H = −Ʃ pᵢ log pᵢ from normalized lifetimes pᵢ = ℓᵢ/L. ScienceDirect
This is purely information-theoretic: it has no temperature parameter β, no partition

function, and no one-parameter family of distributions. The proposed thermodynamic

formalism would subsume persistence entropy as a β → 0 or microcanonical limit, making it

a strict generalization.

Persistence-weighted kernels use lifetime-dependent weights but none employ the

exponential form exp(β·ℓ). Kusano, Fukumizu & Hiraoka (2016/2017) use w(x) = arctan(C ·
pers(x)^p) — bounded, not exponential. Reininghaus et al. (2015) use a heat kernel in the

(birth, death) plane for smoothing, not a Boltzmann weight on lifetimes. Unc  Adams et al.

(2017) persistence images use piecewise-linear weights. JMLR +3  None define partition

functions, compute heat capacities, or offer thermodynamic interpretations.

Large deviation principles for persistence diagrams (Hirsch & Owada 2023+) use

moment generating functions and Fenchel-Legendre transforms that are structurally

analogous to statistical mechanics. Springer  However, these describe the statistics of

random persistence diagrams (from random filtrations), not a canonical ensemble defined
on lifetimes within a single diagram. The thermodynamic analogy is implicit in the LDP

mathematics but never made explicit.

Multiple papers apply TDA to physical phase transitions (e.g., detecting BKT transitions in



the XY model), American Physical Society  but the direction is reversed — they use topology to

study physics, not physics to study topology. The canonical ensemble formalism on

persistence diagrams appears genuinely novel.

3. Feldbrugge symmetry confirms ΔJ = 0 for Gaussian fields,

but the non-Gaussianity test is new

The Feldbrugge et al. (2019, JCAP 09:052) symmetry β_k(ν) = β_{D−1−k}(−ν) for Gaussian

fields is well-established arxiv +2  and has been verified and extended in subsequent work.

The symmetry extends to persistence diagrams. Pranav (2021, arXiv:2109.08721)

explicitly confirms: “Diagrams for all dimensions are triangular in shape, and exhibit a

symmetry. Diagram for islands is symmetric to the diagram for voids.” arXiv  arXiv  Under f

→ −f (which preserves Gaussian field distributions), a birth-death pair (b, d) for H_k in

superlevel filtration maps to (−d, −b) for H_{D−1−k}. This is stronger than Betti number

symmetry — the full persistence diagram structure is mirrored.

The symmetry implies J_peaks = J_troughs for Gaussian fields, since lifetimes ℓ = b − d
are preserved under the (b, d) → (−d, −b) transformation. The distribution of lifetimes for

H_k superlevel persistence equals that of H_{D−1−k} sublevel persistence. Since Pielou’s J

depends only on the lifetime distribution, ΔJ = 0 exactly in expectation for Gaussian

fields. This implication, while mathematically straightforward, has never been explicitly

stated in the literature.

No paper formalizes a non-Gaussianity test based on symmetry violation. Feldbrugge

et al. (2019) showed Betti numbers and persistence diagrams are sensitive to local f_NL,

HAL +3  and Cole & Shiu (2018) constrained f_NL from CMB persistence diagrams

(Δf_NL̂ loc = 35.8 at 68% CL). arXiv +2  Biagetti, Cole & Shiu (2021) detected f_NL = 10 at

97.5% confidence. arXiv +3  But none frame their tests explicitly as measuring violation of

β_k(ν) = β_{D−1−k}(−ν). The idea of constructing a scalar diagnostic — ΔJ — from the

symmetry violation is novel as a formalized test statistic, though the underlying sensitivity of

topological statistics to non-Gaussianity is well-documented.

About 15–20 papers cite arXiv:1908.01619, primarily within the Pranav/van de

Weygaert/Biagetti/Cole/Shiu network. None construct an explicit symmetry-violation test.

5. Exponential lifetime weightings exist but never as a partition

function

The persistence kernel literature uses various weighting schemes on lifetimes for machine

learning, but none employ the specific exponential form exp(β·ℓᵢ) as a Boltzmann
weight or derive thermodynamic quantities from it.



Kusano et al. (2016, JMLR 18) NSF PAGES  use arctan weights w_arc(x) = arctan(C ·

pers(x)^p) JMLR  — bounded and sub-exponential. Reininghaus et al. (2015, CVPR) use

Gaussian kernels in the full (birth, death) plane with a scale parameter σ controlling

smoothing, not temperature. Unc  The heat equation connection in their persistence scale

space kernel is suggestive but mathematically distinct from a canonical ensemble: σ

controls spatial smoothing in the diagram plane, not inverse temperature weighting lifetimes

as energies. Adams et al. (2017) persistence images use piecewise-linear weights growing

linearly from the diagonal. JMLR +2

Divol & Polonik (2019, J. Appl. Comput. Topology) systematically studied weight functions w

for linear representations, Springer  analyzing polynomial weights w(x) = pers(x)^α

Springer  but not exponential weights. The exponential weight exp(β·ℓ) would be
unbounded and require separate convergence analysis.

The key conceptual distinction: existing kernel methods treat weights as fixed

preprocessing (choosing one weight function to upweight persistent features), while the

canonical ensemble treats β as a continuous parameter generating a one-parameter family

of probability distributions, enabling derivatives (entropy, heat capacity) that probe the

lifetime distribution’s structure.

6. Smoothing-scale “RG flow” is partially observed but never

formalized

Several papers track topological statistics across smoothing scales, but no paper frames

this as renormalization group flow or identifies fixed points.

Pranav et al. (2022, A&A) performed the most systematic multiscale analysis, computing

Betti numbers of Planck CMB maps at eight FWHM scales from 5′ to 640′. They found

approximate consistency with Gaussian simulations at most scales, with anomalous

deviations peaking near ~80′ (~3.66°). This identifies a specific crossover scale where non-

Gaussian deviations are maximally detectable, though without RG interpretation.

Abedi et al. (2024) compared Betti curves across six Gaussian smoothing scales (R = 5–30

Mpc/h) on Quijote density fields. arXiv  They observed convergence/stabilization of Betti

number variations and persistent entropy at R ≳ 30 Mpc/h, arXiv  consistent with

Gaussianization of the smoothed field. This is implicit fixed-point behavior — the central

limit theorem guarantees that sufficient smoothing drives any field toward Gaussianity — but

is never identified as such.

Pranav et al. (2019, MNRAS 485) established the Gaussian random field Betti curves as a

function of spectral index, finding they are sensitive to the power spectrum shape HAL



(unlike the Euler characteristic). ADS  Jfeldbrugge  The white-noise case (n = 0) stands out

as a divide between positive and negative spectral index models, potentially marking a

critical point in spectral parameter space. The formal analogy to RG flow — with the

Gaussian field topology as an IR fixed point, crossover scales depending on the non-

Gaussian field’s properties, and β-functions describing the approach — appears to be

entirely novel.

7. Prat et al. DES Y3 uses Betti numbers only; ΔJ would require

pipeline extension

The Prat et al. (2026, MNRAS 545) paper analyzes DES Y3 weak lensing convergence maps

using superlevel set filtration via the TopoS2 tool (developed by Pranav). Oxford Academic

arXiv  Their summary statistics are Betti numbers β₀(ν) and β₁(ν) and persistent Betti

numbers, combined with second moments σ_κ. arXiv  They achieve constraints 70%

tighter than two-point statistics in the Ω_m–S₈ plane, with S₈ = 0.821 ± 0.018. arXiv

They do not compute persistence entropy, persistence images, persistence landscapes,

Pielou’s J, or any entropy-based diagnostic. They use only superlevel filtration — no

sublevel counterpart.

ΔJ could be computed from their data with modest effort. The DES Y3 convergence

maps (Jeffrey et al. 2021) are publicly available. Sublevel filtration is obtained by negating

the map (κ → −κ) and rerunning the superlevel computation. Persistence entropy can be

derived from any persistence diagram. The main barrier is access to TopoS2 (not publicly

released), though standard TDA libraries (GUDHI, Ripser) could substitute Wikipedia  for

flat-sky patches. Oxford Academic  Their neural-network-compressed data vector would

need to be replaced with raw persistence diagrams, but the underlying mass maps are

accessible.

Predecessor work by Heydenreich et al. (2022, A&A 667) applied PH to DES Y1 aperture

8. Information geometry of persistence: the Fisher-heat

capacity connection is novel

No paper defines a Fisher information metric on a parametric family of persistence



diagram distributions in the sense needed for the heat capacity identification.

The closest work is the Persistence Fisher kernel (Le & Yamada, NeurIPS 2018), which

represents each persistence diagram as a Gaussian mixture Google Sites  ρ_D(x) = (1/|D|) Ʃ

N(x; p, σ²I) and computes the Fisher information distance between two such mixture

representations. This uses Fisher geometry to compare individual persistence diagrams
(treated as distributions on ℝ²), not to define a Fisher metric on a parametric family P_θ of
persistence diagram distributions. Frontiers  The distinction is fundamental: Le & Yamada

measure distance between two data points, while the proposed framework defines

curvature of a model manifold.

Edelsbrunner, Virk & Wagner (2019, arXiv:1903.08510) compute persistent homology

using information-theoretic distances (Fisher metric, KL divergence) as the ambient metric

arXiv  — the reverse direction. Turner, Mukherjee & Boyer (2014) noted that exponential

family models can be defined on the Euler Characteristic Transform but called the

analogous construction for persistence diagrams “problematic.” arXiv  A 2025 preprint

(arXiv:2511.11996) models persistence bar events using exponential distributions in a

Bayesian latent-position model arXiv  — the closest to “exponential family on persistence

lifetimes” — but does not compute Fisher information or connect to thermodynamics.

the canonical ensemble to the heat capacity of the persistence diagram, appears

information, variance — are elementary, but their assembly into a thermodynamic-

information-geometric interpretation of TDA has no precedent.

Conclusion: a well-positioned gap with strong existing

scaffolding

Across all eight verification targets, the pattern is consistent: the individual ingredients

are established, but the specific synthesis is novel. The Feldbrugge symmetry provides a

Gaussian null Perimeterinstitute  arXiv  (ΔJ = 0). Perimeterinstitute  arXiv  Persistence

entropy exists but lacks a temperature parameter. ScienceDirect  Topological evolution

across redshifts has been tracked but only in superlevel filtration. arXiv  ResearchGate

Kernel methods weight lifetimes but never exponentially. JMLR  Information geometry

touches TDA but never through canonical ensembles.

The three strongest novelty claims are: (1) ΔJ as a scalar non-Gaussianity diagnostic

derived from the Feldbrugge symmetry, (2) the canonical partition function Z(β) on



persistence lifetimes with derived heat capacity, and (3) the smoothing-scale RG-flow

analogy. The most immediate computational opportunity is the Abedi et al. (2024) Quijote

pipeline at z = 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 with superlevel filtration arXiv  — extending it to sublevel

filtration would directly test the topological second law. The Prat et al. DES Y3 convergence

maps offer a path to observational validation, arXiv  requiring only map negation and

persistence entropy computation on publicly available data.
