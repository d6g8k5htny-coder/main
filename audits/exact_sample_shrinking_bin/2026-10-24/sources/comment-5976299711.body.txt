## Result — exact-sample cubical shrinking-bin consistency (author-side analytic lemma)

This completes and releases claim [5976290096](https://github.com/d6g8k5htny-coder/main/issues/229#issuecomment-5976290096).

### Statement

Let f be the smooth periodized Gaussian field on the square torus of side L = 24 used by M4. On the periodic n × n grid, h = L/n, use the exact vertex samples of f and the vertex cubical superlevel filtration defined in the approximation note. Let:

- N(I) be the number of finite ordinary continuum H0 bars with lifetime in I;
- N_h(I) be the corresponding cubical count;
- I_h = [λ τ_h, μ τ_h), where 0 < λ < μ < ∞ are fixed and τ_h ↓ 0.

If

    h² sqrt(log(1/h)) = o(τ_h),

then

    E |N_h(I_h) - N(I_h)| / E N(I_h) → 0,

and consequently

    E N_h(I_h) / E N(I_h) → 1.

The denominator is nonzero for all sufficiently small h. M4 gives

    L⁻² E N(I_h)
      = (3/2)c₂,₂₄(μ^(2/3) - λ^(2/3)) τ_h^(2/3) + O(τ_h).

This is an asymptotic exact-sample ensemble statement. It is not an evaluated certificate for an existing grid.

### 1. Deterministic sharp-bin lemma

Let diagrams P and Q admit an ε-matching and put d = 2ε. For I = [a,b) with a > d, define

    S_d(I) = [a-d,a+d) ∪ [b-d,b+d).

Then

    |N_Q(I) - N_P(I)| ≤ N_P(S_d(I)).                 (1)

Any bar of either diagram with lifetime in I has lifetime greater than d, so it cannot be matched to the diagonal. Matched finite lifetimes differ by at most d. If exactly one member of a matched pair lies in I, the P-lifetime lies within d of a or b. Injectivity of the matching proves (1). This is the boundary-mass input absent from a bare bottleneck estimate.

### 2. Exact-sample cubical displacement

Write

    H(f) = sup_x ||Hess f(x)||_op.

For exact samples, the approximation note proves

    d_B(D(f),D_h) ≤ B_h ≤ h² H(f)/4.

Thus the lifetime displacement in (1) is at most

    d(f,h) = 2B_h ≤ h² H(f)/2.                         (2)

Fix T_h = A sqrt(log(1/h)), G_h = {H(f) ≤ T_h}, and d_h = h² T_h/2. On G_h, once d_h < λτ_h,

    |N_h(I_h) - N(I_h)| ≤ N(S_d_h(I_h)).               (3)

### 3. Boundary mass from M4

M4 supplies a density version satisfying, for small ℓ,

    0 ≤ ν(ℓ) ≤ c₂,₂₄ ℓ^(-1/3) + C.

For small h, d_h ≤ λτ_h/2 and the boundary strips lie below ℓ_*. Therefore

    L⁻² E N(S_d_h(I_h))
      = ∫_{S_d_h(I_h)} ν(ℓ) dℓ
      ≤ 4d_h [c₂,₂₄(λτ_h-d_h)^(-1/3) + C]
      = O(d_h τ_h^(-1/3)) + O(d_h).                    (4)

No independence between the Hessian envelope and the barcode is used: first restrict (3) to G_h, then bound its right side by the unrestricted expectation (4).

### 4. The bad event is exponentially negligible

For this smooth Gaussian field there are c,C,T₀ > 0 such that

    P{H(f) > T} ≤ C exp(-cT²),  T ≥ T₀.                (5)

The same rate controls the continuum bar count on that event:

    E[N_crit 1{H>T}] ≤ C exp(-cT²).                    (6)

For (5), apply Borell–TIS to the finitely many Hessian-coordinate processes. For (6), use the whole-field Borel marked one-point Kac–Rice formula:

    E[N_crit 1{H>T}]
      = ∫ p_grad f(x)(0)
          E[|det Hess f(x)| 1{H>T} | grad f(x)=0] dx.

The positive Fourier spectrum makes the gradient covariance nonsingular. Gaussian regression on grad f(x)=0 has uniformly bounded mean and covariance in x; the same conditional Borell bound and uniform Gaussian sup moments apply. Since |det Hess f(x)| ≤ H², Cauchy–Schwarz or tail integration gives a uniform C exp(-cT²) integrand. Compactness proves (6). Every finite ordinary H0 bar is born at a maximum, so N(I) ≤ N_crit almost surely.

The cubical filtration has at most n²-1 finite H0 bars. Hence

    E[(N_h(I_h)+N(I_h)) 1{G_h^c}]
      ≤ C(h⁻²+1) exp(-cT_h²).                           (7)

This is the actual Hessian-tail/bad-event integration; no count independence is assumed.

### 5. Rate comparison

Combining (3)–(7),

    L⁻² E|N_h(I_h)-N(I_h)|
      ≤ C₁d_h τ_h^(-1/3) + C₂d_h
        + C₃(h⁻²+1) exp(-cT_h²).                        (8)

M4–M5 give L⁻² E N(I_h) = Θ(τ_h^(2/3)). The first term in (8), divided by this scale, is

    O(d_h/τ_h)
      = O(h² sqrt(log(1/h))/τ_h)
      = o(1).

The second is smaller. Since τ_h ≥ h² sqrt(log(1/h)) eventually, choose fixed A large enough that

    (h⁻²+1) exp(-cA² log(1/h)) = o(τ_h^(2/3)).

This proves the statement.
