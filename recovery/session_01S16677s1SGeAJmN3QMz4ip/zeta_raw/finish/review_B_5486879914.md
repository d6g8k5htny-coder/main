### ZF-B-01 — Selberg's observable does not establish the selected amplitude law

CLAIMS provenance “log-normal amplitudes”; README §6.2, lines 811–813. [Radziwiłł–Soundararajan, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.06827) treats log|ζ(1/2+it)| for Lebesgue-uniform t in [T,2T], normalized by sqrt((1/2)log log T). It does not establish the distribution of per-lobe maxima, the nearby-lobe median, contact-conditioned amplitudes, or the persistence coefficient.

Minimal replacement: “Selberg's CLT describes log|ζ| at uniformly sampled heights. An analogous law for these selected local amplitudes, or slow drift of the bar coefficient, is heuristic and is not supplied by that theorem.” Keep the amplitude-law provenance qualified.

### ZF-B-03 — narrow the Euler-product inference

CLAIMS 4.6, final sentence. [Polymath15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12438), the deformed Dirichlet coefficients used in its approximation, gives b_n^t = exp((t/4)(log n)^2). Directly,
b_mn^t/(b_m^t b_n^t) = exp((t/2)log m log n),
which differs from 1 for coprime m,n > 1 and real t ≠ 0.

This establishes failure of the **usual multiplicative coefficient Euler factorization for that deformed Dirichlet series**. It does not establish a theorem excluding every possible Euler-product representation of H_t. Replace the broad “destroys the Euler product” sentence with this narrower statement, and distinguish the approximation's series from H_t itself.

### Supported source checks and minimal provenance updates

1. **Lagarias–Montague: supported; replace the second-hand/unverified tag.** [Equations (1.2), (1.6), (1.9), Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.4348) concern zeros of their normalized primitive. In their notation the exact conversion is H_t(z) = Ξ_{t/4}(z/2)/8 and, with F_t(0)=0, F_t(z) = Ξ_{t/4}^{(−1)}(z/2)/4. The theorem gives finitely many real and infinitely many nonreal zeros of that primitive for each real parameter; for parameter ≤0, its only real zero is 0. These are **primitive zeros**, not the derivative zeros that give the packet's critical points. An arbitrary added constant changes the zero-set statement. Add the scaling and theorem pointer; no persistence or novelty conclusion follows from this citation.
