## Checked local calculation

Writing sigma = sqrt(tau), the scaled heat solution is

    h(y,sigma) = A(y²−2) + sigma B(y³−6y)
                 + sigma² C(y⁴−12y²+12) + O(sigma³).

The positive root has expansion

    y+ = sqrt(2) + (2B/A)sigma
         + sqrt(2)(2C/A−B²/A²)sigma² + O(sigma³).

The symmetry y−(sigma)=−y+(−sigma) implies analytic midpoint and r²/(8tau) in tau. Thus

    midpoint − z0 = (2B/A)tau + O(tau²),
    kappa_r = 4C/A−2B²/A².

For midpoint m and centered coordinate s, use the exact endpoint identity

    integral H = −(1/2) integral (r²/4−s²) H'' ds.

The moments are r³/6, 0 and r⁵/120. Since

    H'' = 2A+6B(m+s)+C(12(m+s)²−24tau)+higher orders,
    m = (2B/A)tau+O(tau²), r² = 8tau(1+O(tau)),

the coefficient of tau in the lobe ratio is

    kappa_ell = 6B²/A²−48C/(5A),
    kappa_t = kappa_ell+(3/2)kappa_r
            = 3B²/A²−18C/(5A).

Evenness in sigma removes half-integer corrections and supplies the stated O(tau²) relative remainders. Analyticity and A≠0 give bounded, uniform local Taylor remainders on a fixed smaller neighborhood. A real-analytic germ has a local complexification, so the conjugate-pair conclusion on the negative-time side is justified locally. Multiplicity three is outside these hypotheses.

An independent SymPy calculation (written by this reviewer, not imported from project code) solved the two root coefficients, integrated the kernel moments, and returned exactly these expressions. This is algebraic verification, not an execution of `test_exact.py` or a formal kernel proof.

## Required amendments

### ZF-AD-01: C1 normalization remainder is false

README lines 420–424 and 833–836 claim that any positive C1 weight gives ell_w = w(x0)ell(1+O(r²)). This regularity is insufficient even for the exact symmetric fold.

Take H(t,x)=x²−2t, a=sqrt(2t), r=2a, and w(x)=1+|x|^(1+eta), with 0<eta<1. This is a fixed positive C1 weight. Direct integration gives

    ell = 4a³/3,
    ell_w/ell = 1 + 3a^(1+eta)/((2+eta)(4+eta)).

The relative correction is not O(r²). For eta=1/2 it is (4/15)a^(3/2). A fixed interval containing the local window makes both quantities actual bars for sufficiently small a, so this is not just a pairing counterexample.

Repair by requiring bounded second derivative (or C1,1) on a fixed nondegenerate fold neighborhood and stating the needed controlled fold asymmetry, or weaken the error term to what the actual C1 assumptions establish. Odd-linear cancellation is not enough to bound a general C1 Taylor remainder by O(r²). Any exponent/coefficient-transfer conclusion additionally needs uniform positive-weight and marked-measure hypotheses; a pointwise reweighting formula alone is insufficient across height-dependent ensembles.

### ZF-AD-02: finite-window estimates are stated as a settled exponent and calibrated total-error result

README lines 36–44, 54, 620–639, 718–725 and 851–852; CLAIMS 5.4; PR-body headline. The clauses “decisive against −1/3,” “far larger than any error source,” and “the zeta short-bar density exponent is about 0” exceed the displayed evidence. The displayed intervals are sampling-only under the chosen block bootstrap; finite-cutoff bias, dependence, estimator misspecification, post-inspection selection, joint gap/mark behavior and a limiting measure have not been bounded. Sensitivity across three block sizes is useful but is not a validated coverage theorem for this deterministic sequence.

The controls are not certified total-error calibrators for this observable. Fixed-N CUE's full bar law is explicitly the unproved T-CUE target, while the exact Gaussian primitive's theorem hypotheses are explicitly not checked (README 637–639). Label 0 and −1/3 as theoretical expectations under the relevant conditions, rather than established answers for these precise controls used to bound zeta's bias.

Suggested headline: “For the specified Hardy-Z primitive, windows and estimator, the observed finite-tail estimates are substantially above −1/3 and near 0. Sampling-only bootstrap intervals exclude −1/3 at the reported cutoffs. This is exploratory evidence about that finite-window statistic; it does not establish a limiting density exponent or a calibrated rejection accounting for all errors.”

Carry the existing D1 caveats into the summary, not just the final paragraph. Pair correlation is not a nearest-neighbor law or an actual-persistence-pair law. Montgomery explicitly separates the two-point conjecture and consecutive-spacing conjecture (primary paper pp. 184–185). The random sine process has the stated leading nearest-gap behavior, but transferring it to zeta and then to bars requires additional assumptions.

The negative mark moment in CLAIMS 5.4 must be under the limiting collision-mark measure, with the joint-law/envelope/selection hypotheses already cited. Finiteness of an ordinary marginal moment is insufficient: if r has density 3r² on (0,1), k=r and ell=k r³=r⁴, then E[k^−1]=3/2 is finite but the exact lifetime density is (3/4)ell^−1/4. This does not contradict D1; it shows why the summary cannot omit its hypotheses.

The numerical primitive is built from Hardy Z with unfolding/amplitude choices and endpoint-born bars dropped. It is not the xi-weighted primitive or a positive/negative-time de Bruijn–Newman barcode. The packet usefully distinguishes them later; preserve that distinction in all headline statements.

### ZF-AD-03: complete the local persistence statement's domain and proof conventions

README lines 262–294 assumes H only on a neighborhood of (t0,z0), then defines an integral from any z* and a barcode on any closed interval I containing the local window. Those objects need not exist under the local-germ hypothesis. Either choose a compact I inside a domain where H is defined and F is analytic, with z* there, or assume an explicitly defined continuous tame extension of the local primitive to compact I. State the coefficient field and superlevel interval convention once.

With that completion, the elder argument is sound: for A>0, the local maximum's component is trapped inside W above the local minimum, while the component through the right side already contains a point of strictly higher value. It therefore kills the new younger bar at the local minimum. For A<0, the same argument uses the left side. Merely replacing H by −H swaps superlevel and sublevel persistence; add that left-side argument or use simultaneous reflection H(t,x)↦−H(t,−x), F(t,x)↦F(t,−x) to preserve superlevels.

This establishes the local bar for small tau on a fixed surrounding interval, not arbitrary close pairs or pairing at t=0. The supplied boundary countercontrol is correct: for F=x³/3−x on [−3/2,3/2], the finite superlevel bar is 7/24, although the critical-value gap is 4/3. The triple-zero and information-loss countercontrols retain their stated roles.

Also write r²~8tau or r²=8tau(1+O(tau)) in README line 383 and the PR summary. The exact equality r²=8tau is generally false, as the verified nonzero kappa_r itself demonstrates.

## Informational classical clarification: positive versus zero threshold

CLAIMS 6.2's missing local-uniformity input can be supplied from already-established literature. Polymath15 Theorem 1.5(i), pp. 9–10, has an absolute C and states that for 0<t≤1/2 every zero with real part x≥exp(C/t) is real. If Lambda>0, this is uniform for t∈[Lambda/2,Lambda]. Together with symmetry and the strip bound, choose nonreal roots for t_n increasing to Lambda; they stay in a fixed compact set and a subsequence converges to a real zero of H_Lambda. If that zero were simple, unique real-analytic continuation would force nearby roots to be real, a contradiction. Thus a positive Lambda forces a finite multiple real zero. This is a deduction from existing global estimates, not from the local fold and not a new RH result.

It does not establish a nondegenerate double zero: multiplicity may be higher. It does not give a uniform compact bound at Lambda=0, because exp(C/t) diverges. Do not replace “all multiple-zero events” by “generic folds” in an equivalence, or infer that the zero threshold must be achieved at finite height. Keep RH/no-progress language intact.
