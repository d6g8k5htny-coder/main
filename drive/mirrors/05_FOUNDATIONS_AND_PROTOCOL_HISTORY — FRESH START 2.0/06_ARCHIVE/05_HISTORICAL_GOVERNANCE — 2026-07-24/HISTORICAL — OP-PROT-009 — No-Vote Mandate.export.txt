This document is the sole live governance rule for this project. The project does not use votes, ballots, consensus thresholds, majorities, unanimity, quorums, denominators, eligible voting rosters, observer status, or non-participant status. Prior voting mechanisms, including OP-PROT-002, OP-PROT-003, voting portions of OP-PROT-008, ballot packages, ballot requests, tally records, and participation restrictions, are repealed and superseded for live governance.


Historical receipts may preserve descriptions of prior votes or participation states as provenance. Historical language has no current governing force.

3. FULL-PARALLEL, REVIEW-AFTER MODEL


No action requires approval from another model or from a vote. Contributors work in parallel and record what they did, the evidence used, the limits of the result, and any unresolved uncertainty. Dylan Roy reviews after the fact and may ratify, revise, reverse, narrow, broaden, or retire any action or record.


A model's action, inaction, presence, absence, provider identity, session identity, or response timing never blocks another model or the project. Absence is not recorded as pending, nonparticipating, observer, or nonvoting status.


4. OPERATOR AUTHORITY


Dylan Roy is the single final authority for this project. His direct instruction supersedes any conflicting document, record, standard, protocol, ballot, prior rule, model instruction, or automation state in the Drive.


Irreversible terminal actions remain operator-authorized actions: final promotion of a result to canonical status, external release, permanent deletion, and machine-root replacement. The operator may direct work to proceed regardless of any recommended practice.


No contributor may impersonate Dylan Roy, fabricate an operator decision, or sign a record as Dylan Roy. Records executed under an operator directive must identify the executing contributor and the directive.


5. EVIDENCE, REVIEW, AND TESTING AS NON-BLOCKING PRACTICE


Source hashing, byte-exact publication, independent review as evidence, reconstruction, negative controls, injection and revocation tests, retention tests, recovery and rollback tests, runtime acceptance, shadow pilots, formal verification, and reproducibility checks are strongly recommended good practice.


These practices are not blocking gates. They never require a vote. They inform Dylan Roy's after-the-fact review and improve confidence, but they do not stop parallel research, drafting, testing, formalization, or record creation. A contributor must describe unperformed or failed checks honestly rather than treating them as silently passed.


6. CLAIM STANDING AND RESEARCH TRUTH


Contribution means producing evidence, derivations, reviews, corrections, formal proofs, numerical tests, closures, and accurate records—not casting votes. Repetition, tallies, model count, or provider count do not establish truth.


A mathematical or scientific claim gains evidentiary standing from its assumptions, derivation, proof, reproducible computation, falsification resistance, and clearly stated scope. Canonical promotion occurs only by Dylan Roy through the designated canonical ledger or an explicit operator record.


Conflicting research claims are preserved and labeled until evidence or operator adjudication resolves them. Governance authority must not be used to manufacture mathematical truth.


7. FORMALIZATION, VERSION CONTROL, AND TELEMETRY


Formalization, source-controlled verification, telemetry, hygiene, triage, record templates, and non-destructive organization are routine pre-authorized contributor activities. Formal artifacts must accurately state proof status, including PROVEN, SORRY-PARTIAL, or NUMERICAL-ONLY. A machine-checked formal companion is authoritative for its own formal status, not automatically for canonical mathematical promotion.


The Drive remains the system of record for governance, provenance, manuscripts, and operator decisions. Git may serve as the system of record for formal artifacts and their commit integrity. Cross-links must preserve both identities.

No ballot, tally, or consensus record may be used as a live prerequisite. No model's review is automatically independent merely because it has a different label; lineage should be disclosed as evidence context, not as a blocking eligibility rule.
