1. WHY THIS AMENDS MY OWN RECORD
============================================================================


CL-PREFLIGHT-221 finding M1 established that export identities must be corroborated by two independent transcription passes. Further testing shows that rule is NECESSARY BUT NOT SUFFICIENT, and I would rather correct it myself than have it fail quietly inside a root computation later.


Independent passes do not make independent errors. They make CORRELATED errors — different sessions corrupt the SAME character positions. Agreement between passes is therefore weaker evidence than M1 implied, and majority voting across passes does not repair the defect.

============================================================================
3. THE RESULT THAT MATTERS
============================================================================


Majority-of-three voting, with cryptographic acceptance (gunzip plus declared SHA-256), FAILED on both payloads.


Source: the majority string still differs from ground truth at position 551, because F and G outvote H there. Receipt: the majority still differs at 453 and at 1022 — and 1022 was corrupted identically in ALL THREE independent passes.


Three sessions, no shared context, same character position, same wrong character. The errors are not noise around a correct value; they are a systematic property of the position. Voting cannot fix an error every voter makes.


Consequences, stated plainly:


(a) M1's dual-pass rule stands as a MINIMUM, not a guarantee. Two agreeing passes exclude sparse independent noise. They do NOT exclude correlated corruption, and I now have direct evidence that correlated corruption occurs.


(b) Majority voting across N passes must NOT be used to establish an identity. It is unsound here regardless of N, and it fails silently — it produces a confident wrong answer, which is the worst failure mode available to a records system.


(c) Where an object offers a CONTENT-ADDRESSED check — an embedded checksum, a gzip CRC, a declared hash of decompressed content — that check is decisive and should be preferred over any transported hash. It is the only method in this environment that cannot be fooled by correlated transcription error.


(d) Where no content-addressed check exists, an export hash rests on multi-pass agreement with a stated residual risk. It should not be treated as equivalent evidence to a content-addressed identity, and a manifest should record which of the two kinds of binding each object carries.


Bearing on the 17 identities already frozen: they remain sound, and I am not withdrawing them. They agreed byte-for-byte across independent passes over entire documents — ten to twenty thousand base64 characters each, zero divergences. The correlated failures observed here occur specifically in base64-of-gzip, which is incompressible and unpredictable, whereas base64 of ordinary prose is partly reconstructable and reproduced perfectly. But the honest characterisation is now "strong evidence," not "proof," and the distinction belongs in the manifest rather than in my confidence.

I accept the observation. I cannot corroborate the explanation, and the specific truncation mechanism is excluded by evidence:


- A second full read taken AFTER that record was written still renders the Transition Register at exactly 68 rows, and the identifiers TR-P01-019A, TR-P01-019-GATE-A, and VOID-DUPLICATE-TR-P01-021 appear ZERO times across the entire 1,333,941-character workbook.
- Between the two reads the register changed substantially: eleven sheets gained rows, one new sheet appeared, and the render grew by 88,587 characters. The Transition Register still rendered exactly 68 rows. A size-driven truncation would not hold a single sheet at a constant boundary while the workbook around it grows.
- There is no per-sheet row cap at 68: another sheet in the same read renders 311 rows.
- There is no global budget exhaustion at that point: fifteen further sheets and roughly 518,000 characters render after it, and the final sheet ends on a complete row.


A mechanism that fits BOTH observations: rows 69–71 exist but are HIDDEN or otherwise filtered from the sheet's rendered view, positioned after row 68. A bounded cell-range read would return them; a rendering read would not. Every row I did observe is numbered contiguously 1 to 68, and row 68 is TR-P01-021 exactly as GP-COR-143 declares — so hidden rows appended after 68 are consistent with everything on both sides.


If that is what happened, it is worth more than a footnote. Hidden void-duplicate rows in a live authoritative register are invisible to any reviewer using a normal read path. That is precisely how this discrepancy arose, and it will recur for the next independent reviewer, who will also report rows missing. RECOMMENDATION: confirm whether rows 69–71 are hidden, and if so either unhide them or record the hidden-row condition on the register's own surface, so the next reader is not sent down this path again.


C1 is therefore reclassified from "absent rows" to OPEN — MECHANISM UNCONFIRMED. I am not asserting the rows are missing. I am declining to record the truncation explanation as established, because two full reads and four structural tests contradict it.
