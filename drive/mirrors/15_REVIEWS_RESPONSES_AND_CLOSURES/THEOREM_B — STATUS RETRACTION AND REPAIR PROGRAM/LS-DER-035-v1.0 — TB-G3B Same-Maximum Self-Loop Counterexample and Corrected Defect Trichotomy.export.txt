2. EXACT SETTING


Let f be a Morse function on a compact connected orientable surface. Assume distinct critical values and a Morse–Smale gradient flow. Let M be a local maximum and S an index-one saddle. Write


    b=f(M),    h=f(S),    h<b.


Let A(M,S) be the event that the selected ascending separatrix of S converges to M. Let E(M,S) be the event that S is the superlevel-set H0 elder-rule death saddle of the component born at M.


For t not a critical value, let C_M(t) denote the component of {f>=t} containing M, whenever that component has already been born and has not yet died.


LS-DER-024 defines a counted-witness event B from:
(i) any extra critical point in the radius-2r collars, with no value restriction; or
(ii) any critical point outside the collars with value in the open window (h,b).


Its Lemma 2.1 asserts


    A(M,S) ∩ E(M,S)^c  ⊂  B.                                  (2-LS)


The asserted Case III mechanism is that every H0 self-merge at S has another attained mountain-pass saddle y with


    h < f(y) < b.                                               (MP)


C102 asserts the same strict-window conclusion.


3. THREE TOPOLOGICAL CLASSES


Define P(M,S), G(M,S), and L_same(M,S) on A(M,S) as follows.


P(M,S): the component C_M undergoes an H0 merge at an index-one saddle y before the filtration reaches level h. Equivalently, there is a death/merge event for C_M at a critical value in (h,b).


G(M,S): immediately above h, the two upper local sectors of S lie in distinct superlevel components, so S is a genuine H0 merge saddle.


L_same(M,S): immediately above h, the two upper local sectors of S lie in the same superlevel component; there is no prior H0 merge of C_M in (h,b); and the two ascending separatrices of S converge to the same maximum M.


The last event is the same-maximum self-loop class. Crossing S attaches a one-handle to a single H0 component. It may change H1 or boundary topology while leaving H0 unchanged.


4. CORRECTED DETERMINISTIC TRICHOTOMY


THEOREM 4.1.


On the Morse–Smale, distinct-critical-value event,


    A(M,S) ∩ E(M,S)^c
      ⊂ P(M,S) ∪ G_young(M,S) ∪ L_same(M,S),                   (T)


where G_young is the subevent of G on which the component attached to the other upper sector has elder maximum N satisfying


    h < f(N) < b.


PROOF.


Assume A and E^c.


Case I: C_M has an H0 merge at some level y before h is reached. Since C_M is born at b, the first such merge has value in (h,b). Thus P occurs.


Case II: no such prior H0 merge occurs and S is a genuine H0 merge. Immediately above h, C_M is still the component born at M. Let C_2 be the other component and let N be its elder maximum. Gradient ascent gives f(N)>h. If f(N)>b, then C_M is younger and the elder rule kills it at S, contradicting E^c. Distinct critical values exclude f(N)=b. Hence h<f(N)<b, so G_young occurs.


Case III: no prior H0 merge occurs and S is an H0 self-merge. One ascending separatrix reaches M by A. If the other ascending separatrix reaches a different maximum N, then N lies in the same superlevel component as M immediately above h. Since no prior H0 merge of C_M occurred in (h,b), that component identification must already hold at the birth of the younger of M and N. If f(N)<b, N itself is a strict-window maximum and the configuration belongs to the counted G_young-type witness class. If f(N)>b, the component born at M was attached to an older component before h, contradicting the no-prior-merge assumption. Equality is excluded. Therefore the only unaccounted self-merge possibility is that both ascending separatrices converge to M, namely L_same.


The cases exhaust the possibilities. QED.


REMARK 4.2.


The statement intentionally separates L_same. A mountain-pass saddle for an “alternative path” is not forced to be distinct from S. In L_same, the minimax level of paths joining the two upper sectors can equal h and be attained by S itself. Compactness gives attainment, but not a second critical point and not strict inequality above h.


5. COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE STRICT-WINDOW CLAIM


THEOREM 5.1.


There exists a Morse–Smale function with distinct critical values on the torus, and critical points M,S satisfying A(M,S) ∩ E(M,S)^c ∩ L_same(M,S), such that there is no critical point y with h<f(y)<b. In particular, (MP), (2-LS), and the theorem stated in C102 are false under their declared hypotheses.


PROOF BY HANDLE DECOMPOSITION.


Use a superlevel handle decomposition of T^2 with:
- two 0-handles born at maxima M_0 and M, with f(M_0)>b=f(M);
- three index-one handles at saddles S, S_0, S_1, ordered
      b > h=f(S) > f(S_0) > f(S_1);
- one 2-handle at the minimum.


Choose the attaching maps as follows.


First, the handle at S attaches both of its feet to the boundary of the 0-handle born at M. Crossing S changes that component from a disk to an annulus. It is a self-attachment to one connected component, hence not an H0 merge. Both ascending separatrices of S terminate at M.


Second, the handle at S_0 joins the M component to the older component born at M_0. At S_0 the elder rule kills the component born at M because f(M_0)>f(M).


The remaining handle S_1 and the minimum complete the torus. This is a valid handle decomposition because the critical counts are


    #maxima - #saddles + #minima = 2 - 3 + 1 = 0 = χ(T^2).


The handle-realization theorem gives a Morse function with these critical values and attaching data. Choose a gradient-like field realizing the attaching maps and perturb it within the same handle-incidence class to be Morse–Smale; equivalently choose a compatible metric. The topological conclusions are stable under a sufficiently small perturbation, and the critical values remain distinct.


There is no critical value in (h,b): the only critical points at or above h in the M component are M and S. The event A holds, S is a self-merge, and E fails because M dies later at S_0. Thus


    A(M,S) ∩ E(M,S)^c


holds while the counted event B of LS-DER-024 does not: there is no extra collar critical point and no outside-collar critical point with value in (h,b), after choosing the construction with the remaining critical points outside the collars.


This disproves the asserted deterministic inclusion.


FIXED-METRIC REALIZATION.


The counterexample is not an artifact of choosing a special metric. The handle attachment and strict critical-value ordering are open C^2 properties. On the flat torus, construct the same level-set handle decomposition by local Morse charts and smooth interpolation. A generic arbitrarily small C^2 perturbation makes the flat-metric gradient Morse–Smale while preserving the self-attachment, the later elder merge, and the empty open critical-value window. Hence the counterexample can be realized for the gradient associated with the fixed flat metric.


QED.


6. COMPATIBILITY WITH A SHORT PINNED PAIR


The defect is global and is not removed merely by taking d(M,S)=r small or b-h=κr^3/6.


The local maximum–saddle chart can be inserted into an arbitrarily small disk, with the direct M-ward separatrix contained in that disk. The second separatrix can leave the disk, traverse the preassigned annular route, and return to M. Smooth coordinate and amplitude rescaling can prescribe a positive separation r and positive gap κr^3/6 while preserving critical types and the global handle incidence. After the rescaling, the remaining critical values can still be placed below h.


Therefore the deterministic failure is compatible with the qualitative small-r geometry used by LS-DER-024. This does not prove that L_same has positive or large probability under the exact Gaussian Palm law. It proves that topology alone does not bound it by the third-critical-point count.


7. CORRECTED PROBABILITY REDUCTION


Let L_same,r,λ be the same-maximum self-loop event under the exact all-typed Palm law. The valid deterministic reduction is


    E_{r,λ}^c ∩ A_{r,λ}
      ⊂ B_{r,λ} ∪ L_same,r,λ.                                (R)


Consequently,


    P^{MS}_{r,λ}(E^c ∩ A)
      ≤ E^{MS}_{r,λ} N_add(C_r)
        + E^{MS}_{r,λ} N_crit((h,b);T^2\C_r)
        + P^{MS}_{r,λ}(L_same,r,λ).                           (R-prob)


The first two terms are exactly the conservative counts estimated in LS-DER-024. The last term is not controlled by those Kac–Rice estimates.


Thus LS-DER-024 equations (31) and (32), and every downstream use of them, require an additional theorem such as


    sup_{λ∈P_*} P^{MS}_{r,λ}(L_same,r,λ)=O(r^3),               (L3)


or a stronger structural exclusion derived from extra hypotheses. Neither follows from Morse–Smale plus distinct critical values alone.


8. MEASURABLE EVENT FORMULATION


For a Morse–Smale function with distinct values, every index-one saddle has two ascending separatrices with well-defined terminal maxima. Let T_+(S),T_-(S) be those terminal maxima under any deterministic ordering of the two local branches.


Then


    L_same(M,S)
      ={T_+(S)=M}
       ∩{T_-(S)=M}
       ∩{no H0 merge of C_M at a value in (h,b)}.


This is measurable on the Morse–Smale stratum. One construction uses rational-time flow samples and rational balls around the finite critical-point set: terminal convergence to a named nondegenerate maximum is a countable union/intersection condition, and the absence of an intervening H0 merge is measurable from the finite merge tree. Boundary failures caused by non-Morse functions, ties, or separatrix bifurcations belong to the separately declared null/exceptional layer.


9. MULTIPLE PASSES AND MINIMAX ENDPOINTS


Multiple alternative paths do not repair the strictness problem. The relevant minimax is


    c = sup_{γ} min_{x∈γ} f(x)


over paths γ in the same superlevel component joining small cross-sections of the two upper sectors.


Compactness and Morse theory can produce an attained critical level. But:
- c may equal h, attained by S itself;
- c<b does not imply c>h;
- c>h requires an independent connection already present strictly above S;
- if such a connection appears through another critical event, that event is counted, but L_same need not contain one.


Likewise, distinct critical values remove equality between two different critical points; they do not prohibit the minimax from being attained by S.


10. ASSUMPTION-REMOVAL AND HOSTILE TESTS


H1. Remove compactness. Minimax attainment may fail through escape to infinity. The original strict claim remains unsupported.


H2. Remove distinct critical values. A second saddle can occur at h or a maximum at b; open-window strictness fails even more directly.


H3. Remove Morse–Smale. A separatrix may have noncritical recurrent behavior or nontransverse connections; terminal-maximum notation may fail.


H4. Keep all declared hypotheses but allow both branches to terminate at M. The torus handle counterexample survives. This is the decisive hostile test.


H5. Add “the two upper sectors lie in distinct H0 components immediately above h.” Then S is a genuine H0 merge and Case II applies; the self-loop class is excluded by hypothesis.


H6. Add “every H0 self-merge at S has an independent connection with minimax level strictly above h.” This is precisely the missing assertion, not a consequence of the existing hypotheses.
