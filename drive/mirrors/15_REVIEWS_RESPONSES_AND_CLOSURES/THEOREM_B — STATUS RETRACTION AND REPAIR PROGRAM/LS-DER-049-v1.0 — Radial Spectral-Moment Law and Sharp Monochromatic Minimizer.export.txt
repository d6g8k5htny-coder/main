RADIAL SPECTRAL-MOMENT FORMULA, AUTOMATIC CONTACT NONDEGENERACY, AND THE MONOCHROMATIC MINIMIZER


1. SPECTRAL NORMALIZATION


Let f be a unit-variance stationary isotropic Gaussian field on R^2 satisfying the smooth contact hypotheses of LS-DER-048. Let mu be its normalized spectral probability measure and let R=|xi| under mu. Define


    M2=E[R^2],   M4=E[R^4],   M6=E[R^6].                 (1)


Assume M2>0 and M6<infinity. Isotropy gives the directional derivative moments


    a=M2/2,
    m4=3M4/8,
    chi=5M6/16.                                           (2)


2. AUTOMATIC NONDEGENERACY


The even and odd axial contact determinants of LS-DER-048 become


    Delta=(3M4-2M2^2)/8,                                  (3)


    Omega=(10M2 M6-9M4^2)/64.                             (4)


Moment Cauchy-Schwarz gives


    M4>=M2^2,
    M2 M6>=M4^2.                                          (5)


Consequently


    Delta>=M2^2/8>0,                                      (6)


    Omega>=M4^2/64>=M2^4/64>0.                            (7)


THEOREM 2.1 — AUTOMATIC ISOTROPIC CONTACT NONDEGENERACY.
For every nonconstant unit-variance isotropic spectral measure with finite sixth radial moment, the two contact determinants required by the coefficient algebra are automatically strictly positive. No separate Delta>0 or Omega>0 assumption is needed at the level of the limiting six-pin covariance.


This does not replace the sample-regularity, finite-r covariance, Kac-Rice, or global domination assumptions of LS-DER-048.


3. RADIAL-MOMENT COEFFICIENT LAW


Substitution of (2)-(4) into the two-invariant formula of LS-DER-048 gives


    c_iso
      =C_rad
       (10M2 M6-9M4^2)^(2/3)/M2^(5/3),                   (8)


where


    C_rad=3^(1/6)Gamma(1/6)/
          [54*2^(7/3)pi^(3/2)].                           (9)


Introduce the scale-free moment ratios


    x=M4/M2^2,
    y=M6/M2^3,                                            (10)


and the fold bandwidth index


    B_fold=10y-9x^2.                                      (11)


Then


    c_iso=C_rad M2 B_fold^(2/3).                          (12)


Thus M2 supplies the inverse-area scale and B_fold supplies the dimensionless spectral-shape correction.


4. SHARP LOWER BOUND


From (5),


    y>=x^2,
    x>=1.                                                 (13)


Therefore


    B_fold=10y-9x^2
           >=x^2
           >=1.                                           (14)


It follows that


    c_iso>=C_rad M2.                                      (15)


Equality in (15) requires equality in both moment inequalities. Equivalently, R^2 is constant mu-almost surely. Under isotropy the spectral measure is then supported on one circle |xi|=k, the monochromatic random-wave spectrum.


THEOREM 4.1 — MONOCHROMATIC MINIMIZER.
Among all unit-variance stationary isotropic Gaussian fields with fixed M2 and finite M6 that satisfy the LS-DER-048 contact interfaces, the all-typed local generic-fold coefficient obeys the sharp bound


    c_iso>=C_rad M2.                                      (16)


Equality holds exactly for a monochromatic radial spectrum. Spectral bandwidth can only increase the local fold coefficient at fixed gradient scale.

6. INTERPRETATION


The local fold coefficient separates into:


    correlation scale: M2,
    spectral shape:    B_fold^(2/3).                      (22)


The minimizer theorem identifies the monochromatic random wave as the least locally fold-dense isotropic spectrum at fixed gradient energy. Broadband spectra have larger sixth-versus-fourth moment imbalance and therefore a larger short-lifetime fold amplitude.


The result is coefficient-level and local. It does not imply that monochromatic fields minimize the fully selected persistence coefficient, because elder-rule selection and long-range topology may depend on the complete spectral measure.
