SPECTRAL COLLAPSE OF THE THREE-DIMENSIONAL GAUSSIAN NEGATIVE-DEFINITE CONE


1. OBJECT


LS-DER-056 expresses the exact finite-torus three-dimensional short-lifetime coefficient through


    D_t
      =E[(det Q_t)^2 1{Q_t negative definite}|h_t=0],        (1)


where q_t=(Q_11,Q_12,Q_22) is a centered Gaussian vector with positive-definite conditional covariance Gamma_t.


Equation (1) was previously reduced to an indicator-weighted integral over an inner sphere. The indicator is the remaining nonsmooth numerical object. The present theorem removes it exactly. The complete cone expectation depends only on three eigenvalues and one smooth one-dimensional integral.


2. DETERMINANT AS A QUADRATIC FORM


Use the coordinate order


    q=(Q_11,Q_12,Q_22)^T.                                   (2)


Then


    det Q=Q_11 Q_22-Q_12^2=q^T H q,                         (3)


with


    H=[[0,0,1/2],
       [0,-1,0],
       [1/2,0,0]].                                          (4)


Let Gamma be any positive-definite covariance on q and choose a symmetric positive square root Gamma^(1/2). Put


    B=Gamma^(1/2) H Gamma^(1/2).                             (5)


The matrix H has inertia (one positive, two negative). Sylvester's law of inertia gives the same inertia for B. Write its eigenvalues as


    lambda>0,  -mu<0,  -nu<0,                               (6)


with mu,nu>0.


No square root is required to obtain these values: Gamma H is similar to B, because


    Gamma H
      =Gamma^(1/2) B Gamma^(-1/2).                          (7)


Hence lambda,-mu,-nu are the three real eigenvalues of the directly computable matrix Gamma H.


3. NEGATIVE-DEFINITE CONE SYMMETRY


Let q~N(0,Gamma). Since q and -q have the same law, Q and -Q have the same law. The map Q->-Q exchanges the negative-definite and positive-definite cones while preserving det Q. Therefore


    E[(det Q)^2 1{Q negative definite}]
      =(1/2)E[(det Q)^2 1{det Q>0}].                        (8)


This identity is special to centered two-by-two symmetric matrices. It removes the trace test completely.


Let z~N(0,I_3). Under q=Gamma^(1/2)z and an orthogonal diagonalization of B,


    det Q
      =lambda z_1^2-mu z_2^2-nu z_3^2.                     (9)


Thus the cone expectation is a spectral function of Gamma H.


4. EXACT CAP INTEGRATION


Write z=r u with u uniform on S^2 and r independent. Since E[r^4]=15,


    D(Gamma)
      :=E[(det Q)^2 1{Q negative definite}]
      =15/(8pi) integral_(S^2)
         [lambda u_1^2-mu u_2^2-nu u_3^2]^2
         1{lambda u_1^2>mu u_2^2+nu u_3^2} du.             (10)


Use u_1=x and, in the negative eigenspace,


    u_2=sqrt(1-x^2)cos(phi),
    u_3=sqrt(1-x^2)sin(phi).                                (11)


Define


    h(phi)=mu cos^2(phi)+nu sin^2(phi).                     (12)


The positive-determinant condition is


    |x|>sqrt[h/(lambda+h)].                                 (13)


For each fixed phi, exact integration over the two caps gives

Substitution into (10) yields the main formula.


5. ONE-DIMENSIONAL SPECTRAL FORMULA
