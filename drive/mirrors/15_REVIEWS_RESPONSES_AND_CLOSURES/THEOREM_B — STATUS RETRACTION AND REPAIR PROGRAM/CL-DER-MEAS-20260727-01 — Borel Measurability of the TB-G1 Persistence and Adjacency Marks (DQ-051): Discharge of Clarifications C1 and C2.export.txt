0. STANDING SETUP AND NOTATION


X is a compact connected smooth surface, in the application the flat torus T_L^2 with L = 24. C^2(X) carries the C^2 topology. G is the set of f in C^2(X) that are Morse with pairwise distinct critical values; G is open. Fix 0 < rho < inj(X).


For f in G write Max(f), Sad_1(f) for the maxima and index-one saddles, D_f(M) for the death saddle of a non-global maximum, and A_f(M,S) for the Boolean event that at least one ascending separatrix branch of S has forward omega-limit M under the gradient-ascent flow. Xi_pers,rho and Xi_adj,rho are as defined in S2-DER-010 Sections 3 and 6.


Gradient ascent means the flow of gamma' = grad f(gamma), along which f increases. At a Morse index-one critical point of a surface the Hessian has one negative and one positive eigenvalue, so under ascent the unstable manifold is one-dimensional with two branches; these are the ascending separatrices.


IMPORTED INGREDIENTS, DECLARED RATHER THAN REPROVED
I1. Implicit function theorem: nondegenerate critical points continue uniquely and continuously with unchanged Morse index on a C^2 neighborhood.
I2. Continuous dependence of ODE solutions on initial conditions and on the vector field; for f in C^2, grad f is C^1, so the flow (t,x,f) -> phi_t^f(x) is continuous, and X compact gives completeness.
I3. Hadamard-Perron local unstable manifold with parameters: at a hyperbolic rest point the local unstable manifold is a C^1 curve depending continuously on the vector field in C^1.
I4. Convergence of gradient trajectories, proved in Lemma 3.1 below because it is short and is the load-bearing step.
I5. On the space of finite point measures with the vague topology, the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the evaluation maps mu -> integral of phi against mu, for phi bounded continuous.


1. WHY THE CONTINUITY ROUTE FAILS — TWO LOCI, STATED EXACTLY


Let f_0 be in G and let U be a neighborhood as produced by I1, shrunk so that critical values keep their order and the merge-tree combinatorics is constant. The original argument then asserts that Xi_pers,rho is continuous on U in the vague topology. It is not, for two reasons.


L1, THE INCLUSION BOUNDARY. The atom for a pair carries the label 1{0 < dist(M,S) < rho}. If f_0 has a candidate pair with dist(M,S) exactly rho, then every neighborhood of f_0 contains functions on both sides of that threshold, so an atom appears or disappears and the measure jumps in the vague topology. U cannot be shrunk to remove this, because the condition is an equality at f_0 itself, not an open condition that fails only nearby.


L2, THE ANGULAR BRANCH CUT. theta = Arg(v) takes values in the half-open interval [0,2pi). That convention is not incidental: S2-DER-010 Proposition 4.2 requires it to defeat the theta versus theta+pi double count. But Arg is discontinuous across the positive real axis, so as a pair rotates through theta = 0 the atom's angular coordinate jumps from near 2pi to 0 and the measure again moves discontinuously.


Neither locus threatens the conclusion. Both are removed by not claiming continuity in the first place.


2. THEOREM A — THE PERSISTENCE PROCESS IS BOREL, PROVED DIRECTLY


THEOREM A. The map f -> Xi_pers,rho(f) is Borel from G into the space of finite point measures with the vague topology, and its extension by the zero measure off G is Borel on all of C^2(X).


PROOF. Fix f_0 in G and U as above, with continued critical points M_i(f) and S_j(f), each continuous on U by I1. By I5 it suffices to show that for every bounded continuous test function phi the real-valued map


    f -> integral phi d Xi_pers,rho(f)
       = sum over i,j of 1{D_f(M_i) = S_j} . 1{0 < dist(M_i(f), S_j(f)) < rho} . phi(m_ij(f))


is Borel on U, where m_ij(f) = (M_i, r, theta, b, kappa)(f) is the mark vector. The sum is finite and its number of terms is constant on U, so it suffices that each factor is Borel.


(a) The pairing indicator 1{D_f(M_i) = S_j} is locally CONSTANT on U. This is the one part of the original argument that is correct and it is retained verbatim: with critical-value order fixed and regular levels chosen between consecutive critical values, gradient-flow continuation identifies the superlevel cobordisms, so which saddles merge which components, which are loop saddles, the elder representative above each merge saddle, and hence the pairing label are all constant in the continued labels. A locally constant function is Borel.


(b) The inclusion indicator is Borel but NOT locally constant. The map f -> dist(M_i(f), S_j(f)) is continuous on U, being a composition of continuous maps. The set {f in U : 0 < dist(M_i(f), S_j(f)) < rho} is therefore the preimage of an open interval under a continuous map, hence open, hence Borel. This is exactly the step that replaces the false claim at locus L1: the indicator of an open set is Borel whether or not it is locally constant.


(c) The mark map is Borel. The components M_i, r = dist, b = f(M_i) and ell = f(M_i) − f(S_j) are continuous on U, and kappa = 6 ell / r^3 is continuous wherever r > 0, which holds on the set in (b) since a maximum and a saddle are distinct points. The angular component theta = Arg(v) is continuous off the closed half-line where the cut sits and is therefore Borel as a map into [0,2pi); it is not continuous, which is locus L2, and it does not need to be. A composition of a continuous function phi with a Borel map is Borel.


Each summand is a product of Borel functions, so the sum is Borel on U. G is a separable metrizable space, hence Lindelof, so the cover of G by such neighborhoods U admits a countable subcover; a function Borel on each member of a countable open cover is Borel on the union. Finally G is open in C^2(X), so extension by the zero measure off G is Borel. QED.


REMARK A.1. The proof uses strictly less than the original: it never asserts continuity, and it needs no shrinking of U beyond what I1 already provides. The two loci L1 and L2 are not exceptions to be excluded, they are simply places where a Borel argument is required and a continuity argument is not available.


3. THEOREM B — THE ADJACENCY MARK IS BOREL, AND MORSE-SMALE IS NOT REQUIRED


This closes C2. First the convergence lemma, stated separately because it is what makes the argument work without any transversality hypothesis.


LEMMA 3.1. Let f be in G and let gamma be any maximal trajectory of gradient ascent on X. Then gamma(t) converges to a single critical point of f as t tends to plus infinity.


PROOF. Along the flow, d/dt f(gamma(t)) = |grad f(gamma(t))|^2 >= 0, so f is nondecreasing along gamma and bounded above by max f, hence f(gamma(t)) increases to a limit c. X is compact so the omega-limit set omega(gamma) is nonempty, compact, connected and flow-invariant, and f equals c identically on it. An invariant set on which f is constant consists of critical points, since f strictly increases along any nonconstant trajectory. Thus omega(gamma) is a connected subset of Crit(f). Because f is Morse on a compact manifold, Crit(f) is finite and therefore totally disconnected, so omega(gamma) is a single point. QED.


Note what this avoids. No Lojasiewicz inequality is needed, because Morse gives finiteness of Crit(f) directly. No Morse-Smale transversality is needed, because the conclusion is only that the limit exists and is a critical point, not what kind of critical point it is.


THEOREM B. Let M_i and S_j be continued critical points on U as above, with S_j of Morse index one. Then


    {f in U : A_f(M_i, S_j) = 1}


is a Borel subset of U. Consequently A_f(M_i,S_j) is a Borel function on G, and its extension by 0 off G is Borel on C^2(X). No Morse-Smale hypothesis is used.


PROOF. At S_j(f) the Hessian of f is a symmetric 2 by 2 matrix with one negative and one positive eigenvalue; the eigenvalues are distinct so each is simple, and the unit eigenvector e_u(f) of the positive eigenvalue is determined up to sign and depends continuously on f on U. By I3 the local unstable manifold W^u_loc(S_j(f)) is a C^1 curve through S_j(f) tangent to e_u(f), depending continuously on f in C^1. Fix epsilon > 0 small enough, uniformly on a possibly smaller U, that the two points at arclength epsilon from S_j(f) along W^u_loc lie in it; call them x^+(f) and x^-(f). Each depends continuously on f. The labeling by sign is ambiguous, but the unordered pair {x^+, x^-} is well defined and the event below is symmetric in the two, so A is well defined.


The two ascending separatrix branches of S_j are the forward trajectories through x^+(f) and x^-(f). By Lemma 3.1 each converges to a single critical point. Hence


    A_f(M_i,S_j) = 1  if and only if  lim_{t->infinity} phi_t^f(x^+(f)) = M_i(f)  or the same with x^-.


Fix the plus branch. For each fixed t the map f -> d(phi_t^f(x^+(f)), M_i(f)) is continuous on U, by I2 together with continuity of x^+ and of M_i. Because the limit exists by Lemma 3.1, convergence to M_i(f) is equivalent to the Cauchy-style condition below, and we may restrict to rational times because the trajectory is continuous in t:


    {f : lim phi_t^f(x^+(f)) = M_i(f)}
      = intersection over k >= 1 of union over T in N of intersection over rational t >= T of
        {f in U : d(phi_t^f(x^+(f)), M_i(f)) < 1/k}.


Each innermost set is open, being the preimage of an open ray under a continuous map. The expression is a countable intersection of countable unions of countable intersections of open sets, hence Borel. The same holds for the minus branch, and A is the union of the two events, hence Borel. Countable subcover of G and extension by zero off G finish the proof exactly as in Theorem A. QED.


REMARK B.1 — WHAT MORSE-SMALE IS AND IS NOT FOR. If Morse-Smale transversality fails, an ascending branch may limit to another saddle rather than to a maximum. Lemma 3.1 still applies: the limit exists and is a critical point, and if it is not M_i then the event simply does not occur. So transversality is irrelevant to measurability. What transversality controls is STABILITY: whether A_f(M,S) is locally constant in f. It is not, in general, and that is exactly why the adjacency mark cannot inherit the locally-constant argument used for the pairing label in Theorem A(a), and why C2 could not be closed by citing Theorem 5.1.
This sharpens S2-DER-010 Remark 5.2, which says Morse-Smale transversality "is relevant to the separate gradient-adjacency mark". The precise statement is: relevant to its stability, not to its measurability. Measurability holds on all of G.


4. COROLLARY — SECTION 7 OF S2-DER-010 IS WELL POSED


COROLLARY 4.1. Xi_adj,rho and Xi_adj-pers,rho are Borel as maps into finite point measures, by Theorem A, Theorem B and the fact that a product of Borel factors is Borel.


COROLLARY 4.2. For a typed two-point conditioning object T_r under which the relevant regular conditional probabilities exist, the quantities


    p_r = P{D_f(M)=S | T_r},   a_r = P{A_f(M,S)=1 | T_r},
    q_r^adj = P{D_f(M)=S | A_f(M,S)=1, T_r},   eta_r = P{D_f(M)=S, A_f(M,S)=0 | T_r}


are probabilities of measurable events. Hence S2-DER-010 Proposition 7.1, p_r = a_r q_r^adj + eta_r, and Corollary 7.2, 0 <= eta_r <= 1 − a_r, are well posed as stated, and the reading of a_r q_r^adj as the joint probability when a_r = 0 remains the correct convention.


This is the whole content of the discharge: the algebra of Section 7 was never in doubt, its objects were.
