1. PURPOSE


LS-DER-050 expresses the arbitrary-dimensional isotropic generic-fold coefficient through the radial spectral moments


    M2=E[R^2],
    M4=E[R^4],
    M6=E[R^6],


and the scale-free ratios


    x=M4/M2^2,
    y=M6/M2^3.                                              (1)


At fixed M2, the previous theorem proved the global minimum at the monochromatic spectrum. The remaining moment geometry can be solved completely: at fixed M2 and fixed x, the admissible coefficient values form an exact half-line. The lower endpoint is attained by one active spectral sphere plus a zero-frequency Gaussian mode, and every larger value is attained by adding one additional high-frequency sphere.


This result isolates exactly what spectral bandwidth information the sixth moment contributes and gives a complete extremal classification at the three-moment level.


2. NORMALIZED RADIAL MOMENT PROBLEM


Assume M2>0 and define


    X=R^2/M2.                                                (2)


Then X is nonnegative and


    E[X]=1,
    E[X^2]=x,
    E[X^3]=y.                                               (3)


Cauchy--Schwarz applied to X^(3/2) and X^(1/2) gives


    y E[X] >= E[X^2]^2,


hence


    y>=x^2.                                                 (4)


Also x>=1 by E[X^2]>=E[X]^2.


Equality in (4) holds exactly when X^(3/2) and X^(1/2) are linearly dependent. Since X>=0, this means that X is constant on its positive support. Therefore


    y=x^2


if and only if the law of X is supported on {0,x}, with


    P(X=x)=1/x,
    P(X=0)=1-1/x.                                           (5)


For x=1 this reduces to X=1 almost surely.


3. COMPLETE REALIZABILITY REGION


The inequalities


    x>=1,
    y>=x^2                                                  (6)


are not only necessary; they are sufficient.


For x=1, (6) forces y=1 and X=1 almost surely.


Fix x>1 and any y>=x^2. Put


    T=(y-x)/(x-1).                                          (7)


Then T>=x. Define a three-point law on {0,1,T} by


    p_0=(x-1)/T,
    p_1=(T-x)/(T-1),
    p_T=(x-1)/[T(T-1)].                                     (8)


These numbers are nonnegative and sum to one. Direct calculation gives


    p_1+T p_T=1,
    p_1+T^2 p_T=x,
    p_1+T^3 p_T=x+(x-1)T=y.                                (9)


Thus every pair (x,y) satisfying (6) is realized by a radial spectral probability measure supported on at most three radii:


    R=0,
    R=sqrt(M2),
    R=sqrt(T M2).                                           (10)


THEOREM 3.1 -- COMPLETE THREE-MOMENT REALIZABILITY.

The lower boundary y=x^2 consists precisely of zero-plus-one-sphere spectra. Every interior point is realized by the explicit zero-plus-two-sphere law (8)--(10).

THEOREM 4.1 -- COMPLETE FIXED-BANDWIDTH COEFFICIENT RANGE.

5. CONSTANT-MODE GAUGE INTERPRETATION


Let g_k be a centered unit-variance monochromatic isotropic Gaussian field with spectral radius k, and let Z_0 be an independent standard Gaussian random variable constant in space. Put


    p=1/x,
    k^2=x M2,


and define


    f(x_space)=sqrt(1-p) Z_0+sqrt(p) g_k(x_space).            (15)


Then f has unit variance and its normalized radial spectrum is exactly the lower-bound law (5). Its derivative moments satisfy


    M_(2j)=p k^(2j),


so


    M2=p k^2,
    x=1/p,
    y=1/p^2=x^2.                                           (16)


For every realization, the constant mode shifts all field values equally and therefore leaves critical locations, gradient-flow adjacency, elder-rule pairing, and persistence lifetimes unchanged. The factor sqrt(p) multiplying g_k rescales every lifetime by sqrt(p).


If a field g has short-lifetime density


    nu_g(ell)=c_g ell^(-1/3)(1+o(1)),


then for t>0 the amplitude-scaled field tg has


    nu_(tg)(ell)
      =t^(-1)nu_g(ell/t)
      =t^(-2/3)c_g ell^(-1/3)(1+o(1)).                      (17)


With t=sqrt(p),


    c_f=p^(-1/3)c_(g_k).                                    (18)


The monochromatic coefficient scales spatially as k^d. Hence


    c_f
      =C_d^mono k^d p^(-1/3)
      =C_d^mono M2^(d/2)x^((3d+2)/6),                       (19)


which is exactly the lower envelope (13).


Thus the fixed-x extremizer has an exact geometric meaning: it is a monochromatic critical-point geometry with a statistically independent constant level mode and a reduced active amplitude. The constant mode itself cannot change persistence; it only changes the unit-variance normalization through which the active amplitude is measured.
