C6 — FREEZE-STANDARD UNDER-SPECIFICATION, NONMATERIAL TO THIS OBJECT, MATERIAL TO THE PROGRAM. ROUTES TO DQ-054.
The declared FREEZE POLICY says to extract the text strictly after BEGIN_X and before END_X. It does not require the marker match to be line anchored. In six of the seven documents I processed, each marker token occurs twice — once inside the FREEZE POLICY sentence itself and once as the delimiter line — so a conforming implementation that takes the first substring occurrence extracts a different region and computes a different hash. S2-DER-010 is the exception; its policy sentence refers to "the unique marker lines" without naming the tokens, which is the safer wording.
The policy is also silent on leading whitespace. It mandates removing all trailing LF but says nothing about leading LF. S2-DER-010's frozen body begins with two blank lines, so applying a leading strip changes its byte count and hash, while the other six are insensitive. A correct implementation must therefore apply no leading strip, and that is currently a matter of inference rather than specification.
Exact normative sentence recommended for adoption by DQ-054: "Locate the BEGIN and END markers as the unique lines equal to the marker token after stripping an optional trailing CR. Take the region strictly after the BEGIN marker line terminator and strictly before the first character of the END marker line. Normalize CRLF and lone CR to LF. Do not strip leading whitespace or leading LF. Remove all trailing LF and append exactly one LF. Encode UTF-8 without BOM."
All seven identities in this review were reproduced under exactly that rule, so it is empirically consistent with every currently declared hash in the Theorem B chain.
