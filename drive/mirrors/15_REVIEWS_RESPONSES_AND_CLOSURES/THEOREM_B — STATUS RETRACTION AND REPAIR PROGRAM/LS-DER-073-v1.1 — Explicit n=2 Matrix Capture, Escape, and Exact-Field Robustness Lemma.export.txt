2. NORMALIZED MATRIX POTENTIAL


Let Y in R^2 and let


    p_0(X)=X^3/3-X/4-1/12.


On a fixed compact convex normalized chart D^+ containing


    [-1/2,5/4] x {Y:|Y|<=1},


consider the pin-preserving degree-four potential


    P(X,Y)
      =p_0(X)
       +(1/2)(X^2-1/4) a·Y
       -(1/2)Y^T T Y
       +(1/2)X Y^T W Y
       +(1/6)C[Y,Y,Y]
       +r Q(X,Y),                                            (1)


where:
- a is in R^2;
- T,W are symmetric 2 by 2 matrices;
- C is a symmetric cubic tensor;
- Q is C^2 on D^+, Q(M)=Q(S)=0, and grad Q(M)=grad Q(S)=0 for M=(-1/2,0), S=(1/2,0).


Put F=grad P and


    R=1+|a|+||W||_op+||C||_op+||Q||_(C^2(D^+)) >=1,
    tau=lambda_min(T).                                       (2)


For the side-24 field,


    T=-Q_perp/(kappa r),                                     (3)


as established in LS-DER-072. Thus tau is the physical smallest negative transverse curvature divided by kappa r.


Fix


    K=4096,
    epsilon_0=1/(1024K),                                    (4)


and assume


    r R^5<=epsilon_0,
    tau>=2K R^5+2R.                                         (5)


Define


    m=2R/tau,
    delta=1/(512R^3),
    X_*=5/4,
    eta_R=1/(8192R^3).                                      (6)


Then m<=1/(KR^4)<=1/K.


The gradient components are


    F_X
      =X^2-1/4+X a·Y+(1/2)Y^T W Y+r Q_X,                    (7)


    F_Y
      =(1/2)(X^2-1/4)a-TY+XWY
       +(1/2)C[Y,Y,·]+r grad_Y Q.                            (8)


3. ENDPOINT SPECTRUM AND THE TWO UNSTABLE HALF-BRANCHES


At S, the Jacobian of F has symmetric block form


    J_S=[[A,B^T],[B,D]],                                    (9)


with


    A=1+O(rR),
    B=a/2+O(rR),
    D=-T+W/2+O(rR).


Equations (4)--(5) imply, after decreasing a fixed r endpoint if necessary,


    A>=3/4,
    D<=-(tau/2)I,
    |B|<=R.                                                  (10)


Hence J_S has exactly one positive eigenvalue lambda_+. If v=(v_X,v_Y) is its eigenvector, then


    (lambda_+ I-D)v_Y=Bv_X,


and therefore


    |v_Y|/|v_X|
      <=|B|/(lambda_++tau/2)
      <2R/tau=m.                                             (11)


The two orientations of v enter respectively the inward and outward invariant regions constructed below.


4. OUTWARD INVARIANT CONE


Write xi=X-1/2 and define


    C_out={0<=xi<=delta, |Y|<=m xi}.                         (12)


On C_out, pin preservation and the C^2 bound on Q give


    |grad Q(X,Y)|<=2R(xi+|Y|).


Using (4)--(6) in (7), every adverse term is less than one quarter of the leading xi(1+xi), and hence


    F_X>=3xi/4.                                              (13)


On the curved boundary |Y|=m xi, set


    G_out=|Y|^2-m^2 xi^2.


Then


    (1/2) dG_out/dt
      =Y·F_Y-m^2 xi F_X.                                    (14)


The T term is


    -Y^T T Y<=-tau m^2 xi^2
               =-(4R^2/tau)xi^2.


The remaining a,W,C,Q terms in Y·F_Y are bounded, in the same units, by


    [257/256+2/K+1/K^2+6epsilon_0]
      (R^2/tau)xi^2.


The last term in (14) is nonpositive by (13). Since the bracket is less than 2,


    (1/2) dG_out/dt
      <=-2(R^2/tau)xi^2<0                                   (15)


on the nonzero boundary. Thus C_out is strictly forward invariant. By (11), the outward unstable half-branch enters C_out and reaches xi=delta.


5. OUTWARD INVARIANT STRIP AND MONOTONE TRANSIT


Define


    S_out={1/2+delta<=X<=5/4, |Y|<=m}.                       (16)


On |Y|=m,


    Y·F_Y
      <=-[4-(21/16+5/(2K)+1/K^2+2epsilon_0)]
         (R^2/tau)
      <-2(R^2/tau)<0.                                       (17)


Therefore the cylindrical boundary is inward pointing.


For X in [1/2+delta,5/4], equation (7) and (4)--(6) give


    F_X
      >=delta[1-(640/K+256/K^2+1/(2K))]
      >=3delta/4>0.                                         (18)


The outward branch therefore crosses S_out monotonically and reaches X=X_* without leaving the fixed chart.


At X_*=5/4,


    p_0(5/4)=49/192.                                        (19)


The absolute sum of every Y-dependent term, the rQ term, and a potential perturbation whose gradient is bounded by eta_R is at most


    21/(32K)+1/(2K^2)+3epsilon_0+3/8192
      <1/768.                                                (20)


Consequently


    P(5/4,Y)>49/192-1/768=65/256>1/4.                       (21)


Because P(M)=0, the outward comparison branch reaches a point above the maximum level.


6. INWARD TAPERED TUBE


Put


    h(X)=1/4-X^2


and define the tapered tube


    T_in={-1/2<X<1/2, |Y|<=m h(X)}.                          (22)


Near S, h(X)=(1/2-X)(1/2+X), so (11) implies that the M-ward orientation of the unstable eigendirection enters T_in.


Throughout T_in, pin preservation at both endpoints implies


    |grad Q(X,Y)|
      <=3R h(X).                                             (23)


Equations (4)--(8) then give the strict longitudinal inequality


    F_X<=-(3/4)h(X)<0.                                       (24)


Set


    G_in=|Y|^2-m^2 h(X)^2.


Since h'(X)=-2X,


    (1/2)dG_in/dt
      =Y·F_Y+2m^2 X h(X)F_X.                                (25)


On |Y|=m h(X), the T term again equals


    -(4R^2/tau)h(X)^2.


All adverse terms in (25), including the moving-boundary term for X<0, are bounded by


    [1+1/K+1/(4K^2)+6epsilon_0+4/K]
      (R^2/tau)h(X)^2
      <2(R^2/tau)h(X)^2.


Therefore


    (1/2)dG_in/dt
      <=-2(R^2/tau)h(X)^2<0.                                (26)


Thus T_in is strictly forward invariant.


Along the M-ward branch, X decreases monotonically by (24). If its limit X_infinity were greater than -1/2, then h(X_infinity)>0 and (24) would keep X decreasing at a nonzero rate, a contradiction. Hence


    X(t)->-1/2.


The tube inequality gives |Y(t)|<=m h(X(t))->0, so the branch converges to M. Near M, h(X)=(X+1/2)(1/2-X), and (24) gives exponential convergence after an ordinary upper bound |F_X|<=2h.


7. EXACT-FIELD C1 ROBUSTNESS


Let F_exact=grad P_exact be a pin-preserving exact gradient field on D^+, normalized by P_exact(M)=P(M)=0, and satisfying


    ||F_exact-F||_(C^1(D^+))<=eta_R.                         (27)


Because the perturbation vanishes at M and S, its size in the cone and tapered tube is bounded by eta_R times the distance to the relevant endpoint. Substitution in (14) and (25) consumes less than one quarter of the margins in (15) and (26). On S_out, eta_R<=delta/16 consumes less than one quarter of the longitudinal margin in (18) and less than one quarter of the radial margin in (17). The path-integral potential error to X=5/4 is less than 3eta_R and is already included in (20).


Therefore every strict cone, tube, strip, monotonicity, and level inequality survives for F_exact.


For the conditioned side-24 field on a fixed normalized chart, the interpolation estimate has the form


    ||F_exact-F||_(C^1(D^+))<=C_+ r                         (28)


on the fifth-derivative good event. Under rR^5<=epsilon_0,


    eta_R
      >=(1/8192)(r/epsilon_0)^(3/5).


Hence (28) implies (27) uniformly whenever


    r<=r_mat
      :=min{1,[1/(8192 C_+ epsilon_0^(3/5))]^(5/2)}.         (29)


This is one fixed positive endpoint depending only on the fixed chart constant.
