TB-G2 UNIFORM SIX-PIN COVARIANCE AND CONDITIONAL-MOMENT THEOREM


1. FOURIER MODEL AND NOTATION


Let f be the normalized periodized Bargmann-Fock field on the ordinary side-L square torus, with L=24 in the current program. It has a Fourier realization


    f(x)=sum_{k in Z^2} sqrt(w_k) xi_k exp(i <lambda_k,x>),           (1)


where


    lambda_k=(2 pi/L)k,
    w_k>0 for every k in Z^2,


and the weights have Gaussian decay. Reality is imposed by the usual conjugacy relation. The only properties used below are:


- every lattice spectral weight is strictly positive;
- for every integer m>=0,


    sum_k w_k (1+|lambda_k|)^m < infinity.                            (2)


For theta in S^1, set


    t=t_theta,
    s=t_theta^perp,
    u_k=<lambda_k,t>,
    v_k=<lambda_k,s>.                                                 (3)


Let r=2h. The six corrected pin variables in LS-DER-021 have Fourier multipliers


    phi_1=cos(h u),
    phi_2=i[sin(h u)-h u cos(h u)]/(4 h^3),
    phi_3=i u cos(h u),
    phi_4=-u sin(h u)/h,
    phi_5=i v cos(h u),
    phi_6=-v sin(h u)/h.                                              (4)


At h=0 they extend continuously to


    phi_1^0=1,
    phi_2^0=i u^3/12,
    phi_3^0=i u,
    phi_4^0=-u^2,
    phi_5^0=i v,
    phi_6^0=-u v.                                                     (5)


These are exactly the symbols of


    (f,-f_ttt/12,f_t,f_tt,f_s,f_ts).                                 (6)


For a vector of real coefficients a=(a_1,...,a_6), the variance of the corresponding limiting linear functional is


    Var(sum_j a_j L_j^theta f)
      =sum_{k in Z^2} w_k |P_{a,theta}(lambda_k)|^2,                  (7)


where P_{a,theta} is the complex polynomial obtained by combining the six symbols in (5).


2. LATTICE POLYNOMIAL RIGIDITY


LEMMA 2.1.
If a polynomial P in C[x,y] vanishes at every point of Z^2, then P is the zero polynomial.


PROOF.
Write


    P(x,y)=sum_{j=0}^d p_j(y)x^j.                                    (8)


For each fixed integer n, the one-variable polynomial x -> P(x,n) vanishes at every integer x and hence is identically zero. Therefore p_j(n)=0 for every integer n and every j. Each p_j is a one-variable polynomial with infinitely many zeros, so every p_j is identically zero. Thus P=0. END PROOF.


LEMMA 2.2 — POINTWISE SIX-SYMBOL INDEPENDENCE.
For every theta, the six limiting pin functionals in (6) are linearly independent in the Gaussian Hilbert space.


PROOF.
Suppose (7) is zero. Every summand is nonnegative and every w_k is positive, so


    P_{a,theta}(lambda_k)=0 for every k in Z^2.                       (9)


The map lambda -> (u,v)=(<lambda,t>,<lambda,s>) is an invertible linear change of variables. Hence lambda -> P_{a,theta}(lambda) is a polynomial in the Cartesian components of lambda and vanishes on the scaled lattice (2 pi/L)Z^2. Rescaling and Lemma 2.1 imply it is the zero polynomial.


After the invertible rotation, its monomials are


    1, u^3, u, u^2, v, u v.                                         (10)


They are pairwise distinct. Therefore every coefficient a_j is zero. END PROOF.


3. UNIFORM LIMITING EIGENFLOOR


Let G_0(theta) be the covariance Gram matrix of the six limiting functionals. Lemma 2.2 gives


    lambda_min(G_0(theta))>0 for every theta.                         (11)


Every entry is a convergent weighted sum of products of polynomial symbols in u_k and v_k. The summability (2) permits termwise continuity in theta by dominated convergence. Therefore theta -> G_0(theta) is continuous. Compactness of S^1 gives


    lambda_0:=min_{theta in S^1} lambda_min(G_0(theta))>0,            (12)


and


    Lambda_0:=max_{theta in S^1} lambda_max(G_0(theta))<infinity.     (13)


This proves the limiting eigenfloor without numerical sampling or an unstated genericity assumption.


4. UNIFORM DIVIDED-DIFFERENCE EXTENSION


The elementary inequalities


    |cos z-1|<=z^2/2,
    |sin z/z-1|<=z^2/6,
    |sin z-z cos z-z^3/3|<=C |z|^5                                (14)


for real z near zero imply, uniformly in theta,


    |phi_j(h,u_k,v_k)-phi_j^0(u_k,v_k)|
      <=C h^2(1+|lambda_k|)^5.                                      (15)


The exact exponent five is not important; it is integrable against the Gaussian spectral weights. Consequently the six-functional vector converges in Gaussian Hilbert norm uniformly in theta:


    sup_theta sum_j ||L_{j,h,theta}-L_{j,0,theta}||_H^2 =O(h^4).     (16)


The covariance matrices therefore satisfy


    sup_theta ||G_h(theta)-G_0(theta)||_op ->0.                      (17)


Choose h_pin>0 so the left side is at most lambda_0/2. Weyl's inequality gives, for 0<=h<=h_pin,


    (lambda_0/2) I_6 <=G_h(theta)<=Lambda_pin I_6                  (18)


uniformly in theta, for some finite Lambda_pin. In the original variable r=2h, this is the uniform six-pin covariance eigenfloor asserted in LS-DER-021.


5. EXPLICIT UNIFORM GAUSSIAN DENSITY BOUNDS


Let Sigma_pin(r,theta)=G_{r/2}(theta), and let y_r(theta,b,kappa) be the exact transformed target. On the compact set


    0<=r<=r_pin,
    theta in S^1,
    b in B_*=[23/20,5/4],
    kappa in K_*=[3/4,5/4],                                    (19)


there is M_y<infinity with ||y_r||<=M_y. For a six-dimensional centered Gaussian with covariance Sigma, the density at y is


    p_Sigma(y)=(2 pi)^(-3)(det Sigma)^(-1/2)
               exp[-(1/2)y^T Sigma^(-1)y].                         (20)


Using (18),


    p_Sigma(y)
      >=(2 pi)^(-3) Lambda_pin^(-3)
        exp[-M_y^2/lambda_0],                                      (21)


because the lower eigenvalue in (18) is lambda_0/2, and


    p_Sigma(y)
      <=(2 pi)^(-3)(lambda_0/2)^(-3).                              (22)


Thus the pin density has positive finite uniform lower and upper bounds. Since Sigma_pin(r,theta) and y_r converge uniformly and the density formula is smooth on the compact set of matrices satisfying (18),


    p_Y(r,theta,b,kappa)->p_0(theta,b,kappa)                        (23)


uniformly on P_*. This supplies the exact density interface used in LS-DER-021.


6. ENDPOINT VARIABLES AND UNIFORM REGRESSION BLOCKS


Let V_r=(A_M^r,A_S^r,B_M^r,B_S^r,C_M^r,C_S^r). On a Fourier mode, with z=h u, the multipliers are


    psi_AM=[-u^2 exp(-i z)+u sin(z)/h]/(2h),
    psi_AS=[-u^2 exp(+i z)+u sin(z)/h]/(2h),
    psi_BM=[-u v exp(-i z)+v sin(z)/h]/(2h),
    psi_BS=[-u v exp(+i z)+v sin(z)/h]/(2h),
    psi_CM=-v^2 exp(-i z),
    psi_CS=-v^2 exp(+i z).                                         (24)


Taylor expansion with integral remainder gives the uniform Gaussian-Hilbert limits


    psi_AM -> i u^3/2,
    psi_AS -> -i u^3/2,
    psi_BM -> i u^2 v/2,
    psi_BS -> -i u^2 v/2,
    psi_CM,psi_CS -> -v^2,                                        (25)


with errors bounded by C h(1+|lambda|)^4. Therefore the joint covariance blocks of (Y_r,V_r) extend continuously to r=0, uniformly in theta.


Condition on Y_r=y_r. Gaussian regression gives


    m_r=C_VY(r,theta) Sigma_pin(r,theta)^(-1)y_r,                   (26)


    Gamma_r=Sigma_V(r,theta)
            -C_VY(r,theta)Sigma_pin(r,theta)^(-1)C_YV(r,theta).    (27)


All covariance blocks are uniformly bounded, ||Sigma_pin^-1||<=2/lambda_0, and ||y_r||<=M_y. Hence there are finite M_m and M_Gamma with


    ||m_r||<=M_m,
    ||Gamma_r||_op<=M_Gamma                                      (28)


uniformly on P_* and small r.


If Z_r is the centered conditional Gaussian vector, then for a dimension-dependent constant C_6,


    E||Z_r||^8<=C_6 (tr Gamma_r)^4.                               (29)


Using ||m+z||^8<=2^7(||m||^8+||z||^8),


    sup_{P_*,0<=r<=r_pin} E[||V_r||^8 | Y_r=y_r] < infinity.       (30)


This is the uniform eighth-moment envelope needed for


    0<=W_r/r^2<=||V_r||^4                                       (31)


and uniform integrability of the normalized determinant weight.


7. NONDEGENERATE TRANSVERSE CURVATURE


Add the limiting functional q=f_ss(x), whose Fourier symbol is


    phi_7^0=-v^2.                                                  (32)


The seven symbols are now


    1,u^3,u,u^2,v,u v,v^2,                                       (33)


up to nonzero complex constants. They are distinct monomials. The proof of Lemma 2.2 therefore gives positive definiteness of the seven-functional Gram matrix H_0(theta) for every theta. Continuity and compactness give


    mu_0:=min_theta lambda_min(H_0(theta))>0.                      (34)


Let sigma_Q^2(theta) be the conditional variance of q given the six limiting pins. It is the Schur complement of G_0(theta) in H_0(theta). Equivalently,


    sigma_Q^2(theta)
      =inf_{a in R^6} ||q-sum_j a_j L_j^theta||_H^2.               (35)


For every a, the joint Gram lower bound gives


    ||q-sum_j a_j L_j^theta||_H^2
      >=mu_0(1+||a||^2)>=mu_0.                                    (36)


Thus


    sigma_Q^2(theta)>=mu_0>0                                     (37)


uniformly in theta. Its upper bound follows from Var(q), which is finite and continuous. The conditional mean is


    m_Q(theta,b,kappa)
      =C_QY(theta)G_0(theta)^(-1)y_0(theta,b,kappa),               (38)


so compactness gives


    |m_Q|<=M_Q,
    0<sigma_Q^-<=sd(Q)<=sigma_Q^+<infinity.                       (39)


In particular Q has no atom at zero. Moreover


    inf_{P_*} P(Q<=-1)
      >=Phi[(-1-M_Q)/sigma_Q^-]>0,                                (40)


where Phi is the standard normal distribution function. This supplies the strict positive lower event used for z_0.


8. UNIFORM EXPECTATION LIMIT


The joint conditional Gaussian mean and covariance of V_r converge continuously along any sequence


    r_n->0,
    (theta_n,b_n,kappa_n)->(theta_*,b_*,kappa_*).                  (41)


Therefore V_{r_n} converges in distribution to the limiting conditioned endpoint vector. The only discontinuity of the limiting typed determinant functional occurs at Q=0, which has probability zero by (37). Hence


    W_r/r^2
      ->kappa^2 Q^2 1{Q<0}                                      (42)


in distribution along every such sequence. The uniform eighth-moment bound (30) gives uniform integrability. Thus expectations converge along every convergent parameter sequence.


If the convergence were not uniform on P_*, one could select a sequence with discrepancy bounded below, pass to a convergent parameter subsequence by compactness, and contradict the preceding sequential convergence. Therefore


    Z_r(theta,b,kappa)/r^2
      ->kappa^2 E[Q^2 1{Q<0}]                                   (43)


uniformly on P_*.
