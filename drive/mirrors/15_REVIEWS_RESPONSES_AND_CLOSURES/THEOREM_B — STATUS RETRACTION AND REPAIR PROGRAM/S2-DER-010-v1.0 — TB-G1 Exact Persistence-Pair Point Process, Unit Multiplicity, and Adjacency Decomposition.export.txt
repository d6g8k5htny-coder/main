TB-G1 — EXACT PERSISTENCE-PAIR PROCESS AND MULTIPLICITY THEOREM


1. DETERMINISTIC SETTING


Let X be a compact connected smooth surface. The application is X=T_L^2 with L=24.


Let f:X->R be a C^2 Morse function with pairwise distinct critical values. Define the superlevel filtration


    X_t={x in X : f(x)>=t}.


Every local maximum creates one connected component when the threshold passes downward through its critical value. At an index-one saddle, one of two events occurs:


MERGE EVENT:
two distinct connected components immediately above the saddle level become one component immediately below it;


LOOP EVENT:
one connected component acquires a one-handle, so the two local upper sectors already belong to the same connected component and H0 does not change.


Only merge saddles are deaths of superlevel H0 classes.


Because the critical values are distinct, every connected component C of a regular superlevel set has a unique elder representative


    e_f(C)=the maximum M in C with maximal f(M).


At a merge saddle S, let C_1(S) and C_2(S) be the two distinct components immediately above f(S) that merge at S. Their elder representatives are distinct maxima. The elder rule retains the representative with larger birth value and kills the representative with smaller birth value.


Define


    Y_f(S)=the younger of e_f(C_1(S)) and e_f(C_2(S)),


for every H0-merge saddle S.


Equivalently, for a non-global maximum M, define D_f(M) to be the unique merge saddle at which the superlevel component whose elder representative is M first merges into a component whose elder representative has a larger maximum value.


The unique global maximum is excluded because its H0 class is essential on a connected compact surface.


2. THE UNIT-MULTIPLICITY THEOREM


THEOREM 2.1 — CANONICAL H0 PERSISTENCE PAIRING


For a C^2 Morse function f on a compact connected surface with pairwise distinct critical values:


(a) every non-global local maximum M has a unique finite death saddle D_f(M);


(b) D_f(M) is an index-one merge saddle;


(c) every index-one merge saddle S equals D_f(M) for exactly one non-global maximum M, namely M=Y_f(S);


(d) the map


    M -> (M,D_f(M))


is a bijection from the set of non-global local maxima to the set of finite superlevel H0 persistence pairs;


(e) the induced map


    (M,D_f(M)) -> [f(D_f(M)), f(M))


is a bijection onto the non-essential superlevel H0 barcode intervals.


In particular, every non-essential H0 bar is counted with multiplicity exactly one.


PROOF


Step 1: every merge saddle kills exactly one elder representative.


Immediately above a merge saddle S there are two distinct connected components C_1 and C_2. Immediately below S they form one component. Each C_i has one elder representative e_f(C_i). The critical values are distinct, so the two representative birth values are unequal. The elder rule retains the larger birth value and kills the smaller. Thus S kills exactly one H0 class and exactly one representative maximum Y_f(S).


Step 2: every non-global maximum eventually dies.


Let M be a non-global maximum. Immediately below f(M), M is the elder representative of its newly born component. Decrease the threshold. The ambient surface X is connected, so for thresholds below the global minimum the superlevel set is X and has one connected component whose elder representative is the unique global maximum. Therefore the component represented by M cannot remain a distinct elder component forever. At the first merge at which it meets a component with a larger elder representative, the class born at M dies. Distinct saddle levels make this first death level unique. Call the saddle D_f(M).


Step 3: no maximum dies twice.


After the elder class born at M is killed, it is no longer the representative of any surviving H0 component. Hence M cannot be killed at another saddle.


Step 4: no merge saddle kills two maxima.


A binary H0 merge reduces the number of connected components by exactly one. Therefore it kills exactly one H0 class and one elder representative.


Step 5: bijection.


Steps 1–4 show that M->D_f(M) maps every non-global maximum to exactly one merge saddle, and every merge saddle has exactly one inverse Y_f(S). The standard interval associated with M has birth f(M) and death f(D_f(M)). These are precisely all finite H0 intervals. QED.


COROLLARY 2.2 — CARDINALITY IDENTITY


For every such f,


    #Max(f)-1 = #MergeSaddle_H0(f) = #FiniteBars_H0(f).

3. THE EXACT ORDERED CANDIDATE-PAIR PROCESS


Fix a radius


    0<rho<inj(X).


For X=T_L^2 with the flat metric, one may take any rho<L/2. The strict inequality avoids the torus cut locus.


For every ordered pair (M,S) with M a local maximum, S an index-one saddle, and


    0<dist(M,S)<rho,


let


    v(M,S)=Log_M(S),
    r(M,S)=|v(M,S)|,
    theta(M,S)=Arg(v(M,S)) in [0,2pi),
    b(M)=f(M),
    ell(M,S)=f(M)-f(S).


When ell(M,S)>0, define the exact normalized gap


    kappa(M,S)=6 ell(M,S)/r(M,S)^3.


The directed angle convention is always maximum-to-saddle:


    S-M = r(cos theta, sin theta)


in the unique short torus lift.


Define the all-typed local candidate measure


    Xi_all,rho(f)
      = sum over M in Max(f)
        sum over S in Sad_1(f)
        1{0<dist(M,S)<rho}
        1{f(M)>f(S)}
        delta_(M,r,theta,b,kappa,M,S).


The final two labels M,S are retained conceptually even when a Kac–Rice density later pushes the process to only the geometric marks.


Define the persistence-selected measure


    Xi_pers,rho(f)
      = sum over M,S
        1{0<dist(M,S)<rho}
        1{D_f(M)=S}
        delta_(M,r,theta,b,kappa,M,S).


The event D_f(M)=S automatically implies f(M)>f(S), maximum type, saddle type, merge-saddle status, non-essential birth, and finite positive lifetime.


THEOREM 3.1 — EXACT LOCAL BAR-PROCESS IDENTITY


The pushforward


    (M,S) -> (b=f(M), d=f(S), ell=f(M)-f(S))


maps Xi_pers,rho(f) bijectively onto the finite H0 bars whose paired critical-point distance is strictly less than rho.


Every such bar appears once.


PROOF


Theorem 2.1 gives a unique ordered persistence pair (M,D_f(M)) for each finite bar. The strict distance restriction either retains or removes that unique pair. No other pair maps to the same labeled bar because the birth maximum and death saddle are unique. QED.


4. ORDERING, ORIENTATION, AND THE FACTOR-OF-TWO FIREWALL


PROPOSITION 4.1 — NO ORDERING FACTOR


The process Max(f) x Sad_1(f) is already canonically ordered by critical-point type. Reversing (M,S) produces (S,M), which is not an element of Max x Sad_1. Therefore the ordered candidate process has no unordered-pair factor 1/2.


Even if one writes the pair as an unordered set {M,S}, the types recover the unique ordering. No factor two is created.


PROPOSITION 4.2 — NO ANGULAR DOUBLE COUNT


With theta in the half-open interval [0,2pi) and the directed displacement from M to S, every pair with distance below rho has one angle. The angle theta+pi corresponds to the reversed displacement from S to M and is not another atom of the ordered maximum-to-saddle process.


If a later source instead uses an unoriented line angle modulo pi, it must explicitly convert its angular intensity. The directed and unoriented conventions must not be mixed.


PROPOSITION 4.3 — BRANCH INCIDENCE IS A DIFFERENT PROCESS


Let S have two ascending separatrix branches under the gradient-ascent flow.


A branch-incidence process that counts each branch separately can count the same ordered pair (M,S) twice when both branches terminate at M.


The TB-G1 candidate process does not count branches. It uses a Boolean adjacency mark on an already once-counted ordered pair. Therefore branch multiplicity cannot enter the persistence coefficient silently.


5. MEASURABILITY


Let G be the set of C^2 functions on X that are Morse and have pairwise distinct critical values.


G is open in the C^2 topology.


THEOREM 5.1 — BOREL PERSISTENCE-PAIR MARK


On G, the map


    f -> Xi_pers,rho(f)


is Borel as a map into the space of finite point measures with the vague topology.


It may be extended fail-closed by the zero measure outside G, producing a Borel map on all C^2(X).


PROOF


Fix f_0 in G.


Because f_0 has finitely many nondegenerate critical points, the implicit-function theorem gives a C^2 neighborhood U of f_0 in which every critical point continues uniquely, continuously, and with unchanged Morse index.


Because the critical values of f_0 are pairwise distinct, U may be shrunk so their order remains unchanged and no critical value collision occurs.


Choose one regular level between every two consecutive critical values of f_0. Shrink U so these levels remain regular for every f in U. Gradient-flow continuation between neighboring regular levels identifies the corresponding superlevel cobordisms. Consequently the following finite combinatorial data are constant on U when expressed in the continued critical-point labels:


- which index-one saddles merge two distinct H0 components;
- which saddles are loop saddles;
- the elder representative of each component immediately above every merge saddle;
- the pairing label D_f(M)=S.


Thus the indicator 1{D_f(M_i(f))=S_j(f)} is locally constant in the continued labels. The critical-point positions and values, and therefore r, theta, b, ell, and kappa away from r=0 and the cut locus, vary continuously. A finite sum of continuously moving atoms with locally constant inclusion labels is continuous on U in the vague topology.


Hence the map is locally continuous on G and therefore Borel. The set G is open, so extension by zero outside G is Borel. QED.


REMARK 5.2 — MORSE–SMALE IS NOT NEEDED FOR MULTIPLICITY


The H0 elder pairing and unit multiplicity use only Morse critical points, distinct critical values, and superlevel connectivity.


Morse–Smale transversality is relevant to the separate gradient-adjacency mark, not to the existence or uniqueness of D_f(M).


6. GRADIENT ADJACENCY IS A SEPARATE MARK


Define the Boolean direct-gradient-adjacency event


    A_f(M,S)=1


when at least one ascending branch of the index-one saddle S has forward omega-limit M under the gradient-ascent flow.


This is an existential pair mark. It does not count the number of branches.


Define


    Xi_adj,rho(f)
      = 1{A_f(M,S)=1} Xi_all,rho(f),


and


    Xi_adj-pers,rho(f)
      = 1{A_f(M,S)=1} Xi_pers,rho(f).


The exact persistence process decomposes as


    Xi_pers,rho
      = Xi_adj-pers,rho + Xi_nonadj-pers,rho,


where


    Xi_nonadj-pers,rho
      = 1{A_f(M,S)=0} Xi_pers,rho.


The unit-multiplicity theorem applies to Xi_pers,rho. It does not state that Xi_adj-pers,rho equals Xi_pers,rho.


The elder representative of a connected superlevel component is defined by the highest maximum in that component. A separatrix branch entering that component is defined by the gradient basin containing the branch. These are different constructions. A component may contain several maxima after earlier merges. Therefore direct gradient adjacency of the elder representative to the later death saddle is an additional geometric property, not part of the elder-rule definition.


This document does not require a deterministic counterexample to use the exact all-pair process. It establishes the correct object identity without assuming the additional property.

PROPOSITION 7.1 — EXACT SELECTION IDENTITY

PROOF
