3.5 Strict positivity needed—and not needed


The limiting pin functionals correspond, up to nonzero factors, to the Fourier monomials


1, x, x^2, y, xy, x^3.


The periodized field has strictly positive spectral weight at every lattice frequency. If a nonzero linear combination of these functionals had zero variance, its Fourier polynomial would vanish at every point of Z^2. A finite polynomial vanishing on all of Z^2 is identically zero. Since the monomials are distinct, all coefficients must vanish. Hence G_0 is positive definite.


By continuity, G_r remains uniformly invertible for sufficiently small r.


For Q_L nondegeneracy, add the target monomial y^2. It is not in the span of


1,x,x^2,y,xy,x^3.


The joint Gram of the six limiting pins plus y^2 is therefore positive definite. The Schur complement gives


Var(Q_L | limiting pins)>0.


Important distinction:


The full limiting covariance of V_r need not be positive definite. In fact it is necessarily singular because A_M^0 and A_S^0 are deterministic under the conditioned law and D_M^0=D_S^0=Q_L. The theorem does not require strict positivity of Cov(V_0). It requires only:


- G_0 positive definite;
- Q_L nondegenerate after conditioning;
- continuous bounded means and covariance entries for V_r.


Those are sufficient for uniform fixed-order Gaussian moments.


3.6 Conditional law and moments

A Gaussian vector with uniformly bounded mean and covariance has uniformly bounded moments of every fixed order. Singular covariance at r=0 causes no problem for moment boundedness.
