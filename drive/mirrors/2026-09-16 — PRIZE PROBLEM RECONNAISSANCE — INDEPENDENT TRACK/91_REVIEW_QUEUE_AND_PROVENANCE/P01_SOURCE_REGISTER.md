## S01 — Thomas F. Bloom, Erdős Problem #3

https://www.erdosproblems.com/3

Search-index access; direct open returned 403. Listed open, $5,000. Full target: divergent reciprocal sum forces arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. This is a problem registry, not a guaranteed payment contract.

## S02 — Thomas F. Bloom, Erdős Problem #39

https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/39

Search-index access. Listed open, $500. Infinite Sidon all-large-N density N^(1/2-epsilon) for every epsilon>0. No solution in this package.

## S04 — Joseph L. Gerver (1977), The sum of the reciprocals of a set of integers with no arithmetic progression of k terms

https://studylib.net/doc/18211266/the-sum-of-the-reciprocals-of-a-set-of-integers-with

Original Proc. AMS article rehosted as parsed text, pages 211–214. Some formulas are garbled in the web rendering. Used for prior-art recognition of harmonic supremum questions; the same-k argument here is separately proved, not inferred from garbled formulas.

## S05 — Alexander Walker (2020), Fractal Sets and Arithmetic Progressions

https://awwalker.com/2020/02/14/fractal-sets-and-arithmetic-progressions/

Original research exposition. Gives the prime-digit AP-free construction, base-11 alphabet {0,1,2,4,5,7}, and approximate sum 4.4217. This construction is explicitly credited, not claimed new.

## S06 — Thomas Schmelzer and Robert Baillie (2008), Summing a Curious, Slowly Convergent Series

https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.2008.11920559

Prior art for efficient reciprocal summation with restricted digit strings. Full paper was not consumed as a dependency of the exact recurrence proved here. Bibliographic/abstract screening only.

## S07 — Christian Táfula (2026), Infinite Sidon-type sets for zero-sum linear forms

https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-026-02211-4

Primary research article. Introduction describes classical square-root subsequences and Ruzsa all-scales exponent sqrt(2)-1. Our elementary splice does not improve these records. No full audit of the article is claimed.

## S08 — Youness Lamzouri (2026), A new proof that more than 2/3 of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are simple and on the critical line

https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02882v1

Primary preprint. Remark 3.4 discusses optimality within the stated test-function family. Not a general RH impossibility theorem; our generic trace-moment construction is different.

## S09 — Terence Tao, Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values

https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03562

Primary research preprint, originally 2019 with later revisions. An almost-all/logarithmic-density statement is not the every-orbit conjecture. The elementary restricted no-go results here have no novelty claim.

## S10 — Jinyoung Park, IBS Discrete Math Seminar, 18 August 2026

https://dimag.ibs.re.kr/event/2026-08-18/

First-party seminar announcement: continuous convexity recently proved, discrete conjecture still open; identifies a $1,000 prize for discrete version. Corrects earlier blanket exclusion of Talagrand convexity.

## S11 — Ruben Ascoli, Xiaoyu He, Jinyoung Park, Michel Talagrand (2026), A reformulation of the discrete Convexity Conjecture via k-thresholds

https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11183

Primary preprint. Equivalent threshold formulation and special graph-property cases. Abstract-level frontier screening, not a full proof audit or a new attack completed here.
