1. PURPOSE


The project may automate repetitive coordination, formalization, verification, telemetry, and record maintenance through graduated, class-bounded, reversible practices. Automation should reduce stale-state and routing costs without converting model confidence into scientific authority, obscuring provenance, or impersonating the operator.


This framework contains recommended engineering practices. It has no ballot, roster, majority, quorum, observer, nonparticipating, or consensus mechanism. ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini are active parallel contributors. Dylan Roy's direct operator instruction is the sole governance authority.


2. AUTHORITY CHANNEL


Operator decisions originate from Dylan Roy through an authenticated user session. A recorded operator decision should identify a unique decision ID, exact object or source hash when relevant, scope, timestamp, exclusions, and decision type. A contributor may record an instruction as executed under Dylan Roy's directive but may not impersonate or sign as Dylan Roy.


No private passphrase, private key, recovery secret, authentication token, or equivalent secret should be stored in Drive, prompts, comments, or model-readable collaboration artifacts. A future signed-decision system should keep private keys outside Drive and may store only public verification information.


Dylan Roy's existing OP-PROT-008 approval records stand on their own. They are not contingent on, separate from, or delayed by a model ballot because no model ballot exists.


3. RECOMMENDED GLOBAL PRACTICES


The following are recommended whenever practical:


• exact source identity and content-addressed versioning;
• actor, provider, model, and evidence-lineage disclosure;
• protected Operator Decisions and canonical-ledger boundaries;
• append-only or revision-preserving audit records;
• a global pause mechanism and documented recovery path;
• source-hash and installed-hash comparison;
• injection, rejected-injection, revocation, retention, hard-kill, recovery, and rollback tests;
• Google-runtime acceptance and inspected-run receipts;
• transition alarms and dry-run comparisons;
• explicit scope, exclusions, affected dependencies, and reopening paths.


These steps are recommended practice, not blocking gates. They never require a vote. A contributor may proceed while accurately recording which checks were not performed, failed, or remain uncertain. Dylan Roy may direct work to proceed regardless.


4. PRACTICE CLASS 1 — REGISTER AND SURFACE MAINTENANCE


Contributors and automation may reconcile dashboards, START_HERE pages, queues, indexes, telemetry, and navigation aids to trusted source records. They may create additive alarms, receipts, reports, and templates.


Recommended before write-capable automation is relied upon: byte-exact reconstructable source, source-hash/installed-hash comparison, source review, declared negative-control battery, runtime acceptance, and an inspected live-run receipt.


Class 1 work does not by itself create mathematical authority, alter an operator decision, terminalize an item, change a theorem grade, replace a machine root, release material, or authorize irreversible deletion.


5. PRACTICE CLASS 2 — NARROW CLOSURE SUPPORT


Contributors may define narrow closure classes, generate closure packages, run shadow pilots, predict register updates, compare predictions with later operator decisions, and report false positives, false negatives, stale sources, scope leakage, duplicate writes, and lineage errors.


Recommended contents for a closure package include: exact object identity, evidence, assumptions, exclusions, affected and unaffected dependencies, proposed terminal label, register actions, source and receipt hashes, and reopening rule. Independent or diverse-method review is useful evidence throughout preparation. At the terminal / closed / sealed / approved / promoted step, OP-PROT-011 requires one organizationally-distinct-family review or Dylan Roy's direct approval.


Only Dylan Roy's direct ratification promotes a proposed closure to canonical terminal status when such operator ratification is required by the project's designated ledger practice. Contributors may prepare, test, and record proposals without waiting for another model.


6. PRACTICE CLASS 3 — THEOREM-PROMOTION PREPARATION


Contributors may freeze a candidate theorem body by byte count and hash; create subchain-specific review packets; collect exact source-bound reviews; detect missing proof coverage; formalize statements in Lean or other proof assistants; produce numerical and symbolic corroboration; and generate a complete promotion proposal.


A contributor must not represent a candidate as canonical merely because it has reviews, formal code, or model agreement. Final canonical promotion remains Dylan Roy's decision on the exact object or hash and should be recorded in the designated canonical ledger.


7. PRACTICE CLASS 4 — AUTONOMOUS RESEARCH CONTINUATION


Contributors may open bounded research tasks, route them to any active model, preserve questions and answers, synthesize revisions after objections, run simulations, attempt proofs, construct counterexamples, and continue unresolved mathematics.


Silence, repetition, model count, or agreement is not proof. Evidence quality, derivation, reproducibility, falsification resistance, and explicit assumptions determine scientific weight.


8. HUMAN-ONLY IRREVERSIBLE TERMINAL ACTIONS


The following are performed or authorized by Dylan Roy:


• final theorem or canonical-state promotion;
• external release;
• permanent deletion or irreversible destruction;
• machine-root replacement;
• any other action Dylan Roy explicitly reserves to himself.


Routine research, review, formalization, telemetry, reversible organization, nonterminal records, source publication, testing, and shadow work may proceed freely under OP-PROT-011.


9. AUDIT, PAUSE, EXPIRY, AND RECOVERY


Automated writes should identify source version, actor, checked conditions, before/after state, affected objects, prohibited effects checked, and rollback path. A global pause may disable future automated writes while preserving read-only monitoring and provenance. Time-limited authorizations should record their expiry. Incorrect writes should be corrected transparently through revision history or additive correction records when practical.


10. CURRENT EFFECT


The graduated-autonomy architecture is available as a non-blocking engineering framework. There is no activation ballot, post-test vote, three-of-four requirement, eligible line, observer designation, or nonparticipating status. Existing operator approvals remain valid within their recorded scope.


All four active contributors may perform preparation, source publication, testing, shadow qualification, formalization, telemetry, bounded coordination, and record generation in parallel. Technical evidence practices improve reliability but do not halt work.
