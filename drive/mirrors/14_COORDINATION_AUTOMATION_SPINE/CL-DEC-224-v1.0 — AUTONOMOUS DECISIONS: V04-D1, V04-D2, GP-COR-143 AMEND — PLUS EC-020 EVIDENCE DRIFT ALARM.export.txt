1. DECISION — V04-D2: CARRIER RE-UPLOAD POLICY
============================================================================


QUESTION: must v0.4 carriers be re-uploaded as R0.2-canonical bytes (no BOM, one LF), or may v0.3-era BOM-bearing carriers be enumerated as-is with exact stored identities?


DECISION: YES — ENUMERATE AS-IS. Do NOT re-upload existing carriers. Carriers created from this point forward SHALL be written R0.2-canonical (UTF-8 no BOM, exactly one trailing LF).


PREDICATE AND EVIDENCE:
(a) TRC-STANDARD-001-v1.3-R0.2 §2.3 governs canonicalization OF THE MANIFEST. §3.1 binds pre-root leaves by ROOT_SHA256 over their EXACT STORED BYTES. A BOM is simply part of those bytes; it does not impair binding. The standard does not require leaf canonicality, and reading it as though it did would be a scope expansion, not compliance.
(b) Re-uploading destroys every existing verified identity and forces complete re-verification of the leaf set, for zero gain in binding strength.
(c) DECISIVE, and new tonight: re-upload requires moving carrier bytes through the retrieval/transfer channel, which CL-AUD-223 proved is lossy AND correlated-error-prone. Every re-upload is a fresh opportunity for silent corruption that multi-pass agreement cannot reliably detect. A policy that mandates unnecessary byte transport in this environment actively manufactures risk.


Enumerating stored bytes requires no transport at all. That is the safer path and it is also the cheaper one.


CONSEQUENCE: v0.4 may bind existing carriers by exact stored identity. Where a bound carrier internally declares capsule_id "TRC-EC020-v0.3", the manifest SHALL record that label discrepancy explicitly as creation provenance rather than rewriting the file.
