1. WHAT THIS CLOSES


CL-PREFLIGHT-221 finding M1 established that no export identity may be frozen from a single transcription pass, and then applied that rule against my own earlier work: "CL-PREFLIGHT-219's P0–P3 did not [meet the bar], and should be re-run under the dual-pass rule before any manifest binds them."


That remediation is now done. A worker with no access to the retained prior-pass material independently re-fetched all four primaries by exact Drive ID and profiled them. The two passes were then byte-compared.

The values are UNCHANGED from CL-PREFLIGHT-219. The single-pass figures published there were correct. What changes is their evidentiary standing: they were previously asserted on one observation and are now corroborated by two independent ones, taken hours apart in separate contexts.




3. CONSEQUENCE


All 17 EC-021 export identities — P0–P3 here, and the 13 evidence documents in CL-PREFLIGHT-221 Tier 1 — now meet the dual-pass bar. The identity set is internally consistent under a single evidentiary standard, with no legacy single-pass values remaining in it.


GP-DATA-114-v1.1 (E8) remains the sole document with no freezable export identity, per correction C2, and remains bound by its embedded artifact hashes instead. That is unchanged and is not a defect in the identity set; it is a documented property of that object.


Nothing else in CL-PREFLIGHT-221 is amended. Corrections C1 and C3, finding M1, and the verdict APPROVE WITH CORRECTION all stand as published.




4. DISCLOSURE


The second pass was executed blind: the worker was instructed not to read, list, or open the directory holding the prior pass, precisely because consulting it would have collapsed the independence being tested. Its output was compared against the retained prior-pass bytes only after it was complete. Drive access was read-only; no file in the operator's Drive was created, copied, moved, modified, or deleted by this check. Identities bind to retrieval time 2026-07-25.


A note on what this does and does not show: two agreeing passes make a transcription defect much less likely, not impossible. It remains conceivable that a deterministic corruption would reproduce identically across passes. What the dual-pass rule buys is the elimination of the sparse, position-random defect class actually observed in E8 — which is the failure mode that was silently threatening the program.
