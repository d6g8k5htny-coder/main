============================================================================
FINDING M1 — RETRIEVAL-PATH FIDELITY (methodological; affects the whole TRC program)
============================================================================


The C2 defect is not confined to E8, and the program should not treat it as one document's problem.


Observed: for documents whose text is ordinary prose, two independent passes reproduce byte-identically — 13 for 13 this pass, across sizes from 2,432 to 15,564 bytes. For GP-DATA-114-v1.1, which carries two long unbroken high-entropy base64 lines (4,556 and 2,256 characters) as document CONTENT, three passes produced three different results, with sparse single-character substitutions and one multi-character insertion.


Assessment: the corruption is in the retrieval and transfer path, not in the stored document. Two same-length fetches differing by sparse single-character substitutions is the signature of a lossy copy channel, not of a document exporter, which would be expected to emit identical bytes each run. The concentration in the incompressible base64 region, and the perfect reproduction of 13 prose documents through the identical path, both point the same way. I cannot localize the defect further between the connector and the model-mediated transfer step with the tooling available, and I am not going to guess.


Consequences the program should adopt regardless of cause:
1. No export hash may be frozen from a single pass. Two independent passes must agree byte-for-byte. Every identity in this record meets that bar; CL-PREFLIGHT-219's P0–P3 did not, and should be re-run under the dual-pass rule before any manifest binds them.
2. Documents carrying embedded high-entropy payloads must be bound by payload hashes with internal checksums, not by document-export hashes.
3. A single-pass export hash appearing in any prior TRC record is not evidence of drift if it fails to reproduce — it is first evidence of a transcription event, and must be re-fetched before any drift verdict is issued.


This finding costs the program one prior assumption and buys back a defect class that would otherwise have surfaced as a false drift alarm or, worse, as a silently wrong root.
