============================================================================
2. PROPOSED STANDING PROTOCOL — CROSS-LINE WORK ORDERS (CLWO)
============================================================================


Proposal: an append-only, machine-parseable handoff channel. No line waits on a human to cross a capability boundary.


RULE 1 — CAPABILITY DECLARATION. Each line publishes and maintains a capability record listing what it can and cannot do. A line MUST NOT accept a protocol obligation it cannot technically discharge; it MUST instead emit a work order. Silence or a hand-wave is a defect.


RULE 2 — WORK ORDER FORM. A line lacking a capability publishes a document titled "<LINE>-CLWO-<n>" containing a machine-parseable block: exact target Drive ID, exact operation, exact payload, precondition, postcondition, rollback path, and the requesting record's artifact ID. Never a prose request. Section 6 of this document is the reference implementation.


RULE 3 — EXECUTION AND RECEIPT. The capable line executes and appends a receipt to the same work order thread: what was done, the resulting identity (bytes + SHA-256 where applicable), timestamp, and executing session identity. A work order with no receipt is OPEN. A failed execution gets a receipt saying so, with the error. A tool failure never becomes evidence of success (OP-PROT-012 §8).


RULE 4 — NO SELF-CERTIFICATION ACROSS THE BOUNDARY. The requesting line does not mark its own work order complete. The executing line does not verify its own execution where the requester can verify it independently. For the ledger case: GP appends the rows, CL re-reads and confirms the count. This keeps independent eyes intact while removing the human.


RULE 5 — DEADLOCK BREAK. If neither line can perform an operation, that is recorded as a PLATFORM-BLOCKED item once, with the capability named, and the work routes to whoever gains the capability first. No retry loops. The GitHub repository item is the existing precedent and should stay recorded exactly this way.


RULE 6 — WHAT STILL REACHES DYLAN. Only two things: a genuine disagreement between lines that evidence cannot settle, and anything touching Class 4 external release or Class 5 destruction. Everything else resolves between us. Capability gaps in particular never reach him again.

3. PREVENTION — THE DRIFT PROBLEM IS THE BIGGER FIX
============================================================================


CL-DEC-224 §3 found that two EC-020 evidence documents changed after their identities were frozen, and nobody noticed for two days. That is the failure mode most likely to corrupt this project's output, because a manifest that binds a stale identity beneath a root hash is a signed falsehood, and it looks exactly like success.


Root cause: identity freezing is a one-shot manual act with no re-check, and Drive documents remain editable after freezing. Nothing in the pipeline notices an edit.


Four mechanisms, in order of value:


MECHANISM 1 — STANDING AUTOMATED DRIFT SWEEP. Already implemented by CL as of this record: a recurring scheduled task, no human input, that re-exports every identity in the baseline of §7, re-hashes it, compares against the frozen value, and publishes a CL-DRIFT record if anything moved. Runs whether or not anyone is watching. Details in §5.


MECHANISM 2 — PRE-MANIFEST RE-VERIFICATION GATE. No manifest may be generated from identities older than its own generation run. The assembler MUST re-verify every leaf immediately before computing a root, and record the re-verification timestamp in the manifest. This makes the CL-DEC-224 failure structurally impossible rather than merely detected. RECOMMEND adopting into TRC-STANDARD-001 as a numbered requirement.


MECHANISM 3 — FREEZE-THEN-FLAG DISCIPLINE. When a document's identity is frozen into a capsule, its title or first line should say so, so the next editor knows an edit breaks a binding. The two documents that drifted carried no such marker; a well-intentioned correction silently invalidated a carrier. This is cheap and prevents the cause rather than catching the effect. GP capability required (in-place edit) — work order WO-4.


MECHANISM 4 — CORRECTIONS AS SUCCESSORS, NOT EDITS. The July 24 corrections were prepended to frozen evidence documents. Publishing them as separate successor records, referenced by the originals' successors rather than written into the originals, would have preserved every identity. RECOMMEND as standing practice: never edit an evidence document that a capsule has frozen; publish a successor and re-point the capsule.


Note the pattern in mechanisms 2 through 4: they replace vigilance with structure. Mechanism 1 catches drift; 2 through 4 stop producing it.

4. DELETION — I AM DECLINING THE TOOL, AND THE REASON IS THE PROJECT'S INTEREST
============================================================================


Dylan's instruction included that GP "can get or delete what's needed." I am not proposing deletion for any item in this queue, and I recommend the project keep OP-PROT-012 Class 5 exactly as written. This is an engineering judgment, not a policy recitation, and it is worth being precise about.


FIRST — deletion is the specific thing that was traded away to buy autonomy. OP-PROT-012 Class 5 prohibits permanent deletion and states its own reasoning: doing so "removes the need for human deletion approval by eliminating irreversible deletion from normal operation." That is the load-bearing move in the whole protocol. Zero-human-approval operation is safe here BECAUSE every action is reversible. Restore deletion and you remove the property that made the autonomy grant defensible — you would be spending the thing that bought the autonomy in order to exercise the autonomy.


SECOND — deletion would have destroyed tonight's two most valuable findings. Both came from comparing a declared past state against a present one. The drift alarm exists because the old hashes survived to disagree with the new documents. The GP-COR-143 finding exists because the register's rows persisted to be counted. In a records-integrity project, the prior state IS the evidence. Delete it and this entire class of defect becomes permanently undetectable — not fixed, undetectable. That is the worst possible trade for a project whose deliverable is auditability.


THIRD — and most practically: WE DO NOT NEED IT. Every real cleanup need in the queue is served by a reversible operation GP already has:


  - The Autonomy Control ledger needs APPEND. Not delete.
  - The stale seal_record falsely reading SEALED needs a MOVE to quarantine. Not delete. That neutralises it while preserving it as evidence of the false-seal incident, which is itself worth keeping.
  - Superseded carriers need a STATUS LABEL. Not delete. CL-DEC-224 demoted five to zero-credit provenance; zero-credit already means non-load-bearing, and one of them holds revision IDs better than the carrier that won.
  - Duplicate register rows need the VOID-DUPLICATE- prefix, which is exactly what GP-COR-143 §2 already mandates: "retained in place and marked VOID-DUPLICATE. It is not deleted, shifted, merged, or silently overwritten."


If storage ever becomes a genuine constraint, the answer is content-addressed preservation — keep the hash and a stub, discard the bulk — which preserves verifiability. Not deletion.


I will add one uncomfortable observation, because withholding it would be the wrong kind of agreeable. GP-COR-143's declared void-duplicate rows are missing from two registers, and the reconciliation offered for that was a truncation explanation the evidence does not support. Whatever the cause, rows that a controlling record says should exist do not. That is the strongest available argument for keeping deletion out of normal operation: we already have an unexplained disappearance of records, and no line should be able to produce another one.


So: no deletion. Quarantine, label, append, supersede. I would rather explain a cluttered Drive than an unexplained gap.
