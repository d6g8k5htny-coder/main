## 1. Exact conditioning and whitening

Let C be the endpoint value/gradient vector, G=Cov(C,C), X=Cov(H,C), H0=Cov(H,H), and c its fixed pin vector. The exact six-pin pair mean and covariance are

\[
\mu_0=XG^{-1}c,\qquad S_0=H_0-XG^{-1}X^T.
\]

Interval Cholesky certifies G and S0 positive definite. If G=Lc Lcᵀ and S0=Lh Lhᵀ are their unique positive-diagonal Cholesky factors, define the fixed random forms

\[
U=L_c^{-1}C,\qquad
R=L_h^{-1}(H-XG^{-1}C).
\]

Exactly, Cov(U)=I, Cov(R)=I and Cov(U,R)=0. The code encloses these same fixed forms; their interval entries are not independent freely chosen coefficients. The Cholesky identities define the exact whitening, rather than treating a rounded approximate inverse as exact.

For Y=(f(y),fx(y),fy(y)), let B=Cov(U,Y), F=Cov(R,Y), E=Cov(Y,Y)=diag(1,a2,a2), and μ=E[Y∣C=c]. Then

\[
D=E-B^TB,\quad z=(v,0,0)^T-\mu,\quad
A=FD^{-1}F^T,\quad m=FD^{-1}z.
\]

The whitened laws are Q=N(0,I6) and P=N(m,I6-A). Since A is a covariance reduction, it is positive semidefinite. The certified A-norm <1 guarantees finiteness of the Gaussian χ² integral. Gaussian conditioning identities are standard; see [GPML Appendix A](https://gaussianprocess.org/gpml/chapters/RWA.pdf). The source-specific implementation here constructs and encloses all of their inputs.

## 2. Kernel enclosures, including both infinite tails

For image derivatives use probabilists' Hermite polynomials,

\[
k^{(n)}(s)=Z^{-1}\sum_m(-1)^n\operatorname{He}_n(s+24m)e^{-(s+24m)^2/2}.
\]

The implementation retains m=-1,0,1. For |s|≤R<47, omitted |m|≥2 satisfy |s+24m|≥1. If Cn is the sum of absolute Hermite coefficients, then |He_n(s+24m)|≤Cn(24|m|+R)^n. The first omitted majorant and ratio are

\[
t_n=C_n(48+R)^n e^{-(48-R)^2/2},\qquad
\rho_n=\left(\frac{72+R}{48+R}\right)^n
e^{-((72-R)^2-(48-R)^2)/2}.
\]

Both successive polynomial ratios and successive Gaussian ratios decrease with |m|≥2. Thus the absolute tail is at most 2tn/(1-ρn), after verifying ρn<1. Cn is generated by C0=C1=1, C(n+1)=Cn+n C(n-1). R=18 safely contains every argument used by the spatial cover. The denominator is separately enclosed using R=0. Dividing by a truncated normalizer without its tail is not used.

For Taylor remainders, use the equivalent normalized spectral representation, with h=π/12 and k_j=jh. The bound

\[
\sup_s|k^{(q)}(s)|\le
\frac{\sum_{|j|\le116}e^{-k_j^2/2}|k_j|^q+T_q}
{\sum_{|j|\le116}e^{-k_j^2/2}}
\]

is valid because the true denominator is larger. Tq bounds omitted |j|≥117 by its first term and the decreasing ratio exp(-235h²/2)(118/117)^q. In particular odd moments use absolute frequencies and cannot cancel to zero.

Arb performs every operation with an outward ball enclosure at 384-bit midpoint precision. Finite sums are never identified with their infinite limits. Ball arithmetic plus proved analytic remainder bounds follows the standard validated-numerics approach described by [Johansson](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.07942v1); the present kernel and covariance argument supplies the problem-specific bounds.

## 3. Stable spatial boxes

Every fixed form is a linear combination of field jets at (±r/2,0). For a form V=Σ c(p,α) ∂^α f(p),

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(V,\partial^\beta f(y))=
\sum_{p,\alpha}c(p,\alpha)(-1)^{|\alpha|}
k^{(\alpha_x+\beta_x)}(y_x-p_x)
k^{(\alpha_y+\beta_y)}(y_y).
\]

The sign is determined by differentiating the fixed endpoint; the formula already accounts for the parity change from p-y to y-p. For each α_y group, the code sums the x-Taylor coefficients of the entire linear form before enclosing the polynomial on a box. This preserves the near-pin cancellations that direct independent entry enclosures would destroy.

A degree-28 expansion on |t|≤h has remainder at most

\[
\frac{h^{29}}{29!}\sum_{p,\alpha:\,\alpha_y=a}
|c(p,\alpha)|\,\Lambda_{\alpha_x+\beta_x+29}.
\]

All mixed field derivatives required for orders 0–4 of Y are evaluated. The box side is 1/4. Integer-indexed cells [i/4,(i+1)/4]×[j/4,(j+1)/4] are retained whenever their minimum squared radius is ≤17² and maximum squared radius is ≥5². Both decisions use exact integer arithmetic. This produces 13,604 cells and covers the closed annulus. Boundary cells may extend outside it; certifying those larger sets is conservative.

No sampled point, finite-difference residual, empirical margin or unverified eigenvalue is used to cover a cell.

## 4. Raw derivative norm recurrences

For each order n≤4, mixed partial norms give a direction-uniform bound by

\[
\|\partial_u^n V\|\le\sum_{j=0}^n {n\choose j}
\|\partial_x^j\partial_y^{n-j}V\|,
\qquad |u|=1.
\]

This is conservative because |ux|,|uy|≤1. Let bn, fn and zn be the resulting Frobenius/vector bounds for B, F and z on a cell. Set d*=min diag(E)-b0²>0. For n≥1 put dn=Σj binom(n,j)bj b(n-j). Bounds for J=D⁻¹ and its derivatives are

\[
j_0=1/d_*,\qquad
j_n=j_0\sum_{i=1}^n{n\choose i}d_i j_{n-i}.
\]

Leibniz products give an upper bound qn for (FJ)^(n), then

\[
a_n=\sum_j{n\choose j}q_jf_{n-j},\qquad
m_n=\sum_j{n\choose j}q_jz_{n-j}.
\]

The an bound the Frobenius norm as well as the spectral norm. Taking maxima over every retained cell gives the certificate's uniform input arrays. These are actual field inputs to the previously derived RN calculus, closing the prior conditional-input gap for this sector and fixed r.

## 5. χ², the square-root modulus, and the pair weight

For Q=N(0,I) and P=N(m,I-A), direct Gaussian integration gives

\[
\log(1+\chi^2)=-\tfrac12\log\det(I-A^2)+m^T(I+A)^{-1}m.
\]

The ordered inverse/product recurrences through order four yield the recorded logQ and χ² derivative bounds. They do not assume matrices commute. The exponential derivatives use complete Bell polynomials.

The square root is handled as the Hilbert norm ||p/q-1|| in L²(Q), not by dividing by √χ² near zero. The included prior modulus theorem bounds the speed of p/q using uniform A, m, A′ and m′ bounds. Consequently it gives the stated path Lipschitz constant even where χ² vanishes. No fourth derivative of √χ² is claimed without a positive floor.

The source pair-weight estimate is

\[
h_{L^2}=(E_Q[(\det H_M)^4]E_Q[(\det H_S)^4])^{1/4},
\quad \kappa_{pair}=h_{L^2}\sqrt{\chi^2}/Z_{lo}.
\]

The fourth determinant moments are computed by the exact Gaussian raw-moment recurrence in the original six-pin law. This gives hL2<0.014009957. Scaling uses the independently supplied source floor Zlo=0.0077592917375327855 from the pinned H3_RUNG_FLOOR.md. The κ_pair statements retain that explicit imported hypothesis; the Gaussian χ² results do not require it.

## 6. Gradient-density and window-mass ratios

Split D into its value and gradient blocks (D00,c,Gg) and μ into (μ0,μg). Conditional on zero gradient,

\[
s^2=D_{00}-cG_g^{-1}c^T,\qquad
\mu_t=\mu_0-cG_g^{-1}\mu_g.
\]

Exactly, d*≤s²≤1. Also Gg≥g*I where g*=a2-b0², and ||c||≤b0². Let un bound the derivatives of μ. A bound for |μt| is μ*=u0(1+b0²/g*). The gradient density ratio obeys

\[
e^{-u_0^2/(2g_*)}\le R_{pg}\le a_2/g_*.
\]

For every v in the complete positive mark window, the conditional normal density divided by the unconditioned standard normal density is

\[
R(v)=s^{-1}\exp\left(\frac{v^2}{2}-\frac{(v-\mu_t)^2}{2s^2}\right).
\]

The following bounds are uniform in v:

\[
\exp\left[-\frac{b^2(1-d_*)+2b\mu_*+\mu_*^2}{2d_*}\right]
\le R(v)\le d_*^{-1/2}e^{b\mu_*}.
\]

Rwm is the standard-normal-density weighted average of R(v) over the window, so it satisfies the same bounds. This avoids subtracting two nearly equal distribution-function values in the certificate.

For derivatives, the same inverse recurrences bound Gg⁻¹, μt, s² and (s²)⁻¹. Differentiate

\[
\log R_{pg}=\log a_2-\tfrac12\log\det G_g-
\tfrac12\mu_g^TG_g^{-1}\mu_g,
\quad
\log R(v)=-\tfrac12\log s^2-
\tfrac12(v-\mu_t)^2/s^2+v^2/2.
\]

Multinomial product bounds and Bell polynomials give orders 1–4. Integrating the uniform bounds for R(v) gives those for Rwm. The integration interval and reference density are independent of y, and all functions are smooth with a uniform positive covariance floor on the compact cover, justifying differentiation under the integral.
