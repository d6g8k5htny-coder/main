P02-LM-009 — EXACT ADAPTIVE REGULAR-DOMAIN TAIL


1. SETTING


Fix an integer m>=1, a number r_0 in (0,1], and epsilon_0>0. For every r in (0,r_0], let U_r be an R^m-valued random vector with law Q_r.


Define


    R(U)=1+sum_{j=1}^m |U_j|.


Assume that every fixed positive integer moment of R is uniformly bounded: for every integer p>=1 there exists M_p<infinity such that


    sup_{0<r<=r_0} E_{Q_r}[R(U_r)^p]<=M_p.


Define the adaptive regular event


    REG_r={r R(U_r)^5<=epsilon_0}.


2. THEOREM


For every N>0 there exists a finite constant C_N, independent of r, such that


    Q_r(REG_r^c)<=C_N r^N


for every r in (0,r_0].


Equivalently,


    Q_r(REG_r^c)=O(r^N)


for every N>0. Thus the regular-domain failure is super-polynomially small.


No exponential tail is claimed.


3. PROOF


Fix N>0. Choose an integer p satisfying


    p>=5N.


On REG_r^c,


    rR(U_r)^5>epsilon_0,


so


    R(U_r)>(epsilon_0/r)^{1/5}.


Markov's inequality gives


    Q_r(REG_r^c)
      <=E_{Q_r}[R(U_r)^p]
        /(epsilon_0/r)^{p/5}
      <=M_p epsilon_0^{-p/5} r^{p/5}.


Since 0<r<=r_0<=1 and p/5>=N,


    r^{p/5}<=r^N.


Therefore


    Q_r(REG_r^c)
      <=C_N r^N,


where


    C_N=M_p epsilon_0^{-p/5}<infinity.


This proves the theorem. QED.

6. WHY AN EXPONENTIAL TAIL IS NOT CLAIMED


Uniform boundedness of every fixed moment implies super-polynomial decay through a different chosen moment for each requested exponent N. It does not by itself provide constants A,b,p>0 satisfying a single bound


    Q_r(REG_r^c)<=A exp(-b r^{-p}).


Such an exponential statement requires stronger uniform moment-growth or Gaussian concentration information. The present theorem deliberately avoids that overclaim.
