2. FROZEN STATEMENT
============================================================


Fix an integer m>=1, r_0 in (0,1], and epsilon_0>0. For each r in (0,r_0], let U_r be an R^m-valued random vector with law Q_r. Define


R(U)=1+sum_{j=1}^m |U_j|.


Assume that for every positive integer p there exists M_p<infinity such that


sup_{0<r<=r_0} E_{Q_r}[R(U_r)^p] <= M_p.


Define


REG_r={r R(U_r)^5 <= epsilon_0}.


Then, for every N>0, there exists C_N<infinity independent of r such that


Q_r(REG_r^c) <= C_N r^N


for all r in (0,r_0]. Equivalently, Q_r(REG_r^c)=O(r^N) for every N>0.


EXACT SCOPE
- Q_r may vary with r.
- Coordinates of U_r may be dependent.
- No density, Gaussianity, or coordinate independence is required.
- The requested exponent N need not be an integer.
- A different fixed moment order p may be selected for each requested N.


KEY EXCLUSIONS
The lemma does not prove a single fixed exponential or stretched-exponential tail, the moment hypothesis itself, the Palm-transfer interface, a determinant-weighted estimate by itself, exact bad-event inclusion, P0.2, theorem promotion, or external release.

3. COMPLETE LINEAR PROOF
============================================================


Fix N>0. Choose an integer p>=5N. On REG_r^c,


r R(U_r)^5 > epsilon_0,


hence


R(U_r) > (epsilon_0/r)^(1/5).


Markov's inequality therefore gives


Q_r(REG_r^c)
<= E_{Q_r}[R(U_r)^p] / (epsilon_0/r)^(p/5)
<= M_p epsilon_0^(-p/5) r^(p/5).


Because 0<r<=r_0<=1 and p/5>=N,


r^(p/5) <= r^N.


Thus


Q_r(REG_r^c) <= C_N r^N,


where C_N=M_p epsilon_0^(-p/5)<infinity. This proves the frozen theorem.


PRIMARY-VERIFICATION VERDICT
PASS WITHIN EXACT SCOPE. Every implication is elementary and complete. No hidden density, independence, or Gaussian assumption is used. The direction of the power comparison is correct only because r<=1, which is explicitly included through r_0<=1.
