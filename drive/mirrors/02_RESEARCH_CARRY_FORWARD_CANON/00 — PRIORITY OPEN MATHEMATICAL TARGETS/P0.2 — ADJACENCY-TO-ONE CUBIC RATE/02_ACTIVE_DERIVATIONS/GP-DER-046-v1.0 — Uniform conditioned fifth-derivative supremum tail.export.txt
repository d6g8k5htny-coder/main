# Uniform conditioned fifth-derivative supremum tail


## Executive result


The named obligation GT5 from GP-DER-045 is proved for the exact normalized-periodized Bargmann–Fock field conditioned on the exact six endpoint value/gradient pins.


Fix a compact normalized chart D. Let rD denote its shrinking physical realization, and define


    H_{5,r}(D)
      = max_{|alpha|=5} sup_{x in rD} |partial^alpha f(x)|.


There exist constants C,c,t_0,r_0>0, depending only on the fixed torus, the fixed pin marks, and D, such that for every 0<r<=r_0 and t>=t_0,


    P( H_{5,r}(D) > t | exact six pins )
      <= C exp(-c t^2).


More explicitly, if


    sigma_5^2
      = max_{|alpha|=5} Var(partial^alpha f(0)),


and M_5,B_5 are the uniform conditioned-mean and centered-supremum constants constructed below, then


    P( H_{5,r}(D) > M_5+B_5+u | pins )
      <= 6 exp(-u^2/(2 sigma_5^2)),
      u>=0.


Consequently, for every gamma>0,


    P( H_{5,r}(D) > r^{-gamma} | pins )
      <= C_gamma exp(-c_gamma r^{-2gamma})


for all sufficiently small r. Under the determinant-weighted typed pair-Palm law, the same event has super-polynomially small probability once the already established r^2 normalizer and r^4 weight-second-moment bounds are used.


This closes the high-derivative probabilistic input required to transfer the degree-four endpoint certificate to the exact field. It does not construct the remaining finite-jet capture-margin event.

## 3. Conditional derivative processes


For every multi-index alpha=(alpha_1,alpha_2) with |alpha|=5, define on K_r


    X_{r,alpha}(x)
      = partial^alpha f(x) | {L_r=y_r}.


This is a noncentered Gaussian process. Write


    X_{r,alpha}(x)
      = m_{r,alpha}(x)+Z_{r,alpha}(x),


where Z_{r,alpha} is centered.


Let


    c_{r,alpha}(x)
      = Cov(partial^alpha f(x),L_r).


Then


    m_{r,alpha}(x)
      = c_{r,alpha}(x) G_r^{-1} y_r,


and


    Cov(Z_{r,alpha}(x),Z_{r,alpha}(x'))
      = Cov(partial^alpha f(x),partial^alpha f(x'))
        -c_{r,alpha}(x)G_r^{-1}c_{r,alpha}(x')^T.


The conditional covariance does not depend on the numerical pin values y_r.


## 4. Uniform bound on the conditioned mean


### Lemma 4.1 — bounded fifth-derivative conditional mean


There exists M_5<infinity such that


    max_{|alpha|=5}
    sup_{0<r<=r_0}
    sup_{x in K_r}
      |m_{r,alpha}(x)|
      <= M_5.


### Proof


Write x=rX with X in D. Each component of


    c_{r,alpha}(rX)


is a covariance between a fixed fifth derivative at rX and one of the six divided-difference pin functionals. The apparent inverse powers of r in the divided differences are removable singularities: Taylor expansion of the analytic exact-torus kernel shows that these covariances extend continuously to r=0, uniformly for X in D.


Thus


    sup_{r,X,alpha} ||c_{r,alpha}(rX)|| < infinity.


The exact-torus regularity theorem supplies a uniform bound on ||G_r^{-1}||, and y_r is uniformly bounded. Multiplying the three bounds proves the claim. QED.


The same argument applies to any fixed derivative order, not only the nine midpoint jets used in the finite-dimensional law.


## 5. Uniform residual variance


Conditioning a Gaussian vector on linear observations subtracts a positive-semidefinite Schur term. Therefore for every linear functional A of the field,


    Var(A | L_r)
      <= Var(A).


Apply this to A=partial^alpha f(x). Stationarity gives


    Var(Z_{r,alpha}(x))
      <= Var(partial^alpha f(x))
      = Var(partial^alpha f(0))
      <= sigma_5^2,


where


    sigma_5^2
      = max_{|alpha|=5} Var(partial^alpha f(0))
      < infinity.


Finiteness follows from analyticity of the normalized-periodized covariance kernel.


This variance bound is uniform in r, x, and alpha.


## 6. Uniform canonical-metric Lipschitz bound


For fixed alpha, let


    d_{r,alpha}(x,x')^2
      = E[(Z_{r,alpha}(x)-Z_{r,alpha}(x'))^2]


be the canonical metric.


Conditioning reduces the variance of the difference functional, so


    d_{r,alpha}(x,x')^2
      <= E[(partial^alpha f(x)-partial^alpha f(x'))^2]


under the unconditioned stationary field.


Let v=x-x' and parameterize the segment


    x_t=x'+tv,
    0<=t<=1.


Then


    partial^alpha f(x)-partial^alpha f(x')
      = integral_0^1 v dot grad(partial^alpha f)(x_t) dt.


By Cauchy–Schwarz in t,


    E[(partial^alpha f(x)-partial^alpha f(x'))^2]
      <= |v|^2
         sup_z E[|grad(partial^alpha f)(z)|^2].


Stationarity makes the final supremum constant. Define


    L_6^2
      = max_{|alpha|=5}
          sum_{j=1}^2
            Var(partial^{alpha+e_j} f(0))
      < infinity.


Therefore


    d_{r,alpha}(x,x')
      <= L_6 |x-x'|.


This is uniform in r and alpha.


## 7. Uniform entropy and expected supremum


Because d_{r,alpha} is bounded by L_6 times Euclidean distance, a Euclidean epsilon/L_6-net is an epsilon-net for the canonical metric. Since K_r is contained in a fixed bounded planar set,


    N(K_r,d_{r,alpha},epsilon)
      <= C_D (1+A_D/epsilon)^2


for constants C_D,A_D independent of r and alpha.


The Dudley entropy integral is consequently uniformly finite:


    integral_0^{diam_d(K_r)}
      sqrt(log N(K_r,d_{r,alpha},epsilon)) d epsilon
      <= J_D < infinity.


The standard Dudley bound therefore gives a constant B_+ such that


    E sup_{x in K_r} Z_{r,alpha}(x)
      <= B_+


uniformly in r and alpha.


Define the centered signed process Y_{r,alpha}(s,x)=s Z_{r,alpha}(x) on {-1,+1} x K_r. Its supremum is exactly sup_x |Z_{r,alpha}(x)|. Within either sign copy the canonical metric is d_{r,alpha}; the union of two epsilon-nets covers the signed set, while its full metric diameter is at most 2 sigma_5. The signed Dudley entropy integral is uniformly finite. Therefore


    E sup_{x in K_r}|Z_{r,alpha}(x)| <= B_5,


for a finite uniform signed-index constant B_5. Equivalently, there is a uniform constant


    B_5=B_signed<infinity


such that


    E sup_{x in K_r}|Z_{r,alpha}(x)|
      <= B_5.


This signed-index construction is the governing argument. The former two-one-sided-suprema inequality is retracted.


## 8. Borell–TIS concentration


For fixed r and alpha, the signed process


    Y_{r,alpha}(sign,x)=sign Z_{r,alpha}(x)


is centered, separable, and has maximal pointwise variance at most sigma_5^2. Its supremum is


    sup_{x in K_r}|Z_{r,alpha}(x)|.


The standard Borell–TIS inequality yields, for every u>=0,


    P(
      sup_{x in K_r}|Z_{r,alpha}(x)|
        > E sup_{x in K_r}|Z_{r,alpha}(x)| + u
      )
      <= exp(-u^2/(2 sigma_5^2)).


Since the expectation is at most B_5,


    P(
      sup_{x in K_r}|Z_{r,alpha}(x)|
        > B_5+u
      )
      <= exp(-u^2/(2 sigma_5^2)).


There are exactly six multi-indices of total order five in two dimensions. A union bound gives


    P(
      max_{|alpha|=5}
      sup_{x in K_r}|Z_{r,alpha}(x)|
        > B_5+u
      )
      <= 6 exp(-u^2/(2 sigma_5^2)).


Combining this with the deterministic mean bound proves


    P(
      H_{5,r}(D)>M_5+B_5+u
      | exact six pins
      )
      <= 6 exp(-u^2/(2 sigma_5^2)).


This estimate is uniform for 0<r<=r_0.


## 9. GT5 theorem


### Theorem 9.1 — uniform conditioned high-jet tail


For every fixed compact normalized chart D, there exist C,c,t_0,r_0>0 such that


    P(
      H_{5,r}(D)>t
      | exact six pins
      )
      <= C exp(-c t^2)


for all 0<r<=r_0 and t>=t_0.


### Proof


Take


    t_0=2(M_5+B_5).


For t>=t_0, set


    u=t-(M_5+B_5)>=t/2.


The estimate from Section 8 gives


    P(H_{5,r}>t | pins)
      <= 6 exp(-t^2/(8 sigma_5^2)).


Thus one may take


    C=6,
    c=1/(8 sigma_5^2).


QED.


This is precisely the GT5 statement named in GP-DER-045.

## 11. Determinant-weighted Palm corollary


Let gamma_r denote the exact six-pin conditioned Gaussian law and let W_r be the nonnegative typed determinant product used to define the exact pair-Palm law:


    P_r^MS(E)

Use the finite-dimensional determinant-weight estimates already isolated in GP-DER-043 and the exact-torus regularity chain:


    Z_r >= c_Z r^2,


    E_{gamma_r}[W_r^2] <= C_W r^4


for all sufficiently small r.


Then by Cauchy–Schwarz,


    P_r^MS(H_{5,r}>u)
      <= [E W_r^2]^{1/2}
          [P_{gamma_r}(H_{5,r}>u)]^{1/2}
          /Z_r


      <= C_0 [P_{gamma_r}(H_{5,r}>u)]^{1/2}.


Applying Theorem 9.1 gives


    P_r^MS(H_{5,r}>t)
      <= C_1 exp(-c_1 t^2)


for t>=t_0. In particular,


    P_r^MS(H_{5,r}>r^{-gamma})
      <= C_1 exp(-c_1 r^{-2gamma})
      = o(r^N)


for every N>0.
