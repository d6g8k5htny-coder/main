# Determinant-weighted boundary-layer Palm reduction


## Executive result


The probabilistic power count behind the P0.2 boundary-layer route can be isolated and proved cleanly.


For the finite-r six-pin maximum–saddle model, suppose a bad typed configuration can occur only when the rescaled transverse curvature


    s=q/r


lies above a jet-dependent lower threshold


    s>-C_r(U),


where U denotes the remaining normalized jets and C_r has at most polynomial growth. The maximum type condition automatically gives the opposite bound


    s < w/2-r c22/8


in the normalized Q4 coordinates. Thus every bad typed configuration lies in a finite random interval of s-width O(poly(U)).


Inside that interval, both endpoint determinants are order r^2, so their product is order r^4. The change of variables q=rs contributes one further power r. The pair-Palm normalizer is order r^2. Consequently


    P_r^MS(bad) = O(r^5)/O(r^2)=O(r^3).


This file proves that implication. It also proves the r^2 normalizer scaling for the normalized finite-Q4 Gaussian model by dominated convergence.


The theorem does not prove that nonadjacency satisfies the required deterministic boundary-layer inclusion. GP-DER-037 provides an exact invariant-strip threshold and central-transit criterion for the truncated flow, but endpoint entry, endpoint capture, higher-order remainder control, and global adjacency remain open.


## 1. Exact finite-r local object


Fix b=6/5 and 0<r<=r0. Under the exact six-pin conditioned Gaussian law gamma_r, let


    J_r=(q,a,w,z,c40,c31,c22,c13,c04)


be the midpoint jet vector, with


    q=f_yy,
    a=f_xxy,
    w=f_xyy,
    z=f_yyy,
    c40=f_xxxx,
    c31=f_xxxy,
    c22=f_xxyy,
    c13=f_xyyy,
    c04=f_yyyy.


The exact normalized-periodized finite-r mean and covariance are constructed in GP-DER-034 / GP-DATA-035. Write


    U=(a,w,z,c40,c31,c22,c13,c04).


The normalized quartic endpoint Hessians are


    H_M =
      [[-r+r^2 c40/12, -ar/2+r^2 c31/12],
       [-ar/2+r^2 c31/12, q-rw/2+r^2 c22/8]],


    H_S =
      [[ r+r^2 c40/12,  ar/2+r^2 c31/12],
       [ ar/2+r^2 c31/12, q+rw/2+r^2 c22/8]].


Define the typed determinant weight


    W_r=|det H_M det H_S|
        1{H_M negative definite}
        1{det H_S<0},


and the normalizer


    Z_r=E_gamma_r[W_r].


The corresponding typed pair-Palm law is


    dP_r^MS = W_r dgamma_r / Z_r.


No adjacency conditioning is present in this definition.


## 2. Gaussian-regularity premise


The reduction uses one named premise.


GAUSSIAN REGULARITY (GR):


1. gamma_r=N(m_r,Sigma_r) in R^9;
2. m_r and Sigma_r extend continuously to r=0;
3. Sigma_0 is positive definite;
4. the limiting q-coordinate has law N(-b,2).


Equivalently, for sufficiently small r there are constants


    0<lambda_-<=lambda_+<infinity,
    M<infinity,


such that


    lambda_- I <= Sigma_r <= lambda_+ I,
    ||m_r||<=M.


The exact computations in GP-DER-034 verify these properties numerically over the declared rung ladder, and the coalesced divided-difference law gives the positive-definite limit. A fully symbolic exact-torus proof of the removable coalescence and uniform eigenvalue bounds is a separate verification obligation; the theorem below is exact once GR is granted.


A useful consequence of GR is the uniform density factorization bound


    phi_r(q,U)
      <= K exp(-c||U||^2),


where phi_r is the joint density and the q-dependence has simply been bounded above. One way to see this is to write the density as the marginal density of U times the conditional density of q given U. Uniform positive definiteness bounds both the marginal covariance and the conditional variance away from zero.


## 3. Normalizer lemma


### Lemma 3.1 — pair-Palm normalizer


Under GR,


    Z_r = r^2 z_r,


where


    z_r -> z_0


and


    z_0=E[Q^2 1{Q<0}]>0,
    Q~N(-b,2).


Consequently there are r1>0 and c_Z,C_Z>0 such that


    c_Z r^2 <= Z_r <= C_Z r^2


for 0<r<=r1.


### Proof


Expand the endpoint determinants without introducing s=q/r:


    det H_M
      =(-r+r^2 c40/12)(q-rw/2+r^2 c22/8)
       -(-ar/2+r^2 c31/12)^2,


    det H_S
      =( r+r^2 c40/12)(q+rw/2+r^2 c22/8)
       -( ar/2+r^2 c31/12)^2.


For every fixed jet vector,


    det H_M/r -> -q,
    det H_S/r ->  q.


Away from q=0, the type indicators converge pointwise to 1{q<0}: if q<0, H_M is negative definite and H_S has negative determinant for all sufficiently small r; if q>0, the limiting type conditions fail. The boundary q=0 has Gaussian probability zero.


Therefore


    W_r/r^2 -> q^2 1{q<0}


pointwise almost surely.


The ratio W_r/r^2 is bounded by a fixed polynomial in the nine jets for r<=1. GR supplies uniform Gaussian moments of every polynomial degree, so the family is uniformly integrable. Since gamma_r converges to gamma_0, dominated convergence gives


    Z_r/r^2 -> E_gamma_0[q^2 1{q<0}]=z_0.


For Q~N(-b,2), letting t=b/sqrt(2),


    z_0=(b^2+2)Phi(t)+sqrt(2)b phi(t).


At b=6/5,


    z_0=3.230978535287005...>0.


The two-sided r^2 bounds follow. QED.


## 4. Abstract boundary-layer lemma


We now separate the deterministic and probabilistic parts.


Let B_r be any event measurable with respect to J_r. Think of B_r as an obstruction, failure of a sufficient capture event, or—after a future deterministic theorem—nonadjacency itself.


Assume there is a nonnegative measurable threshold C_r(U) satisfying:


BL-1. On the typed event,


    B_r implies q/r > -C_r(U).


BL-2. Uniform polynomial growth: for some integer d and constant K_C,


    C_r(U) <= K_C(1+||U||^d)


for all 0<r<=r1.


### Theorem 4.1 — determinant-weighted boundary-layer reduction


Under GR, BL-1, and BL-2,


    P_r^MS(B_r) <= K_B r^3


for 0<r<=r2, with K_B finite and independent of r.


No numerical value of K_B is claimed.


### Proof


Set


    s=q/r.


On the maximum type event H_M negative definite, every diagonal entry of H_M is negative. In particular,


    q-rw/2+r^2 c22/8 < 0,


so


    s < U_r(U):=w/2-r c22/8.


Combining this automatic type upper bound with BL-1 shows that every typed configuration in B_r satisfies


    -C_r(U) < s < U_r(U).


If the upper endpoint is below the lower endpoint, the interval is empty.


After substituting q=rs, factor r from every Hessian entry:


    H_M=r Htilde_M,
    H_S=r Htilde_S,


where


    Htilde_M =
      [[-1+r c40/12, -a/2+r c31/12],
       [-a/2+r c31/12, s-w/2+r c22/8]],


    Htilde_S =
      [[ 1+r c40/12,  a/2+r c31/12],
       [ a/2+r c31/12, s+w/2+r c22/8]].


Define


    D_M=det Htilde_M,
    D_S=det Htilde_S.


Then, exactly,


    W_r=r^4 |D_M D_S| 1{type}.


The unnormalized bad mass is therefore


    N_r:=E_gamma_r[W_r 1{B_r}]


       =r^5 ∫_{R^8} ∫_{-C_r(U)}^{U_r(U)}
             |D_M(s,U;r)D_S(s,U;r)|
             1{type and B_r}
             phi_r(rs,U)
           ds dU,


with the convention that an empty interval contributes zero.


For 0<r<=min(r1,1),


    |D_M|+|D_S|
      <= K[ (1+|c40|)(|s|+|w|+|c22|)
             +( |a|+|c31| )^2 + 1 ].


Thus |D_M D_S| is bounded by a fixed polynomial in |s| and ||U||.


BL-2 and the definition of U_r imply that both interval endpoints have polynomial growth in U. Consequently there exist an integer m and a constant K_P such that


    ∫_{-C_r(U)}^{U_r(U)}
      |D_M D_S| ds
      <= K_P(1+||U||^m)


uniformly in r.


Using the GR density bound,


    N_r
      <= K r^5 ∫_{R^8}
           exp(-c||U||^2)(1+||U||^m)dU
      <= K' r^5.


Lemma 3.1 gives Z_r>=c_Z r^2. Hence


    P_r^MS(B_r)
      =N_r/Z_r
      <= (K'/c_Z) r^3.


QED.


## 5. Corollary for the explicit invariant strip


GP-DER-037 defines, for a fixed contraction target mu>0,


    F_r(U)=|a|/8+r|c31|/48,


    C_mu,r(U)
      = |w|/2 + mu + r|c22|/8
        +( |z|+r|c13|/2 )F_r(U)/mu
        +r|c04|F_r(U)^2/(2mu^2).


For the explicit cubic–quartic truncation, the condition


    q/r <= -C_mu,r(U)


makes the strip


    |X|<=1/2,
    |Y|<=F_r(U)/mu


forward invariant while the trajectory remains in the X chart.


Take mu=1. Then C_1,r is a polynomial-growth function of U. Let


    B_strip,r
      ={typed and q/r > -C_1,r(U)}.


This is exactly the typed complement of the sufficient strip condition. Theorem 4.1 gives:


### Corollary 5.1


Under GR,


    P_r^MS(B_strip,r)=O(r^3).


Thus the determinant-weighted Gaussian cost of failing the explicit invariant-strip certificate is cubic, analytically and without fitting a slope.


This corollary does not state that every field outside B_strip,r is adjacent. It states only that the portion not certified by the exact strip lemma has cubic pair-Palm mass.

## 7. Transfer to the exact field: precise remaining bridge


The full Gaussian field contains fifth-and-higher Taylor terms and global geometry. A theorem for the exact field requires a transfer statement rather than silently identifying the truncation with the field.


One sufficient structure is the following.


Let R_r denote a finite collection of scaled remainder variables or a chart norm. Suppose there are events G_rem,r and G_global,r such that:


1. on G_rem,r, the exact local flow is trapped and captured whenever


       q/r <= -C_cap,r(U,R_r),


   with C_cap,r having uniformly finite polynomial moments;


2. the determinant-weighted pair-Palm probability of G_rem,r^c is O(r^3), or smaller;


3. on G_global,r, local capture at M implies the exact gradient-adjacency event A_r;


4. P_r^MS(G_global,r^c)=O(r^3), or smaller.


Then


    1-a_r=P_r^MS(A_r^c)=O(r^3).


This identifies the exact missing interfaces:


- deterministic endpoint/remainder capture;
- and local-to-global adjacency localization.


The Gaussian boundary-layer integration is no longer an unspecified step.


## 8. Why the maximum type mark is essential


The proof uses the inequality


    H_M,22<0,


which follows from H_M negative definite. Without the maximum type mark, BL-1 gives only a lower bound on s and the s-integration can extend over a nonshrinking q-range. The extra power r would then be unavailable.


This is a concrete example of why an unmarked Gaussian estimate cannot discharge a typed Palm obligation. The cubic power is produced jointly by:


1. the deterministic lower boundary q/r>-C(U);
2. the maximum type upper boundary q/r<w/2+O(r);
3. two endpoint determinant zeros;
4. the r^2 Palm normalizer.


Dropping any one of these changes the power count.


## 9. Exact exponent ledger


On the typed boundary layer q=rs with s in a finite jet-dependent interval:


    dq = r ds                         contributes +1,
    |det H_M det H_S| = r^4|D_MD_S| contributes +4,
    Z_r^{-1} = O(r^{-2})              contributes -2.


Total:


    +1+4-2=+3.


This is the determinant-weighted counterpart of the heuristic “one density power plus two determinant powers.” The exact determinant product supplies four raw powers because each determinant becomes order r^2 on q=O(r); division by the ordinary pair normalizer returns two of them.


## 10. Scope and falsification


Proved modulo GR:


- Z_r=r^2 z_r with z_r bounded above and below;
- any typed finite-jet bad event confined to a polynomial-width q/r boundary interval has pair-Palm probability O(r^3);
- the complement of the explicit mu=1 invariant-strip certificate has O(r^3) pair-Palm mass in the normalized finite-Q4 model.


Not proved:


- the selected S branch enters the strip;
- endpoint-layer transit;
- M capture;
- robustness to the exact Taylor remainder;
- local capture implies global adjacency;
- a numerical cubic coefficient;
- or the full P0.2 theorem.


This derivation is falsified if:


1. the maximum type event does not imply H_M,22<0 under the governing object card;
2. the endpoint Hessian scaling or determinant factorization is wrong;
3. the exact finite-r jet family lacks a uniformly nondegenerate divided-difference Gaussian limit;
4. C_r grows faster than allowed and lacks the Gaussian moments used in the proof;
5. or the normalizer fails to scale as r^2.
