## 3. Exact nonuniqueness calculation


Let P4 and P4_tilde be degree-at-most-four polynomials with the same five fourth derivatives at the origin and the same endpoint value/gradient constraints. Their difference


    R = P4_tilde - P4


has degree at most three and must satisfy the six linear conditions


    R(±1/2,0)   = 0,
    R_X(±1/2,0) = 0,
    R_Y(±1/2,0) = 0.


A general degree-at-most-three polynomial has ten coefficients. The six endpoint constraints have rank six, leaving a four-dimensional nullspace. A convenient exact basis is


    R1 = Y^2,
    R2 = Y^3,
    R3 = X Y^2,
    R4 = (X^2-1/4)Y.


Therefore every polynomial


    P4_tilde = P4
             + lambda1 Y^2
             + lambda2 Y^3
             + lambda3 X Y^2
             + lambda4 (X^2-1/4)Y


has the same prescribed fourth derivatives and preserves the endpoint values and gradients.


This directly falsifies the unqualified sentence “The unique quartic polynomial...” in GP-DER-019 and the corresponding uniqueness language in CM-025.


A uniqueness statement could be restored only by adding an explicit normalization or gauge condition that fixes all four lambda coefficients. No such condition appears in the stated interpolation problem or in CW-PRP-006.


## 4. Why the nullspace is dynamically load-bearing


The corrected scaled flow uses


    dX/dtau = P3_X + r P4_X,
    dY/dtau = (q/r)Y + P3_Y + r P4_Y.


Adding the four null modes changes the scaled vector field by


1. R1 = lambda1 Y^2:


       Delta(dX/dtau) = 0,
       Delta(dY/dtau) = 2 lambda1 r Y.


2. R2 = lambda2 Y^3:


       Delta(dX/dtau) = 0,
       Delta(dY/dtau) = 3 lambda2 r Y^2.


3. R3 = lambda3 X Y^2:


       Delta(dX/dtau) = lambda3 r Y^2,
       Delta(dY/dtau) = 2 lambda3 r X Y.


4. R4 = lambda4 (X^2-1/4)Y:


       Delta(dX/dtau) = 2 lambda4 r X Y,
       Delta(dY/dtau) = lambda4 r (X^2-1/4).


These are the same relative O(r) order as the retained quartic correction. They can alter transverse stability, branch bending, chart escape, endpoint Hessians, determinant weights, and stall/escape classifications at precisely the order the Q4-on experiment is intended to test.


For example, lambda1 Y^2 changes P4_YY at both endpoints by 2 lambda1, and lambda4 (X^2-1/4)Y changes the mixed structure and creates a transverse forcing away from the endpoints. These are not automatically dynamically irrelevant coordinate terms.


Consequently, the equality of the pure-cubic and selected-Q4 normalized complements does not establish quartic stability of the actual finite-r conditioned law unless one proves either:


- the true conditional law fixes lambda1=lambda2=lambda3=lambda4=0;
- the relevant adjacency observable is invariant under all admissible null modes; or
- the omitted lambda modes are of smaller order than r after the exact finite-r conditioning is derived.


None of those statements is currently proved.


## 5. Where the missing coefficients should come from


The fourth derivatives alone do not determine the full order-r correction to the rescaled flow. The actual finite-r six-pin conditioned field can also produce order-r corrections through:


- finite-r changes in the conditional means and covariances of the cubic free jets;
- divided-difference relations induced by the two separated pinned points;
- lower-degree pin-shift compensation terms;
- periodization and normalization corrections;
- correlations between the nine displayed jets and the omitted lower-order correction coefficients;

A true finite-r conditioned Q4 model should therefore be derived from the exact six-pin conditional Gaussian field or from an exact Hermite/divided-difference expansion, not by selecting a representative solely from the limiting fourth-jet data.


The frozen CW-PRP-006 specification does not resolve this. It asks for a pin-preserving quartic correction and finite-r re-evaluation, but it does not impose a four-condition gauge that uniquely selects the displayed representative.
