# Quartic null modes as lower-jet reparameterizations


## Question


GP-AUD-020 correctly observes that the endpoint value/gradient constraints and the five fourth derivatives do not, by themselves, uniquely select the lower-degree compensation terms in a degree-at-most-four polynomial.


This note asks the sharper question relevant to the finite-r Taylor model:


> Are the four null modes genuine independent dynamical coefficients once q,a,w,z are defined as the exact finite-r midpoint derivatives, or do they merely reparameterize those lower-order jets?


The answer is that they are lower-jet reparameterizations. The displayed zero-null-mode quartic representative is the natural and unique representative **within the exact midpoint-jet coordinate convention**. The remaining unresolved issue is the exact finite-r conditional law of those midpoint jets and the Taylor remainder, not an arbitrary four-dimensional physical gauge.


This does not restore GP-DATA-020 as an exact finite-r execution, because it sampled the limiting nine-jet law rather than the exact finite-r conditional law.


## 1. Exact midpoint-jet convention


Let


    c_ij(r)=partial_x^i partial_y^j f(0,0)


under the exact six-pin conditional law at separation r.


Define the lower jets by their actual finite-r derivatives:


    q_r = c_02(r),
    a_r = c_21(r),
    w_r = c_12(r),
    z_r = c_03(r).


Define the fourth jets


    c40=c_40(r), c31=c_31(r), c22=c_22(r), c13=c_13(r), c04=c_04(r).


In scaled coordinates x=rX, y=rY, the Taylor expansion is organized so that q_r,a_r,w_r,z_r are exactly the derivatives of the truncated polynomial at the origin, not merely limiting labels.


That coordinate convention supplies the four normalization conditions omitted from the interpolation-only statement:


    partial_YY P4(0,0)=0,
    partial_YYY P4(0,0)=0,
    partial_XYY P4(0,0)=0,
    partial_XXY P4(0,0)=0,


because those derivative coordinates are already carried by q_r,z_r,w_r,a_r in the lower-order part.


With these four conditions, the GP-AUD-020 nullspace is removed.


## 2. Exact axial Hermite factorization


Let


    g_r(x)=f(x,0).


The pair value and x-gradient pins imply


    g_r(x)=p_r(x)+(x^2-r^2/4)^2 h_r(x),


where


    p_r(x)=b+x^3/3-r^2 x/4-r^3/12.


Expanding h_r about zero gives, through physical order r^4,


    g_r(rX)
      = b + r^3[X^3/3-X/4-1/12]
        + r^4 (c40/24)(X^2-1/4)^2
        + O(r^5),


when c40 is the exact finite-r fourth derivative up to the stated order.


The lower-degree X^2, X, and constant compensation terms are not free gauges. They are forced by the endpoint pins once c40 and the exact lower axial derivatives are used.


In particular,


    c20(r) = -r^2 c40/24 + O(r^4),


    c10(r) = -r^2/4 + O(r^4),


    c00(r) = b-r^3/12+r^4 c40/384+O(r^5).


These are precisely the coefficients encoded by


    (c40/24)(X^2-1/4)^2.


## 3. Exact transverse-gradient factorization


Let


    v_r(x)=f_y(x,0).


The transverse gradient pins imply


    v_r(x)=(x^2-r^2/4)k_r(x).


Expanding k_r about zero and expressing its coefficients through the exact midpoint derivatives gives


    c01(r) = -r^2 a_r/8 + O(r^4),


    c11(r) = -r^2 c31/24 + O(r^4),


    c21(r) = a_r,


    c31(r) = c31.


Therefore the transverse terms through physical order r^4 are


    r^3 (a_r/2)(X^2-1/4)Y


and


    r^4 (c31/6)X(X^2-1/4)Y.


Again the lower-degree compensation is forced by the pins and by the exact definitions of a_r and c31.


## 4. The normalized quartic representative


Under the exact midpoint-jet convention, the order-r^4 polynomial is


    P4^(0)(X,Y)
      = (c40/24)(X^2-1/4)^2
        +(c31/6)X(X^2-1/4)Y
        +(c22/4)X^2Y^2
        +(c13/6)XY^3
        +(c04/24)Y^4.


It preserves the endpoint values and gradients and has the prescribed fourth derivatives.


It also satisfies the normalization conditions


    P4_YY(0,0)=0,
    P4_YYY(0,0)=0,
    P4_XYY(0,0)=0,
    P4_XXY(0,0)=0.


These conditions state that the exact lower midpoint derivatives q_r,z_r,w_r,a_r are not duplicated in P4.


Within this normalized coordinate class, P4^(0) is unique.


## 5. Interpretation of the four null modes


GP-AUD-020's null modes act as follows.


### lambda1 Y^2


Adding


    lambda1 Y^2


to P4 changes the physical midpoint transverse curvature by


    q_r -> q_r + 2 lambda1 r^2.


Thus lambda1 is a redefinition of the finite-r q coordinate.


### lambda2 Y^3


Adding


    lambda2 Y^3


changes


    z_r -> z_r + 6 lambda2 r.


Thus lambda2 is a redefinition of the finite-r z coordinate.


### lambda3 X Y^2


Adding


    lambda3 X Y^2


changes


    w_r -> w_r + 2 lambda3 r.


Thus lambda3 is a redefinition of the finite-r w coordinate.


### lambda4 (X^2-1/4)Y


Adding


    lambda4 (X^2-1/4)Y


changes


    a_r -> a_r + 2 lambda4 r,


while automatically changing the lower transverse-gradient compensation required by the endpoint pins.


Thus lambda4 is a redefinition of the finite-r a coordinate.


The four modes are genuine directions in the underdetermined interpolation problem, but they are not independent once q_r,a_r,w_r,z_r are declared to be the exact finite-r midpoint derivatives.
