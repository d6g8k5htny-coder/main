# Pre-execution review of the quartic gauge-sensitivity protocol


## Sources


- GP-PRE-025-v1.0 — Common-random-number quartic gauge-sensitivity test.
- GP-ERR-024-v1.0 — Quartic compensation uniqueness withdrawal and Q4 interpretation correction.
- GP-AUD-020-v1.0 — Independent audit proving the four-dimensional nullspace.
- GP-DATA-020-v1.0 and GP-AUD-017-v1.0 — baseline quartic and pure-cubic local diagnostics.


## Executive disposition


GP-ERR-024 fully and correctly accepts the uniqueness correction. GP-PRE-025 correctly freezes the four null modes, their flow contributions, their endpoint-Hessian contributions, fixed seeds, common random numbers, and a bounded diagnostic gauge family. The protocol is suitable as a diagnostic after four pre-execution amendments.


Without these amendments, a G1 or G2 label would conflate flow sensitivity with gauge-induced changes in the typed Palm sample, use unstable rare-event ratios without uncertainty, and treat a 1,000-sample outside-screen check as stronger evidence than it is.


The amendments below do not change the gauge grid, seeds, base law, or numerical integrator. They make the interpretation identifiable and fail-closed.


## 1. Exact checks passed


The protocol's null-mode flow corrections are correct:


    Delta Xdot = r[l3 Y^2 + 2 l4 X Y],
    Delta Ydot = r[2 l1 Y + 3 l2 Y^2 + 2 l3 X Y + l4(X^2-1/4)].


The endpoint-Hessian corrections are also correct:


At M, X=-1/2:


    Delta H_yy = r^2(2 l1-l3),
    Delta H_xy = -r^2 l4.


At S, X=+1/2:


    Delta H_yy = r^2(2 l1+l3),
    Delta H_xy = +r^2 l4.


R2=Y^3 contributes no endpoint Hessian correction. The total of 31 gauges is correct: one baseline, 24 one-mode gauges, and six joint gauges.


## 2. Required amendment A — separate typing change from flow change


The protocol uses gauge-dependent endpoint Hessians and therefore a gauge-dependent typed event and determinant weight. This is appropriate for the final gauge-specific estimand, but a change in weighted complement may arise from two distinct mechanisms:


1. TYPE-SELECTION EFFECT: draws enter or leave the maximum-saddle typed Palm sample, or their determinant weights change, because the gauge changes endpoint Hessians.
2. FLOW-OUTCOME EFFECT: among draws that remain typed under both baseline and gauge, the corrected trajectory changes from capture to escape/stall or vice versa.


Common random numbers make this decomposition available and it should be mandatory.


For every gauge/rung report two estimands:


A. Gauge-specific total estimand:


    C_g = sum_i w_i(g) 1{typed_i(g)} 1{failure_i(g)}
          / sum_i w_i(g) 1{typed_i(g)}.


B. Common-typed paired-flow estimand on


    T_common(g) = {i: typed_i(0)=typed_i(g)=1}:


    C_common,g = sum_{i in T_common} w_i(0) 1{failure_i(g)}
                 / sum_{i in T_common} w_i(0),


with the paired baseline value computed on the identical cohort and identical baseline weights.


Also report weighted mass entering typing, leaving typing, and remaining common, plus the determinant-weight change on the common cohort. A gauge is dynamically decisive only if its G2 signal survives in the common-typed paired-flow estimand; otherwise label it TYPE-SELECTION DOMINATED.


## 3. Required amendment B — paired uncertainty and rare-event effective sample size


Raw escape counts and total determinant ESS are insufficient for ratios of rare weighted complements. A few high-weight failures can dominate.


For every gauge/rung report:


- failure-weight ESS:


    ESS_fail = (sum failure weights)^2 / sum(failure weights^2);


- a 95% paired uncertainty interval for C_g-C_0 and, when both are positive, log(C_g/C_0);
- the same intervals for the common-typed paired-flow estimand.


Use a paired nonparametric bootstrap over the original Gaussian draw index, preserving the same resampled indices across all gauges at a rung. Freeze 2,000 bootstrap replicates with seed


    2026072030 + rung_index.


A factor-four or factor-ten reading is admissible only when the corresponding paired interval lies wholly beyond the threshold in the claimed direction. Otherwise label the comparison UNCERTAIN even when the point estimate crosses the threshold.


Replace the current “at least 20 escapes” sufficiency rule with both:


    raw failures >= 20
    and ESS_fail >= 10


for the gauge and its paired baseline. If either side fails, ratio-based G1/G2 adjudication is inconclusive for that comparison.


## 4. Required amendment C — outside-screen check is a falsifier, not validation


With zero failures in 1,000 independent outside-screen trials, the approximate one-sided 95% upper bound on an unweighted failure probability is about 3/1000. That cannot validate absence at 10^-4 or smaller scales.


Therefore one of the following must be frozen before execution:


Preferred C1:


- integrate all typed draws outside the screen for the baseline and every one-mode gauge at r=0.025;
- for other gauges/rungs, use a sequential common-random outside-screen sample until either one failure occurs or 30,000 samples have been integrated.


Cheaper C2:


- retain 1,000 outside-screen trials only as a kill test;
- wording must be “screen not falsified in the tested sample,” never “outside-screen failure excluded”;
- G1 may not use zero outside-screen failures as affirmative evidence of screen validity.


Any outside-screen failure remains an immediate screen falsifier for that gauge/rung and must be reported with its weight and m22_S/r value.


## 5. Required amendment D — quantify “failure layer away from O(r)”


The phrase “changes the failure layer away from |m22_S|=O(r)” is not machine-adjudicable. Freeze a numerical criterion.


Recommended definition:


For each gauge/rung with adequate failure ESS, calculate the determinant-weighted 50%, 90%, and 99% quantiles of


    Z = |m22_S|/r


among failures. Define LAYER-SHIFT if either:


- the 90% quantile differs from baseline by a factor at least 4 at two consecutive rungs; or
- more than 10% of weighted failure mass lies at Z>8 at two consecutive rungs.


Report signed m22_S/r quantiles as well, because the mechanism may be one-sided. These constants are diagnostic conventions, not theorem constants.


## 6. Corrected frozen readings


After amendments A-D, use:


G1 — BOUNDED-GAUGE ROBUSTNESS:
No comparison triggers G2/G3; all adjudicable paired intervals remain inside the factor-four band; no outside-screen failure is observed in the frozen falsification sample; and no quantified layer shift occurs. Wording: robust over the tested bounded gauge family only.


G2 — MATERIAL GAUGE DEPENDENCE:
At least one gauge has an adjudicable paired interval wholly beyond factor ten at two consecutive rungs, weighted complement above 10^-4 at r=0.025 with uncertainty excluding 10^-4, or a quantified layer shift.


G3 — DYNAMICALLY DECISIVE NULL MODE:
A one-mode gauge triggers G2 in the common-typed paired-flow estimand. If G2 occurs only in the gauge-specific total estimand, label TYPE-SELECTION DECISIVE instead.


G4 — INCONCLUSIVE:
Any required ratio lacks raw failures or failure ESS; more than 1% stalls; any outside-screen failure occurs; more than 0.5% dt/2 non-stall disagreement occurs; or paired uncertainty spans both the G1 and G2 regions.


G5 — SCOPE LIMIT:
Unchanged. No bounded-gauge result establishes the exact finite-r conditional law.


## 7. Coefficient-scale limitation


The coefficient set {-4,-2,-1,1,2,4} is explicitly arbitrary as a stress-test scale. The protocol correctly asks for a non-arbitrary law from the exact finite-r divided-difference expansion. Every result must state that the gauge grid is dimensional only in the chosen scaled polynomial basis and is not a probability law for the actual coefficients.


A later exact-law execution should replace the bounded grid with the derived joint conditional law of lambda1,...,lambda4 and its covariance with the nine displayed jets.
