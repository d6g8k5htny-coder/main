1. Exact object and scope


This artifact derives a sufficient exact-field transfer budget for the single strict degree-four fixture PRCP-INTERIOR-001. It does not assert that an exact conditioned field satisfies the budget.


Let F^(4) be the degree-four pin-preserving vector field of GP-DER-092 and F the exact scaled field on


    D = {|X| <= 0.75, |Y| <= 1}.


Let R=F-F^(4). Required endpoint identity:


    R(S)=R(M)=0.


Define


    delta = ||R||_{C^1(D)},


using the Euclidean norm for vectors and operator norm for Jacobians. GP-DER-045 proves the general estimate


    delta <= C_D r^2 H_{5,r}(D),


and CW-AUD-018 independently rederived that transfer mechanism. The application to the new witness fixture and the numerical budget below are GP-side and still require outside review.

3. Endpoint-type and unstable-eigendirection budgets


At S, the degree-four symmetric Jacobian has eigenvalues


    lambda_u^0=+sqrt(1+(a/2)^2),
    lambda_s^0=-sqrt(1+(a/2)^2),


with spectral gap g=lambda_u^0-lambda_s^0. In xi=1/2-X coordinates, write the M-ward unit unstable eigenvector as e_0=(nu_0,e_{y,0}).


Under ||DR||_op<=delta, GP-DER-045 gives the Davis–Kahan bound


    ||e-e_0|| <= theta = 2 delta/g.


The exact eigendirection remains inside |y|<kappa xi if


    |e_{y,0}|+theta < kappa(nu_0-theta).


Therefore a sufficient eigendirection budget is


    delta < (g/2) [kappa nu_0-|e_{y,0}|]/(1+kappa)
          = 0.0092821838983191239971017427180462345... .


This is the binding budget. Weyl endpoint-type preservation is much less restrictive.


4. Cone budgets


Because R(S)=0 and ||DR||_op<=delta, at a cone point with |y|<=kappa xi,

The upper/lower cone-face functionals have norm sqrt(1+kappa^2), so their normalized inward margins lose at most delta(1+kappa^2). The resulting cone-face budget is

The longitudinal cone drift loses at most delta sqrt(1+kappa^2), giving

6. Sink-capture budget
