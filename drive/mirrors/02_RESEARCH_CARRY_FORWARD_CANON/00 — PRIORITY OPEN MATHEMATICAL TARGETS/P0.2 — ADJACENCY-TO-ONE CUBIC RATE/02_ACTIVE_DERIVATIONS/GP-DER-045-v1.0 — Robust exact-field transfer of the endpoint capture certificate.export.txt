## 4. Scaled C^1 Taylor-remainder theorem


### Theorem 4.1 — degree-four exact-field remainder


Fix a compact normalized chart D contained in a ball of radius R_D. Let


    H_{5,r}(D)
      = max_{|alpha|=5} sup_{(x,y) in rD}
          |partial^alpha f(x,y)|.


There exists a deterministic constant C_D, depending only on D and the fixed pin-preserving interpolation operator, such that


    ||F_r-F_r^(4)||_{C^1(D)}
       <= C_D r^2 H_{5,r}(D).


Here the C^1 norm contains both the vector field and its Jacobian.


### Proof


Let T_4 f be the ordinary midpoint Taylor polynomial of physical degree four. For any multi-index beta with |beta|<=2, Taylor's theorem applied to partial^beta f gives


    |partial^beta f(rX,rY)
       -partial^beta T_4f(rX,rY)|
      <= C_{D,beta} r^{5-|beta|} H_{5,r}(D).


After multiplication by the scaling factor r^{|beta|-3},


    |partial_X^beta [r^{-3}(f(rX,rY)-T_4f(rX,rY))]|
      <= C_{D,beta} r^2 H_{5,r}(D).


Taking |beta|=1 controls the scaled vector field difference from the ordinary Taylor vector field; |beta|=2 controls its Jacobian.


The ordinary Taylor polynomial does not satisfy the exact endpoint value and gradient pins. At the fixed normalized endpoints, its six scaled value/gradient residuals relative to the exact field are also O(r^2 H_{5,r}(D)) by the same estimates.


The map that sends six endpoint value/gradient residuals at the fixed normalized points (+/-1/2,0) to the unique declared pin-correcting Hermite polynomial is a linear map between finite-dimensional normed spaces. Its operator norm from the six residual coordinates to C^2(D) is finite and depends only on D and the chosen normalized interpolation basis. Therefore the pin-correcting polynomial has C^2 norm at most


    C'_D r^2 H_{5,r}(D).


Adding the ordinary Taylor remainder and the bounded pin correction proves


    ||P_r-P_r^(4)||_{C^2(D)}
       <= C_D r^2 H_{5,r}(D),


which is equivalent to the stated C^1 vector-field bound. QED.
