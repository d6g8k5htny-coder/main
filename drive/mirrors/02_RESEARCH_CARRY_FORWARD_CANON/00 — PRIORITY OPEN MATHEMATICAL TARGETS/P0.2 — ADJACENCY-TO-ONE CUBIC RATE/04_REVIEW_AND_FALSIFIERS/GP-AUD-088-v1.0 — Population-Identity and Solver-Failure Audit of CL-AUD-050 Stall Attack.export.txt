2. FINDING F1 — POPULATION IDENTITY DOES NOT MATCH THE ORIGINAL STALL SET


The corrected CL-AUD-056 run sampled eight non-q jets U=(a,w,z,c40,c31,c22,c13,c04) and reported 154 stalls at r=0.025. The published CL-DATA-050 source explicitly states that its 21-dimensional jet stream differs from the prior eight-dimensional stream even under the same seed and that the follow-up is a fresh sample.


Consequently, the 83 CL-DATA-050 stalls are not the original 154 configurations. Same seed is not object identity when the random draw dimension, draw order, or sampling program changes.

- r, q, and all non-q jet values;
- branch orientation and initial point;
- source and receipt hashes;
- original outcome and every rerun outcome.


No claim about the disposition of the original 154 stalls may be inferred from the fresh 83.


3. FINDING F2 — SOLVER FAILURE AND NON-FINITE STATES COLLAPSE INTO STALL


Both published classifiers call solve_ivp and then classify the final state without first requiring solver success, a successful terminal status, a finite terminal vector, or a certified event.


The effective order is:
1. read sol.y[:,-1];
2. test capture radius;
3. test chart escape;
4. return STALL for every remaining case.


If a terminal coordinate is NaN, comparisons against capture and escape thresholds evaluate false and the case falls through to STALL. A failed integration with a finite but premature terminal point can also fall through to STALL.


Therefore STALL is currently a mixed residual class that may contain:
- finite horizon-censored trajectories;
- genuine finite slow trajectories;
- solver failures;
- non-finite states;
- premature termination;
- unrecorded event behavior.


This is a fail-closed defect. Solver failure and non-finiteness must be separate outcomes and must never be counted as geometric STALL or ESCAPE.
