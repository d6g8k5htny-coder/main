F1 — UNDISCHARGED LOAD-BEARING CONSTANT: planar_derivative_bound = 100
     Class: RECORD / COMPLETENESS. Severity: MATERIAL for review, not for truth.


     The source sets planar_derivative_bound = 100 and uses it twice, in


         error_2d = normalized_error_1d * (100 + normalized_error_1d)
                    + 100 * normalized_error_1d


     This is the step that converts the one-dimensional periodization image
     error into the two-dimensional transformed-entry error. It is the hinge of
     the entire r-uniform repair. The constant appears nowhere in GP-DER-214.
     A full read of the derivation companion confirms the string
     "planar_derivative_bound", the value 100, and any 1D-to-2D conversion
     constant are all absent. GP-AUD-228 does not address it. RS2-AUD-214 does
     not address it. It is discharged in no artifact in the set.


     CL SUPPLIES THE MISSING JUSTIFICATION, so that the amendment is cheap:
     the main (index zero) planar factor is |He_m(t)| exp(-t^2/2) with m <= 6
     and |t| <= 1. The absolute coefficient sum of He_6 is 76, so
     |He_m(t)| <= 76 for |t| <= 1, and exp(-t^2/2) <= 1. Hence the planar
     principal part is bounded by 76 < 100. The constant is TRUE with 24 units
     of slack.


     AMENDMENT REQUESTED: state the constant and this two-line proof in
     GP-DER-214 section 5, or derive it in the source with a fail-closed
     assertion. Do not leave a magic number carrying the repair.


F2 — UNDISCHARGED LOAD-BEARING CONSTANT: ||G0^{-1}|| <= 3
     Class: RECORD / COMPLETENESS. Severity: MATERIAL for review, not for truth.


     The source sets g0_inverse_bound = mp.mpf(3) with no derivation. Every
     downstream quantity depends on it: inverse_G_bound = 3/(1 - 3 delta_G),
     inverse_difference_bound, mean_difference_bound, covariance_difference_
     bound, and through them the mean and variance envelopes, the fourth
     moments, and finally C_Z. GP-DER-214 contains no operator-norm bound on any
     inverse of G0; its only "< 3" is the unrelated target-vector bound
     ||y_r|| < 3. GP-AUD-228 and RS2-AUD-214 do not address it either.


     CL SUPPLIES THE MISSING PROOF: G0 decomposes under permutation into the
     blocks [[1,-1],[-1,3]] on indices {0,2}, [[1,-3],[-3,15]] on {1,5}, and
     two unit blocks. The second block has trace 16 and determinant 6, so its
     eigenvalues are 8 +- sqrt(58) and lambda_min(G0) = 8 - sqrt(58) =
     0.3842268... . CL confirms by exact LDL that G0 - (1/3) I is positive
     definite and brackets lambda_min in (0.3841, 0.3843). Therefore
     ||G0^{-1}||_2 = 1/(8 - sqrt(58)) = 2.6026... < 3. The constant is TRUE.


     AMENDMENT REQUESTED: state lambda_min(G0) = 8 - sqrt(58) and the resulting
     norm bound in GP-DER-214 section 7, or compute it in the source.


     Observation offered without prejudice: 8 - sqrt(58) is the same value that

F4 — THE RADIUS LADDER IS ONE CONSTANT EVALUATED FIVE TIMES
     Class: PRESENTATION. Severity: MINOR but capable of misleading a reviewer.


     The source computes


         radius_ladder_pass = all(radius > 0 and
                                  transformed_entry_error < declared_allowance
                                  for radius in radius_ladder)


     The predicate does not depend on radius except through radius > 0. The
     five ladder points r_D, r_D/2, 1e-6, 1e-8, 1e-12 all evaluate the same
     rational constant. GP-DER-214 section 5 says "The same bound is explicitly
     checked on the radius ladder", and GP-AUD-228 section 8 says "The
     executable radius ladder ... passes at every point". A reviewer skimming
     either sentence would reasonably infer five independent evaluations.


     This is not an error. It is the CORRECT behaviour and it is exactly why the
     repair works: the bound is r-free, so there is nothing radius-dependent
     left to test. But the r-uniformity is established by the r-freeness of the
     Peano constants — CL's B4 — and NOT by the ladder. The ladder is
     decoration on a result that stands without it.
