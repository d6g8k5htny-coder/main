1. READ FIRST


One-sentence result:


If a nonnegative determinant weight has second moment at most C_W r^4 and its normalizer is at least c_Z r^2, then reweighting any event E from the conditioned law Q_r to the corresponding typed Palm law P_r costs at most a square root:


P_r(E)<=sqrt(C_W)/c_Z · Q_r(E)^(1/2).


Why it matters:


This closes the exact abstract passage from a conditioned-law high-jet or bad-event tail to the determinant-weighted Palm law. It explains why the r^4 weight second moment and r^2 normalizer powers cancel exactly. It is independent of how the conditioned-law event probability is proved.

2. FROZEN EXACT STATEMENT


Let (Omega,F,Q_r) be a probability space for each 0<r<=r_0. Let


W_r:Omega->[0,infinity)


be measurable with


0<Z_r:=E_{Q_r}[W_r]<infinity.


Define the W_r-reweighted probability law P_r by


P_r(E)=E_{Q_r}[W_r 1_E]/Z_r


for every measurable event E.


Assume constants c_Z>0 and C_W<infinity, independent of r, such that


Z_r>=c_Zr^2


and


E_{Q_r}[W_r^2]<=C_Wr^4


for every 0<r<=r_0.


THEOREM P02-LM-008.


For every measurable event E and every 0<r<=r_0,


P_r(E)
<=sqrt(C_W)/c_Z · Q_r(E)^(1/2).


Consequences:


A. Polynomial tail transfer.


If


Q_r(E_r)<=C_E r^beta


for some beta>=0 and all sufficiently small r, then


P_r(E_r)
<=sqrt(C_W C_E)/c_Z · r^(beta/2).


B. Sub-Gaussian small-r transfer.


If


Q_r(E_r)<=C_E exp(-c_E r^-2),


then


P_r(E_r)
<=sqrt(C_W C_E)/c_Z · exp[-(c_E/2)r^-2].


C. Super-polynomial transfer.


If Q_r(E_r)=o(r^m) for every m>0, then P_r(E_r)=o(r^p) for every p>0.


No independence assumption, Gaussian assumption, or relationship between E_r and the weight coordinates is required.


3. EXACT PROOF


By Cauchy–Schwarz under Q_r,


E_{Q_r}[W_r1_E]
<=E_{Q_r}[W_r^2]^(1/2)
  E_{Q_r}[1_E^2]^(1/2).


Since 1_E^2=1_E,


E_{Q_r}[1_E^2]^(1/2)=Q_r(E)^(1/2).


Using the second-moment premise,


E_{Q_r}[W_r1_E]
<=sqrt(C_W)r^2 Q_r(E)^(1/2).


Dividing by the lower normalizer bound gives


P_r(E)
=E_{Q_r}[W_r1_E]/Z_r
<=sqrt(C_W)r^2 Q_r(E)^(1/2)/(c_Zr^2)
=sqrt(C_W)/c_Z · Q_r(E)^(1/2).


This proves the theorem.


The three corollaries follow by direct substitution.


For the super-polynomial statement, fix p>0. Apply the theorem using any conditioned-law rate o(r^(2p)); the square root is o(r^p).

6. ADVERSARIAL MUTATIONS


M1 — Use an upper normalizer bound.


Mutation:


Z_r<=C_Zr^2.


Result:


Does not upper-bound the ratio. This mutation kills the theorem’s conclusion.


M2 — Replace the second moment by a first moment.


A bound E[W_r]<=Cr^2 alone does not give the same event-uniform square-root transfer through Cauchy–Schwarz. Additional structure would be needed.


M3 — Let the second moment scale as r^gamma.


The general ratio power becomes r^(gamma/2-2). The scale-free conclusion is special to gamma=4.


M4 — Compute the event probability under another law.


Cauchy–Schwarz requires Q_r(E), not an adjacent finite-Q4, unconditioned, or differently conditioned event probability.


M5 — Assume independence between W_r and E.


Unnecessary. The theorem is stronger because it does not use independence.


M6 — Use a signed weight.


The ratio may fail to define a probability law. Nonnegativity and positive finite normalizer are load-bearing.


M7 — Infer a conditioned-law tail from the theorem.


The implication is one-way: conditioned-law tail plus moment/normalizer premises gives Palm tail. The theorem does not prove Q_r(E).


M8 — Import the retracted GT5 step.


Irrelevant and prohibited. P02-LM-008 accepts any valid event-probability premise, regardless of its source.


7. EDGE CASES


Q_r(E)=0:


Then 1_E=0 Q_r-almost surely, the weighted numerator is zero, and P_r(E)=0.


Q_r(E)=1:


The theorem gives P_r(E)<=sqrt(C_W)/c_Z. This constant may exceed one; the true probability also satisfies the trivial bound one. The theorem remains correct but not necessarily sharp.


W_r=0 on E:


Then P_r(E)=0, consistent with the bound.


Type-boundary or singular-weight events:


Allowed. No continuity of the weight is required.


Different r-dependent events:


Allowed. The proof is pointwise in r.
