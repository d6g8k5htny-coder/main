P02-LM-008 — EXACT DETERMINANT-WEIGHTED PALM RARE-EVENT TRANSFER


1. SETTING


Fix r_0 in (0,1]. For every r in (0,r_0], let (Omega_r,F_r,Q_r) be a probability space. Let


    W_r:Omega_r->[0,infinity)


be measurable with


    0<Z_r:=E_{Q_r}[W_r]<infinity.


Define the weighted probability measure P_r by


    dP_r = (W_r/Z_r)dQ_r.


Assume there are constants


    C_W<infinity,
    c_Z>0,


independent of r, such that


    E_{Q_r}[W_r^2]<=C_W r^4,


    Z_r>=c_Z r^2


for every r in (0,r_0].


2. THEOREM


For every measurable event E_r in F_r,


    P_r(E_r)
      <=(sqrt(C_W)/c_Z)
        Q_r(E_r)^{1/2}.


No independence assumption is required. The spaces, laws, weights, and events may vary with r.


3. PROOF


By definition,


    P_r(E_r)
      =E_{Q_r}[W_r 1_{E_r}]/Z_r.


Cauchy–Schwarz gives


    E_{Q_r}[W_r 1_{E_r}]
      <=E_{Q_r}[W_r^2]^{1/2}
        E_{Q_r}[1_{E_r}^2]^{1/2}
      =E_{Q_r}[W_r^2]^{1/2}
        Q_r(E_r)^{1/2}.


Using the second-moment upper bound and normalizer lower bound,


    P_r(E_r)
      <=(sqrt(C_W)r^2)/(c_Zr^2)
        Q_r(E_r)^{1/2}
      =(sqrt(C_W)/c_Z)
        Q_r(E_r)^{1/2}.


This proves the theorem. QED.


4. EXPONENTIAL AND SUPER-POLYNOMIAL COROLLARIES


Suppose that for some A,b,p>0,


    Q_r(E_r)<=A exp(-b r^{-p}).


Then


    P_r(E_r)
      <=(sqrt(A C_W)/c_Z)
        exp[-(b/2)r^{-p}].


For every N>0,


    exp[-(b/2)r^{-p}]=o(r^N)


as r->0. Hence


    P_r(E_r)=o(r^N)


for every N>0.


More generally, if


    Q_r(E_r)<=A r^k


for some k>0, then


    P_r(E_r)
      <=(sqrt(A C_W)/c_Z)r^{k/2}.


The square-root loss is intrinsic to this second-moment/Cauchy–Schwarz interface unless stronger information about W_r on E_r is supplied.

6. DIRECTION AND POWER LEDGER


- Numerator tool: Cauchy–Schwarz.
- Weight second moment: O(r^4).
- Numerator after square root: O(r^2) times Q_r(E_r)^{1/2}.
- Normalizer lower bound: Omega(r^2).
- r^2 factors cancel.
- Rare-event probability incurs a square root.


The normalizer lower bound is the correct direction. A normalizer upper bound would not provide an upper bound on P_r(E_r).
