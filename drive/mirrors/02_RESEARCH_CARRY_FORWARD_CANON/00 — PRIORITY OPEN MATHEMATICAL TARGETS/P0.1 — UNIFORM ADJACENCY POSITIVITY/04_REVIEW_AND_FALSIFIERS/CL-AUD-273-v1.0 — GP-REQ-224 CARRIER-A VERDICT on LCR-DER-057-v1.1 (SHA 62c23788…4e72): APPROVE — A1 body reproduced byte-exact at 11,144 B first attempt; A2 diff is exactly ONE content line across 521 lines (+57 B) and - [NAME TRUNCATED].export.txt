A4 — HYPOTHESIS MATCHES EVERY PROOF USE OF ORTHOGONAL EIGENVECTORS AND e_s = J e_u. AFFIRMATIVE.
I enumerated every use of symmetry in the frozen body and checked each against the restored hypothesis. Three uses, all covered:


(i) Section 1 defines e_u(p) = P_u(A_p)d / ||P_u(A_p)d|| where P_u(A_p) is called the ORTHOGONAL unstable spectral projector. A spectral projector is orthogonal exactly when the matrix is self-adjoint. Covered by "symmetric".


(ii) Section 1 sets e_s(p) = J e_u(p) with J the rotation through +pi/2 and asserts "Because A_p is symmetric in dimension two, e_s(p) spans the stable eigenspace." For a symmetric 2x2 matrix with distinct eigenvalues the eigenspaces are orthogonal complements, so span(J e_u) is the stable eigenspace. Distinctness comes from the saddle type alpha_p > 0 > -beta_p. Covered by "symmetric hyperbolic saddles". This is the use LCR-DATA-068's interpretation names.


(iii) NEW OBSERVATION, not named in LCR-DATA-068 and not named in the v1.1 successor notice. Symmetry is ALSO load-bearing for the orientation agreement with GP-PROT-065, and this is not obvious. GP-PROT-065 §2 fixes the selected direction by e_u · (M - S) > 0. Carrier A section 9 orients by taking the reference vector d to be the displacement toward the designated maximum, i.e. d = M - S. Then


    e_u · (M - S) = (P_u d · d) / ||P_u d||.


For an ORTHOGONAL projector P_u d · d = ||P_u d||^2 > 0, so the sign condition holds automatically. For an oblique spectral projector — which is what one gets without symmetry — P_u d · d carries no sign guarantee and the constructed direction can fail GP-PROT-065's convention. Symmetry is therefore load-bearing for J2 as well as for the eigenframe. This strengthens rather than weakens the case for the v1.1 repair, and it means the restored hypothesis is doing more work than the repair notice claims.


Also verified: the frame Q_p = (e_u(p), e_s(p)) is orthogonal, and Q_p^T A_p Q_p is DIAGONAL — which is what licenses the decoupled coordinate system u' = alpha_p u + F_p, v' = -beta_p v + G_p. Diagonality requires e_u and e_s to be eigenvectors, hence requires symmetry. Continuity of alpha_p and beta_p is Weyl perturbation for symmetric matrices. Both covered.
