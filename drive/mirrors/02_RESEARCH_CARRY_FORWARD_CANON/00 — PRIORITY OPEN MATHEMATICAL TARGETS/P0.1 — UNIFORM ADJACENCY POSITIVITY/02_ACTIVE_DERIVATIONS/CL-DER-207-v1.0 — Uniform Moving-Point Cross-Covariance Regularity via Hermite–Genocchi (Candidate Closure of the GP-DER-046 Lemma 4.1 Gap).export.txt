GP-DER-046 Lemma 4.1 asserts the uniform conditional-mean bound
  M_5 := sup_{0<r<=r_0} sup_{alpha:|alpha|=5} sup_{X in D} |m_{r,alpha}(rX)| < infinity,
where m_{r,alpha}(z) = c_{r,alpha}(z)^T G_r^{-1} y_r is the conditional mean of partial^alpha f(z)
under the exact six-pin law Q_{6,r}. GP-DER-044 proves (verified in CL-AUD-203) the uniform frame
bounds g_- I <= G_r <= g_+ I and boundedness of y_r. The remaining ingredient — that every
component of the cross-covariance vector


  c_{r,alpha}(rX) = Cov( partial^alpha f(rX), L_{r,j} ),   j = 0..5,


extends continuously through r = 0 UNIFORMLY for X in the compact chart D — is asserted in one
sentence ("Taylor expansion of the analytic exact-torus kernel shows...") followed by QED. The

==================================================================
1. STATEMENT
==================================================================
Let K be the normalized-periodized Bargmann–Fock covariance kernel on the side-24 torus,
stationary (Cov(f(u), f(v)) = K(u - v)) and real-analytic. Fix M_r = (-r/2, 0), S_r = (+r/2, 0)
and let L_{r,0..5} be the GP-DER-044 confluent frame functionals: each L_{r,j} = D^{beta_j} o
DD_{k_j}, a fixed partial derivative D^{beta_j} of order |beta_j| <= 1 composed with a divided
difference DD_{k_j} of order k_j <= 3 taken along the x-axis over a node multiset contained in
{M_r, S_r} (equivalently, the raw six pins, by the invertible frame transformation of
GP-DER-044 Section 3). Let D be any fixed compact chart, R_D := sup_{X in D} max(|X_1|, |X_2|).


THEOREM. There exist r_0 > 0 and C_c < infinity, depending only on K, D and the frame, such that
for every 0 < r <= r_0, every multi-index alpha with |alpha| <= 5, every j, and every X in D:


  | c_{r,alpha,j}(rX)  -  c_{0,alpha,j} |  <=  C_c * r,          (*)


where the limit constant is the fixed number
  c_{0,alpha,j} = (-1)^{k_j} ( partial^{alpha + beta_j + (k_j,0)} K )(0) / k_j! .
In particular sup_{r <= r_0} sup_{alpha} sup_{X in D} |c_{r,alpha}(rX)| < infinity, and
c_{r,alpha}(rX) -> c_{0,alpha} uniformly on D at rate O(r).


COROLLARY (= GP-DER-046 Lemma 4.1). M_5 <= 6 * (sup_j |c_{0,alpha,j}| + C_c r_0) * g_-^{-1} *
sup_{r<=r_0} |y_r| < infinity, uniformly in r. Consequently the GP-DER-046 threshold
t_0 = 2(M_5 + B_5) is a genuine r-independent constant, as its Theorem 9.1 requires.


==================================================================
2. MECHANISM IN ONE PARAGRAPH
==================================================================
The difficulty is only apparent. A divided difference over colliding nodes LOOKS singular
(differences over gaps of size r, divided by r), but the Hermite–Genocchi formula rewrites it as
an AVERAGE of a higher derivative over the convex hull of the nodes — an expression with no
division at all. Applying the pin functional in the second kernel argument therefore converts
c_{r,alpha,j} into a simplex average of one FIXED, bounded-order derivative of K, evaluated at
points that all lie within distance O(r) of the origin — because the evaluation point rX and the
node hull BOTH shrink at rate r. Uniform convergence at rate O(r) then follows from nothing more
than the local Lipschitz property of that fixed derivative, which real-analyticity supplies. This
is the same cancellation principle as CL-DER-204 (pinned-slice interpolation): the r-singularity
is removed by an exact algebraic identity, not by a delicate estimate.


==================================================================
3. LEMMA 1 (HERMITE–GENOCCHI, deterministic; VERIFIED T1, T2)
==================================================================
For g in C^k on an interval containing the node multiset {t_0, ..., t_k},


  g[t_0, ..., t_k] = INT_{Sigma_k} g^{(k)}( lambda_0 t_0 + ... + lambda_k t_k ) d lambda,     (HG)


where Sigma_k = {lambda_i >= 0, sum lambda_i = 1} carries total mass 1/k!. (HG) is valid for
CONFLUENT (repeated) nodes and involves no division by node gaps. Equivalent closed form, used
for the machine verification: g[t] = sum_m c_m (m!/(m+k)!) h_m(t_0..t_k) with c_m the coefficients
of g^{(k)} and h_m the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial; specializing g = x^n recovers
the classical x^n[t_0..t_k] = h_{n-k}(t).
Verified: (HG) computed by two independent routes (symmetric-function closed form; direct
Dirichlet multi-index simplex integration) agrees EXACTLY with the recursive confluent divided
difference on 30 random polynomials with repeated nodes; the classical identity likewise (T1, T2).


==================================================================
4. LEMMA 2 (NO 1/r BLOW-UP; VERIFIED T3, control T5b)
==================================================================
If every node lies in [-r/2, r/2] then, by (HG) with |v(lambda)| <= r/2,


  | g[t_0..t_k] - g^{(k)}(0)/k! |  <=  sup_{|t| <= r/2} |g^{(k+1)}(t)| * (r/2) / k! .


Verified exactly with rigorous coefficient sup-bounds (T3). Planted control T5b: placing a single
node OUTSIDE the O(r) window breaks the bound — the node-shrinking hypothesis is load-bearing, not
decorative.


==================================================================
5. LEMMA 3 (MOVING-POINT UNIFORM BOUND; VERIFIED T4, T6, control T5a)
==================================================================
Fix alpha and j; write beta = beta_j, k = k_j. By bilinearity of covariance and stationarity,
applying L_{r,j} in the v-argument,


  c_{r,alpha,j}(z) = DD_k^{(v)} [ psi_z(v) ],   psi_z(t) := ( partial^{alpha+beta} K )( z - (t,0) ),


the divided difference being over the node multiset in [-r/2, r/2]. Note psi_z^{(k)}(t) =
(-1)^k ( partial^{alpha+beta+(k,0)} K )( z - (t,0) ), a FIXED derivative of K of total order
|alpha| + |beta| + k <= 5 + 1 + 3 = 9. By (HG),


  c_{r,alpha,j}(rX) = (-1)^k INT_{Sigma_k} Phi( rX - (v(lambda), 0) ) d lambda,
  Phi := partial^{alpha+beta+(k,0)} K,     |v(lambda)| <= r/2.


Every evaluation point lies in the box B_r := [-r(R_D + 1/2), r(R_D + 1/2)]^2. Since K is
real-analytic, Phi is C^1 near 0; let Lambda := sup_{B_{r_0}} ( |partial_x Phi| + |partial_y Phi| ).
Because B_r is a box (hence convex, with axis-parallel paths inside it), the two-coordinate
mean-value bound gives, for every evaluation point p in B_r,


  | Phi(p) - Phi(0) |  <=  Lambda * r (R_D + 1/2).


Integrating over Sigma_k (mass 1/k!) yields (*) with C_c = Lambda (R_D + 1/2) / k!, uniform in
X in D and in alpha (finitely many alpha; take the max). QED.


DESIGN NOTE (caught by the verification, recorded for reviewers): the Lipschitz constant must be
the FULL first-order one. The deviation moves the evaluation point in BOTH coordinates — rX has a
y-component — so a bound using only |partial_x Phi| is INSUFFICIENT. An earlier draft of the test
used the x-derivative alone and failed at ratio 3.65; both the test and the constant above were
corrected before freezing. Any reviewer reconstructing this bound should include both partials.


Verified: T4 (30 random fixed-order surrogates x 6 off-axis points each, exact arithmetic,
rigorous coefficient sup-bounds); T6 (composite family exercising all six functional types —
x-divided-differences of orders 0..3 over {M,M,S,S} and partial_y composed with x-divided-
differences of orders 0..1 over {M,S} — every member obeys one common O(r) bound uniformly over
an off-axis X-grid). Planted control T5a: holding the evaluation point FIXED (not shrinking with
r) violates the origin-limit bound, confirming the moving-point structure is load-bearing.


==================================================================
6. WHY THIS IS EXACTLY THE MISSING STEP
==================================================================
GP-DER-044's displayed argument fixes the target at the midpoint, so its "target block is
independent of r" device applies and only the NODES collide. Here both the target and the nodes
move. The proof above needs no new probabilistic input: covariance bilinearity turns the pin
functional into a divided difference acting on the kernel, (HG) removes the division exactly, and
the shrinking of BOTH the target and the node hull is what makes the same fixed constant the limit
for every X simultaneously — which is precisely the uniformity GP-DER-046 Lemma 4.1 asserts.
