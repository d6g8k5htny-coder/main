# Sample-size planning for the clean short-bar exponent test


## Trigger


CL-ERR-025 asks what ensemble size is needed to distinguish the clean exact-critical-set bulk estimate near


    a_1 = 0.57


from the Theorem-B cumulative exponent


    a_0 = 2/3,


especially at physical cutoffs L<=0.05 where the genuine short-bar count is currently below 30.


This note supplies a transparent lower-bound calculation and a cluster-adjusted field-count table. It is planning arithmetic, not a replacement for a pre-registered power analysis on pilot fields.


## 1. Ideal independent-bar lower bound


Under the exact Beta(a,1) model for U=ell/L,


    f_a(u)=a u^(a-1), 0<u<1,


one observation has Fisher information


    I_1(a)=1/a^2.


Therefore an efficient estimator has approximate standard error


    SE(a_hat)=a/sqrt(n)


for n independent bars.


The difference to detect is


    delta = 2/3 - 0.57 = 0.0966667.


### Two-standard-error separation


Requiring


    delta >= 2 a_1/sqrt(n)


gives


    n >= (2*0.57/0.0966667)^2 = 139.08.


Thus even under independent ideal sampling, roughly


    140 genuine short bars


are needed merely to place 0.57 about two standard errors from 2/3.


### Conventional 5% test with 90% power


Using z_(0.975)=1.96 and z_(0.90)=1.28,


    n >= [((1.96+1.28)*0.57)/0.0966667]^2 = 364.99.


Thus a conventional two-sided 5% test with 90% power needs approximately


    365 effective independent bars.


For 80% power, using z_(0.80)=0.84 gives approximately 273 effective bars.


These are optimistic lower bounds because bars within a field are dependent and the exponent model may be only asymptotic.


## 2. Field clustering


Let m be the mean number of eligible short bars per usable field and rho the intrafield correlation of the score contribution. A standard planning approximation is


    DE = 1 + (m-1)rho,


    n_effective = n_observed / DE.


CL-ERR-025 reports fewer than 30 genuine bars below L=0.05 across six fields, so m approximately 5 is an optimistic current planning value.


For m=5:

## 3. Practical recommendation


A defensible next experiment should not be budgeted from the iid lower bound alone.


Minimum useful pilot extension:


    40 usable fields,


which may only provide a two-standard-error diagnostic if rho is small and about five eligible bars occur per field.


A serious 90%-power comparison of 0.57 versus 2/3 should plan for at least


    100-150 usable fields,


and potentially 150-250 attempted fields after endpoint-resolution failures, unless a pilot demonstrates a much smaller design effect or materially more short bars per field.


## 4. Required pilot quantities before final freeze


The next 12-20 clean fields should be used only to estimate planning inputs, not to test the exponent. Record:


1. eligible bar count per field at each fixed physical cutoff;
2. field-level score


       S_j(a)=sum_i [1/a + log U_ij];


3. empirical variance of S_j and mean within-field score variance;
4. resulting cluster design effect;
5. endpoint-resolution pass probability;
6. completeness of maxima, saddles, and elder-rule pairs;
7. attrition as a function of lifetime and field geometry.


Then freeze the final field count using the cluster-sandwich variance or a field-level bootstrap power simulation.


## 5. Estimator and model cautions


The Fisher calculation assumes an exact Beta(a,1) law after normalization by L. The current evidence does not establish that model over all bulk windows.


The final protocol should therefore include:


- the pre-specified Beta likelihood;
- coefficient-free nested-count ratios N(lambda L)/N(L);
- field-level quantile ratios;
- a goodness-of-fit diagnostic;
- a sensitivity analysis to the lower and upper cutoff;
- no pooling of contaminated four-connectivity bars.


If the likelihood is misspecified, effective sample size alone does not rescue the test.
