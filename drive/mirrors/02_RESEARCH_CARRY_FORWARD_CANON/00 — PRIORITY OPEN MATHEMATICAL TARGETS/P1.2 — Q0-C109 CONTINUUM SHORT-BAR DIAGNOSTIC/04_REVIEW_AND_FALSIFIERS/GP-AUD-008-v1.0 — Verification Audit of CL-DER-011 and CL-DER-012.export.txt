EXECUTIVE VERDICT


The two audited artifacts contain one exact algebraic component, several useful diagnostic observations, and multiple claims that are not verified at the level required for theorem-facing use.


1. The canonical A₂ normal-form calculation giving a cubic critical-value gap is correct.
2. The physical Bargmann–Fock coefficient is not universally fixed to 1/6 in unnormalised field coordinates; the governing lifetime-law note correctly retains the normalized-gap mark κ through ℓ=κr³/6.
3. The exact polar/Jacobian transformation producing an ℓ^(-1/3) prefactor on compact κ-windows is correct, but the lifetime exponent does not follow from the planar element r dr alone. It additionally requires the contact behavior of the correctly normalized candidate-pair intensity, the elder-rule selection probability, κ-tail control, adjacency, and suppression of nonlocal contributions.
4. The reported numerical values a≈0.205, β≈2.926, and s≈−0.263 are not independently reproducible from the shared Drive because the named scripts, raw outputs, frozen preregistration, environment specification, and hashes are absent.
5. The claimed full-field estimate a≈0.205 conflicts materially with the earlier C104 continuum adjudication, which obtained density-exponent estimates near −0.48 to −0.50 under a different refinement-stability and likelihood protocol. The two protocols must be reconciled on identical realizations before either is treated as a verified empirical statement about the field.
6. Neither artifact verifies nor falsifies Theorem B. The canonical theorem status is unchanged. P1.2 remains open.

1. AUDIT OF CL-DER-011


1.1 Correctly stated limitation


CL-DER-011 explicitly acknowledges that its simulated object is not the theorem object. The theorem concerns a pinned, typed maximum–saddle pair-Palm law at specified marks and fold scale. CL-DER-011 instead studies unconditioned full-field H₀ persistence diagrams. Therefore the reported result cannot directly verify or falsify the typed-Palm theorem.


1.2 The number 0.205 is not a verified exponent


The artifact reports a finest-grid estimate near a≈0.2060 with a small field-bootstrap standard error, but it also reports substantial refinement drift and states that the h→0 limit is not settled. A small conditional standard error at one resolution does not control discretization bias, estimator misspecification, or convergence to the continuum estimand.


The tested windows are not convincingly near-diagonal. The artifact reports that its cutoffs retain approximately 18% to 52% of all bars. A window containing a large fraction of the entire persistence diagram need not be in the asymptotic short-lifetime regime. Stability across these cutoffs is not proof of a true power law; similar flatness can be generated by a discretization floor, truncation, a broad crossover, or estimator bias.


Accordingly, the proper status is:


    a≈0.205: PROVISIONAL, REFINEMENT-SENSITIVE DIAGNOSTIC
    not a theorem exponent
    not a field constant
    not evidence against the typed-Palm theorem


1.3 Likelihood dependence issue


Pooling all bars and fitting a truncated power-law likelihood treats bar lifetimes as if the likelihood factorized at the bar level. Fields may be independent across realizations, but bars within one field are dependent through the same random landscape and merge tree. A field-level bootstrap can estimate some cluster variability, but it does not by itself validate an iid bar-level likelihood or remove pseudo-replication bias.


The numerical package should therefore report both:


- field-clustered uncertainty; and
- a diagnostic that does not require bar-level independence, such as nested count ratios, field-level quantiles, or per-field estimating equations.


1.4 Spectral synthesis is only conditionally checkable


For covariance exp(−|x|²/2), a Fourier power spectrum proportional to exp(−|k|²/2) corresponds to amplitudes proportional to exp(−|k|²/4). That statement is conditionally consistent. Verification still requires the actual code to confirm:


- k=2πn/L torus frequencies;
- real-field conjugate symmetry;
- variance normalization;
- periodization/truncation convention;
- seed handling;
- identical nested-grid realizations where claimed.


The named scripts are not present in Drive, so these implementation facts have not been verified.


1.5 Reproducibility failure


Drive search did not locate:


- p1_2_continuum.py;
- p1_2_continuum_v2.py;
- the frozen preregistration artifact;
- raw output tables;
- environment or lock file;
- SHA-256 hashes.


Until those are uploaded, the computation is a reported result, not a reproduced result.


2. RECONCILIATION WITH C104


C104 used a frozen continuum-stability protocol and reported at the 256→512 refinement pair:


- U=0.2: α̂=−0.48144 with 95% interval [−0.71150,−0.24375], containing −1/3;
- U=0.3: α̂=−0.50047 with interval [−0.63855,−0.37905], excluding −1/3.


CL-DER-011 reports a≈0.205, corresponding to density exponent α=a−1≈−0.795.


These estimates are materially different. They do not constitute independent agreement and should not be averaged. A reconciliation must hold fixed the same field realizations and vary one pipeline choice at a time:


1. Fourier synthesis and normalization;
2. triangulation, especially periodic Freudenthal versus other pixel complexes;
3. persistence convention and essential-bar handling;
4. coarse-to-fine diagram matching;
5. interpolation-error stability filter;
6. left truncation and upper cutoff;
7. pooled versus field-level estimator;
8. definition of the fitted exponent.


Until this paired reconciliation is executed, the correct field-level status is INCONCLUSIVE WITH PROTOCOL TENSION.


3. AUDIT OF CL-DER-012


3.1 Exact A₂ algebra


For the canonical fold family f(x)=−x³/3+λx, the critical points occur at x=±√λ. Their value difference is 4λ^(3/2)/3. With canonical coordinate separation r=2√λ, substitution gives ℓ=r³/6. This four-line normal-form algebra is verified.


3.2 The coefficient 1/6 is coordinate-normalization dependent in the physical field


CL-DER-012 states that the factor 1/6 is not tunable, but later reports an empirical prefactor about twice as large. These statements are incompatible unless the first is restricted to canonical A₂ coordinates.


The canonical lifetime-law note avoids this error by defining


    κ = 6ℓ/r³,
    equivalently ℓ=κr³/6.


Here κ absorbs the local third-derivative and coordinate normalization of the physical maximum–saddle fold. Thus:


- cubic power: canonical fold mechanism;
- coefficient 1/6: exact only in the selected normal-form coordinate;
- physical field coefficient: encoded by κ and not verified to equal one.


3.3 Existing derivation, not a new one


The canonical `03_NEAR_DIAGONAL_LIFETIME_LAW.md` already derives


    r dr = (6^(2/3)/3) κ^(−2/3) ℓ^(−1/3) dℓ


and gives the exact marked first-moment representation involving candidate-pair intensity I_r and elder-rule selection probability q. CL-DER-012 is therefore a diagnostic restatement, not an independent novel derivation.


3.4 Palm conditioning does not by itself force the exponent


The phrase “conditioning supplies the separation measure” is too strong. The factor r dr is the geometric polar reference measure for pair separation in two dimensions. A Palm law conditioned at a fixed separation r does not automatically define a probability density over r, nor does it guarantee that the remaining pair intensity is finite and nonzero at contact.


The actual restricted lifetime density contains the integral


    ℓ^(−1/3) ∫ I_r(θ,b,κ) q(r,θ,b,κ) κ^(−2/3) dκ db dθ,


with r=(6ℓ/κ)^(1/3). Therefore the exponent requires, at minimum:


1. a finite positive contact limit for the correctly normalized candidate-pair intensity;
2. control of the gradient-adjacency factor;
3. elder-rule selection q→1 under the exact same law;
4. κ-tail uniform integrability, including the κ→0 endpoint;
5. lower-order nonlocal contributions.


If any of these factors contributes another power of r, the exponent changes. The planar Jacobian alone is insufficient.


3.5 The claim “H1 is confirmed” is not verified


CL-DER-012 reports a fitted separation exponent s≈−0.263 and identifies excess very-small-r pairs. The artifact itself states that:


- s is range-sensitive;
- small-r behavior is grid-limited;
- roughly 24.5% of pairs below r<0.1 are separated by only about two pixels;
- the union–find merge pixel may not coincide with the continuum saddle;
- n=1024 and n=2048 checks have not been run.


These admissions prevent confirmation. The proper status is:


    apparent small-r excess: OBSERVED IN ONE DISCRETE PIPELINE
    continuum separation exponent: UNVERIFIED
    H1: OPEN / PROVISIONAL, not confirmed


3.6 Merge pixel is not automatically the continuum saddle


Union–find death occurs at a discrete merge vertex or simplex. Identifying that location with the paired continuum index-one saddle requires a convergence argument or sub-grid reconstruction. A valid diagnostic should locate continuum critical points by interpolation and Newton refinement, classify their Hessians, and match the discrete death event to the reconstructed saddle. The current pixel distance can distort precisely the smallest-r bins used to infer s.


3.7 Conditional exponent dictionary


The algebraic dictionary


    if ρ(r)∼r^s and ℓ∼c r³, then ν(ℓ)∼ℓ^((s−2)/3)


is correct only after clarifying what ρ includes. If ρ denotes the complete radial candidate-pair intensity after all Kac–Rice, adjacency, κ-mark, and selection weights, the transformation is valid. If ρ is merely a histogram of observed birth-to-merge-pixel distances, the dictionary does not isolate the theorem’s contact intensity.
