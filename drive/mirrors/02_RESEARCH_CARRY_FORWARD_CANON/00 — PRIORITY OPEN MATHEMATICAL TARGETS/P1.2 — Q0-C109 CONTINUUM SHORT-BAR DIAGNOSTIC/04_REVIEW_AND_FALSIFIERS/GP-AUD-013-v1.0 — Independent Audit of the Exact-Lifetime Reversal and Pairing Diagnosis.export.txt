EXECUTIVE VERDICT


The uploaded CL-DATA-019 computation is independently reproducible. It produces exactly 791 pixel persistence pairs, 612 accepted maximum-saddle reconstructions, the reported pixel-versus-exact lifetime table, and the reported ordinary Beta(a,1) MLE values from 0.5870 through 0.7539.


The central interpretation in CL-DER-020 is nevertheless incorrect. The rise from approximately 0.25 to approximately 0.6 is not caused by replacing pixel lifetimes with exact critical-value lifetimes. It is caused almost entirely by selecting the 612 pairs that pass Newton and critical-type checks.


On the identical 612 accepted pairs:


- using their pixel lifetimes gives estimates 0.5891, 0.5895, 0.6553, 0.6444, 0.7228, 0.8189;
- using exact reconstructed lifetimes gives 0.5870, 0.5848, 0.6456, 0.6265, 0.6877, 0.7539.


Thus exact-value substitution moves the estimate slightly downward, not upward. Field-level bootstrap intervals for the exact-minus-matched-pixel shift include zero at every cutoff. The large upward shift is the reconstruction/type-selection effect.


The later statement that “both estimators drift” is only partly supported. The all-pixel ordinary MLE has a statistically visible increase in the smallest window under a 12-field cluster bootstrap. The exact selected-subset sequence is nonmonotone and its endpoint increase has a 95% bootstrap interval that includes zero. No goodness-of-fit or corrected likelihood test establishes model misspecification.


The failure-count claim is broadly reproducible: among 179 rejected pairs, only about seven are Newton nonconvergences while approximately 172 converge to a critical point of the wrong requested type. This proves that the pixel birth/merge locations are often unsuitable identifiers or seeds for the intended continuum maximum-saddle pair. It does not prove that the elder-rule pairing itself is wrong. “Pairing, not basin” is an overstrong false dichotomy.

1. REPRODUCTION METHOD


The unmodified script uses an explicit finite Fourier sum to evaluate each 512×512 field and exceeded the available execution window before completing. The original source was not edited.


For independent reproduction, the identical finite Fourier polynomial was evaluated by zero-padding its Fourier coefficients and applying an inverse FFT. This is mathematically equivalent at the requested grid points. On a validation field, the maximum absolute difference between the explicit-sum and FFT grids was


    2.73115×10^(-14).


All random seeds, spectral amplitudes, mode cutoff, field normalization, four-neighbor union-find persistence, Newton method, Hessian tests, and MLE formulas were kept identical. The optimized evaluator reproduced every published aggregate in a few seconds.


This audit therefore distinguishes:


- source execution equivalence: verified numerically to approximately 3×10^(-14) on the grid;
- reported aggregate reproduction: exact to the displayed digits;
- causal interpretation: independently tested rather than inherited.

3. THE DOMINANT CAUSAL EFFECT IS SELECTION, NOT VALUE CORRECTION


Three estimates were computed at each cutoff:


A. All 791 pixel bars, using pixel lifetimes.
B. The 612 accepted reconstructed pairs, still using their original pixel lifetimes.
C. The same 612 accepted pairs, using exact reconstructed critical-value lifetimes.


The sequences are:


    cutoff L:       0.400   0.200   0.100   0.050   0.025   0.010


    all-pixel:      0.2590  0.2450  0.2486  0.2479  0.2672  0.3091
    matched-pixel:  0.5891  0.5895  0.6553  0.6444  0.7228  0.8189
    exact selected: 0.5870  0.5848  0.6456  0.6265  0.6877  0.7539


The selection shift, matched-pixel minus all-pixel, is


    +0.3301, +0.3445, +0.4068, +0.3965, +0.4556, +0.5097.


The exact-value shift, exact minus matched-pixel, is


    -0.0022, -0.0046, -0.0098, -0.0179, -0.0351, -0.0649.


Therefore the headline “exact lifetimes give 0.63–0.69, not 0.205” attributes the change to the wrong operation. The accepted subset already gives values near or above 0.6 before exact lifetimes are substituted.


Correct statement:


    Critical-point reconstruction and type filtering select a strongly different subset of the pixel diagram. Within that selected subset, replacing pixel values with exact values has a much smaller, downward effect.

They do not establish statistically significant monotone drift of the exact selected estimator, nor do they establish that Beta(a,1) is misspecified. A maximum-minus-minimum range is positive by construction and is not a valid drift test without a null calibration.


5. ESTIMATOR INHOMOGENEITY WITH C104


CL-DER-020 explicitly states that it uses


    Beta(a,1) MLE on u=ell/L,


and its code implements


    a_hat = -m / sum log(ell/L).


There is no left-truncation correction in CL-DATA-019.


C104 used per-realization left truncation and a different continuum-stability protocol. Its estimates near a≈0.47 are therefore not directly comparable to the CL-DER-020 values. AO48-AUD-003 correctly withdrew an earlier comparison that treated unlike estimators as homogeneous.


Before any C104-versus-CL-DER-020 conclusion:


1. use the same fields and exact same accepted pair set;
2. use the same lower truncation for each realization;
3. apply the same likelihood to pixel and exact lifetimes;
4. report field-clustered uncertainty;
5. test estimator fit rather than compare decimal proximity to 2/3.

7. WHY “PAIRING, NOT BASIN” IS NOT YET PROVED


The type-failure count rules out only one narrow explanation: ordinary Newton nonconvergence is not the dominant rejection category.


It does not distinguish among:


- the pixel merge vertex being a poor geometric locator for the continuum saddle;
- four-neighbor cubical connectivity assigning a merge location displaced from the true saddle;
- Newton converging successfully to a nearby but unintended critical point;
- the discrete elder-rule pair not converging to a continuum pair at this resolution;
- an incomplete or incorrect continuum pairing classifier;
- periodic-distance or deduplication issues;
- a true elder-rule pairing mismatch.


A basin of attraction is defined relative to a target root. When the seed is not known to lie near the intended root, “Newton converged” does not establish that basin geometry is irrelevant. The present evidence concerns seed-to-root identification, not the continuum elder-rule pairing theorem.


The accepted set itself is not a validation set. Correct Hessian types and positive critical-value difference do not prove that the reconstructed maximum and saddle are elder-rule partners.


8. ADDITIONAL PAIR-IDENTITY CHECKS


Among the 612 accepted pairs:


- all exact lifetimes are positive;
- no duplicate reconstructed maximum-saddle pair occurs within any field;
- the median birth-pixel-to-maximum distance is 0.0194;
- the median merge-pixel-to-saddle distance is 0.0220;
- 19 accepted saddle reconstructions lie more than one grid spacing from the merge pixel;
- one accepted maximum and one accepted saddle lie more than one unit from their pixel seeds.


These outliers show that successful Newton convergence and correct type can still associate a pixel event with a distant critical point. They do not prove those particular pairs are wrong, but they block automatic acceptance of all 612 as verified continuum elder-rule pairs.


9. WHAT AN EXACT-CRITICAL-SET TEST MUST DO


The next decisive experiment should remove pixel locations from the pairing identity, not merely from the lifetime value.


Required pipeline:


1. Find the complete critical set from a dense periodic seed lattice with certified coverage or an adaptive root-isolation method.
2. Deduplicate roots using torus distance and a tolerance justified by residual and Hessian conditioning.
3. Classify every critical point by Hessian signature.
4. Construct the Morse-Smale or merge-tree connectivity from the exact critical set and gradient flow.
5. Apply the elder rule directly to that continuum graph.
6. Compare the resulting continuum pairs with discrete pairs across nested resolutions.
7. Compute exact lifetimes only after pair identity is fixed.
8. Apply one homogeneous, preregistered estimator with field-level uncertainty and lower-truncation treatment.


Until that pipeline exists, the 612 exact values are exact values of type-correct critical points selected through a pixel-defined pairing map; they are not a verified sample from the continuum elder-rule pair law.


10. STATUS OF THE BIAS FORMULA


For a true Beta(a,1) variable U, conditioning on U>s and then applying the ordinary untruncated MLE gives the asymptotic multiplicative bias


    B(s) = (1-s)/[(1-s)+s log s].


This formula is correct. AO48's earlier attrition table was mislabeled and was properly withdrawn.


The formula does not resolve CL-DATA-019 because its missingness is not known to be a simple threshold U>s. It depends on pixel location, Newton target, critical type, geometry, and likely field-specific local scales. Treating 22.6% total attrition as uniform lower truncation is not justified.
