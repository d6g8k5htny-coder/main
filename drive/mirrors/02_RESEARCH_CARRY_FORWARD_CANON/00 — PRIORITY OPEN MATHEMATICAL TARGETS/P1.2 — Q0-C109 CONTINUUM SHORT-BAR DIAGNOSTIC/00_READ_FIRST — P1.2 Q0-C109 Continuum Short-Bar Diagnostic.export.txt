P1.2 — Q0-C109 CONTINUUM SHORT-BAR DIAGNOSTIC
Read First / Field-Level Falsification Protocol


Priority: P1 — continuum validation and falsification target
Canonical impact: NONE. Numerical results are diagnostic evidence, not theorem proof.


TARGET
Test the coefficient-free consequences of the near-diagonal lifetime law on actual Bargmann–Fock field realizations while controlling both numerical refinement and the lower-tail cutoff.


ANALYTIC INPUT
Conditional on


nu_B(ell)=C_* ell^(-1/3)(1+o(1)),


with 0<C_*<∞, define N_B(L)=∫_0^L nu_B(ell)dell and the normalized short-bar intensity law below cutoff L.


The coefficient-free predictions are:
• N_B(uL)/N_B(L) → u^(2/3), for fixed 0≤u≤1.
• U_L=ell/L | {ell≤L} ⇒ Beta(2/3,1).
• -log U_L ⇒ Exp(rate 2/3).
• Q_L(tau)/L → tau^(3/2).
• cumulative log-slope → 2/3.
• rescaled mean → 2/5.
• rescaled variance → 9/100.
• coefficient of variation → 3/4.


These are intensity-law consequences. They do not imply that persistence bars within one field realization are iid.


CURRENT STATUS
The truncated-moment and coefficient-free shape laws have been independently derived and corrected. Existing iid synthetic simulations establish implementation sanity and nonvacuous identifiability against selected wrong exponents. They do not test continuum refinement, field dependence, persistence extraction, or finite-cutoff bias.


REQUIRED PREREGISTRATION
Freeze before observing outcomes:
1. Exact field covariance, torus size, and normalization.
2. Number of independent field realizations and random seeds.
3. Fourier truncation or exact simulation method.
4. Nested grid resolutions h_1>h_2>... using the same underlying field realization.
5. Filtration, triangulation/cubical convention, boundary handling, and persistence software version.
6. Birth-height window B and any mark restrictions.
7. Cross-resolution diagram/bar matching rule.
8. Stability or interpolation threshold for retaining a bar.
9. Cutoff ladder L_1>...>L_m.
10. Minimum effective count per field and cutoff.
11. Primary statistic, confidence procedure, rejection rule, and multiple-testing rule.
12. Rules for excluding unresolved essential bars or numerical duplicates.


CONTINUUM EXTRACTION
• Generate identical Fourier realizations on all nested grids.
• Compute persistence using one frozen convention.
• Match bars across successive resolutions.
• Retain only bars whose birth, death, and lifetime movement satisfy the preregistered stability bound.
• Record unmatched, split, merged, or unstable bars rather than silently discarding them.
• Report the effective minimum resolvable lifetime at each resolution.


PRIMARY DIAGNOSTICS
A. Nested count ratios
For fixed lambda in (0,1), compute per realization


R_j(lambda,L)=N_j(lambda L)/N_j(L).


Test whether field-level summaries approach lambda^(2/3) as h→0 and L→0.


B. Cumulative log-slope
Estimate the slope of log N_j(L) against log L over preregistered cutoff windows. The target is 2/3, not the density exponent -1/3.


SECONDARY CROSS-CHECKS
• Rescaled mean of ell/L toward 2/5.
• Rescaled variance toward 9/100.
• Quantile curve toward tau^(3/2).
• Exponential QQ behavior of -log(ell/L) against Exp(2/3).
• Beta CDF distance for the pooled intensity measure, reported only with dependence-aware inference.


DEPENDENCE-AWARE INFERENCE
Resample whole independent field realizations, not individual bars. Preferred methods:
• direct confidence intervals across independent realizations;
• field-level bootstrap;
• hierarchical model with field as the sampling unit.
If only one large domain exists, use spatial blocks with a justified block scale and explicitly downgrade the evidence.


TWO REQUIRED LIMITS
A valid continuum diagnostic must demonstrate stability in:
• numerical resolution h→0;
• tail cutoff L→0.
Sample-size convergence at a fixed synthetic distribution is neither limit.


FINITE-L BIAS
Do not force a pure-power fit at one cutoff. Use a cutoff ladder and report drift. A correction model such as


N_B(L)=A L^(2/3)(1+bL^rho+o(L^rho))


may be fitted only when rho and the fitting rule are preregistered or separately justified. No numerical C_* may be promoted from this diagnostic without a separate coefficient-certification cycle.


REQUIRED OUTPUT PACKAGE
1. Immutable prediction snapshot and preregistration.
2. Field-generation code and environment manifest.
3. Persistence-extraction code and exact conventions.
4. Bar-matching and stability-filter code.
5. Per-field, per-grid, per-cutoff ledger.
6. Raw diagrams or compact reproducible representations.
7. Field-level statistical report.
8. Refinement and cutoff-stability plots/tables.
9. Failure log for unstable or unmatched bars.
10. Reproducibility instructions and cryptographic hashes.


DECISIVE FALSIFIERS / CHALLENGES
After cross-resolution stabilization, the prediction is challenged if:
• count ratios do not approach lambda^(2/3);
• cumulative slopes remain systematically separated from 2/3;
• rescaled moments or quantiles drift away from their limits as L decreases;
• conclusions change materially under nearby preregistered cutoffs;
• the apparent law disappears under grid refinement;
• stable bars are too sparse to identify the tail without extrapolation;
• different legitimate persistence conventions yield incompatible limiting behavior.


KILL CONDITIONS
• Treat a refinement-sensitive slope as a discretization diagnostic, not theorem evidence.
• Stop if cutoff selection is data-dependent and cannot be independently validated.
• Do not pool individual bars as independent observations.
• Do not claim continuum support without matched nested-grid stability.
• Do not estimate or promote C_* from coefficient-free tests.


PROMOTION / STATUS CRITERIA
Objective 4 may be marked COMPLETED-AS-DIAGNOSTIC only when the full field-level nested-grid protocol is executed reproducibly. A successful result supports consistency; it does not prove Theorem B. A failed result must be classified as theorem challenge, discretization failure, or unresolved finite-L bias based on the preregistered decision tree.
