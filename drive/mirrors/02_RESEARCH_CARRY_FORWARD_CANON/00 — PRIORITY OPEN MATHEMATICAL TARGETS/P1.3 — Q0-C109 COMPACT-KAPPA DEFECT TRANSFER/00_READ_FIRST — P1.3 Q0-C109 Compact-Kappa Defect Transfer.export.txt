P1.3 — Q0-C109 COMPACT-KAPPA DEFECT TRANSFER
Read First / Proof and Endpoint-Extension Requirements


Priority: P1 — next-order selection-defect target
Canonical impact: NONE until formally promoted.


TARGET
First prove, on a compact mark interval K=[kappa_min,kappa_max] with kappa_min>0, that the selected lifetime density differs from the adjacent-candidate lifetime density by


nu_selected(ell,kappa)-nu_candidate(ell,kappa)=O(ell^(2/3))


uniformly for kappa∈K as ell→0.


Then identify and prove the additional assumptions required to extend the result through kappa→0.


WHY THE EXPONENT 2/3 IS EXPECTED
The fold relation is


ell=r^3/6,


so r=(6ell)^(1/3).


If the adjacent configured-pair selection defect satisfies


1-q_adj(r,kappa)=O(r^3)=O(ell)


uniformly on compact kappa ranges, while the candidate lifetime density has leading size ell^(-1/3), then multiplication by q_adj gives an additive selected-versus-candidate difference of order


ell × ell^(-1/3)=ell^(2/3).


This is a controlled defect-transfer statement. It is not, by itself, a complete second-order asymptotic expansion and does not identify a certified next coefficient.


EXACT OBJECT CARD TO FREEZE BEFORE ANY PROOF
• Definition of the adjacent candidate process.
• Definition of the selected elder-rule process.
• Exact law used for q: Q_ADJ_CONFIGURED, not bare q.
• Meaning and range of kappa.
• Orientation convention for maximum–saddle pairs.
• Multiplicity convention when more than one branch, saddle, or parameterization represents one bar.
• Candidate and selected intensity normalizations.
• Birth-height window and torus/domain convention.
• Fold map ell=r^3/6 and its Jacobian.
• Measure with respect to which each density is defined.


CURRENT STATUS
The leading Theorem B law is available in conditional Morse–Smale form, with full Gaussian status still on external-review track. The compact-kappa defect-transfer objective was not completed in CL-DER-009 because the selected/candidate definitions, compact-kappa marks, orientation, and multiplicity conventions were not available there. The arithmetic O(ell^(2/3)) is plausible and exact once the required uniform O(r^3) selection bound and candidate-density control are supplied.


COMPACT-KAPPA PROOF OBLIGATIONS
1. Freeze K=[kappa_min,kappa_max] with kappa_min>0.
2. State the candidate marked intensity dLambda_candidate(r,kappa) and selected intensity dLambda_selected=q_adj(r,kappa)dLambda_candidate.
3. Prove q_adj(r,kappa)=1+O(r^3) uniformly on K.
4. Prove the candidate intensity and fold coefficient are uniformly bounded and continuous on K at the required order.
5. Apply the exact change of variables ell=r^3/6, including all orientation and multiplicity factors.
6. Show the remainder is uniformly dominated so integration over kappa or the birth window is legitimate.
7. Derive the additive O(ell^(2/3)) density difference with the same measure and normalization on both sides.
8. State whether the result is pointwise in kappa, uniform on K, or integrated over K.
9. Separate the defect bound from any claim of a next asymptotic coefficient.


A CLEAN TARGET PROPOSITION
For every compact K⊂(0,∞), suppose:
• the adjacent candidate density obeys nu_candidate(ell,kappa)=C_0(kappa)ell^(-1/3)+o_K(ell^(-1/3));
• sup_{kappa∈K}|1-q_adj((6ell)^(1/3),kappa)|≤C_K ell;
• C_0 and the candidate remainder are uniformly controlled on K.


Then


sup_{kappa∈K}|nu_selected(ell,kappa)-nu_candidate(ell,kappa)|=O_K(ell^(2/3)).


An integrated version follows under an integrable kappa-dominating envelope.


KAPPA→0 EXTENSION: REQUIRED ADDITIONAL CONTROL
The compact result does not automatically extend to the endpoint. A valid extension must address:
• uniform adjacency positivity as kappa decreases;
• uniform q_adj defect constants;
• limiting behavior of C_0(kappa);
• determinant/Hessian degeneracy near kappa=0;
• possible change in saddle type, orientation, or branch labeling;
• integrable domination of the candidate and defect densities;
• absence of mass concentration or escape into an endpoint boundary layer;
• interchange of ell→0 and kappa→0 limits;
• whether a separate endpoint scaling variable is required.


POSSIBLE ENDPOINT OUTCOMES
A. Uniform extension: the same O(ell^(2/3)) bound holds down to kappa=0.
B. Integrable nonuniformity: pointwise constants diverge but the kappa-integrated defect remains O(ell^(2/3)).
C. Boundary layer: a coupled scaling kappa=kappa(ell) creates a different correction exponent.
D. Endpoint obstruction: orientation, type, or density normalization changes and the compact theorem cannot be extended as stated.

ADVERSARIAL CHECKS
• Are selected and candidate densities defined with the same normalization?
• Is q_adj applied to the correct adjacent candidate process?
• Has an unresolved factor of two, orientation count, or bar multiplicity been hidden in C_0?
• Is O(r^3) uniform in kappa, or only pointwise?
• Does the Jacobian introduce any additional kappa dependence?
• Can a kappa-endpoint singularity dominate ell^(2/3)?
• Is an integrated estimate being misstated as pointwise uniformity?
• Is a bound being promoted as an asymptotic coefficient?


KILL CONDITIONS
• Halt coefficient promotion if orientation or multiplicity remains unresolved.
• Retain only pointwise consequences if compact-mark uniformity fails.
• Do not extend through kappa=0 if constants diverge without integrable domination.
• Rescope to a coupled endpoint scaling if the two limits do not commute.
• Withdraw O(ell^(2/3)) if the selection defect is not uniformly O(ell) on compact K.
