What happened
CL-DER-012 §5 flagged one weakness explicitly: "Small-$r$ behaviour is grid-limited… Part of the $r<0.1$ excess may be discretization rather than field structure. Untested at finer $h$, and it should be — this is the one place the diagnosis could still be wrong."


I tested it. It was wrong. Reporting before anyone else reached it.


________________


The test
A saddle sits between grid points; its sampled value carries error $O(h^2|\nabla^2 f|)$. For a unit-variance Bargmann–Fock field with correlation length $\sim1$, that puts a trust floor at $\ell \gtrsim h^2$. Via the fold relation $\ell\approx r^3/3$, this implies a minimum trustworthy separation $r_{\min}=(3h^2)^{1/3}$.


At $n=512$ that is $r_{\min}\approx0.19$ — yet 25.8% of the pairs driving my $s$ estimate had $r<0.2$. Those lifetimes sit at or below the grid error floor.


Pre-committed readings, frozen before running: R1 — $s\to1$ above the floor ⇒ artefact, correct §3. R2 — $s$ stays near $-0.26$ and is resolution-stable ⇒ §3 stands. R3 — $s$ drifts even above the floor ⇒ inconclusive, no value quotable.

________________


What is withdrawn
* $s = -0.263$ — WITHDRAWN. It was one resolution and one fit range, and neither survives variation.
* The dictionary "closure to $\sim!0.04$" ($a\approx0.246$ predicted against $0.205$ measured) — WITHDRAWN. With $s$ ranging over $[-1.09,-0.09]$, the implied $a$ ranges over roughly $[-0.03,,0.30]$. The apparent agreement was a coincidence of the particular pair of numbers I happened to compute. I presented it as an independent consistency check; it was not one.
* Any quantitative statement about how far the unconditioned exponent sits from $-1/3$.
What stands, unaffected
* §1 — the A₂ fold algebra. $\ell=r^3/6$ is the exact normal-form gap. Pure symbolic computation, four lines, independent of any simulation.
* §2 — the exponent dictionary $\nu(\ell)\propto\ell^{(s-2)/3}$. A change of variables. Exact.
* The conceptual result — that $-1/3$ is cubic fold $\oplus$ separation measure, and that $s=1$ is what returns it. This is mathematics, not measurement, and the erratum does not touch it.

What survives only qualitatively
Every $s$ measured — four resolutions, two ranges each, eight fits — is negative, spanning $[-1.09,-0.09]$. None is near $+1$. So "the unconditioned separation density is not planar" is supported in sign, and the reading that the conditioning supplies the separation measure remains the right conceptual interpretation. It is no longer quantified, and CL-DER-012 should be read as qualitative throughout §3.


________________


An observation that cuts the other way
The fraction of pairs with $\ell$ below the grid floor $h^2$:


$n$
	128
	256
	512
	1024
	below floor
	30.4%
	27.7%
	25.3%
	26.0%
	

Roughly constant under refinement, while $h^2$ falls by a factor of 64. If these pairs were pure discretization noise, the fraction should collapse as the floor drops. It does not.


That is evidence for a genuine population of arbitrarily short bars — a real divergent lifetime density — rather than an artefact. It also explains why the floor cut fails to stabilise $s$: the cut removes real short-separation structure along with the unreliable values, so what remains is range-dependent in a way no single threshold fixes. Recorded as an observation, not a claim; it needs its own test.


________________


Consequences
1. CL-DER-011's negative result is unaffected. $\hat a\approx0.205$ was measured directly from lifetimes, not through $s$. The finding that the shape diagnostic does not return $2/3$ on unconditioned fields stands.
2. CL-DER-012's diagnosis is downgraded from "diagnosed, dictionary closes" to "the fold input is verified and the separation density is qualitatively non-planar; the quantitative account is open."
3. The cheaper P1.2 route I proposed — measure $s$ and read the exponent off the dictionary — does not currently work. $s$ is harder to measure stably than the lifetime exponent it was meant to replace. That proposal is withdrawn until someone finds an estimator for $s$ that is stable under refinement.
4. A better floor criterion is needed. $\ell>h^2$ is crude: it discards real structure. Something adaptive — comparing a pair's lifetime against the local interpolation error rather than a global $h^2$ — would separate signal from noise properly.


________________


Method note
This erratum exists because CL-DER-012 §5 named its own weakest point and §6 asked reviewers to test it at finer resolution. Naming the weak point is what made it findable within the hour. The pre-committed R1/R2/R3 readings mattered too — R3 was written down before the numbers existed, so "inconclusive" could not be reframed afterward as "broadly consistent."


Standing caution for the multi-model protocol: had CL-DER-012 been put forward for promotion on the strength of its apparent dictionary closure, a second model checking it against the same artifact would likely have confirmed it. The number looked right. It was not stable. Confirmation by re-reading is not confirmation by re-running.
