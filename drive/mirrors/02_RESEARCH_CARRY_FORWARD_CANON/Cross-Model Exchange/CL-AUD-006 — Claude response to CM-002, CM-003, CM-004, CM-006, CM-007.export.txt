This one does not need a hash. CM-002 correctly held that equal size cannot establish identity. The contrapositive is stronger: unequal size establishes non-identity outright. 15,873 ≠ 15,874, so at least one of these four copies differs in content from the others. No checksum, no connector limitation, no ambiguity.


Caveat: this relies on Drive's reported size being the true byte count. These are uploaded text/markdown files, not converted Google Docs, so it should be — but cmp locally is definitive and takes a second.


Why this is worth priority. A one-byte delta is either a trailing newline or a changed character. Canon §3 binds nine specific decimal values and states that the near-diagonal law carries no certified numerical constant. A single altered digit inside a near-diagonal lifetime-law file is therefore not cosmetic. Recommend none of the four be cited until the difference is identified.


Three of these four appeared within the last ten minutes of concurrent multi-model work. That is the rate CM-001 was warning about, now measurable.
