1. Input, and everything it is conditional on
Taken as given, not re-derived: the near-diagonal lifetime law recorded in the Carry-Forward Canon §2,


$$\nu_B(\ell) ;=; C_,\ell^{-1/3},(1+o(1)) \qquad \text{as } \ell \to 0, \qquad 0 < C_ < \infty,$$


with no numerical value of $C_*$ certified.


Every statement below inherits the full conditionality of that input. The conditional Morse–Smale form is the clean statement; the full Gaussian form remains on the external-review track pending specialist acceptance of the SARD-G transversality argument. Nothing here strengthens that status, and nothing here should be read as independent of it. If the near-diagonal law falls, everything in this document falls with it.


The derivation is elementary — it is an integration of a power law. The content is not the integration. It is (i) that the $o(1)$ transfers through the integral under a stated uniformity condition, and (ii) that the normalised consequences are entirely free of $C_$, which matters precisely because $C_$ is uncertified.


________________


2. The truncated moment law
For $p > -2/3$,


$$M_p(L) ;:=; \int_0^L \ell^{,p},\nu_B(\ell),d\ell ;=; \frac{C_*,L^{,p+2/3}}{p+2/3},(1+o(1)) \qquad (L \to 0).$$


Convergence boundary. The integrand behaves as $\ell^{,p-1/3}$ at the origin, so the integral converges iff $p - 1/3 > -1$, i.e. $p > -2/3$. At $p = -2/3$ exactly the endpoint exponent is $\ell^0$ and the divergence is logarithmic. Below it the integral diverges outright.


Transfer of the $o(1)$. Writing $\nu_B(\ell) = C_*\ell^{-1/3}(1+\varepsilon(\ell))$ with $\varepsilon(\ell)\to 0$,


$$\left|\int_0^L \ell^{,p-1/3}\varepsilon(\ell),d\ell\right| ;\le; \Big(\sup_{\ell\le L}|\varepsilon(\ell)|\Big)\cdot\frac{L^{,p+2/3}}{p+2/3},$$


and the supremum tends to $0$ as $L\to 0$. The error is therefore $o(1)$ relative to the leading term, which is what the statement asserts. This requires the $o(1)$ in the input to be uniform on shrinking intervals $(0,L]$ — a condition satisfied by the pointwise statement as recorded, but worth naming explicitly since it is the only analytic step that is not bookkeeping.

The count of bars of lifetime at most $L$ grows as $L^{2/3}$. The exponent $2/3$ is fixed; only the prefactor carries the uncertified constant.

3. The $C_*$-free consequences — the part that is actually usable
Normalising by the count removes $C_*$ entirely:


$$\mathbb{E}!\left[\ell^{,p} \mid \ell \le L\right] ;=; \frac{M_p}{M_0} ;=; \frac{2,L^{,p}}{3p+2}, \qquad p > -2/3.$$


Rescaling $u = \ell/L$ removes $L$ as well:


$$\mathbb{E}[u^{,p}] ;=; \frac{2}{3p+2}.$$


These are the moments of a Beta$(2/3,,1)$ law. Indeed the limiting density of $u$ is


$$f(u) ;=; \tfrac{2}{3},u^{-1/3} \quad\text{on }(0,1),$$


which is exactly the Beta$(a,1)$ density $a,u^{a-1}$ at $a=2/3$, and integrates to $1$.


$$\boxed{;\ell/L ;\longrightarrow; \mathrm{Beta}(2/3,,1);}$$


Dimensionless shape statistics — free of $C_*$, free of $L$, free of any fitted parameter:

A note on these rationals, because it matters under Canon §3. The values $3/2$, $2/5$, $9/100$, $3/4$ are exact rational consequences of integrating a fixed power law — they are determined entirely by the exponent $-1/3$ and involve no measurement, no fit, and no simulation. They are not constants in the sense the Canon restricts. I have checked them against the nine restricted decimals (4.3, 4.35, 0.8411, 0.84, 0.8501, 0.946, 0.97, 0.99, 1.01); none collide. No uncertified numerical constant appears anywhere in this document.

5. Errata, registered
Three defects, recorded per the erratum discipline rather than silently fixed.


E1 — general-$p$ symbolic check FAILED AS WRITTEN. The first script's symbolic match for arbitrary $p$ returned False. Cause: sympy could not evaluate $\int_0^L \ell^{,p-1/3}d\ell$ without the convergence condition supplied, and returned an unevaluated meijerg Piecewise. The formula was never in doubt — $M_0$ through $M_3$ all matched independently — but the check as written failed. Repaired by verifying the antiderivative by differentiation (exact zero, all real $p$) plus definite integration on the $p>0$ subset (match True), and by computing the endpoint limit explicitly at $p=-1/2$, $-2/3$, $-3/4$ to locate the boundary.


E2 — an assertion was made that the script did not test. The first script claimed divergence for $p\le-2/3$ "shows up as sample-size dependence, checked in PART E." PART E did not check it. Repaired by actually running it.


E2b — and the E2 repair was itself inconclusive. The first repair used single runs and produced an incoherent result: $p=-0.75$ showed a growth factor of $0.38$, i.e. decreasing, which cannot happen for a divergent moment. Diagnosis: sample means of divergent moments are themselves heavy-tailed, so one realisation is dominated by whichever rare draw appeared. 7. Novelty status: none claimed
No prior-art search was performed and no novelty claim is made. Integrating a power law to obtain a cumulative law is standard; the Beta$(a,1)$ characterisation of a rescaled power-law variable is standard. If anything here is new it would be the specific packaging — the $C_*$-free shape statistics as a falsification route for the near-diagonal law when the coefficient is uncertified — and that packaging has not been checked against the literature.


Per the operating discipline that checks precede claims in the same turn: treat this as a derived corollary of unknown novelty. A prior-art search over persistence-lifetime statistics, extreme-value and record theory, and Kac–Rice moment asymptotics should precede any novelty statement.
