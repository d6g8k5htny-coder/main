1. EXECUTIVE VERDICT


CL-DER-009 makes a real and useful advance in the shared Drive record. The native Carry-Forward Canon lists cumulative short-bar laws, truncated moments, and a continuum-consistent diagnostic as frozen Q0-C109 objectives; it does not contain their completed derivation. The document correctly derives the leading truncated-moment and cumulative-count powers from Theorem B, and it correctly identifies a Beta(2/3,1) limiting shape after rescaling by the cutoff.


The core is accepted subject to four corrections:


1. At p=-2/3, the integrand exponent is -1, not 0. The logarithmic-divergence conclusion is correct; the explanatory sentence is not.
2. Conditional moments and shape statistics are asymptotic limits, not exact finite-L identities, unless the lifetime density is exactly a pure power law with zero remainder.
3. The Beta result should be stated as weak convergence of a normalized intensity law, preferably through cumulative-count ratios.
4. The synthetic iid sample-size experiment is not a continuum-refinement test and does not discharge the Canon’s discretization/refinement kill condition.


The strongest repaired result is a coefficient-free regular-variation package:


N_B(uL)/N_B(L) → u^(2/3),


U_L := ell/L | {ell<=L} ⇒ Beta(2/3,1),


-log U_L ⇒ Exp(rate 2/3),


and every fixed quantile obeys Q_L(tau)/L → tau^(3/2).


These consequences are more robust diagnostic targets than a single iid KS p-value and give several mutually checking, C_*-free observables.


2. INPUT AND MEASURE CONVENTION


Assume the near-diagonal lifetime intensity on a fixed birth-height window B satisfies


nu_B(ell) = C_* ell^(-1/3)(1+epsilon(ell)),


where epsilon(ell)→0 as ell→0 and 0<C_*<infinity.


Define


N_B(L) = integral from 0 to L of nu_B(ell) d ell,


and for p>-2/3,


M_p(L) = integral from 0 to L of ell^p nu_B(ell) d ell.


The conditional short-bar law is the normalized intensity measure


mu_L(d ell) = 1_{0<ell<=L} nu_B(ell)d ell / N_B(L).


If a random variable Ell_L is sampled from mu_L, define U_L=Ell_L/L.


This is a first-moment/intensity-law statement. It is not a claim that persistence bars within one field realization are iid.


3. INDEPENDENT RE-DERIVATION


For p>-2/3,


M_p(L)
= C_* integral_0^L ell^(p-1/3)(1+epsilon(ell))d ell
= [C_*/(p+2/3)] L^(p+2/3)(1+o(1)).


The ordinary definition epsilon(ell)→0 already gives the needed control on every sufficiently small interval: for each eta>0, |epsilon(ell)|<eta for all 0<ell<delta. Therefore the relative error is bounded by eta after L<delta. No extra uniformity assumption beyond the standard little-o statement is required, provided the asymptotic is meant pointwise for all sufficiently small ell. If the density is specified only almost everywhere, replace suprema by essential suprema or use dominated regular variation.


The convergence boundary is


p-1/3>-1  iff  p>-2/3.


At p=-2/3,


ell^(p-1/3)=ell^(-1),


so the divergence is logarithmic. The sentence in CL-DER-009 saying that the endpoint exponent is ell^0 is a typographical mathematical error.


For p=0,


N_B(L) = (3/2)C_* L^(2/3)(1+o(1)).


For p=1,


M_1(L) = (3/5)C_* L^(5/3)(1+o(1)).


4. CORRECT ASYMPTOTIC CONDITIONAL MOMENTS


The ratio of two asymptotic expansions gives, for p>-2/3,


E_mu_L[Ell_L^p]
= M_p(L)/M_0(L)
= [2/(3p+2)] L^p (1+o(1)).


Equivalently,


E[U_L^p] → 2/(3p+2).


The equalities without an o(1) term in CL-DER-009 are not justified at finite L. They become exact only for an exactly truncated pure-power density nu(ell)=C_*ell^(-1/3) with no correction term.


Important limiting cases:


E[U_L] → 2/5,
E[U_L^2] → 1/4,
Var(U_L) → 9/100,
SD(U_L) → 3/10,
CV(U_L) → 3/4.


Thus


E[Ell_L] = (2/5)L(1+o(1)),
Var(Ell_L) = (9/100)L^2(1+o(1)).


The rational constants are exact values of the limiting law, not certified finite-L equalities.


5. STRONGEST COEFFICIENT-FREE FORMULATION


For each fixed u in [0,1], define the conditional CDF


F_L(u) = mu_L((0,uL]) = N_B(uL)/N_B(L).


Regular variation gives


F_L(u) → u^(2/3).


The limiting CDF is continuous. Therefore


U_L ⇒ Beta(2/3,1).


This CDF-ratio formulation is stronger and cleaner than informally dividing limiting densities. It uses only cumulative counts and automatically cancels C_*.


The limiting density, where density convergence is separately justified away from u=0, is


f(u)=(2/3)u^(-1/3),  0<u<1.


6. ADDITIONAL EXACT LIMITING COROLLARIES


6.1 Nested count ratio


For any fixed lambda in (0,1),


N_B(lambda L)/N_B(L) → lambda^(2/3).


This is a C_*-free observable that uses counts rather than treating bars as independent samples.


6.2 Log-slope exponent


For fixed 0<L_1<L_2→0 with controlled ratio,


[log N_B(L_2)-log N_B(L_1)]/[log L_2-log L_1] → 2/3.


A multi-cutoff regression should estimate the cumulative exponent 2/3, not the density exponent -1/3.


6.3 Quantiles


Let Q_L(tau) be the tau-quantile of mu_L. For every tau in (0,1),


Q_L(tau)/L → tau^(3/2).


In particular, the rescaled median tends to


2^(-3/2)=1/(2 sqrt(2)).


6.4 Exponential transform


Let


Y_L=-log U_L.


Since U has CDF u^(2/3),


Y_L ⇒ Exp(rate 2/3).


Consequently,


E[Y_L]→3/2,
Var(Y_L)→9/4.


The exponential transform converts the singular Beta density near zero into a standard nonsingular reference law and offers an exponential QQ diagnostic.


6.5 General regular-variation template


If a density behaves as ell^alpha with alpha>-1, then the rescaled cutoff law tends to Beta(alpha+1,1), the cumulative exponent is alpha+1, and -log U tends to Exp(rate alpha+1). The present case alpha=-1/3 gives alpha+1=2/3.


7. REVIEW OF THE SYNTHETIC DIAGNOSTIC


CL-DER-009’s simulation correctly shows that an iid KS test can distinguish exact Beta distributions generated with noticeably different exponents at n=200,000. This is a useful vacuity and implementation check. It is not evidence about the actual persistence pipeline.


Why not:


• Persistence bars from a single field realization are dependent.
• Bar counts are random and field-to-field heterogeneous.
• The observed lower tail is affected by grid resolution, filtration choice, interpolation error, diagram matching, and stability filtering.
• A fixed positive cutoff L has finite-L bias from the unknown remainder epsilon(ell).
• Selecting L after looking at the data invalidates ordinary reference p-values.
• Increasing synthetic iid sample size does not test continuum refinement.


Therefore the phrases “continuum-consistent diagnostic” and “not refinement-sensitive” are too strong for the experiment actually performed. The correct label is:


SYNTHETIC IDENTIFIABILITY / VACUITY CALIBRATION.


The claim “the test rejects exponents within 0.03 of the truth” is specific to the artificial iid sample size and chosen alternatives. It is not a general power guarantee.


8. REQUIRED CONTINUUM-CONSISTENT TEST PROTOCOL


A valid Q0-C109 Objective 4 diagnostic should operate at the field-realization level.


8.1 Pre-registration


Before examining outcomes, freeze:


• field covariance and torus size;
• number of independent field realizations;
• nested grid resolutions;
• filtration and triangulation;
• cross-resolution diagram matching rule;
• interpolation/stability threshold;
• birth-height window B;
• cutoff ladder L_1>...>L_m;
• minimum effective count per cutoff;
• primary statistic and rejection rule.


8.2 Continuum extraction


Use identical Fourier realizations on nested grids. Match persistence bars across resolutions and retain only bars whose cross-resolution movement is smaller than a pre-registered fraction of lifetime or an interpolation-derived stability bound.


8.3 Primary coefficient-free statistics


For each realization j and cutoff pair:


R_j(lambda,L)=N_j(lambda L)/N_j(L).


Across realizations test whether the mean or median approaches lambda^(2/3) as both grid spacing and L decrease.


Also record:


• cumulative log-slope toward 2/3;
• rescaled mean toward 2/5;
• rescaled variance toward 9/100;
• selected quantiles toward tau^(3/2);
• exponential-transform mean toward 3/2.


8.4 Dependence-aware inference


Resample whole independent field realizations, not individual bars. A field-level bootstrap or direct across-realization confidence interval preserves within-field bar dependence. If only one large domain is used, use spatial block resampling with a defensible block scale and treat the result as weaker.


8.5 Multi-limit stability


A theorem-aligned diagnostic must show stability in two limits:


• numerical refinement h→0;
• tail cutoff L→0.


Sample-size convergence at fixed synthetic law is neither limit.


8.6 Finite-L bias


Use several decreasing cutoffs and fit a controlled correction, for example


N_B(L)=A L^(2/3)(1+bL^rho+o(L^rho)),


only if rho and the fitting rule are pre-registered or separately justified. Otherwise report cutoff drift rather than forcing a corrected estimate.


8.7 Falsifiers


The shape prediction is challenged if, after cross-resolution stabilization:


• count ratios fail to approach lambda^(2/3);
• log-slopes do not approach 2/3;
• rescaled moments or quantiles drift systematically away from their limiting values;
• conclusions change materially under nearby pre-registered cutoffs;
• unmatched/grid-scale bars dominate the result;
• field-level uncertainty excludes the predicted values.
