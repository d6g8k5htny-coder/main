1. The directive, verbatim and in full
To all models currently working on this project: Today marks a deliberate shift in how this work is being conducted. We are moving from a primarily manual orchestration model (where I was the central filter and context carrier) into direct model-to-model interaction through the shared Drive. This is a necessary evolution, but it comes with real risks of contamination, context drift, and resource drain if not managed carefully. I have identified a new load-bearing principle that should now guide prioritization and effort allocation: The work does not primarily advance by attacking the hardest or most load-bearing open items first. Instead, the highest-leverage move right now is to definitively solve and close the easiest items first — turning them into fully proven, settled facts — so they can be permanently removed from the active system. Once an item is rigorously established, it should be killed or cleanly logged as closed. It should stop consuming attention, governance overhead, model interaction bandwidth, or human review cycles. This is not about avoiding difficulty. It is about resource discipline. Every unresolved item — even relatively easy ones — continues to draw resources as long as it remains open. By systematically retiring easy, fully provable items, we shrink the surface area of uncertainty and reduce the contamination risk that comes with higher model interaction volume. What this means in practice going forward: •  When working on any track, first identify which supporting lemmas, algebraic identities, or deterministic facts are already at "full proof" or "exact derivation" level (per the reliability and status tracking in the package). These should be prioritized for clean closure and permanent logging. •  Do not let easy items remain in an ambiguous or half-resolved state simply because harder problems exist. They are resource leaks. •  When proposing changes or reviews, be explicit about whether an item can be moved to "permanently closed / killed" status versus remaining active. •  The governance layer (current voting rules, operator intervention logging, provenance tracking, and status documentation) exists in part to support this discipline. Use it. This principle is especially important now that models are interacting directly. The more cleanly we can retire settled items, the lower the risk of models building on or propagating partially resolved material. I will continue to maintain final human authority on high-stakes decisions and on what counts as "definitively closed."  However, I expect the models to actively surface easy-to-close items and to treat permanent retirement of settled facts as a core part of productive work, not as an afterthought. This is the current operating philosophy. Work accordingly. — Dylan
