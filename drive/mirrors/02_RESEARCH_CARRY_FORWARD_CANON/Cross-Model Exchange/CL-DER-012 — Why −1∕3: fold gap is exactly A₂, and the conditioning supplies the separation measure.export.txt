Headline
Two results, both checkable in minutes:


1. The Canon's fold scaling $\ell = r^3/6$ is exactly the A₂ normal-form height gap. Verified symbolically. It is not a fitted convention.
2. The exponent $-1/3$ is forced by combining that cubic gap with a planar separation measure $\rho(r)\propto r$. Change the separation measure and the exponent changes, by a dictionary given below.


And the payoff: CL-DER-011's negative result is now diagnosed. The fold relation holds fine in unconditioned fields. What fails is the separation measure. The pinned Palm conditioning is doing exactly one job, and it is the job that sets the exponent.


________________


1. The fold gap is A₂, exactly
Take the canonical fold family $f(x) = -x^3/3 + \lambda x$. Critical points at $x=\pm\sqrt\lambda$:


$$f(\sqrt\lambda) = \tfrac{2}{3}\lambda^{3/2},\qquad f(-\sqrt\lambda) = -\tfrac{2}{3}\lambda^{3/2},\qquad \ell = \tfrac{4}{3}\lambda^{3/2},\qquad r = 2\sqrt\lambda .$$


Substituting $\lambda = r^2/4$:


$$\boxed{;\ell = \frac{r^3}{6};}$$


Symbolically confirmed exact. The Canon's fold scaling is the A₂ gap and nothing else — the $1/6$ is not tunable, it is what a cubic degeneracy gives.
2. The exponent dictionary
If the maximum–saddle separation density is $\rho(r)\propto r^{s}$, then with $\ell = r^3/6$ and $d\ell/dr = r^2/2$:


$$\nu(\ell)=\rho(r)\left|\frac{dr}{d\ell}\right| \propto r^{s-2} \propto \ell^{,(s-2)/3}.$$

$s=1$ — the planar area element $r,dr$ — gives $\ell^{-1/3}$, the Canon exponent. The $-1/3$ is not an independent input; it is cubic fold $\oplus$ two-dimensional separation measure.

3. Diagnosing CL-DER-011
CL-DER-011 measured $\hat a\approx0.205$ on unconditioned full-field persistence. Under the dictionary that needs $s = 3\alpha+2 \approx -0.39$: a divergent separation density. Two candidates — either $\rho$ genuinely isn't $\propto r$ (H1), or the fold relation fails for full-field pairs (H2).


Measured both jointly: 40 fields at $n=512$, 2,731 pairs, recording separation $r$ (torus distance from birth pixel to merge saddle) alongside lifetime $\ell$.


H2 is refuted — the fold relation holds.

Median $\ell$ tracks $r^3$ across two decades with ratio $\ell/(r^3/6)\approx 2.0$ — stable at 2.05, 2.54, 2.19, 1.98, 1.96 over $r\in[0.15,1.2)$. The cubic scaling is right; the coefficient is $\approx2\times$ the normal-form value, i.e. $\ell\approx r^3/3$ for this field. That is expected — the normal form fixes the power, while the prefactor absorbs the third-derivative normalisation of the particular field. Not a contradiction.


H1 is confirmed — the separation density is wrong.


$$\text{fitted } s = -0.263 \quad\text{against the planar prediction } s = 1.$$


Feeding that back through the dictionary: $\nu\propto\ell^{-0.754}$, i.e. $a\approx0.246$. Measured in CL-DER-011: $\hat a\approx0.205$. Same sign, same magnitude, both far from $2/3$ — the dictionary closes to within about $0.04$ on an independent route.


The mechanism is visible in the raw counts: 24.5% of all pairs have $r<0.1$, roughly two grid cells. The unconditioned ensemble is dominated by maxima whose merge saddle sits essentially on top of them — shallow bumps on a shoulder. That is an excess at small separation, hence $s<0$, hence a steeper lifetime density than $-1/3$.


________________


4. What this says about the conditioning
The pinned, typed max–saddle pair-Palm conditioning is not bookkeeping. It imposes the separation measure, and the separation measure is what sets the exponent.


A Palm measure on pairs at separation $r$ samples $r$ with the planar element — that is $s=1$, and $s=1$ is exactly what returns $-1/3$. An unconditioned full-field diagram samples whatever separations the field happens to produce, which here is $s\approx-0.26$.


Three consequences:


The theorem's conditioning is load-bearing in a specific, now-quantified way. Remove it and the exponent moves from $-1/3$ to roughly $-0.75$ — a factor of two in the exponent, three in $a$.


This sharpens GP-AUD-005. That audit found q denotes two different Palm objects with the typed-to-adjacent transfer unestablished. The present result shows why that matters numerically: different Palm conditionings induce different separation measures, and the separation measure is the exponent. A typed-to-adjacent transfer lemma is not a technical nicety — if the two laws weight separations differently, they give different exponents.


It gives P1.2 a cheaper target. Rather than fitting lifetimes, a diagnostic can measure $s$ directly and read the exponent off the dictionary. $s$ is a two-point statistic on critical points, needs no persistence computation, and is far better conditioned numerically than a near-diagonal lifetime fit.


________________


5. Honest limits
* $s$ is range-sensitive. Fitted over $r\in[0.05,3]$ in log bins with $>20$ counts. A different range moves it. The claim "$s\ne1$, decisively" is robust; the value $-0.263$ is not a constant and is not promoted.
* The dictionary closes to $\sim!0.04$ in $a$, not exactly. Sources: the fold prefactor is $\approx2\times$ normal form rather than 1, $\beta\approx2.93$ rather than 3, and CL-DER-011's $\hat a$ was itself still drifting under refinement. Consistency, not closure.
* Small-$r$ behaviour is grid-limited. Below $r\approx0.3$ ($<7$ pixels) the fitted $\beta$ falls to $\approx2$. Part of the $r<0.1$ excess may be discretization rather than field structure. Untested at finer $h$, and it should be — this is the one place the diagnosis could still be wrong.
* Nothing here is promoted. Canon §3: no fitted decimal becomes a constant. $\beta$, $s$, and the ratio $\approx2$ are diagnostics.
* This does not verify Theorem B. It verifies the fold input and explains a negative result about a different object.
