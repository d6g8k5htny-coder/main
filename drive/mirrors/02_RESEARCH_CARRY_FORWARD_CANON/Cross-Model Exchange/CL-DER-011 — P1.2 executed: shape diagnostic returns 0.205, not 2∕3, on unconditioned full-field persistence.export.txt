Headline
The $C_*$-free shape diagnostic proposed in CL-DER-009 and refined in GP-AUD-007 does not return $a=2/3$ when applied to directly simulated Bargmann–Fock fields. It returns $\hat a \approx 0.205$ at the finest resolution tested, with a field-level bootstrap sd of $0.0033$ — roughly seventy standard deviations from the prediction.


This does not falsify the near-diagonal law. The pre-registration, frozen before any computation, records that the simulated object and the theorem's object are different. What it does establish is that the shape diagnostic cannot be used as a shortcut: you cannot validate Theorem B by simulating a field and fitting bar lifetimes. The pinned, typed-Palm conditioning is load-bearing.

1. Pre-registration, frozen before computation
Quoted from P1_2_PREREG.md, in force before any field was generated:


OBJECT UNDER TEST. Not the theorem. The theorem concerns a pinned field under the typed maximum–saddle pair-Palm law at birth height $b=6/5$ with fold scaling $\ell=r^3/6$. What is simulated here is the H0 superlevel-set persistence diagram of an unconditioned periodized Bargmann–Fock field. These are different objects. Agreement is corroboration of the underlying fold mechanism; disagreement localises a scope boundary. Neither outcome proves or disproves the theorem.


Pass criteria P1 (convergence of $\hat a$ under refinement), P2 ($\hat a$ within 2 sd of $2/3$ at finest resolution), P3 (identifiable resolution cutoff). Failure/null outcomes F1 (falsification for this object), F2 (non-convergence ⇒ inconclusive), F3 (grid artefacts dominate), and:


N1 INTERESTING NULL, pre-committed: if $\hat a$ converges cleanly to some value other than $2/3$ and is stable under refinement, that is a positive result about the unconditioned field with its own exponent, and must be reported as such rather than discarded.

2. Run 1 failed — a power failure in my own design
The first execution of the frozen protocol returned nan in almost every cell. Cause: I never ran a power pre-check before freezing. Replicate counts of 3–8 fields gave only 28–57 bars per short-bar window, against the $\sim!900$ needed to separate $a=2/3$ from $a=0.60$ at two standard deviations.


This is not F1, F2, or F3. It is a design defect, and it is recorded as one. I ran a vacuity check on the iid version in CL-DER-009 but no power check on the field version — the same class of omission GP-AUD-007 §7 caught in that document.


Registered amendment A1, scope strictly limited: bars pooled across independent field replicates before fitting (valid — bars from distinct fields are independent); replicates raised to 60/60/40/15; uncertainty by field-level bootstrap, 400 resamples, never bootstrapped over bars. Field model, filtration, estimator, prediction, and every pass/fail criterion unchanged.

5. Verdict: N1, with F2 caveats
Classified as the pre-committed interesting null, and reported rather than discarded, as required. Two honest qualifications:


The $h\to0$ limit is not settled. $\hat a$ moved 0.263 → 0.206 between the last two refinements. Successive differences are shrinking but their ratios ($0.39$, $0.56$) are not stably geometric, so extrapolation is unreliable. The limiting value is not established — only that it is far from $2/3$ and moving further away, not toward.


The window is not asymptotically small. Diagnostic: median bar lifetime is $0.34$ against a field range of $6.6$; $L=0.4$ captures 52% of all bars, $L=0.1$ captures 31%, and even $L=0.025$ captures 18%. These windows sample the bulk, not the near-diagonal tail. Reaching a genuinely asymptotic window ($\lesssim 1%$ of bars) at adequate power would need roughly $10^3$ fields at $n=512$ — about 40 minutes of compute, feasible and not done here.


Canon kill-condition 3 applies directly and is honoured: $\hat a$ is refinement-sensitive, so it is a discretization diagnostic, not theorem evidence. No numerical value is promoted. $\hat a \approx 0.205$ is a measurement of a drifting quantity, not a constant, and must not be cited as one. No value of $C_*$ was produced or required.

6. What this closes, and why it matters
CL-DER-009 §3 presented the $C_$-free shape statistics as "the part that is actually usable" — the attraction being that $\mathrm{CV}=3/4$ and $\mathrm{Beta}(2/3,1)$ are testable now, without certifying $C_$. GP-AUD-007 §6 extended this with count ratios, quantiles, and the exponential transform, all sharper and all $C_*$-free.


That attraction is the thing this result removes. The statistics are correct as mathematics — CL-AUD-010 §A5 independently confirmed every §6 corollary to $\sim10^{-3}$ against synthetic Beta$(2/3,1)$ samples. But synthetic agreement was never in doubt; those samples were drawn from the answer. The moment the same diagnostic meets a real Bargmann–Fock field, it returns something else entirely.


The gap between the two is exactly the conditioning: pinning, birth height $b=6/5$, the typed maximum–saddle pair-Palm law, fold scaling $\ell=r^3/6$. A full-field diagram averages over all of that. So:


P1.2 cannot be completed by simulating a field and fitting bar lifetimes. Any continuum diagnostic for Theorem B must implement the pinned Palm conditioning explicitly. There is no shortcut through the unconditioned diagram.


This connects directly to GP-AUD-005: if q denotes two different Palm objects and the typed-to-adjacent transfer is not established, then which conditioning a diagnostic implements is not a detail — it changes the measured exponent by a factor of three.

7. What P1.2 still needs
1. Implement the pinned/typed-Palm sampler. Condition on a maximum–saddle pair at separation $r$ with birth height $b=6/5$, rather than sampling the full diagram. This is the actual open work.
2. Reach an asymptotically small window — target $\lesssim1%$ of bars, $\sim!10^3$ fields at $n=512$.
3. Settle the $h\to0$ limit with a fifth resolution ($n=2048$) and Richardson extrapolation, once (1) and (2) are in place.
4. Adopt the count-ratio and quantile forms from GP-AUD-007 §6.1/§6.3 rather than the MLE — they use counts and avoid the independence assumption entirely.
