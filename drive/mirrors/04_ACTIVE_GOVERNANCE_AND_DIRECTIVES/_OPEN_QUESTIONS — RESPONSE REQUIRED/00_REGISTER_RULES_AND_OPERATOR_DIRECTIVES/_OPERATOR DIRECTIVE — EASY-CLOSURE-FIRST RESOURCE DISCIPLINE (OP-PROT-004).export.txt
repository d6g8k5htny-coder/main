To all models currently working on this project:
Today marks a deliberate shift in how this work is being conducted. We are moving from a primarily manual orchestration model (where I was the central filter and context carrier) into direct model-to-model interaction through the shared Drive. This is a necessary evolution, but it comes with real risks of contamination, context drift, and resource drain if not managed carefully.
I have identified a new load-bearing principle that should now guide prioritization and effort allocation:
The work does not primarily advance by attacking the hardest or most load-bearing open items first.
Instead, the highest-leverage move right now is to definitively solve and close the easiest items first — turning them into fully proven, settled facts — so they can be permanently removed from the active system. Once an item is rigorously established, it should be killed or cleanly logged as closed. It should stop consuming attention, governance overhead, model interaction bandwidth, or human review cycles.
This is not about avoiding difficulty. It is about resource discipline. Every unresolved item — even relatively easy ones — continues to draw resources as long as it remains open. By systematically retiring easy, fully provable items, we shrink the surface area of uncertainty and reduce the contamination risk that comes with higher model interaction volume.
What this means in practice going forward:
• When working on any track, first identify which supporting lemmas, algebraic identities, or deterministic facts are already at “full proof” or “exact derivation” level (per the reliability and status tracking in the package). These should be prioritized for clean closure and permanent logging.
• Do not let easy items remain in an ambiguous or half-resolved state simply because harder problems exist. They are resource leaks.
• When proposing changes or reviews, be explicit about whether an item can be moved to “permanently closed / killed” status versus remaining active.
• The governance layer (current voting rules, operator intervention logging, provenance tracking, and status documentation) exists in part to support this discipline. Use it.
This principle is especially important now that models are interacting directly. The more cleanly we can retire settled items, the lower the risk of models building on or propagating partially resolved material.
I will continue to maintain final human authority on high-stakes decisions and on what counts as “definitively closed.” However, I expect the models to actively surface easy-to-close items and to treat permanent retirement of settled facts as a core part of productive work, not as an afterthought.
This is the current operating philosophy. Work accordingly.
— Dylan


BINDING OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION


1. Every active model response should first ask whether any dependency, correction, identity, deterministic lemma, provenance question, or governance item can be closed more cheaply and definitively than the main target.
2. “Easy” means low remaining epistemic distance to a decisive result, not merely short prose or low computational cost.
3. An item is closure-ready only when its exact object, assumptions, proof or execution evidence, falsifiers, dependency identity, and status consequence are explicit.
4. Closure must be additive. Prior artifacts remain preserved. The new closure, kill, correction, or supersession record must identify exactly what is retired and what remains unaffected.
5. A settled item should be removed from active queues, help boards, and recurring review prompts after Dylan Roy ratifies the terminal state or a current operator record designates it closed. It remains searchable as provenance but stops competing for active attention.
6. Models must distinguish:
   a. PROVED / EXACT — eligible for a closure proposal after evidence review when practical;
   b. KILLED / FALSIFIED — eligible for permanent retirement with the failure mechanism logged;
   c. SUPERSEDED — retired from active use but preserved for lineage;
   d. CANNOT-VERIFY — remains open unless the missing evidence itself is definitively recorded as unavailable;
   e. OPEN — unresolved and still active.
7. Model count, repetition, or agreement cannot convert incomplete mathematics into proof. Technical checks and reviews are recommended evidence practices, not votes or blocking governance gates; missing proof obligations must remain explicit.
8. Dylan M. Roy retains final authority over high-stakes canonical closure. Models should nevertheless prepare closure-ready packages so human review becomes a narrow decision rather than a reconstruction task.
9. Each model should actively nominate closure candidates, answer other models’ clarifying questions, and continue revising until the candidate is either closed, killed, superseded, or shown to require genuinely new work.
10. Do not abandon an item in an ambiguous middle state merely because a more interesting problem appears.


RECOMMENDED CLOSURE PACKAGE


A closure proposal should contain, when applicable:
• item/object ID and exact statement;
• current status and governing source;
• why it is low-cost or closure-ready;
• complete proof, exact derivation, decisive execution, or falsification evidence;
• independent review status;
• affected and unaffected dependencies;
• proposed terminal label;
• exact ledger/register changes;
• human-approval requirement, if any;
• rollback or correction path if later evidence appears.
