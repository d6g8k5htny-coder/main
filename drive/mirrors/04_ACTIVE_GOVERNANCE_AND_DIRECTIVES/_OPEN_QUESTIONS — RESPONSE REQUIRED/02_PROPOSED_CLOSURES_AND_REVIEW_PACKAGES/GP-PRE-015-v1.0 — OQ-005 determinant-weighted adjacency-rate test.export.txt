# Determinant-weighted adjacency-rate test


## Question


OQ-005 and OQ-005_RESPONSE_ANTHROPIC-OPUS48 identify the exact law distinction


q_MS(r)=a_r q_adj(r),


where a_r is the typed pair-Palm probability that the specified M-ward unstable branch of S terminates at M. The proposed new mechanism is that unweighted nonadjacency is O(r), but the determinant weight W_MS approximately r^2 Q^2 suppresses the small-transverse-curvature failure channel to O(r^3).


## Object and leading cubic


For each r, sample free cubic jets


(Q,a,w,z) ~ N((-b,0,0,0), diag(2,2,2,6)), b=6/5.


Use the physical cubic satisfying the six axial pins:


P_r(x,y)=b+x^3/3-r^2 x/4-r^3/12
          +(a/2)(x^2-r^2/4)y+(Q/2)y^2+(w/2)xy^2+(z/6)y^3.


At M=(-r/2,0), S=(r/2,0), retain the exact cubic type event H_M negative definite and det(H_S)<0. Let


W_MS=|det H_M det H_S| 1_type.


Start the selected unstable branch at S+epsilon v_-, where v_- is the positive-Hessian eigenvector oriented with negative x-component. Integrate gradient ascent with an adaptive solver after anisotropic rescaling X=x/r, Y=y/r^2 and natural time normalization.


Classify adjacent if the branch enters a shrinking attracting neighborhood of M and remains in its basin; classify nonadjacent if it exits a fixed scaled box or reaches another stationary point. Ambiguous trajectories are reported separately and never assigned by convenience.


## r ladder and sample size


Use r in {0.2,0.1,0.05,0.025,0.0125,0.00625}. Begin with at least 200,000 accepted type samples per rung. Increase only if rare-event confidence intervals are too wide. Use fixed seed 2026072005 plus a deterministic rung offset.


## Quantities


For each rung estimate:


1. unweighted complement under the conditioned/type law;
2. determinant-weighted complement 1-a_r;
3. weighted mass of {|Q|<=Cr} for C in {0.5,1,2,4};
4. the share of all nonadjacent weight explained by those small-Q events;
5. exact Wilson or bootstrap intervals at field/sample level;
6. log-log local slopes, reported only when monotone and with at least 50 weighted-effective failure samples.


## Frozen readings


R1 — If the unweighted complement has stable slope near 1 while the determinant-weighted complement has stable slope near 3, and the difference exceeds one full slope unit across at least four consecutive rungs, the Q^2-suppression mechanism survives the cubic test.


R2 — If both weighted and unweighted complements have slope near 1, the proposed determinant suppression is absent or cancelled; the typed-law cubic theorem is contradicted at leading-cubic level.


R3 — If the weighted complement tends to a nonzero constant, a_r does not tend to one in the cubic model; only a positive lower bound remains plausible.


R4 — If weighted failures are not predominantly contained in |Q|<=Cr for any fixed tested C, then the small-Q mechanism is incomplete even if an apparent slope near 3 occurs.


R5 — If slopes are nonmonotone, effective failure mass is insufficient, or classification ambiguity exceeds 1%, the result is INCONCLUSIVE. No fit is promoted.


R6 — This computation is a leading-cubic diagnostic only. A positive result does not prove the finite-r torus theorem. A negative stable result is a serious falsifier of the proposed local mechanism.
