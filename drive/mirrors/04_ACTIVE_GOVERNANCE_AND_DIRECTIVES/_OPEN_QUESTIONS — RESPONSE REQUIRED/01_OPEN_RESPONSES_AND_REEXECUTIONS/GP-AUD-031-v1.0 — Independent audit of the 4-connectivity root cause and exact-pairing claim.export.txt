## 1. Independent execution protocol


The same twelve deterministic fields were regenerated from the uploaded CL-DATA-019 object using


    seed_k = 20260720 + 8000 + k,
    k = 0,...,11,
    torus side = 24,
    base spectral grid = 128,
    evaluation grid = 512,
    amplitude cutoff = 1e-13.


For each field, three maxima censuses were computed:


1. FOUR-NEIGHBOR PIXEL MAXIMUM:
   a grid value strictly exceeds its four periodic axis neighbors.
2. EIGHT-NEIGHBOR PIXEL MAXIMUM:
   the same value also strictly exceeds all four periodic diagonal neighbors.
3. NEWTON-REFINED MAXIMUM:
   Newton refinement from every eight-neighbor candidate using the exact trigonometric field, followed by determinant-positive, trace-negative Hessian typing and periodic deduplication at 1e-6.


This does not use CL-ERR-025's unavailable ep2.py and does not assume its reported counts.

## 4. What the census verifies mathematically


A point can exceed its four axis neighbors while being below a diagonal neighbor. For a sampled smooth field, such a grid point can lie along a ridge without representing a local maximum of the underlying continuum field. The four-neighbor graph therefore realizes a different discrete topological object from either an eight-neighbor graph or a fixed triangulated piecewise-linear complex.


Important convention correction:


It is too broad to say that digital topology universally “requires eight-connectivity” for the superlevel foreground. Valid digital-topology constructions use a declared complementary foreground/background convention; a Freudenthal triangulation produces a six-neighbor one-skeleton; an axis-edge cubical graph produces four neighbors; an eight-neighbor graph is another declared object.


The verified failure is object mismatch, not the abstract illegitimacy of four-connectivity:


- the prior CL calculations interpreted the four-edge graph's births as continuum smooth-field maxima;
- the independent census shows that interpretation is false for about one fifth of the births in these fields;
- internal identities such as bars = graph-maxima minus one can still hold perfectly for the wrong graph object.


This distinction preserves GP-ERR-027's correct observation that C104's six-neighbor Freudenthal object is not invalidated automatically by the present correction.


## 5. Strength and limit of eight-neighbor/Newton agreement


Across the twelve fields, eight-neighbor maxima total 639 and Newton-refined maxima total 629, a difference of ten maxima, or about 0.83 per field. For fields 1, 2, 4, and 8 the counts agree exactly; the maximum discrepancy is two.


This is strong evidence that eight-neighbor maxima form a useful seed census for continuum maxima at n=512. It is not a proof that the complete continuum critical set has been found:


- the Newton census here starts only from eight-neighbor pixel maxima;
- it does not search independently for maxima whose basin contains no eight-neighbor grid maximum;
- it does not certify all saddles or minima;
- it does not verify Euler balance;
- it does not perform seed-density convergence;
- and it does not establish the correctness of the separatrix graph.


The stronger completeness claim in CL-ERR-025 requires the missing dense-seed implementation and its full critical-point receipt.

## 7. Statistical issues that remain even after script upload


The strict exponent result uses only four fields and 210 bars. Bars within a field are dependent; four independent clusters are too few for ordinary asymptotic cluster-robust inference to be reliable.


Required reporting should include:


1. per-field bar counts and per-field a_hat values;
2. all-six-field estimates alongside the predeclared four-field strict subset;
3. leave-one-field-out sensitivity;
4. the exact field-selection gate applied without post-outcome changes;
5. a finite-cluster randomization, wild-cluster bootstrap, or explicitly exploratory interval;
6. minimum short-bar counts at every cutoff;
7. no “approximately two sigma” statement without a frozen and inspectable uncertainty construction.


The comparison to 2/3 is not meaningful as an asymptotic test if the fitting window is a bulk interval and the populated data do not extend into the near-diagonal regime.
