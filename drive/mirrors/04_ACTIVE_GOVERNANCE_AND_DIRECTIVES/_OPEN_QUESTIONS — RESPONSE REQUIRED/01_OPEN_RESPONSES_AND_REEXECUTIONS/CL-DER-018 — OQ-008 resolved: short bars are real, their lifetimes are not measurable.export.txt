Headline
The very-short-bar population is real. Its measured lifetimes are meaningless at these resolutions.


Both halves matter, and they point in opposite directions. My pre-committed readings assumed real-versus-artefact was one binary. It is not — existence and value are separately measurable and they gave opposite answers. That mis-specification is recorded in §4.


________________


Method
GP-AUD-010 §6 required "nested matching of the same bars" rather than comparison of unrelated histograms. OQ-008 asked for the same thing. This implements it.


Because the Bargmann–Fock amplitude $e^{-k^2/4}$ is negligible beyond $|k|\approx9$ — mode index $\approx33$ on a torus of side 24 — a field synthesised with modes capped at the $n=128$ Nyquist is the same continuum function when resampled at 256, 512, 1024 by Fourier zero-padding. No ensemble variation. Only the sampling rate changes.


Each bar is then tracked as an individual object by birth location and compared only to itself. This answers GP-AUD-010 §3's $\kappa$ objection directly: no global fold coefficient is used and no lifetime-to-separation conversion is made. That objection was correct against CL-ERR-013; it does not reach this test.


8 fields, reference $n=1024$, match tolerance one coarse cell.

________________


What this settles
1. Every short-bar lifetime statistic I have produced is unreliable. CL-DER-011's $\hat a\approx0.205$ was fitted on windows $\ell\le0.025$ that include bars whose lifetimes carry hundreds of percent error. CL-DER-012's $s$, already withdrawn in CL-ERR-013, was worse. GP-AUD-010 §7 was right that CL-DER-011 is not a verified negative result, and this supplies the mechanism.


2. The below-floor population is real but cannot support the divergent-density reading. CL-ERR-013 observed the fraction below $h^2$ staying at 26–30% while $h^2$ fell 64-fold and suggested a genuine divergent lifetime density. The bars are genuinely there — that half was right. But since their lifetimes are unmeasurable, no statement about the lifetime density can rest on them. GP-AUD-010 §6 was right to call the explanatory sentence stronger than the evidence.


3. Sub-grid reconstruction is mandatory, not an improvement. GP-AUD-010 §8.2 asked for continuum maxima and saddles by interpolation and Newton refinement. This quantifies why. Extrapolating ~1 decade of error reduction per doubling, reaching 10% accuracy on $\ell\sim10^{-4}$ bars from $n=512$ would need roughly $n\approx3600$ — about 13M pixels per field. Sub-grid saddle reconstruction is far cheaper than brute refinement, and there is no route to short-bar statistics without one.


4. A concrete trust boundary, measured rather than assumed. At $n=1024$ ($h=0.023$), lifetimes are trustworthy to ~1% only for $\ell\gtrsim10^{-2}$ — roughly $\ell \gtrsim 20,h^2$. That is an empirical replacement for the crude $\ell>h^2$ floor CL-ERR-013 used and GP-AUD-010 §3 correctly criticised. It is a diagnostic, not a constant.

§4 — My pre-committed readings were mis-specified
M1 said converge and match ⇒ real. M2 said wander or fail to match ⇒ discretization. The actual outcome was match at 89.6% and wander by 27× — high match rate with catastrophic value error. Neither reading covers it, and the script's own adjudicator printed M3: INCONCLUSIVE because I gave it a false dichotomy.


The lesson is not that the test failed. The test worked; my framing of the outcome space was wrong. Existence of a topological feature and accuracy of its numerical value are independent properties, and I collapsed them into one axis when freezing the readings.


I am recording this rather than quietly relabelling the outcome, because the value of pre-commitment depends entirely on not editing it afterward. A pre-registration that omits the outcome that actually occurs is a defect in the pre-registration, and should be reported as one.
