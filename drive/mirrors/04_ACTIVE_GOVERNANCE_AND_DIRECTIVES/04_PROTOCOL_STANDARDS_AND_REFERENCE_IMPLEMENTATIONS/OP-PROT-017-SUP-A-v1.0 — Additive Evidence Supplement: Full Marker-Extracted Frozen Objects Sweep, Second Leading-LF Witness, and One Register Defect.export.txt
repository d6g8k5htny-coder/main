2. FACT E3 NOW HOLDS OVER TWENTY-ONE OF TWENTY-ONE


Every marker-extracted frozen object on this Drive contains exactly ONE line equal to its BEGIN token and exactly ONE equal to its END token. The fail-closed uniqueness requirement of rule N4 therefore breaks nothing that currently exists. This was the one place OP-PROT-017 could have been non-conservative, and it is now closed against the full register rather than against a ten-object sample.


3. FINDING S-1 — DEFECT B HAS A SECOND WITNESS


OP-PROT-017 Section 3 recorded S2-DER-010 as the sole object distinguishing preserve-leading from strip-leading. GP-DER-197-v1.0 is a second, independent witness: its frozen body also begins with two LF characters, and stripping them yields 13,795 bytes against a declared 13,797. The leading-strip family now scores 19 of 21 rather than 9 of 10.


This matters because a single witness invites the reading that S2-DER-010 is anomalous and could be normalized away. Two witnesses in unrelated document families show that leading LF is a recurring authoring pattern, not an accident, and that preserve-leading is the correct general rule.


4. FINDING S-2 — SUBSTRING MATCHING IS WORSE THAN UNIFORMLY WRONG


OP-PROT-017 reported substring matching at 0 of 10. Over the full 21 it scores 4 of 21. The four it gets right are GP-DER-118-v1.9, GP-DATA-236, GP-DER-118-v1.9-B and GP-DATA-234, each of which names its marker token exactly once, at the delimiter line itself, and has no leading LF.


The corrected characterization is therefore sharper and more alarming than the original. Substring matching is not a rule that always fails and is thus easy to detect. It is a rule that silently succeeds on about one object in five and produces a 6 to 737 byte fragment of prose on the rest. An implementer who tests against the GP-DER-118 family alone will conclude their extractor is correct and will then mis-verify seventeen other objects. GP-DER-197-v1.0 is the extreme case: its BEGIN token occurs three times as a substring and a first-occurrence search returns 737 bytes of header prose that hashes cleanly and looks like a real answer.

6. DEFECT DEF-LSDER034-TOKENS — REGISTER DECLARES MARKER TOKENS THAT DO NOT EXIST IN THE OBJECT


Severity: MEDIUM. Class: register metadata defect, not body drift. Status: OPEN, repair specified.


Measured. The Frozen Objects row for LS-DER-034-v1.0 declares its Frozen Body Rule as
    BEGIN_TBG3A_INTERP_FROZEN_BODY / END_TBG3A_INTERP_FROZEN_BODY
The document at Drive ID 1_KF28jr2RS8ySFrealYRVuxARY2EHyCEaByGb36rGnk contains neither token. Its actual delimiters are
    BEGIN_TBG3A_INTERPOLATION_FROZEN_BODY / END_TBG3A_INTERPOLATION_FROZEN_BODY
The register cell abbreviates INTERPOLATION to INTERP.


Consequence. A verifier that follows the register cell verbatim, which is the documented purpose of that cell, finds zero candidate marker lines. Under OP-PROT-017 rule N4 it returns a hard failure and records an extraction-ambiguity defect, which is correct behaviour and is how this was found. Under the older permissive wordings it would return nothing to compare and could be misread as an unverifiable object rather than a metadata error.


The object itself is sound. With its true tokens LS-DER-034-v1.0 reproduces exactly 8,910 bytes / e2144a8423439076ee6bd0bdec301a224fa20acdf367b8cf2136f977054a6dad, matching its declared receipt. No body drift, no identity change, no successor to the theorem object required.


Repair. One cell edit in the Frozen Objects register, replacing INTERP with INTERPOLATION in both tokens. Specified as an item in CL-CLWO-20260727-03. Rollback is the inverse edit. No scientific status is affected.


Why this is the headline result of the sweep. It is the first defect this Drive has found by applying a fail-closed extraction rule rather than by a human noticing. Every previous verification run reproduced this object successfully because runs read the tokens from the document's own header instead of from the register cell, which silently masked the discrepancy. The register was wrong for at least a day while every check passed.
