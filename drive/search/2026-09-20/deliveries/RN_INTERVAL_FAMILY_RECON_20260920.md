# RN interval-family reconnaissance — 2026-09-20

Scope: extend the repository’s fixed exact affine Gaussian determinant moments to exact rational coefficient intervals for uncertain means and covariance. This is an implementation candidate, not a novelty or scientific status claim.

Three bounded primary-source queries were run before implementation: TU Hamburg/Rump interval inclusion and polynomial coefficients; NIST Bernstein convex-hull bounds (no useful new result); arXiv Gaussian moments/Stein/Mamis. Sources inspected: [Rump, Verification methods: rigorous results using floating-point arithmetic, Acta Numerica 2010](https://www.tuhh.de/ti3/rump/intlab/ActaNumerica2010.pdf), especially inclusion and dependency sections 5–6; [Mamis, multivariate normal moments](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.00189v6). The previously inspected Bernstein literature and the repository’s explicit basis-conversion proof remain reusable. This bounded search does not establish literature completeness or priority.

Rump explains why interval operations enclose expressions but repeated uncertain quantities can greatly widen them. Mamis supplies the established Gaussian moment/Stein context. Our elementary argument will induct over the same Gaussian recurrence and use exact interval polynomial arithmetic; then a rational linear conversion to Bernstein coefficients gives a full mark-interval enclosure. Complete dyadic subdivisions must retain both children. Singleton coefficient intervals must agree exactly with the existing fixed-law engine.

Covariance feasibility is a separate obligation. A symmetric interval matrix is not automatically a family of Gaussian laws. The sufficient all-PSD criterion will require each diagonal lower bound to exceed the sum of off-diagonal absolute upper bounds. Its proof follows directly from twice the absolute cross product being bounded by the sum of squares. If this sufficient test fails, the enclosure quantifies only PSD members; a PSD midpoint proves nonemptiness but never proves all members PSD. An unverified midpoint gives no nonemptiness claim.

RN5 already uses Arb interval spatial calculations, grouped spatial monomials and positive interval Cholesky pivots. This generic family implementation does not replace those obligations, identify its actual field law, or cover the annulus.
