## 4. Hypotheses, in the object's own terms

1. X is partitioned into disjoint nonempty blocks X_i; each A_i is a decreasing family on X_i; G is ANY finite simple graph on the block indices.
2. D = { U : U intersect X_i lies in A_i for every i, and the set of indices i whose block U meets is independent in G } - definition (D1).
3. The cross-block lifting is COMPLETE: for every macro-edge ij, EVERY pair uv with u in X_i and v in X_j is forbidden. The object states that completeness is essential and that a few cross edges will not do.
4. After separating genuine singleton-forbidden original coordinates, each ACTUAL local restriction admits a P14 scalar sandwich of width at most kappa, with kappa >= 1. Then L = ceil(408 kappa) and K = 256 L.
5. Coordinates are selected independently with arbitrary p_v, and prices satisfy z_v <= phi(p_v). The consumed P14-A low-failure result is stated at z <= 2p and the consumed P11-A result at c_i <= 2q_i; the bridge is phi(t) <= 2t, which P11-A states in its definitions and P15-D does not restate.
6. For the uncapped (D3) bound: there are no singleton forbidders and Q = 1 - mu_p(D) <= 2/3.
7. P14-A's low-failure result: at prices z <= 2p and Q_i <= 2/3, a cover of A_i^(L) of cost at most Q_i/2 exists.
8. P11-A Theorem A: at Q_G <= 2/3 and prices c <= 2q, a 128-colouring has monochromatic-edge cost at most (85/96) Q_G, its generators being the actual monochromatic graph edges.
9. An empty generator costs one and is distinct from an empty family, which costs zero.

## 5. Reconstructed argument

I rebuilt the cost allocation end to end.

D2, the local side. Since every local failure is a global failure, Q_i <= Q, so P14-A's low-failure branch applies and each block has a cover of A_i^(L) costing at most Q_i/2. The local good events live on disjoint blocks and are therefore independent, and D is contained in the intersection of the local-good events, so mu_p(D) <= product of (1 - Q_i). Using x <= -log(1-x) termwise, sum of Q_i <= -log(product) <= -log mu_p(D) = H. The local covers therefore cost at most H/2.

D3, the occupancy side. q_i = P(U_i nonempty) = 1 - product over v in X_i of (1 - p_v); these indicators are independent across blocks under the original product law. The nonempty event in block i is covered by the family of all its singleton coordinates, at price sum of z_v over X_i, and the empty generator replaces it when that exceeds one; so c_i = min(1, sum z_v) <= min(1, sum of -log(1-p_v)) = min(1, -log(1-q_i)) = phi(q_i). I checked that identity: sum over X_i of -log(1-p_v) is exactly -log of the product, which is -log(1-q_i). No conditioning on local goodness happens anywhere, and c_i is a price, not q_i. Then Q_G, the probability that the occupied macro-indices contain a G-edge, is at most Q: if blocks i and j are both occupied and ij is a macro-edge, complete lifting makes some cross pair inside U forbidden, so U is globally bad. This is the step that needs completeness.

D4, the macro cover. I compared the compressed restatement against the attached P11-A bytes clause by clause and it is faithful: peel vertices of current weighted neighbourhood mass above 2; the disjoint first-selected core events give 1 - product over the core of (1 - q_i) <= 7 Q_G / 6; a chord bound for the convex function of Q_G on [0, 2/3] gives the core mass sum below 5 Q_G / 2 (the chord is taken in Q_G, not in the intermediate quantity, and at Q_G = 2/3 it is an equality, so it is legitimate on the whole range); at prices at most 2q the core price sum is at most 5 Q_G, and 32 colours give monochromatic cost at most (5 Q_G)^2 / 64, which is at most 25 Q_G / 96 exactly because Q_G <= 2/3; the residual has weighted degree at most 2, so the second-moment bound of P11-A section 1 gives p-edge mass at most 5 Q_G / (1 - Q_G) <= 15 Q_G, price inflation multiplies by at most 4, and 96 colours give 5 Q_G / 8; crossing core/residual edges cannot be monochromatic because the two palettes are disjoint; 25/96 + 5/8 = 85/96 and 32 + 96 = 128. Then the binary refinement: split each of the 128 classes independently in two and keep only edges whose endpoints agree. A monochromatic edge survives with probability one half, so the expected retained cost is (85/192) Q_G and some deterministic refinement achieves it. Lifting a macro edge ij to all pairs of one occupancy generator from each block costs exactly c_i c_j because the supports are disjoint.

D5, assembly. If U avoids every local L-generator and every lifted macro generator, its occupied indices are 256-colourable and each U_i splits into at most L good local parts. Give each occupied block its macro colour and pair it with the local part colour: at most 256 L global parts, each of whose occupied index sets is a subset of one macro colour class and hence G-independent, and each of whose block fragments is locally good. By (D1) every such part lies in D.

D6, the budget. (1/2) sum Q_i <= H/2 and (85/192) Q_G <= (85/192) Q <= (85/192) H, using Q <= H because -log x >= 1 - x. Total 1/2 + 85/192 = 181/192 < 1, so the cover is a no-dilution cover. If Q > 2/3 then H > -log(1/3) > 1, the cap is 1, and the empty generator supplies it. At kappa = 1, K = 256 * 408 = 104448.
