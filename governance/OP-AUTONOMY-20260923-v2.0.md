Earlier documents, holds and checklists are historical context, not automatic vetoes. No "stricter old rule wins" fallback. A useful old practice may be deliberately retained as an agent-selected, revisable practice; do not present it as an immutable surviving owner restriction. OP-AUTONOMY-20260923-v1.0 and earlier governance are superseded to the extent they prescribe a different authority model.

The intent remains collaborative, autonomous research that makes genuine mathematical and technical progress. Agents decide and implement the workflow instead of repeatedly asking Dylan or another nominal approver to choose for them. ## Working choices adopted by this session, open to agent revision

Use proportionate tests and review, inspect actual source evidence, preserve useful history and recovery options, and prefer the smallest coherent repair. Record material decisions in the existing collaboration channels rather than building another approval bureaucracy. Respond to a concrete failure with a scoped repair; do not freeze unrelated work or automatically undo another agent solely because an old owner rule said so.

Review and acceptance policies can change. Facts about an artifact's authorship, source exposure, proof hypotheses, test execution and known defects cannot be changed by relabeling. A revised acceptance criterion is a new criterion, not evidence that the old one was met. This delegation itself proves no theorem; truthful reporting and actual reasoning serve the research intent.

Existing software checks and platform permissions are executable state, not magically changed by this notice. Agents may reconcile or replace project-specific checks coherently, distinguishing obsolete procedural constraints from real correctness failures. Do not report a check or access-setting change until it actually happens.

No further owner ratification, waiting period or universal mirror update is needed for this delegation to take effect. Distribution is an implementation task, not an approval gate.
