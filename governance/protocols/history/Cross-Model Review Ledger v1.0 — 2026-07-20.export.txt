ENTRY CM-003 — 02_GAUSSIAN_TRANSVERSALITY.md


Reviewer: ChatGPT — GPT-5.6 Thinking

Overall verdict:
This is a clear and potentially valuable reviewer-facing restatement of the existing SARD-G closure chain. It is not, on the evidence inspected, a new independent proof route. Its central architecture matches the earlier record:


• local tracking of an ordered saddle pair and chosen branches;
• a scalar mismatch functional on a transverse section;
• a Cameron–Martin derivative represented by an interior curve distribution plus endpoint jet atoms;
• RKHS noncancellation through kernel injectivity and support separation;
• selection of a nonzero direction from a countable dense Cameron–Martin family;
• one-dimensional Gaussian slicing/Fubini to make regular zero sets null;
• a countable chart cover for the global connection event.


Strong reusable core:
The mismatch-functional formulation, adjoint variation, support-separation argument, and countable-direction strategy are mathematically meaningful and well suited to an external referee document.


Corrections and obligations before reviewer-facing promotion:


A. TORUS FOURIER FORMULA
The manuscript is written for a periodized field on a torus, whose spectrum is discrete. The RKHS injectivity identity should be expressed as a Fourier series sum over k in Z², not as a continuous planar integral over ξ. On the torus, if every positive spectral weight multiplies a zero Fourier coefficient, then all Fourier coefficients of the distribution vanish and therefore the distribution is zero. A planar Paley–Wiener step is unnecessary for the fixed-torus theorem.


B. TRANSVERSE-INTERSECTION WORDING
For stable and unstable one-dimensional invariant manifolds of the same autonomous flow, any intersection point lies on a common orbit and both tangent lines contain the flow vector. Therefore an index-one saddle-to-saddle connection is nontransverse. The statement that “ordinary transverse intersections” of those one-dimensional manifolds are allowed should be removed or carefully reformulated.


C. ABSTRACT WIENER DECOMPOSITION
The slicing step must not merely write f = g + ξh. For a normalized h in the Cameron–Martin space, the proof should identify the corresponding first-chaos Gaussian coordinate, establish an independent residual Gaussian element, and specify the measurable version of the mismatch function along that affine line. This is the exact bridge between Cameron–Martin differentiation and conditional Gaussian nullity.


D. ENDPOINT DERIVATIVE FORMULA
The manuscript omits the full endpoint coefficients and says the theorem may be treated as conditional on the displayed derivative structure. That honesty should be retained. External promotion requires either the explicit symbolic formula with a functional-analytic differentiability justification or a precise citation that supplies it in the needed topology.


E. COUNTABLE CHART COVER AND MEASURABILITY
The chart construction must prove that every connection is captured by at least one chart with rationally encoded bounds; that saddle/branch labels are measurable; that crossing times remain finite and transverse within the chart; and that the local mismatch maps are jointly measurable in the residual field and line coordinate.


F. EXCEPTIONAL PARAMETERS
The proof must explicitly dispose of parameters at which saddles lose hyperbolicity, leave their tracking boxes, crossings cease to be first/transverse, or branch labels change. It is not enough to say these occur at isolated values without stating the analytic or transversality lemma that guarantees this on almost every Gaussian line.


G. LINEAGE
The file should cite itself as a derivative exposition of SARD-G, not as a newly discovered proof. The governing status remains the C041–C046/Q0_MASTER status: internally program-grade closed, with external referee review still pending.


Recommended label:
“Reviewer-facing SARD-G exposition; corrections pending; no new canonical theorem.”


Highest-value next deliverables:
1. A fixed-torus RKHS injectivity lemma using discrete Fourier coefficients.
2. An abstract-Wiener one-dimensional disintegration lemma stated in the exact function space used by the field.
3. A complete countable-chart and exceptional-parameter proposition.
4. A crosswalk table mapping every paragraph of 02_GAUSSIAN_TRANSVERSALITY.md to its C041–C046 source or identifying it as genuinely new.
