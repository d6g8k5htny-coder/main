## 1. Keep the contact tail in the exact identity

Write F(rho,ell) for the actual elder density from endpoint distances at least
rho. Let r_* > 0 be the common admissible radius r_0^* of #198, and shrink the
fixed lifetime threshold ell_* if necessary. Put

    Abar_0(k) = integral_R integral_S^(d-1) A_0(b,k,u) d sigma(u) db,
    T(rho,ell) = integral_rho^infinity r^(-2) Abar_0(ell/r^3) dr.

This is the formal contact-kernel tail. Its integration to infinity is part
of the contact coefficient identity; it is not a claim that the torus has
unbounded physical separation. The ordinary sphere measure is used, without
a role-order factor 1/2.

For 0 < rho <= r_* and 0 < ell <= ell_*, the proof of #198 W.2 gives the
**signed identity**

    nu_cand^(near,rho)(ell)
      = c ell^(-1/3) - T(rho,ell) + E(rho,ell),
    |E(rho,ell)| <= C[min(ell^(1/4),rho) + rho^3].           (1.1)

This is stronger information than the displayed absolute upper bound W.2:
that bound replaced T by C ell^2 rho^(-7). Here it is retained. To check
uniformity, split its difference integral at r_1=ell^(1/4). On
(0,min(r_1,rho)), #191 E.1 gives |r^(-2)(A_r-A_0)| <= H(b,k), and the
integral is O(min(r_1,rho)). Above r_1, W.1 and R5 give
C(ell^2 r^(-8)+r^2) after integrating b,u, whose integral is
O(ell^(1/4)+rho^3). The second interval is empty if rho <= r_1. These are
constants independent of both rho and ell on their stated domain.

The marked measure identity and W.3 give

    F = nu_eld - nu_cand^(near,rho) + rho_rej^(near,rho),
    0 <= rho_rej^(near,rho) <= C(ell^(1/4)+rho^3).

Combining with R++, nu_eld=c ell^(-1/3)+O(ell^(1/4)), proves

    |F(rho,ell)-T(rho,ell)| <= C(ell^(1/4)+rho^3).         (1.2)

The signs in (1.1)--(1.2) are essential: omitting candidate contact mass
appears with a positive sign in the far actual-elder density. This argument
uses the actual elder mark, not a candidate-only estimate promoted to an
elder assertion.

## 2. The positive contact coefficient and a uniform two-parameter estimate

Use the notation of P §15: G=grad f, V_u=H_f u, A_u the transverse Hessian,
tau_u^2=Var(partial_u^3 f | G=0), and
D_u=E[(det A_u)^2 1{A_u<0} | V_u=0]. Define

    a_0 = 432 integral_S^(d-1)
          p_G(0) p_(V_u)(0) D_u phi_(tau_u)(0) d sigma(u).       (2.1)

Here phi_tau is the centered scalar Gaussian density with standard deviation
tau. P's finite-jet rank and compactness over frames imply positive finite
p_G(0), p_(V_u)(0), D_u and a positive lower bound for tau_u. Hence
0 < a_0 < infinity. The transverse conditional Gaussian has full support on
Sym(d-1); the negative-definite cone contains an open set where det squared
is strictly positive. This supplies positivity, not just an upper envelope.
No rotational isotropy or evaluated numerical constant is used.

P's odd/even factorization and birth disintegration give exactly

    Abar_0(k) = 432 k^2 integral_S^(d-1)
                 p_G(0)p_(V_u)(0)D_u phi_(tau_u)(12k) d sigma(u).

Because tau_u is bounded below, phi_(tau_u)(12k)
=phi_(tau_u)(0)+O(k^2) uniformly in u for 0<=k<=1. Thus

    Abar_0(k) = a_0 k^2 + O(k^4),  0<=k<=1.              (2.2)

The factor 432 is 12*(6k)^2/k^2. Birth is already integrated out; no extra
height, direction or role-order normalization is inserted.

For ell/rho^3<=1, (2.2) can be integrated directly for all r>=rho:

    T(rho,ell) = (a_0/7) ell^2 rho^(-7)
                  + O(ell^4 rho^(-13)).                 (2.3)

Equivalently, the exact substitution k=ell/r^3 gives
T=(ell^(-1/3)/3) integral_0^(ell/rho^3) Abar_0(k) k^(-2/3) dk,
with coefficient (1/3)*(3/7)=1/7. The remainder integrates r^(-14),
giving rho^(-13)/13 inside its constant.

Consequently there is C=C(d,L, imported source bounds) such that

    |F(rho,ell) - (a_0/7) ell^2 rho^(-7)|
      <= C[ell^(1/4)+rho^3+ell^4 rho^(-13)]               (2.4)

whenever 0<rho<=r_* and 0<ell<=min(ell_*,rho^3). This is a uniform
two-parameter estimate, not a substitution into a fixed-rho little-o.

## 3. Simultaneous shrinking-cutoff asymptotic and its boundary

Let rho(ell)>0 satisfy

    ell^(1/3)/rho(ell) -> 0,
    rho(ell)/ell^(1/4) -> 0.                             (3.1)

Then eventually rho<=r_* and ell<=rho^3. Dividing (2.4) by its positive
main term gives a relative error bounded by a constant times

    rho^7 ell^(-7/4) + rho^10 ell^(-2) + ell^2 rho^(-6).  (3.2)

The first term is (rho/ell^(1/4))^7 and the third is (ell/rho^3)^2.
The second equals ell^(1/2)(rho/ell^(1/4))^10. All tend to zero under
(3.1). Therefore

    F(rho(ell),ell) ~ (a_0/7) ell^2 rho(ell)^(-7).        (3.3)

In particular, for fixed eta>0 and 1/4<a<1/3,

    F(eta ell^a,ell) ~ a_0/(7 eta^7) ell^(2-7a).         (3.4)

Its relative error is
O(ell^(7a-7/4)+ell^(10a-2)+ell^(2-6a)). The inequalities a>1/4 and
a<1/3 are strict and load-bearing. At a=1/4 the cusp-size remainder can
match the proposed main term; at a=1/3 the contact k cutoff no longer tends
to zero. Neither endpoint is included in (3.4).

Within this interval, (3.4) gives absolute density decay for a<2/7, a
strictly positive finite limit a_0/(7 eta^7) at a=2/7, and divergence for
a>2/7. In all three cases it is negligible relative to the full leading
density c ell^(-1/3), since their ratio is asymptotic to
(a_0/(7c eta^7)) ell^(7/3-7a), which tends to zero.

Thus **all-order far-density decay fails in this simultaneous range** for
the stated actual-model source interface: it is not even O(ell) there,
because 2-7a<1/4<1 and a_0>0. This is compatible with #188's fixed-cutoff
Theorem G and its much slower, existential diagonal cutoff. It does not
settle all-order decay for the slower algebraic range 0<a<=1/4.

## 4. Common cutoff for all short bars; lifetime-dependent cutoff is different

Let N_(t,rho) count actual finite bars on the whole torus with 0<ell<=t
and endpoint separation at least the one common radius rho. The marked
counting identity gives E N_(t,rho)=L^d integral_0^t F(rho,ell) d ell.

For 0<rho<=r_* and 0<t<=min(ell_*,rho^3), (2.4) holds uniformly throughout this lifetime
integral. Integrating its three errors gives

    E N_(t,rho)/L^d
      = (a_0/21)t^3 rho^(-7)
          + O(t^(5/4)+t rho^3+t^5 rho^(-13)).            (4.1)

For any common cutoff rho(t) with t^(1/3)<<rho(t)<<t^(1/4), the relative
errors have exactly the form (3.2) with t in place of ell. Hence

    E N_(t,rho(t)) ~ L^d (a_0/21)t^3 rho(t)^(-7).        (4.2)

In particular rho(t)=eta t^a gives L^d a_0/(21 eta^7)t^(3-7a).
The coefficient 1/21 is (1/7)*integral_0^1 y^2 dy. No monotonicity of
rho(t) is needed: at each t it is held fixed through the lifetime integral.

By contrast, counting each bar with its own lifetime-dependent rule
r>=eta ell^a gives, from (3.4),

    E N_lifetime(t)
       ~ L^d a_0/[7 eta^7(3-7a)] t^(3-7a).             (4.3)

The two counts have different coefficients. The latter integral is finite
because 2-7a>-1 in the stated range. A uniform small-lifetime relative
bound from (3.4) justifies integrating its asymptotic; no differentiation
of a cumulative statement is used.

Equations (4.1)--(4.3) are expected counts only. Markov supplies an occurrence
upper bound, but a matching occurrence probability, singleton conditional
law, field-first mark law or rare-event total-variation theorem would need
separate relative cluster/mark control. Those claims are not made here.
