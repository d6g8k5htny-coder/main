## Proposition J: joint tail geometry, including lifetime bias

Under T4's limiting actual-bar law let \(Y=\ell/t\), \(K=\ell/r^3\), and \(V=(Y/K)^{1/3}\). Conditional on \(V>a\), set \(Z=V/a>1\). As \(a\to\infty\),
\[
\mathcal L(Y,Z,b,u\mid V>a)
\ \xrightarrow{\rm TV}\
(3y^2\,dy)\otimes(7z^{-8}\,dz)\otimes H_0(db,du),
\quad 0<y<1,\quad z>1,
\]
with TV error \(O(a^{-6})\), at fixed d,L.

Thus the tail-conditioned lifetime is Beta(3,1), rather than the unconditioned T4 density \((2/3)y^{-1/3}\). The separation overshoot is Pareto with exponent 7; it is independent of the normalized lifetime and birth-direction mark in this limit. Birth and direction need not be independent of each other. The tail mark has the zero-cubic Gaussian weighting \(H_0\), not an assumed uniform direction law.

**Proof.** P(15.1) gives
\[
A_0(b,k,u)=432k^2 B(b,u)\phi_{\tau_u}(12k).
\]
Consequently T4's joint density is
\[
f(y,b,k,u)=\frac{96}{c}y^{-1/3}k^{4/3}
 B(b,u)\phi_{\tau_u}(12k).
\]
On \(V>a\), the exact change of coordinates \(k=y/(a^3z^3)\) has absolute Jacobian \(3y/(a^3z^4)\). Its unnormalized tail density becomes
\[
h_a(y,z,b,u)=\frac{288}{c}a^{-7}y^2z^{-8}
 B(b,u)\phi_{\tau_u}\!\left(\frac{12y}{a^3z^3}\right).
\]
Replacing the last Gaussian density by its value at zero gives mass
\[
p_a^0=\frac{288}{c}a^{-7}W
 \left(\int_0^1y^2dy\right)\left(\int_1^\infty z^{-8}dz\right)
 =\frac{96W}{7c}a^{-7}.
\]
Its normalized density is exactly the product in J. Moreover
\[
\frac{\phi_{\tau_u}(12y/(a^3z^3))}{\phi_{\tau_u}(0)}
=\exp\!\left(-\frac{72y^2}{\tau_u^2a^6z^6}\right).
\]
For a>=1, this ratio lies between \(e^{-72/(\tau_{\min}^2a^6)}\) and 1, uniformly in every displayed variable. Thus both the unnormalized density and its normalization differ relatively by \(O(a^{-6})\). Dividing by their positive masses proves the TV assertion. This argument also recovers \(p_a=P(V>a)=(96W/(7c))a^{-7}(1+O(a^{-6}))\), consistently with T5, but the new conclusion is the joint conditioned law. All integrations are finite by W<infinity and the displayed y,z integrals. No unbounded moment convergence is used. QED.

## Proposition F: transfer to fields, with the limits in the correct order

Use d=2 and additionally assume C52's exact O1 and mean asymptotic. Let N_t be the total number of actual short bars, m_t=E N_t, and \(B_t=E[N_t;N_t\ge2]=o(m_t)\). For a fixed a>0, call a bar elongated if \(\ell\le t\) and \(r>a t^{1/3}\). Let N_t^a count these bars.

First let t decrease to zero with a fixed. Then
\[
E N_t^a/m_t\longrightarrow p_a,\qquad
P(N_t^a>0)/m_t\longrightarrow p_a,\qquad
P(N_t^a\ge2\mid N_t^a>0)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Condition a FIELD on \(N_t^a>0\), then choose an elongated bar uniformly in that field. Its law of \((\ell/t,r/(a t^{1/3}),b,u)\) converges in TV to the law in J before taking a to infinity. Therefore the iterated limit t->0, then a->infinity is exactly J's product law.

**Proof.** T4's pushforward gives TV convergence of the normalized actual-bar intensity on the true \((Y,V,b,u)\) marks; assigning the true marks on its far/cemetery sector changes the law by at most its vanishing mass. Since p_a>0, restriction to V>a and normalization preserve convergence for each fixed a. This proves the first display's expectation statement. Pathwise
\[
0\le E N_t^a-P(N_t^a>0)
 =E[(N_t^a-1)_+]\le B_t,
\qquad
2P(N_t^a\ge2)\le B_t.
\]
Divide by m_t and then the positive limit p_a to prove the occurrence/singleton claims. C52's size-bias identity applies to this restricted population itself: its field-first mark law differs from its normalized intensity law by at most
\[
E[(N_t^a-1)_+]/E N_t^a\le B_t/E N_t^a\to0.
\]
This is stronger than merely conditioning an independently selected bar on elongation: the field is conditioned on containing an elongated bar. The restricted identity proves that precise convention. QED.

## Moving thresholds: the relative error that a consumer must supply

Let I_t be the true marked normalized actual-bar intensity, I_0 its T4 pushforward limit, and define
\[
\delta_t=\operatorname{TV}(I_t,I_0),\qquad
\varepsilon_t=\delta_t+B_t/m_t\longrightarrow0.
\]
For any deterministic a(t)->infinity satisfying
\[
\varepsilon_t\,a(t)^7\longrightarrow0,
\]
the preceding conclusions extend jointly:
\[
P(N_t^{a(t)}>0)
 \sim m_t\,\frac{96W}{7c}\,a(t)^{-7}
 \sim L^2\frac32c\,t^{2/3}\frac{96W}{7c}\,a(t)^{-7},
\]
and the field-first elongated mark law converges in TV to J.

Indeed |E N_t^a/m_t-p_a|<=delta_t for every a, because TV controls all measurable events simultaneously. Hence \(E N_t^a/m_t\sim p_a\) under the displayed condition. Restriction/normalization gives conditional TV \(O(\delta_t/p_a)\); the restricted size-bias error is at most \(B_t/E N_t^a=O((B_t/m_t)/p_a)\). Both vanish, and J supplies its separate \(O(a^{-6})\) error. The mark map Z=V/a depends on t but pushforward still contracts TV. This proves the joint claims without exchanging two unquantified limits.

An admissible threshold EXISTS: for all sufficiently small t choose
\[
a(t)=\min\{t^{-1/6},\varepsilon_t^{-1/14}\},
\]
interpreting the second entry as infinity if epsilon_t=0. Both entries tend to infinity and \(\varepsilon_t a(t)^7\le\sqrt{\varepsilon_t}\). This choice also retains \(a(t)t^{1/3}\le t^{1/6}\to0\). It is existential and depends on the unknown error; it is not a numerical cutoff, an explicit algebraic rate, or a uniform assertion for every growing a(t). Neither T nor O supplies a rate for epsilon_t, so a prescribed power threshold is not licensed.

**Limitation counterexample (abstract marks, not a Gaussian-field construction).** Absolute TV convergence plus perfect single-bar occurrence does not by itself justify a growing threshold. Choose any a_t->infinity, set eta_t=a_t^(-3), and replace I_0 by
\[
\widetilde I_t=(1-\eta_t)I_0+\eta_t\delta_{(Y,V,b,u)=(1/2,\,2a_t,\,0,\,u_0)}.
\]
Then TV(tilde I_t,I_0)<=eta_t->0, but its V>a_t tail is asymptotic to eta_t, which dominates I_0's order a_t^-7 tail. Conditional on that tail, Y converges to the atom at1/2, not Beta(3,1), and Z converges to2, not Pareto7. Realize this abstract population by a Bernoulli occurrence event of probability m_t=Ct^(2/3), with exactly one mark drawn from tilde I_t when it occurs. Then B_t=0 and all singleton/size-bias conclusions hold, but the moving-tail claim still fails. Thus even perfect occurrence isolation cannot replace the missing relative-error control. This falsifies that logical shortcut, not any cited Gaussian theorem.
