## 2. Fix the law before counting

Fix d=2 and L>0. On the flat torus X=R²/(LZ²), use unnormalized physical area and its usual global orthonormal frame. Let f have the smooth, centered, variance-one Gaussian law with covariance

    K_L(z) = sum_(n in Z²) exp(-|z+Ln|²/2)
             / sum_(n in Z²) exp(-|Ln|²/2).

Its real Fourier coefficients have strictly positive spectral weights and decay faster than every polynomial. The corresponding random field is a measurable Gaussian element of the separable Banach space E=C²(X), with finite moments of every needed C^q norm. For example, absolute summability of the L² norms of the Fourier terms in C^q gives the required convergence and moment bounds. These are the exact source-field hypotheses; no Fourier cutoff, independent Hessian-entry model or finite grid replaces them.

Fix r>0 small enough that the two pin sites a=-(r/2)Re1 and c=(r/2)Re1 are distinct on X, for R in O(2). Define the six real pin observations and their target by

    P_r f = (f(a), grad f(a), f(c), grad f(c)),
    p = (b,0,0,b-k r³,0,0),
    Q = law(f | P_r f=p).

Use the canonical whole-field Gaussian regression version for every p. All statements through Section5 are for this fixed r,b,k,R. No conditioning on a probability-zero event is left to an arbitrary version. Write m=E_Q f and C_Q for the centered conditional covariance. The means are retained everywhere.

For a real symmetric two-by-two matrix H, put F_j(H)=|det H| when H is nonsingular with j negative eigenvalues, and zero otherwise. The typed weight is

    W(f) = F_2(H_a) F_1(H_c),
    Z = E_Q W,
    dQ^W = (W/Z) dQ,             provided 0<Z<infinity.       (1)

Sections3–5 in fact allow any nonnegative Borel W on E with that finite positive normalizer. The typed W is continuous, including at singular matrices, because the determinant vanishes at the index boundary. Finiteness follows from Gaussian Hessian moments; its positive small-r lower bound is a separate source premise in Section6. An upper bound on |det H_a det H_c| by itself supplies no lower bound for this typed Z.

Let U=X\{a,c} and

    Omega = {(x,y) in U x U: x != y},
    Y_f(x,y) = (grad f(x),grad f(y)) in R⁴,
    Delta_f(x,y) = |det H_x det H_y|.                         (2)

For any Borel D contained in Omega and any nonnegative Borel function i:Omega x E -> [0,infinity], define the ordered marked count

    C_(D,i)(f) = sum_((x,y) in D: grad f(x)=grad f(y)=0) i(x,y,f).  (3)

Both sites are actual critical points and are different from one another and the pins. This is a count over field realizations; it is not a name for a spatial integral. For the probability bounds below i is an indicator. For general nonnegative marks the sum may be infinite.

Throughout, extended nonnegative products use the convention 0 times infinity = 0. Equivalently W i and W C are the increasing limits of products of their finite truncations. Thus (3), (6), (8) and (9) are defined even when a mark or count is infinite on a field where W vanishes; the exceptional-field convention below is a separate matter.

## 3. Local nondegeneracy and a canonical conditional field

At each t=(x,y) in Omega, the covariance Sigma_t=Cov_Q(Y(t)) is positive definite. To check this, suppose a linear combination of the four witness gradients had zero conditional variance. In the original law it would equal a linear combination of the six pin observations in Gaussian L². Their difference is a distribution supported at the four distinct sites a,c,x,y, consisting of point evaluations and first derivatives. Zero variance under a strictly positive Fourier spectrum forces every Fourier coefficient of that distribution to vanish, hence the distribution is zero. Distributions supported at distinct sites, and the point/first-derivative distributions at each fixed site, are linearly independent. The witness coefficients therefore vanish. This proves the assertion for the exact infinite law, not only a truncated covariance matrix.

Consequently, on each compact subset K of Omega the smallest eigenvalue has a positive minimum. The density p_t of Y(t) under Q is continuous and uniformly bounded there. No bound uniform in r, or across a pin or witness diagonal, is asserted by this compactness argument.

Construct a further conditional law on E, under Q, explicitly. Put

    B_t v = Cov_Q(f,Y(t)) Sigma_t^(-1) v,
    Phi_(t,v)(f) = f + B_t(v-Y_f(t)),
    K(t,v,.) = law_Q(Phi_(t,v)(f)).                            (4)

The covariance in B_t is centered; equivalently Phi is the sum of m+B_t(v-E_QY(t)) and the centered regression residual. The residual is independent of Y(t), by joint Gaussianity first on finite continuous linear functionals and then on a countable determining family for E. Thus K is the canonical regular conditional law of the whole f given Y(t)=v under Q, defined for every v. It satisfies both P_r Phi=p and Y_Phi(t)=v. In particular the six pins are not lost on conditioning the witnesses.

The map B_t into E varies continuously on compact subsets of Omega because the covariance is smooth and Sigma_t is invertible there. If t_n->t and v_n->v, then for every f in E,

    Phi_(t_n,v_n)(f) -> Phi_(t,v)(f) in C²(X).                 (5)

This single coupling implies joint conditional weak convergence of the zero field Y(.) in C¹_loc(Omega), the full continuous second-jet mark in compact-open topology, and every finite vector of fixed jet evaluations. It proves the needed joint statement rather than separate marginal convergence. A cylinder mark may have singular covariance; no inverse of that mark covariance is used. It also makes K a measurable probability kernel. These facts are the pinned-law adaptation of C317; they do not assert uniformity in a degenerating geometry.

In torus charts, DY is the block matrix diag(H_x,H_y), so its absolute determinant is Delta. AAL Theorem2.2 applies locally. Its regularity conclusion, equivalently Proposition2.1 on compact neighborhoods with bounded density, implies almost surely that the zeros of Y in Omega are regular. Exhausting Omega by compact subsets then gives locally finite ordered-pair zero sets and finite expected counts on each compact subset. No claim is made about the singular covariance on the excluded diagonal itself.

For completeness the zero-count kernel used below is measurable: for an open set in Omega, exhaust it by compact sets. Existence of n distinct zeros in one such compact set is the union over integers j of the event that an n-tuple separated by at least 1/j has maximum |Y| equal to zero. The minimum over that compact tuple space is a continuous function of f in C²; take its infimum as +infinity when the tuple space is empty. Thus counts on open sets are measurable. On the regular locus they define a locally finite counting measure, and the usual monotone-class extension gives its measurable evaluation on Borel sets. The exceptional nonregular locus is Borel by the same compact zero test applied to (Y,det DY); it has Q measure zero and is assigned the zero measure. This convention avoids undefined products on exceptional fields.

## 4. Extend the continuous formula to full Borel marks

Fix compact K contained in Omega. Define two measures on K x E:

    mu(A) = E_Q sum_(t in K:Y_f(t)=0) 1_A(t,f),

    nu(A) = integral_K p_t(0)
              integral_E Delta_phi(t) 1_A(t,phi) K(t,0,dphi) dt.  (6)

The preceding measurable counting kernel makes mu a Borel measure. The canonical kernel (4) makes nu one. Their total masses are equal and finite by the unweighted Gaussian formula; in particular there is no attempted finite-measure argument on the entire noncompact Omega.

For a bounded nonnegative continuous cylinder function h(t,f), use the continuous-weight case of AAL Theorem7.1. Take the auxiliary mark to be the full continuous second-jet field with compact-open topology. Its joint conditional continuity with Y is (5); h is continuous in location and this mark. This verifies the continuous base case, including the path-space conditioning required by the theorem. One can work in chart neighborhoods of K, with bounded continuous extensions of h; a finite chart partition then gives

    integral h dmu = integral h dnu.                           (7)

To pass to all Borel h, choose countably many dense torus sites q and all jet evaluations partial^alpha f(q), |alpha|<=2. They generate Borel(E): suprema over this countable dense family recover the C² norm, and E is separable. Combine them with bounded continuous functions on the compact metric space K. The resulting bounded real continuous cylinder algebra contains constants and generates Borel(K x E). The real bounded functions for which (7) holds form a vector space closed under bounded pointwise convergence, since both measures are finite. The functional monotone-class theorem identifies mu and nu on every bounded Borel function. Increasing truncations extend (7) to every nonnegative Borel function, with infinite values allowed.

Apply this to h(t,f)=W(f)i(t,f)1_D(t). For instance a global elder predicate, if independently established to be Borel for the prescribed convention, can appear in i. The argument does not assume that such a predicate is continuous or lower semicontinuous, and does not itself prove an elder correspondence. Likewise a choice of an actual height-window or Hessian-index indicator is Borel and permissible; including it does not introduce independent witness heights or detach it from the conditional field.

Finally take an increasing compact exhaustion K_n of Omega. Monotone convergence on both sides, with their nonnegative integrands, proves

    E_Q[ W C_(D,i) ]
      = integral_D p_t(0)
          E_K(t,0)[ W Delta_f(t) i(t,f) ] dx dy,               (8)

    E_(Q^W) C_(D,i)
      = (1/Z) integral_D p_t(0)
          E_K(t,0)[ W |det H_x det H_y| i(t,f) ] dx dy.         (9)

Equation (9) remains an extended nonnegative identity for arbitrary D and i. In the compact geometries with the bounds in Section6 its right side is finite, so the corresponding count is finite Q^W-almost surely. No dominated convergence, collision-diagonal density value, or near-diagonal uniform covariance lower bound is required for the exhaustion step. Infinite global counts on an exceptional or uncontrolled region are not used in a subtraction of infinities.

There is exactly one endpoint W and exactly one division by its own Z. The two witness determinants come from the block Jacobian. The pin density does not appear because Q is already the pinned law. Reaggregating pins or dividing by torus volume would be different operations with additional explicitly chosen measures; neither is performed here.

## 5. Ordered, unordered and factorial observables are different

If D=A x A minus the diagonal for a Borel A contained in U, and i(t,f)=j(x,f)j(y,f) for a Borel indicator j, (3) is N_A(N_A-1), where N_A counts the critical points satisfying j. For infinite N_A interpret this through increasing finite-region counts, not the undefined expression infinity times infinity-minus-one in an algebraic manipulation. When the integral is finite, N_A is finite Q^W-almost surely and the usual identity applies. In particular

    Q^W{N_A>=2} <= (1/2) E_(Q^W)[N_A(N_A-1)].                 (10)

If D and i are invariant under (x,y)->(y,x), dividing C by two counts each marked unordered pair once. Symmetry is essential. In the source's short-edge geometry x is the anchor, |xi|<=B, whereas y=xi+epsilon w may lie outside that anchor set. That domain is generally asymmetric; its count is not automatically N_A(N_A-1), and no factor1/2 is licensed. For any indicator mark, without symmetry,

    Q^W{C_(D,i)>=1} <= E_(Q^W) C_(D,i).                       (11)

For a symmetric subdomain specifying only close pairs, C counts close ordered edges, not every pair of N_A. The event N_A>=2 need not imply the existence of a close edge. Equation (10) cannot be applied to such a restricted-domain estimate without a separate bound on the remaining pairs. These distinctions are part of the interface, not conventions to be changed after an estimate is obtained.

## 3. Reconstruction of the Gaussian conditional interface

The image-sum covariance agrees with the retained normalized infinite Fourier law. Gaussian Derivations gives the real sine/cosine expansion with every spectral weight positive, including the constant mode. The coefficient decay makes the sums of the Lp norms of the Fourier terms, weighted by any fixed derivative order, finite. This gives one common smooth version and finite derivative-supremum moments. Its partial sums are measurable C2-valued random elements and converge in that norm, so the field is a Gaussian random element of the separable Banach space E=C2(X). No discrete grid or finite spectral approximation enters the argument.

The two pin sites are distinct. Full-spectrum positivity and independence of the value/first-derivative distributions at two distinct sites give a positive definite covariance for all six pin observations. Thus the canonical initial conditional law Q exists at every target p. It has a generally nonzero smooth mean and a smooth centered covariance. Conditioning by finite continuous linear observations preserves the needed Gaussian random-element structure and finite derivative moments for each fixed target.

For t=(x,y) in Omega, the ten observation functionals consisting of the six pins and the four witness gradients are linearly independent. A linear dependence in Gaussian L2 would annihilate every Fourier mode. Equivalently its finite sum of point masses and first derivatives would be the zero distribution. Smooth test functions supported separately near a,c,x,y and with specified local jets force each coefficient to vanish. The witness Schur complement Sigma_t is therefore positive definite. This proves conditional rank, rather than merely checking unconditional witness rank.

Continuity of the covariance and compactness give a positive conditional covariance floor on each compact K contained in Omega, for the fixed pin configuration. The density bound is independent of the conditional mean because a Gaussian density is bounded by its determinant prefactor. No uniform floor near a pin, a witness diagonal, or r=0 follows from this step, and the candidate does not claim one.

Let mu_t=E_Q Y(t) and use centered covariances in B_t. The field can be decomposed under Q as

    f = m + R_t + B_t (Y(t)-mu_t),

where R_t is a centered E-valued Gaussian residual independent of Y(t). Independence follows first for finite linear evaluation families by zero Gaussian cross-covariance and then for the whole E-valued field through a countable Borel-generating family. Thus the stated Phi map has law
