### R1. The correlated Taylor remainder cannot cancel the covariance floor

Work under the six-pin conditional Gaussian law and center every residual jet. Put
\[
h=\sqrt{\psi}=\sqrt{\beta^2+r^2\alpha^2}.
\]
Let \(J=(S,T,Q)=(f_{zz},f_{xxz},f_{xxxx})\), and let \(\Sigma_{J,r}\) be its residual Schur covariance after the desingularized pin frame. The imported distinct-monomial input needed here is the uniform bound
\[
\lambda I_3\preceq \Sigma_{J,r}\preceq \Lambda I_3 .
\]
It should be cited by immutable blob, not as “the byte-bound parent source”; the already merged nine-jet sources named in comment 5848723841 are sufficient (`reviews/pr28_annulus_bridge_nonauthor_20260925/REVIEW.md`, blob `559d7224`, and `frontiers/rn_thin_tube_20260925/PROOF.md`, blob `338d92d1`).

Let \(T_{0,r}:\mathbb R^2\to\mathcal H_r\) be the two-row Gaussian-Hilbert-space operator contributed by \(J\). Its coefficient matrix is the displayed \(B_r(\alpha,\beta)\). The checked identity gives
\[
\det(B_rB_r^\top)=\Phi\ge \psi^2/576,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}(B_rB_r^\top)\le C_K\psi .
\]
Therefore
\[
s_{\min}(T_{0,r})\ge \sqrt{\lambda}\,s_{\min}(B_r)
 \ge c_0 h,\qquad \|T_{0,r}\|\le C_0 h.
\]

Now write the **actual** centered normalized gradient row operator as
\[
T_r=T_{0,r}+E_r.
\]
No independence between \(E_r\) and \(J\) is assumed or needed. The finite ledger, which belongs in the note, is as follows. After division by \(r^3\) and \(r^2\), respectively, the non-\((S,T,Q)\) terms in the first row include
\[
 {r\beta^2\over2}f_{xzz}
 +\left(-{r\beta\over6}+{r^3\alpha^2\beta\over2}\right)f_{xxxz}
 +{r^3\alpha\beta^2\over2}f_{xxzz}
 +{r^3\beta^3\over6}f_{xzzz},
\]
plus the endpoint-solution tails \(O_{L^2}(r^2|\alpha|+r|\beta|)\). The second row includes
\[
 r^2\alpha\beta f_{xzz}+{r^2\beta^2\over2}f_{zzz}
 +\left(-{r^2\alpha\over6}+{r^4\alpha^3\over6}\right)f_{xxxz},
\]
with the remaining degree-four/integral-remainder coefficients of order
\(r^3|\alpha|\beta^2\), \(r^3|\beta|^3\), or smaller, plus an
\(O_{L^2}(r^2|\alpha|)\) pin-solution tail. Uniform conditional \(C^5\) moments and
\[
|\beta|\le h,\qquad r|\alpha|\le h,\qquad |\alpha|,|\beta|\le K
\]
give the rowwise bound
\[
\|E_r\|_{\rm op}\le C_K r h .
\]
This is the required scale-relative estimate; an absolute \(O(r)\) statement is not enough near the pin.

Weyl’s singular-value inequality in \(\mathcal H_r\) now gives, uniformly for every \((\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)\),
\[
s_{\min}(T_r)\ge (c_0-C_Kr)h\ge {c_0\over2}h
\]
after reducing \(r_*\). Hence, without any “uncorrelated remainder” assumption,
\[
c\,\psi I_2\preceq \operatorname{Cov}(G_r\mid\text{pins})
 \preceq C\,\psi I_2,\qquad
\det\operatorname{Cov}(G_r\mid\text{pins})\ge c^2\psi^2 .
\]
This is the continuum version of the checked Gram identity. The conditioned pin \(\psi=0\) remains excluded.

### R2. One Gaussian tilt controls both the moments and the axial cost

Let \(U\) be the finite residual-jet vector needed for the three Hessian determinants and the \(C^5\) remainders. From R1,
\[
\Sigma_G\asymp h^2I_2,\qquad
\mu_G=(-6k\alpha+O(h),\,O(h)).
\]
Also \(\|\operatorname{Cov}(U,G)\|\le Ch\) by Cauchy–Schwarz and the uniform jet-moment bound. The block-Gaussian conditioning formula therefore yields
\[
\|\mathbb E[U\mid G=0]\|
 \le C\left(1+{|\alpha|\over h}\right),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(U\mid G=0)\preceq CI,
\]
and, for every fixed-degree polynomial \(P_d\),
\[
\mathbb E[|P_d(U)|\mid G=0]
 \le C_d\left(1+\left({|\alpha|\over h}\right)^d\right). \tag{R2.1}
\]
The same calculation gives the density bound
\[
p_G(0)\le C h^{-2}\exp\!\left[-c{\alpha^2\over h^2}\right],
\qquad
p_{\nabla f(X)}(0)\le Cr^{-5}h^{-2}
 \exp\!\left[-c{\alpha^2\over h^2}\right]. \tag{R2.2}
\]
Thus “conditioning shifts the remaining jets by \(O(1)\)” and the later axial exponential are not two assertions; they are the bounded-ratio and large-ratio cases of the same formula.

Consequences:

- If \(|\alpha|\le|\beta|\), then \(|\alpha|/h=O(1)\), so all conditional determinant moments are uniformly bounded.
- If \(r|\alpha|\le|\beta|\le|\alpha|\), put \(u=|\beta|/|\alpha|\). Then \(h\asymp|\beta|\), every polynomial tilt is \(O(u^{-d})\), and (R2.2) supplies \(e^{-c/u^2}\). Hence
  \[
  \int_0^1 u^{-d}e^{-c/u^2}\,du<\infty
  \]
  for every fixed \(d\); this is the rigorous steep/axial-band absorber.
- If \(|\beta|\le r|\alpha|\), then \(h\asymp r|\alpha|\), so (R2.2) gives \(e^{-c/r^2}\); it absorbs every fixed power of \(r^{-1}\).

For the microdisk ledger, the checked cancellation gives the unscaled three-determinant moment
\(O(r^8\beta^2)\) on the transverse chart, with only the polynomial factor in
(R2.1) on the intermediate band. Combining \(Z_r^{-1}=O(r^{-2})\),
\(p_{\nabla f}(0)\) from (R2.2), and physical area \(r^4\,d\alpha\,d\beta\) gives
\[
r^{-2}\,r^{-5}\,h^{-2}\,r^8\beta^2\,r^4
 \le Cr^5
\]
after the bounded/exponentially absorbed ratio integral. The quartic band is superalgebraically smaller.

For the outer cone, write the scaled determinant expansions after \(G=0\) as
\[
D_M=qL_M(t,U)+R_M,\quad D_X=qL_X(t,U)+R_X,\quad
D_S=L_S(t,U)+R_S,
\]
where uniform conditional \(C^5\) moments give
\(\|R_i\|_{L^p(G=0)}\le C_pr(1+|t|^{m_p})\).
On \(|t|\le1\), \(|q|\ge Kr\), this is \(O(|q|)\) for the first two factors and
\(O(1)\) for the third after fixing \(K\) and reducing \(r_*\). Hölder plus (R2.1) gives
\[
\mathbb E[|D_MD_XD_S|\mid G=0]\le Cq^2,
\]
hence \(O(r^6q^2)\) for the unscaled Hessian product. For the steep strip the same
polynomial \(t\)-growth is absorbed by \(e^{-ct^2}\); for the axis strip it is
absorbed by \(e^{-c/r^2}\). This supplies the missing meaning of “the same count
persists with an \(O(r)\) error.”
