- **The pointwise limit.**

### Consistency observation: why "t → 0 first" is essential
The finite-`t` law departs from the limit law exactly where the cusp scale begins. The finite-`t` density uses `A_r` at `r = (ty/k)^{1/3}`. This differs from `A_0` at relative order one once `κ = k/r ≍ 1`, that is, once `k ≍ (ty)^{1/4}`. That corresponds to `V = (y/k)^{1/3} ≍ y^{1/4}t^{−1/12}`. The limit law's tail beyond that point has relative mass `a^{−7} ≍ t^{7/12}`. This is precisely the relative order of the cusp correction `c₁ℓ^{1/4}/(cℓ^{−1/3})` of #207 (Theorem CU, candidate status), which comes from the typed window and the elder window `|φ| < 1/3` at `κ ≍ 1`. So T5's tail describes the limit law, and the finite-`t` endpoint law has its own cusp-scale correction for `a ≳ t^{−1/12}`. This is consistent with the note's explicit refusal of a two-parameter limit. A sentence saying so would sharpen that caveat; it is optional.
