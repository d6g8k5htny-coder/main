## 1. Actual fixed model and original observations

Fix d>=2, L>0 and the smooth centered stationary Gaussian field on X=R^d/(L Z^d), normalized to variance one, with the exact periodized Gaussian covariance

    K_L(z)=Σ_n exp(-|z+Ln|²/2) / Σ_n exp(-|Ln|²/2).

Fix r0>0. Let F_r0={(x,y)∈X²:dist_X(x,y)>=r0}. If this set is empty the conclusion is zero. Otherwise it is compact and contains no equal torus sites. All constants may depend on d,L,r0. The limit of interest is ℓ↓0 while those parameters stay fixed; no shrinking r0, infinite-volume or dimension-uniform assertion is made.

Use the ORIGINAL 2(d+1)-dimensional observation vector

    O_(x,y)=(f(x),grad f(x),f(y),grad f(y))

in physical orthonormal coordinates. For 0<ℓ<=1 its target is

    t_(b,ℓ)=(b,0,...,0,b-ℓ,0,...,0), b∈R.

The covariance Σ_(x,y) is a deterministic matrix depending on the spatial pair, not on b or ℓ. It is not the singular contact covariance or a random field Hessian. No rescaled r^(-1) observation frame is used on this far domain.

## 2. Reconstructed rank and uniform spectral bounds

Poisson summation gives the normalized Fourier coefficients

    a_n=exp(-2π²|n|²/L²) / Σ_j exp(-2π²|j|²/L²)>0

for every n∈Z^d. Their Gaussian decay makes all polynomially weighted covariance/derivative series converge absolutely. For a real linear combination T of the two value evaluations and their coordinate first derivatives, its variance is

    Var(Tf)=Σ_n a_n |T(exp(2π i n·z/L))|².

A zero variance forces every summand to vanish. The finite-order distribution T then annihilates every trigonometric polynomial. Smooth periodic functions have Fourier series converging in any prescribed finite derivative norm, so T annihilates every smooth test function. Choose disjoint neighborhoods of x and y and a smooth cutoff equal to one near either point. A cutoff itself and cutoff times each local coordinate independently prescribe that point's value and gradient while making the other point's jet zero. Every coefficient of T must therefore be zero. This proves positive definiteness for the actual original pin vector whenever x and y are distinct torus sites.

This reasoning uses independent coordinate jet components. Merely having pairwise different linear functionals would not be enough: ∂1,∂2,∂1+∂2 are different but dependent. P§2 is accepted here only at the original value/coordinate-gradient list it actually supplies to §14; no unrestricted mixed-direction claim is inferred. Repeated symmetric Hessian entries are likewise not being treated as independent observations.

The covariance entries are continuous in (x,y), because they are derivatives of the smooth K_L. The functions λmin and λmax are continuous on symmetric matrices. Compactness of F_r0 therefore supplies fixed constants

    0<κ<=λmin(Σ_(x,y))<=λmax(Σ_(x,y))<=Λ<∞.

Both bounds matter. The lower bound controls detΣ and Σ^(-1); the upper bound controls the Gaussian exponential tail below. C4§8 explicitly retains the upper bound; in P§14 it follows from the same smoothness and compactness. No torus rotational invariance, sampled covariance lower bound, or independence of Hessian entries is used.

The torus cut locus causes no missing case: F_r0 is defined by the continuous torus distance, and the observation covariance is a smooth function of the pair itself. The proof chooses no unique shortest displacement, angular chart or globally continuous transverse frame. A fundamental-domain face represents the same periodic field values, not a new diagonal point when torus distance is positive.

## 3. Uniform Gaussian density and full conditional moments

Put m=2(d+1). The centered Gaussian density at the original target satisfies

    p_O(t)=(2π)^(-m/2)(detΣ)^(-1/2) exp[-tᵀΣ^(-1)t/2]
           <=(2π)^(-m/2)κ^(-m/2) exp[-|t|²/(2Λ)]
           <=C exp[-b²/(2Λ)],

since |t|²=b²+(b-ℓ)²>=b². This bound is uniform in every b∈R, 0<ℓ<=1 and spatial pair in F_r0.

Stack the independent symmetric entries of H_x and H_y into Y. Joint Gaussian regression gives

    E[Y|O=t]=Cov(Y,O)Σ^(-1)t,
    Cov(Y|O=t)=Cov(Y)-Cov(Y,O)Σ^(-1)Cov(O,Y).

All unconditional covariance blocks are uniformly bounded by smoothness/compactness, and ||Σ^(-1)||<=κ^(-1). The conditional mean is therefore at most C(|b|+1), because |t|<=C'(|b|+1) for 0<ℓ<=1. The residual covariance is positive semidefinite and no larger than Cov(Y); it has a uniform upper bound. It need not be diagonal or nondegenerate for this estimate. Finite-dimensional Gaussian moments then give, for every fixed finite p,

    E[||H_x||_F^p+||H_y||_F^p | O=t] <=C_p(1+|b|)^p.

Hadamard's determinant inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz imply

    E[|det H_x det H_y| | O=t]
      <=(E[||H_x||_F^(2d)|O=t] E[||H_y||_F^(2d)|O=t])^(1/2)
      <=C(1+|b|)^(2d).

This controls the complete determinant product before index or elder selection. It assumes neither conditional independence of the two Hessians nor independence of their entries. Multiplication by maximum/index-(d-1)-saddle indicators and by any admitted global selection mark in[0,1] can only decrease it. No lower normalizer floor and no division by a determinant-weighted Z appear.

Combining the density and moment estimates gives a single all-height integrable majorant

    p_O(t_(b,ℓ)) E[|det H_x det H_y||O=t_(b,ℓ)]
      <=C(1+|b|)^(2d)e^(-c b²),  c=1/(2Λ)>0.

Thus C4§8's polynomial exponent N can be taken to be2d at this scope. The constants are finite existence constants; they were not numerically evaluated or interval-certified.

## 5. Analytic adversarial controls and limits

These are explicit mathematical checks in reasoning, not executed software tests:

- **Delete full spectral support:** a stationary field supported on one sine/cosine mode has observation covariance rank at most2, while the original two-point pin vector has dimension2(d+1)>2. Positive variance of the field alone therefore cannot justify this rank proof. The actual periodized covariance avoids this counterexample because every Fourier coefficient is strictly positive.
- **Include the diagonal:** at x=y the two sets of value/gradient observations repeat, and Σ is singular. Continuity then prevents a positive uniform κ as r0 shrinks to0. The fixed-separation qualification is load-bearing; this proof does not close a shrinking-annulus or regional multiple-witness collision obligation.
- **Keep only a covariance lower bound:** matrices Σ_s=sI for s>=1 have inverse norm<=1, but at a target of size sqrt(s) the density decays only polynomially in s. No common Ce^(-c|t|²) bound with c>0 follows. A uniform covariance upper bound is needed and is supplied by the actual compact domain.
- **Drop the conditional-mean growth term:** a conditional scalar Gaussian Y=b+Z already has pth absolute moment growing like|b|^p. Compact-b moment bounds cannot simply be reused for unbounded b. The explicit regression calculation, polynomial growth and Gaussian tail make the unrestricted integral finite.
- **Change the height Jacobian:** (b,ℓ)↦(b,b-ℓ) has determinant−1. Importing the near k-coordinate factor r³ or a12 multiplier changes this far density. Those factors belong to a different observation/mark transformation.
- **Replace torus distance by an unreduced Euclidean difference:** points near opposite faces of a fundamental cube can be physically close on the torus. The actual F_r0 uses torus distance and excludes such near-diagonal pairs when appropriate.

The analytic argument survives these checks for its stated actual model and fixed parameters. No claim is made for arbitrary sparse spectra, varying L, arbitrary mixed derivative lists, uniform d, or moving separation cutoffs.
