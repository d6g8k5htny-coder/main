## 1. Fixed object and exact covariance normalization

Fix a finite integer d ≥ 2 and a real L > 0. Write T = ℝᵈ/(Lℤᵈ), ωₖ = 2πk/L,

\[
Z=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}e^{-|\omega_k|^2/2},\qquad
p_k=Z^{-1}e^{-|\omega_k|^2/2}.
\]

Then 0 < Z < ∞, p₋ₖ = pₖ > 0, and Σₖpₖ = 1. Choose one representative from each nonzero pair {k,−k}, called H. On the countable product standard-normal probability space, use independent ξ₀, ξₖ, ηₖ and the specified real series

\[
f(x)=\sqrt{p_0}\xi_0+
\sum_{k\in H}\sqrt{2p_k}
\bigl(\xi_k\cos(\omega_k\cdot x)+\eta_k\sin(\omega_k\cdot x)\bigr).
\]

Its covariance is exactly

\[
\mathbb E[f(x)f(y)]
=p_0+\sum_{k\in H}2p_k\cos(\omega_k\cdot(x-y))
=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}p_ke^{i\omega_k\cdot(x-y)}.
\]

For P(z) = Σₙ exp(−|z+Ln|²/2), Gaussian tails allow differentiation and unfolding the integral over a fundamental cell. Its k-th Fourier coefficient is

\[
L^{-d}\int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{-|u|^2/2}e^{-i\omega_k\cdot u}\,du
=(2\pi)^{d/2}L^{-d}e^{-|\omega_k|^2/2}.
\]

The coefficients are absolutely summable. Fourier reconstruction and evaluation at 0 therefore give P(0) = (2π)^{d/2}L⁻ᵈZ and covariance P(z)/P(0), precisely the requested K_L. Thus Var f(x) = 1. The factor √(2pₖ) in the real half-lattice formula is necessary: replacing it by √pₖ would give the wrong nonconstant covariance. Constants depend on this fixed L and d; no limit uniform in L is asserted.

## 2. One almost-sure smooth field, not separate derivative versions

For each integer m ≥ 0 set

\[
S_m=\sqrt{p_0}|\xi_0|+
\sum_{k\in H}\sqrt{2p_k}(|\xi_k|+|\eta_k|)(1+|\omega_k|)^m.
\]

Since E|ξ| = √(2/π) and √pₖ = Z⁻¹ᐟ²exp(−π²|k|²/L²), E S_m is finite: a polynomial times a Gaussian is summable on ℤᵈ. Tonelli gives S_m < ∞ almost surely. Intersecting these countably many events gives a single event of probability one on which every multi-index derivative series converges uniformly and absolutely. The usual theorem on uniform convergence of derivatives, applied successively to finite partial sums, yields a periodic C∞ function with exactly those derivatives. In particular it is one actual C⁴ field; there is no incompatible choice of modifications at different derivative orders.

For the norm ||u||_{Cᵐ} = max_{|α|≤m} sup_T |∂ᵅu|, Minkowski also gives

\[
\bigl(\mathbb E\|f\|_{C^m}^2\bigr)^{1/2}
\le \sqrt{p_0}+2\sum_{k\in H}\sqrt{2p_k}(1+|\omega_k|)^m<\infty.
\]

The corresponding tail bounds tend to zero, so the finite sums converge in L²(Cᵐ) as well as almost surely in Cᵐ. Pointwise variance 1 does **not** mean C⁴ norm 1.

## 3. Borel Banach Gaussian law and the spectral norm

Cᵐ(T;ℝ), for finite m, is a separable Banach space. Each finite partial sum is a Borel Cᵐ-valued random variable; its almost-sure limit, defined to be 0 on the null exceptional set, is Borel. For every continuous real linear functional ℓ on Cᵐ, ℓ(f_N) is centered Gaussian and converges in L² to ℓ(f). Its variances converge, hence its characteristic functions converge to exp(−t²Var(ℓf)/2). Thus ℓ(f) is centered Gaussian, possibly degenerate. This proves a Borel Banach Gaussian measure, rather than merely Gaussian evaluation marginals. This is the standard continuous-dual definition used in [Karhunen–Loève decomposition of Gaussian measures on Banach spaces](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.01448).

The covariance RKHS spectral norm, if needed to pin deterministic directions, is

\[
\|h\|_H^2=a_0^2/p_0+
\sum_{k\in H}(a_k^2+b_k^2)/(2p_k),\quad
h=a_0+\sum_{k\in H}(a_k\cos\omega_k x+b_k\sin\omega_k x).
\]

Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients makes this norm unambiguous. The associated weighted ℓ² feature map embeds into every Cᵐ by Cauchy–Schwarz and Σₖpₖ(1+|ωₖ|)²ᵐ < ∞. It is the covariance RKHS (equivalently the Cameron–Martin space of this Gaussian law); every finite trigonometric polynomial is included because every pₖ is positive. This deterministic norm is distinct from the random C⁴ norm. No RKHS membership of a typical sample is claimed.

## 4. Point and finite multipoint jet nondegeneracy

Use symmetry-reduced derivative coordinates, indexed by multi-indices, at finitely many distinct torus points, with arbitrary fixed finite jet orders. For a nonzero real linear functional A on these jet coordinates,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Af)=p_0(A1)^2+
2\sum_{k\in H}p_k\bigl((A\cos\omega_k x)^2+(A\sin\omega_k x)^2\bigr).
\]

It is zero only if A annihilates every real trigonometric polynomial. Such polynomials interpolate arbitrary finite jets, so this cannot happen. Here is an algebraic verification of that interpolation, avoiding an assumption about an unspecified covariance.

In angular coordinates θ = 2πx/L, let z_i = exp(iθ_i). For each selected point q and every other point r choose a coordinate i(q,r) whose phases differ. The polynomial

\[
B_q(z)=\prod_{r\ne q}
\left(\frac{z_{i(q,r)}-z_{i(q,r)}(r)}
{z_{i(q,r)}(q)-z_{i(q,r)}(r)}\right)^{m_r+1}
\]

has value 1 at q and all derivatives through order m_r zero at r. Multiplication by B_q is invertible on finite Taylor jets at q. The operators ∂_{θ_i} = iz_i∂_{z_i} give an invertible triangular conversion between polynomial jets in z−z(q) and angular jets, since z_i(q) ≠ 0. Choose a finite Taylor polynomial R_q so that B_qR_q has the desired angular jet at q; sum over q and take the real part. Taking real parts preserves the requested real jets and produces a finite real Fourier polynomial. Physical x derivatives differ by the invertible factor (2π/L)^{|α|}.

Consequently each such finite real Gaussian jet vector has strictly positive definite covariance. Duplicate derivatives, duplicate points or redundant Hessian coordinates would create deterministic relations and must not be described as a nondegenerate vector. This assertion is pointwise in the distinct configuration; it supplies no lower bound uniform as points collide.

Useful exact one-point pins follow directly: Cov(f,∂ᵢf) = 0,
Cov(∂ᵢf,∂ⱼf) = (2π/L)²Σₖpₖkᵢkⱼ = μ₂δᵢⱼ with μ₂ > 0,
Cov(f,∂ᵢ∂ⱼf) = −μ₂δᵢⱼ, and
Cov(∂ᵢ∂ⱼf,∂ₗ∂ᵣf) = (2π/L)⁴Σₖpₖkᵢkⱼkₗkᵣ.
All sums converge. These are pins for the concrete spectrum, not numerical evaluations of μ₂ or a Kac–Rice constant.

There is a further relevant distinction from a rotationally invariant Euclidean Gaussian law. Here pₖ factorizes into d copies of qₙ proportional to exp(−2π²n²/L²). Set ν₄ = (2π/L)⁴E_q[n⁴]. Conditional on f = u and df = 0, the symmetry-reduced Hessian entries are independent Gaussian variables: diagonal entries have mean −μ₂u and variance ν₄−μ₂²; off-diagonal entries have mean 0 and variance μ₂². This follows from the fourth-moment formula, product structure, parity, and subtracting Cov(Hᵢᵢ,f)Cov(Hⱼⱼ,f) in Gaussian conditioning. Both variances are positive: q has positive mass at 0 and 1, so E_q[n²] > 0 and Var_q(n²) > 0. Signed-permutation symmetry of the lattice does not supply full rotational invariance. A shifted GOE description would require the extra identity ν₄ = 3μ₂², which is not provided by the spectral formula. A subsequent mark expectation must use these concrete moments, or separately prove any claimed simplification.

## 5. The degree-three two-point construction really works

For x ≠ y choose a separating coordinate j, with a = z_j(x) ≠ b = z_j(y). Write a_i = z_i(x), b_i = z_i(y). Work with desired angular derivatives. A complex polynomial P(z_j) of degree ≤ 3 uniquely satisfies any four constraints

\[
P(a)=u_x,\quad P(b)=u_y,\quad iaP'(a)=v_{xj},\quad ibP'(b)=v_{yj}.
\]

This is ordinary two-node Hermite interpolation; the homogeneous polynomial would have double zeros at a and b and degree ≤ 3, hence would vanish. Taking Re P gives the prescribed real values and j derivatives, with all other derivatives zero.

For i ≠ j, add the real parts of

\[
R_{xi}(z)=\frac{v_{xi}}{ia_i(a-b)^2}(z_i-a_i)(z_j-b)^2,\qquad
R_{yi}(z)=\frac{v_{yi}}{ib_i(b-a)^2}(z_i-b_i)(z_j-a)^2.
\]

R_xi has zero values at both points, zero j derivative at x because z_i−a_i vanishes there, and all first derivatives zero at y because of the double zero in z_j−b. Its i derivative at x is exactly v_xi. The analogous assertions hold for R_yi. No assumption a_i ≠ b_i is needed. All monomials have total degree ≤ 3; taking real parts adds the negative frequencies but keeps |k|₁ ≤ 3. Scaling to physical derivatives is harmless for L > 0.

Therefore S = span_ℝ{1, cos(ωₖx), sin(ωₖx): 0 < |k|₁ ≤ 3} maps surjectively onto the two value/first-gradient jets, dimension 2d+2. In particular its map to (df_x, df_y, f(x)−f(y)) is surjective, dimension 2d+1. The coefficients may grow as a−b tends to zero; this construction cannot be cited for a uniform off-diagonal estimate.

## 6. Almost-sure Morse property by actual independent finite coordinates

Retain only the 2d fundamental sin/cos coefficients, whose variances 2p_{e_i} are positive, and condition on the independent remaining series g. Almost surely g is C∞. In the unscaled coefficient parametrization a ∈ ℝ²ᵈ,

\[
F(a,x)=\nabla\left(g(x)+\sum_i
(a_i\cos(2\pi x_i/L)+b_i\sin(2\pi x_i/L))\right).
\]

Its derivative in the two i-th parameters gives the i-th row (−(2π/L)sin θ_i, (2π/L)cos θ_i), a nonzero pair, and different rows use disjoint pairs. Thus D_aF is surjective at every x. The zero set Q = F⁻¹(0) is a smooth manifold of dimension 2d. For the projection π:Q→ℝ²ᵈ, its tangent equation is D_aF·δa + Hess(f_a)(x)·δx = 0. Since D_aF is onto, Dπ is onto exactly when that Hessian is onto, equivalently invertible.

Sard's theorem applied in countably many charts makes the critical values of π Lebesgue-null. The retained coefficient vector has an everywhere positive Gaussian density, so for every smooth conditioned g almost every a gives a Morse function. Integrating over g gives probability one. The smooth version of the theorem used here is covered by [Sard's original paper, Theorems 4.1 and 7.2](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/sard.pdf).

The event is measurable without choosing critical-point labels: non-Morse functions form a closed subset of C²(T), since a convergent sequence of degenerate critical points has a convergent subsequence on compact T and the gradient/Hessian evaluations converge. Thus the conditional Fubini step is valid. Compactness and nondegeneracy then imply finitely many critical points.

## 7. Almost-sure distinct critical values, with all off-diagonal pairs covered

Now retain all coordinates in S from section 5 and condition on the independent rest g. Let N = dim S and give these coordinates their actual nondegenerate diagonal Gaussian law. On ℝᴺ × (T×T\Δ), set

\[
G(a,x,y)=(df_a(x),df_a(y),f_a(x)-f_a(y)).
\]

Section 5 proves D_aG is onto ℝ²ᵈ⁺¹ at every distinct pair. Hence G⁻¹(0) is a smooth manifold of dimension N+2d−(2d+1) = N−1. Its projection to ℝᴺ has no surjective derivative because its domain dimension is smaller. In each chart, smoothness gives local Lipschitz maps from dimension N−1 into N, whose images have N-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. A countable chart cover gives a null entire projection image, the lower-dimensional null-image consequence used in the Sard argument. The manifold is second countable, so such a cover exists; compactness/properness of the projection and a uniform submersion constant are unnecessary. Therefore the full-density Gaussian parameter vector almost surely avoids every equal-valued distinct critical pair, for each conditioned smooth g; Fubini gives the unconditional result.

Measurability is explicit. Exhaust the off-diagonal by the compact sets D_n = {(x,y): dist_T(x,y) ≥ 1/n}. For each n, the functions in C¹ with an equal-valued critical pair in D_n form a closed set, by compactness and continuous evaluation of values/first derivatives. Their countable union is the bad event. This establishes a Borel event without claiming globally continuous critical-point marks on C², and covers arbitrarily close but distinct pairs.
