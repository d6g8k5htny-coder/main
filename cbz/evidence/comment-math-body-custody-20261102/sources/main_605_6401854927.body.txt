Let \(R\in O(2)\) be the pin frame, midpoint \(p\), and let
\[
 M=p-\tfrac r2 R(1,0),\quad S=p+\tfrac r2 R(1,0),\quad

## 3. A uniformly bounded exact dimensionless observation family

Set \(g(Y)=F(p+sRY)\). For q>0 let \(H_q\) be the cubic Hermite interpolant of \(g(u,0)\) and \(\partial_u g(u,0)\) at \(u=\pm q/2\). Let \(L_q\) be the affine interpolant of \(\partial_v g(u,0)\) at those two sites. Define six linear rows
\[
 A_qg=(H_q(0),H'_q(0),H''_q(0),H'''_q(0),L_q(0),L'_q(0)).
\]
At q=0 they mean \((g,g_u,g_{uu},g_{uuu},g_v,g_{uv})(0)\). Define
\[
 B_\eta g=\frac{\nabla g(z+\eta w)-\nabla g(z)}{\eta},
 \qquad B_0g=H_g(z)w,\qquad
 T_{q,z,w,\eta}g=(A_qg,\nabla g(z),B_\eta g).              \tag{6}
\]
For positive q,s,eta this ten-row vector is an invertible deterministic linear transform of \(\mathcal T\). Thus it generates exactly the same Gaussian conditioning sigma-algebra and the same conditional height variance. Conditioning does not insert a change-of-variables density factor into (2).

The frame differs from the S06 divided pin frame; it is not silently identified with it. If the latter, applied to g, is \((U_0,U_1,U_2,U_3,V_0,V_1)\), the exact triangular identities are
\[
 H_q(0)=U_0-\tfrac{q^2}{8}U_2,\qquad
 H'_q(0)=U_1-\tfrac{q^2}{24}U_3,\qquad
 (H''_q,H'''_q,L_q,L'_q)(0)=(U_2,U_3,V_0,V_1).           \tag{7}
\]
They follow by solving the endpoint cubic interpolation equations. In particular the exact target under (5) is
\[
 A_qg=(\bar b,-\tfrac32kr^2s,0,12ks^3,0,0),
 \qquad \bar b=b-\tfrac12kr^3,                           \tag{8}
\]
rather than the target for S06's uncorrected U1 coordinate.

All rows in (6) have uniformly bounded operator norm on \(C^3(D)\), for a fixed dimensionless ball D containing the endpoint segment and witness segments. To see the only potentially singular part explicitly, put \(a_q=q/2\), for a univariate h write
\[
 s_0=\tfrac12(h(-a_q)+h(a_q)),\quad
 d_0=\tfrac{h(a_q)-h(-a_q)}{2a_q},\quad
 s_1=\tfrac12(h'(-a_q)+h'(a_q)),\quad
 d_1=\tfrac{h'(a_q)-h'(-a_q)}{2a_q}.
\]
Then
\[
 H(0)=s_0-\tfrac12a_q^2d_1,\quad H'(0)=\tfrac12(3d_0-s_1),
 \quad H''(0)=d_1,
\]
\[
 H'''(0)=\frac{3}{4a_q^3}
       \int_{-a_q}^{a_q}(a_q^2-u^2)h'''(u)\,du.           \tag{9}
\]
The last kernel is nonnegative with mass one; the other divided differences are averages of the indicated derivatives. The affine transverse rows need at most two derivatives of g. Also
\[
 B_\eta g=\int_0^1 H_g(z+\theta\eta w)w\,d\theta,          \tag{10}
\]
so its bound has no negative power of eta. These formulas also give continuity of the rows on each finite polynomial space as q,eta tend to zero.

## 4. Cubic unisolvence on the generic sector

Let \(\mathcal P_m\) denote real polynomials in two variables of total degree at most m. Its dimensions at m=3,4 are 10,15.

At q=eta=0 the six pin rows specify six distinct scalar third-jet coordinates. Their kernel in \(\mathcal P_3\) is spanned by \(v^2,u^2v,uv^2,v^3\). Write the remaining derivative coordinates as
\((J_{02},J_{21},J_{12},J_{03})\), where \(J_{ij}=\partial_u^i\partial_v^jP(0)\). On that kernel, the u gradient, u component of \(H(z)w\), and
the v component of \(H(z)w-(d/t)\nabla P(z)\) have coefficient matrix on \((J_{21},J_{12},J_{03})\)
\[
 M=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 ct&t^2/2&0\\
 at+dc&dt&0\\
 ac-dc^2/(2t)&at&dt/2
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det M=\frac{d\,t^3(dc-at)}4.                            \tag{11}
\]
The remaining v gradient divided by t has coefficient one on \(J_{02}\). Consequently the four witness rows specify all remaining cubic coordinates exactly on (4). Equation (11) follows by expanding the leading two-by-two minor and multiplying by dt/2; no numerical spot check is needed for this rank conclusion.

Represent (6) on a fixed monomial basis of \(\mathcal P_3\). Its ten-by-ten matrix is continuous in q,eta,z,w. At q=eta=0 it is invertible at every point of the compact sector. Compactness and continuity therefore give common sufficiently small q0,eta0 on which the matrix remains invertible, with uniformly bounded inverse. This is an exact finite-q, finite-eta statement, not inversion of a limit matrix while discarding the finite-pin corrections.

            \quad\hbox{for every }P\in\mathcal P_3.      \tag{12}

## 5. Upper residual-variance bound without conditional moment assumptions

For m=3 or 4, Taylor-expand the actual field at p:
\[
 P_{m,s}(Y)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}
               s^{|\alpha|}\frac{D_R^\alpha F(p)}{\alpha!}Y^\alpha.
\]
On the fixed D, the dimensionless remainder obeys
\[
 \|g-P_{m,s}\|_{C^3(D)}
       \leq C_m s^{m+1}\|F\|_{C^{m+1}},\qquad m=3,4.    \tag{13}
\]
For each derivative through order three, differentiate the Taylor polynomial and apply the derivative Taylor remainder; the factor \(s^{|\alpha|}\) from differentiation in Y restores the displayed power. Fixed-frame derivative constants are uniform over O(2). The right side has finite unconditional second moment by S06. No independence between a Taylor jet and its remainder is used.

Use (12), the bounded rows, and (13) at m=3:
\[
 \|F(x)-\lambda Tg\|_{L^2}
 =\|(g-P_{3,s})(z)-\lambda T(g-P_{3,s})\|_{L^2}
 \leq C s^4.                                           \tag{14}
\]
For a jointly Gaussian vector, its conditional residual variance is the minimum variance over deterministic linear predictors. Equivalently, completing the square in its covariance matrix gives
\[
 \sigma^2=\inf_\ell\operatorname{Var}(F(x)-\ell Tg).
\]
Choosing the particular predictor (12) proves \(\sigma^2\leq Cs^8\). This argument uses unconditional derivative moments and exact observation spans, so it does not assume any uniform moment bound after the rare pin/witness target has been imposed.

## 6. Quartic rank and the matching lower residual-variance bound

At each contact configuration in (4), consider
\[
        h_t(u,v)=6(v/t)^2-8(v/t)^3+3(v/t)^4.             \tag{15}
\]
Every pin row in \(A_0h_t\) is zero. At z=(c,t), both its gradient and its entire Hessian vanish: its only varying coordinate has first and second derivatives zero at v=t. Yet \(h_t(z)=1\). Thus h_t is annihilated by all ten contact rows but not by height evaluation.

The ten rows already have rank ten on \(\mathcal P_3\). Equation (15) shows that the augmented eleven-row family
\[
          \mathcal B g=(Tg,g(z))                         \tag{16}
\]
has full row rank on \(\mathcal P_4\) at q=eta=0. In a fixed 15-monomial basis its matrix \(B(q,z,w,\eta)\) is continuous. The same compactness argument, reducing q0,eta0 if necessary, yields
\[
       \sigma_{\min}(B)\geq b_*>0                        \tag{17}
\]
uniformly at the actual finite parameters. No quartic polynomial is asserted to be a sample of the periodic field; it proves linear independence of finite evaluation functionals on a finite Taylor-jet space.

Let \(J_4=(D_R^\alpha F(p)/\alpha!)_{|\alpha|\leq4}\) and let \(D_s\) have diagonal entries \(s^{|\alpha|}\). Positive full Fourier weights imply that Cov(J4) has a common positive lower bound over p,R. For completeness, a zero-variance combination of these one-site jets would have a polynomial Fourier multiplier vanishing at every rotated lattice frequency; the distribution argument in S06, or polynomial uniqueness on the full integer lattice after an invertible rotation, forces every coefficient to vanish. Compactness of p,R gives the uniform positive eigenvalue.

By (13) with m=4 and bounded rows,
\[
           \mathcal B g = B D_s J_4+\mathcal R_s,\qquad
           \|\mathcal R_s\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^{11})}\leq Cs^5. \tag{18}
\]
For any \(u\in\mathbb R^{11}\), since \(0<s\leq1\),
\[
 \operatorname{std}(uBD_sJ_4)
       \geq c\|uBD_s\|\geq c s^4\|uB\|
       \geq c' s^4\|u\|.
\]
The centered \(L^2\) triangle inequality and (18) then give
\[
             \operatorname{Cov}(\mathcal B g)\succeq c s^8 I_{11} \tag{19}
\]
for sufficiently small s0, uniformly in the fixed compact parameters. Correlated Taylor remainders are allowed in this inequality.

In particular the ten observation covariance is positive definite. Apply the Schur variational identity to the last coordinate of (16):
\[
 \sigma^2
 =\inf_\ell\operatorname{Var}(F(x)-\ell Tg)
 \geq \inf_\ell c s^8(1+\|\ell\|^2)
 \geq c s^8.                                            \tag{20}
\]
This proves the lower half of (2) with no covariance determinant guessed from a pathwise envelope. Both halves hold simultaneously as q,eta,s tend to zero at arbitrary relative rates inside the stated rectangular parameter range.

## 7. Mean, targets and the corrected scalar window factor

Let \(\nu\) be the actual ten-row target in (6): its first six entries are (8) and its last four are zero. For the centered original field the exact conditional mean is
\[
 m_{q,s,z,w,\eta}
     =\operatorname{Cov}(F(x),Tg)\operatorname{Cov}(Tg)^{-1}\nu. \tag{21}

For a noncentered source, retain the corresponding usual mean correction. No target has been set to zero merely because the covariance proof is unconditional. The conditional height density is exactly Gaussian with mean (21) and variance (2). Therefore, with different sector-dependent constants,
\[
 p_{F(x)\mid T=\nu}(h)
 \leq C s^{-4}\exp\!\left[-\frac{(h-m)^2}{C s^8}\right]. \tag{22}

For the original common-height interval \(I_r=(b-kr^3,b)\),
\[
 \mathbb P(F(x)\in I_r\mid T=\nu)

The exact mean-dependent exponential in (22) remains available and must not be dropped when a later argument needs its energy gain.
