**W11-METHODS-01 — conditional whitening does not preserve the original Euclidean radius.** Q4c, lines200–212, correctly writes detH=T²−D²−X², but then treats D²+X² conditional on T=t as an ordinary noncentral chi-square with2degrees of freedom and a one-scale Marcum-Q1 tail for general covariance.

For U=(D,X), the one-scale formula for the original radius is valid when the conditional covariance is sigma²I₂ with sigma>0:
UᵀU/sigma² has noncentral chi-square₂ law with noncentrality ||E[U|T=t]||²/sigma².
Under general positive-definite C=Cov(U|T), whitening instead makes UᵀC⁻¹U chi-square. The original event UᵀU>t² becomes an ellipsoidal quadratic threshold in whitened variables; it cannot retain the same scalar radial formula merely by changing coordinates.

An exact positive-definite counterexample needs no numerical approximation. Let T,D,X be independent with variances9,1,4 and zero means. The corresponding symmetric Gaussian matrix H=[[T+D,X],[X,T−D]] even has E[detH]=9−1−4=4>0. Given any T=t, Q=D²+X² has E[Q]=5, E[Q²]=59 and Var(Q)=34. For every c>0 and V having a noncentral chi-square₂ law with parameter lambda>=0,
Var(cV)/E[cV]²=4(1+lambda)/(2+lambda)²<=1,
whereas34/25>1 for Q. Thus even choosing an arbitrary common scale and noncentrality cannot fit this example.

**Implementation qualification:** retain the Marcum-Q1 route only after establishing the required conditional isotropy for the actual law/statistic. Otherwise use the correct general Gaussian quadratic-form distribution or retain the transformed ellipsoidal threshold. **W11-METHODS-02 — bounded densities alone do not imply absence of all zeros.** Q1d, lines58–60, states that a C¹ field with pointwise densities bounded near zero has probability0of vanishing anywhere. As written it lacks essential domain/dimension hypotheses and states all-zero absence where the surrounding heading concerns degenerate critical points.

Take Z standard normal and Y(t)=Z+t on[-1,1]. Every path is smooth, Y'(t)=1, and the densities of Y(t) are uniformly bounded by1/sqrt(2pi), even uniformly over t and all values. Nevertheless,
P{there exists t with Y(t)=0}=P{|Z|<=1}=2Phi(1)−1>0.
All such zeros are nondegenerate. The example is fully consistent with absence of simultaneous zeros of Y and Y'; it disproves the report's unqualified all-zero-absence assertion.
