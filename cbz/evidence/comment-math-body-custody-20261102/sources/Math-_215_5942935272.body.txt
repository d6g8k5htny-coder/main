## 2. CAP's norms, tensor weights, and the entire cylinder

The exact CAP definition is the partial-block norm

`M_j=max_(a+c=j) sup_(z in D) ||partial_x^a D_y^c f(z)||op`,

where `D=[-2r,2r] x closed_ball_R2(0,2r)` and the c transverse unit vectors in the operator norm are **independently chosen**. The code is not bounding merely the largest coordinate derivative. All mixed transverse partials of one block form a symmetric c-tensor. Its Frobenius square is

`sum_(j=0)^c binom(c,j) |X_(a,j,c-j)|^2`.

This is exactly `block_members` and its multiplicity `m`. Cauchy–Schwarz against the tensor product of c unit vectors proves `||X||op<=||X||F`. The dimension-dependent coordinates are therefore:

| Block | Isometric coordinates / multiplicities |
|---|---|
| transverse vector v or nu | two entries, weights `(1,1)` |
| symmetric matrix B or Omega | `(11,22,sqrt(2)12)`, squared weights `(1,1,2)` |
| symmetric cubic Y | `(111,sqrt(3)112,sqrt(3)122,222)`, squared weights `(1,3,3,1)` |
| fourth derivative block with c transverse slots | `c+1` entries, binomial squared weights `binom(c,j)` |

The free Cholesky blocks thus act on independent chi variables of dimensions 2, 3, 4. Off-diagonal Cholesky blocks enter as their Frobenius norms; a square diagonal block has operator norm at most its maximum diagonal magnitude plus the Frobenius norm of its strict lower part. No coordinate independence is asserted for entries that share a Gaussian block.

For the midpoint jet in the rare fourth-derivative branch, the code needs a sharper operator-norm bound than Frobenius. We use the standard real Euclidean symmetric-tensor theorem: a symmetric tensor attains its independent-unit-vector norm on a diagonal tuple. This follows from Friedland's Theorem 9 and the norm/best-rank-one equivalence in §3, for finite-dimensional **real Euclidean** spaces; it is not asserted for arbitrary Banach spaces. The exact primary source is [Friedland, arXiv:1110.5689v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5689v3), retained as a 210,611-byte PDF, SHA-256 `cbcbff36733e1ed6d4f22e21e3a02ca226a215eb494e5f3129aa1f8888fe7f45`. This is an explicit standard analytic input, not something proved by the finite controls.

For a symmetric c-tensor T on R2, put `Q(t)=T(u(t),...,u(t))`, `u(t)=(cos t,sin t)`. The theorem gives `max|Q|=||T||op`, and the chain rule gives

`|Q'(t)|=|c T(u'(t),u(t),...,u(t))|<=c||T||op=c max|Q|`.

This proves the derivative inequality needed here directly; a separate general trigonometric Bernstein theorem is unnecessary. The rational parametrization has angle `2 atan(s)`, derivative `2/(1+s^2)<=2`. Samples `s=j/128`, together with their quarter-turn copies, cover angles `[0,pi]` with gaps at most `2/128`.
