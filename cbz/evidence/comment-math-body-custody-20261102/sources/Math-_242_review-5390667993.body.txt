## 2. Exact Gaussian factorization, including a random soft direction

For derivative multi-indices α,β at a point,

    Cov(∂^αf,∂^βf)=(-1)^|β| ∂^(α+β)exp(−|x|²/2)|₀.

The right side vanishes if any coordinate of α+β is odd; otherwise it is the product of signed even Gaussian derivative moments. Subtracting the covariance through the gradient pins gives, for every fixed unit e perpendicular to u,

    Cov((∂_u³f, γ_e, B_e, C_e) | ∇f=0)=diag(6,2,2,6).

For example Cov(γ_e,C_e)=3 before gradient conditioning and the common gradient component subtracts 1·3=3. Similarly Cov(∂_u³f,B_e)=3−3·1=0. The four conditional coordinates are jointly Gaussian, so the diagonal matrix gives independence. Their residual cross-covariances with every even jet vanish by parity. Conditioning additionally on the even contact pins therefore does not change them. The gap pin ∂_u³f=12k changes only the pinned longitudinal coordinate and contributes

    exp(−(12k)²/(2·6))=exp(−12k²).                    (C.2)

The soft direction e₁ is selected measurably from the even matrix A outside its null repeated-eigenvalue set. The odd jet tensor is independent of A. Conditional on A, rotational invariance in u-perpendicular space gives the same triple law in its chosen direction e₁, independent of which direction was selected. Thus (γ₁,B₁,C₁) still has independent variances (2,2,6) and is independent of the even spectral weight. This is the argument needed for arbitrary d; a fixed-axis covariance check alone would not justify selecting a random eigenvector. Reversing e₁ changes γ₁,C₁ together and leaves t and χ₀ invariant, so no extra orientation factor is introduced.

In (3.1) the weighted spectral density consequently factors out. Since Eγ⁶=120 and I(0,0)=20/3, the denominator is 800. The prefactor is 12·800/384=25, agreeing with F₀=25π₀𝔡 in Theorem 1. Hence

    F(k;b)=F₀(b)H(k),
    H(k)=exp(−12k²) E[γ⁶I(12kB/γ²,576k²C/γ³)]/800,    (C.3)

with γ,B,C independent N(0,2), N(0,2), N(0,6). The formula concerns k>0; the probability-zero value γ=0 can be assigned arbitrarily in the integrand. It supplies no positive lower bound on |γ|.

Using (C.1),

    γ⁶I≤C[γ⁶+1728k³|B|³+331776k⁴C²].                (C.4)

All moments on the right are finite. This proves H≥0 and H≤C(1+k⁴)exp(−12k²), with a finite constant, as claimed in Proposition 4(3).

## 3. An independent exact derivation of the moving edge

Set χ₀=0, β=2tφ and a=1−2tφX. Near (X,φ,t)=(-3/2,1/3,0), a>0, and the normalized ridge equation factors exactly:

    24φa(R−L_S)=(X−1/2)² Q(X,φ,t),
    Q=2(X+1)(1−2tφX)+3φ(X+1/2)².

Put A=3−4t. This quadratic is

    Q=AφX²+(2+Aφ)X+2+3φ/4,
    disc_X Q=4[1−Aφ(1+tφ)].                          (C.5)

Thus the nearby tangency has the exact expressions

    φ_e(t)=2/[A+√(3A)],
    X_e(t)=−3/2−tφ_e(t),
    φ_e=1/3+t/3+(10/27)t²+O(t³),
    X_e=−3/2−t/3−t²/3+O(t³).                         (C.6)

The formulas hold in a neighborhood of t=0 with the indicated positive root, not across all t. The resulting integral over the rejected interval is

    I(t,0)=1/(3φ_e³)−(1−t)²/φ_e+(2/3)(1−t)³
          =20/3−20t+(52/3)t²+O(t³).                  (C.7)

For small nonzero χ₀, the ridge root is analytic using the regular expression z_r=p/(a+√(a²−χp)). Its value and its tangency equations are analytic. Nonzero tangency Jacobian supplies an analytic φ_e(t,χ₀). The strict compact margins on both sides of the edge, the cap region (C.1), and the valley bound for small χ ensure that no extra boundary enters this local integral. At M and S the vanishing margins are handled by their factored quadratic forms; one does not apply a positive uniform margin at the pins themselves. It follows that I(t,χ₀) is analytic in a neighborhood of (0,0).

**Normalization of the Jacobian.** For the literal equations (R−L_S,R_X), the determinant at the displayed point is −3/2. For (Q,Q_X) it is −6. For (N,N_X), N=24φa(R−L_S), it is −96. ## 4. Averaging requires a small-gamma split

Take fixed small t₀,χ_* within the analytic neighborhood and define

    G={|t|≤t₀, |χ₀|≤χ_*},
    t=12kB/γ², χ₀=576k²C/γ³, 0<k≤1.

On G the linear t and χ₀ terms average to zero separately, since B and C are independent and symmetric, and G is invariant under both sign changes. The quadratic t term has exact weighted moment

    E[γ⁶t²]=144k² E[B²]E[γ²]=576k².                  (C.8)

The source's remainder terms are integrable after multiplication by γ⁶:

    γ⁶|t|³=1728k³|B|³,
    γ⁶|tχ₀|=6912k³|BCγ|,
    γ⁶χ₀²=331776k⁴C².

It remains to justify truncation. On G-complement,

    γ²≤C₀ k (|B|+|C|^(2/3))=:ε,

because k^(4/3)≤k for k≤1. The bounded γ density, independent of B,C, gives for j≥0

    E[|γ|^(2j) 1{γ²≤ε} | B,C]≤C_j ε^(j+1/2).

Apply this to (C.4). The three resulting powers of k are respectively 7/2, 7/2 and 9/2; their remaining Gaussian moments are finite. The omitted constant Taylor term and the omitted γ⁶t² term are also O(k^(7/2)), the latter using k²E[γ²1{γ²≤ε}]≤Ck^(7/2)S^(3/2), S=|B|+|C|^(2/3). Thus restoring the full moments is legitimate. This proves

    E[γ⁶I]=800+9984k²+O(k³),
    H(k)=exp(−12k²)[1+(312/25)k²+O(k³)]
        =1+(12/25)k²+O(k³).                           (C.9)

Together with the global bound this yields |H−1|≤C min(1,k²). This is the specific interface consumed by Lemma 5; it has now been reconstructed rather than inferred from quadrature.

## 5. Root-authored improvement: an O(k^(7/2)) upper remainder

This section is additional support authored during review, not an independently reviewed field theorem or a claim that the fractional coefficient is nonzero.

Average the four sign choices in the analytic function I:

    I_even(t,χ)=(1/4)Σ[σ,τ=±1] I(σt,τχ).

On a smaller closed neighborhood, C⁴ regularity, evenness in each variable and (C.7) give

    |I_even(t,χ)−20/3−(52/3)t²|≤C(t⁴+χ²).           (C.10)

The mixed t²χ² and χ⁴ terms are absorbed by χ² there. Since G is invariant under these sign changes, its weighted expectation is unchanged when I is replaced by I_even. The χ² term contributes O(k⁴). For the t⁴ term, on G and B≠0,

    |γ|≥(12k|B|/t₀)^(1/2),
    γ⁶t⁴=12⁴k⁴B⁴γ^−2.

The bounded γ density gives E[γ^−2 1{|γ|≥a}]≤2‖pγ‖∞/a. Therefore

    E[γ⁶t⁴1_G]≤C k^(7/2)E|B|^(7/2)<∞.              (C.11)

At B=0 the integrand is zero; no singular bound is required. The complement estimates from §4 remain O(k^(7/2)). Hence (C.9) strengthens to

    E[γ⁶I]=800+9984k²+O(k^(7/2)),
    H(k)=1+(12/25)k²+O(k^(7/2)).                     (C.12)

The exponential's discarded k⁴ term is O(k^(7/2)) for 0<k≤1. Constants here are finite model constants, not numerically certified values. This proof avoids taking the divergent untruncated fourth moment.

**Why this does not prove a sharp fractional term.** Consider the toy function I_*(t,χ)=20/3+(52/3)t². It has the same local quadratic coefficients after even averaging, is nonnegative, and obeys an envelope of the form (C.1). The formal quantity E[γ⁶t⁴] still diverges. Nevertheless its expectation is exactly 800+9984k², and

    H_*(k)=exp(−12k²)[1+(312/25)k²]
          =1+(12/25)k²−(1944/25)k⁴+O(k⁶).

There is no nonzero k^(7/2) term in this example. It is not asserted to be a geometric rejection integral; it invalidates the inference from moment divergence alone. A sharp asymptotic would require analysis of the γ≈√k transition and a nonzero residual coefficient. Neither the source nor this review supplies that.
