## 1. Polynomial-graph proof and near-zero quantifiers

Let f:(0,h0] -> (0,1] have semialgebraic graph, with h0 a positive finite real number. Its graph can be described by a finite Boolean combination of polynomial sign conditions. Identically zero polynomials may be replaced by their constant truth values and removed. At any graph point where every remaining polynomial is nonzero, all finitely many signs are constant on an open neighborhood. The formula would then hold on that neighborhood, impossible for the graph of a single-valued function. Therefore at least one polynomial vanishes at every graph point. The product of the finitely many nonzero polynomials is nonzero, since the real polynomial ring has no zero divisors, and vanishes on the graph.

A description with no effective polynomial conditions cannot cause an exception: it would define either the empty set or the full plane, neither of which is the graph in question.

Factoring the maximal power of y gives P(h,y)=y^j Q(h,y), with Q not divisible by y. Hence A(h)=Q(h,0) is a nonzero polynomial. The strict positivity of f, rather than any continuity of f, implies Q(h,f(h))=0 at every h in the domain. Write A(h)=h^m a(h) with integer m>=0 and a(0) nonzero. Continuity of this polynomial a permits one h1 in (0,h0] for which |a(h)|>=|a(0)|/2 throughout [0,h1].

The candidate's M is finite because the derivative of Q is continuous on the compact rectangle [0,h0] x [0,1]. Its added 1 guarantees M>0. For each fixed h in (0,h1], integrate the polynomial derivative in the y variable between 0 and f(h). This gives

    (|a(0)|/2) h^m <= |Q(h,0)|
                    = |Q(h,0)-Q(h,f(h))|
                    <= M f(h).

Thus c0=|a(0)|/(2M)>0 proves the stated near-zero bound. Only polynomials are differentiated or assumed continuous. There is no differentiation of f, no continuity assumption on f, no Puiseux import, and no unjustified passage through an interior zero of A. Bounding the derivative on the larger rectangle up to h0 is valid even though the lower bound for |A(h)| is used only up to h1.

The quantifiers are correctly ordered: for each fixed semialgebraic f and h0, there exist h1,c0,m that work for every h in the smaller interval. The proof supplies no lower bound away from zero for arbitrary positive f. That limitation is exactly what the original paragraph needed to make explicit.
