## 1. Observable and conclusion

Let `rho_{d,L}(ell) d ell` be the expected number of **finite ordinary H0
superlevel bars** with lifetime in `d ell`, divided by physical torus volume
`L^d`. Birth is at a local maximum, death is at a merging saddle with one
positive Hessian direction, and the older maximum survives. Essential bars
are omitted. Heights have been integrated out; their difference alone is
retained.

The coefficient obtained independently below is

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
c_{d,L}
=\frac{\Gamma(7/6)}{24^{1/3}\sqrt\pi}\,
  \frac{1}{(2\pi)^d a^{d/2}}
  \int_{S^{d-1}}
    \frac{F_L(e)\,V_L(e)^{2/3}}{\sqrt{\det B_L(e)}}\,d\omega(e).
}\tag{1}
\]

Here `d omega` is ordinary surface area measure, not normalized probability
measure. All ingredients are specified explicitly in Sections 2–3. In
particular this is an angular integral for a finite torus, where lattice
symmetry does not imply rotational invariance.

The collision calculation gives the leading density

\[
\rho_{d,L}(\ell)=c_{d,L}\ell^{-1/3}+o(\ell^{-1/3})\qquad(\ell\downarrow0).
\tag{2}
\]

The logical status of (2) is explicit: its algebraic coefficient and its
all-max/saddle-pair counterpart follow from the two-point calculation below.
The barcode statement also requires the global bridge requirements in
Section 7. Sections 8–9 give an independent deterministic fold argument and
a Gaussian exhaustion/dominance argument intended to discharge those
requirements for the stated full-spectrum covariance. Those arguments are
included rather than replacing them with an unstated assumption that a local
max/saddle pair is a persistence pair. Formal theorem acceptance should check
these arguments, including the marked Kac–Rice approximation and conditional
C4 tightness. No other person's review has yet been used.

Since a density is only defined up to null sets, the density claim may be read
for the Kac–Rice representative when its barcode mark is admitted, or as an
essential asymptotic obtained by the upper/lower measure sandwich in Section
9. The latter also gives the unambiguous integrated statement

\[
L^{-d}\,\mathbb E\#\{\text{finite bars}:0<\operatorname{life}\le\ell\}
\sim \tfrac32 c_{d,L}\ell^{2/3}.\tag{3}
\]

## 2. Spectral moments and the two regressions

Write

\[
q_m=2\pi m/L,\quad
z_L=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}e^{-q_m^2/2},\quad
\mu_{2k}=z_L^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}q_m^{2k}e^{-q_m^2/2}.
\]

Set

\[
a=\mu_2,\quad b=\mu_4,\quad c=\mu_6,\quad
\kappa_4=b-3a^2,\quad
\kappa_6=c-15ab+30a^3.
\tag{4}
\]

The supplied normalization satisfies `Z_L=z_L^d`. The spectral random vector
`Q=(Q_1,...,Q_d)` has independent, symmetric coordinates with masses
`P(Q_i=q_m)=e^{-q_m^2/2}/z_L`. Then `K_L(x)=E cos(Q dot x)`.
All moments in this note refer to this probability distribution, including
the normalization by the zero mode. Centered means ensemble mean zero as
specified by the covariance; an additional samplewise spatial-mean removal
has not been imposed.

At one point denote the gradient by `G`, Hessian by `H`, and third directional
derivative by `u=T(e,e,e)`. Their covariances are

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(G)=aI_d,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(H_{ij},H_{kl})
=M_{ijkl}:=\mathbb E[Q_iQ_jQ_kQ_l].
\]

In coordinates,

\[
M_{ijkl}
=a^2(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}
      +\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})
 +\kappa_4\sum_p\delta_{ip}\delta_{jp}\delta_{kp}\delta_{lp}.
\tag{5}
\]

Even and odd derivative jets are independent by the symmetry of the spectral
measure. In particular `H` is independent of `(G,u)`.

The covariance of the constrained Hessian column is

\[
\boxed{B_L(e):=\operatorname{Cov}(He)
 =a^2(I_d+2ee^T)+\kappa_4\operatorname{diag}(e_1^2,...,e_d^2).}\tag{6}
\]

The sign of the gradient/third-derivative covariance is negative:

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(u,G_i)=-v_i(e),\qquad
v_i(e)=\mathbb E[Q_i(Q\cdot e)^3]
=3a^2e_i+\kappa_4e_i^3.\tag{7}
\]

Therefore `u | G=0` is a centered Gaussian of variance

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
V_L(e)
&=\mathbb E[(Q\cdot e)^6]-a^{-1}|v(e)|^2\\
&=6a^3+9a\kappa_4\sum_i e_i^4
 +(\kappa_6-\kappa_4^2/a)\sum_i e_i^6.
\end{aligned}}\tag{8}
\]

The unregressed sixth directional moment used here is

\[
\mathbb E[(Q\cdot e)^6]
=15a^3+15a\kappa_4\sum_i e_i^4+\kappa_6\sum_i e_i^6.
\tag{9}
\]

For example, in a coordinate direction (8) becomes `c-b^2/a`, which is the
one-dimensional regression variance. Omitting the regression in (7) changes
the coefficient.

All Fourier masses are strictly positive. Thus `a>0`, `B_L(e)` is positive
definite, and `V_L(e)>0`. One way to see strict positivity is that a vanishing
spectral variance would identify a nonzero quadratic polynomial, or the
cubic `(Q dot e)^3` modulo linear polynomials, with zero on every lattice
point. This is impossible. Continuity and compactness of the sphere give
uniform positive lower bounds at fixed `d,L`.

## 3. The transverse Hessian expectation

Choose any matrix `U` with orthonormal columns spanning `e perpendicular`.
Let

\[
A_e=U^THU\ \big|\ He=0,
\qquad
F_L(e):=\mathbb E[(\det A_e)^2\,\mathbf1_{\{A_e\prec0\}}].
\tag{10}
\]

`A_e` is a centered Gaussian symmetric `(d-1)` by `(d-1)` matrix. Its law is
the Gaussian regression of the **unconditioned-height** Hessian on `He=0`.
It is independent of `u | G=0`. No conditioning on the common height of the
pair is required, since the observable integrates that height out.

An explicit covariance is convenient for reproducing (10). Define

\[
R_{ab,i}=a^2\delta_{ab}e_i+\kappa_4U_{ia}U_{ib}e_i.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}((A_e)_{ab},(A_e)_{cd})
={}&a^2(\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}+\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}
                       +\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc})\\
 &+\kappa_4\sum_iU_{ia}U_{ib}U_{ic}U_{id}
 -\sum_{i,j}R_{ab,i}(B_L(e)^{-1})_{ij}R_{cd,j}.
\end{aligned}\tag{11}
\]

Equivalently, this is the Schur complement of `Cov(He)` in the joint
covariance of `(U^THU,He)`. It is nondegenerate on the free symmetric
coordinates because the covariance of the entire Hessian is nondegenerate.
Consequently `P(det A_e=0)=0`, `F_L(e)>0`, and all determinant moments are
finite. Formula (10) is independent of the choice of `U`.

For an entirely deterministic integral specification, let `s(A)` list the
free entries `A_ab`, `a<=b`, once, and let `Gamma_e` be their covariance
from (11). With `N=d(d-1)/2`,

\[
F_L(e)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}\sqrt{\det\Gamma_e}}
\int_{A\prec0}(\det A)^2
\exp\{-\tfrac12s(A)^T\Gamma_e^{-1}s(A)\}
\prod_{a\le b}dA_{ab}.\tag{11a}
\]

The measure here is free symmetric-coordinate Lebesgue measure; no
off-diagonal factor from a different matrix-measure convention is implicit.

In `d=2`, with a unit vector `v perpendicular e`, (10) simplifies to

\[
F_L(e)=\tfrac12\left[3a^2+\kappa_4\sum_i v_i^4
 -D(e)^TB_L(e)^{-1}D(e)\right],
\qquad D_i(e)=e_i(a^2+\kappa_4v_i^2).
\tag{12}
\]

The factor one half in (12) is merely the second moment of a centered scalar
Gaussian restricted to its negative half. It is not a point-pair counting
factor.

## 4. Collision jets and the height gap

Use midpoint coordinates and a directed max-to-saddle displacement `r e`:
the maximum is at `x_-=-r e/2` and the saddle at `x_+=r e/2`. Under
`G_-=G_+=0`, Taylor expansion gives

\[
G(0)=-\frac{r^2}{8}T(e,e,\cdot)+o(r^2),
\qquad H(0)e=O(r^2),
\tag{13}
\]

and

\[
H_-e=-\frac r2 T(e,e,\cdot)+O(r^2),\qquad
H_+e=+\frac r2 T(e,e,\cdot)+O(r^2).
\tag{14}
\]

For a transverse negative definite limit `A_e`, the endpoint signatures are
maximum and index `d-1` saddle precisely when the limiting `u=T(e,e,e)>0`.
The ordinary absolute Kac–Rice determinant product has limit

\[
|\det H_-\det H_+|
=\frac{r^2u^2}{4}(\det A_e)^2+o(r^2).
\tag{15}
\]

The transverse entries of the longitudinal Hessian column are only `O(r)`;
their contributions to a determinant begin one order later. The determinant
limit is a polynomial identity and does not require a matrix inverse near
singular transverse matrices.

Let `Delta=f_- - f_+`. In particular the birth-minus-death sign is positive.
From (13),

\[
\Delta
=-r\,G(0)\cdot e-\frac{r^3}{24}u+o(r^3)
=\boxed{\frac{r^3}{12}u+o(r^3)}.
\tag{16}
\]

The gap is not the unrestricted cubic endpoint difference: the first-order
term forced by the two zero gradients contributes to (16).

For a well-conditioned exact Gaussian transformation, put

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_r&=(G_-+G_+)/2,\\
D_r&=(G_+-G_-)/r,\\
W_r&=\frac{12}{r^3}\{f_- -f_+ +r\,e\cdot P_r\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{17}
\]

Then `(P_r,D_r,W_r)` converges to `(G,He,u)` in every fixed derivative
moment as `r->0`. Odd/even symmetry makes `D_r` independent of `(P_r,W_r)`
exactly. At the imposed gradients, `W_r=12 ell/r^3`.

The exact change of density has Jacobian

\[
p_{G_-,G_+,\Delta}(0,0,\ell)
=12r^{-(d+3)}p_{P_r,D_r,W_r}(0,0,12\ell/r^3).
\tag{18}
\]

The limiting density on its right is

\[
p_G(0)\,p_{He}(0)\,p_{u\mid G=0}(z),
\quad p_G(0)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}a^{-d/2},
\quad p_{He}(0)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}(\det B_L(e))^{-1/2}.
\tag{19}
\]

## 5. Radial integration and the fractional moment

Stationarity removes the base-point integral against volume: integrating the
base point gives `L^d`, which is canceled by the observable's `L^{-d}`.
Near the torus diagonal the remaining physical displacement measure is

\[
dy=r^{d-1}dr\,d\omega(e).
\]

The gradient-pair density is asymptotically `r^{-d}p_G(0)p_{He}(0)`.
Combining it with (15) and the displacement measure leaves

\[
p_G(0)p_{He}(0)F_L(e)\,
\frac14\,r\,dr\,
\mathbb E[u_+^2\,\delta(\ell-u_+r^3/12)].
\tag{20}
\]

For a fixed positive `u`,

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{u^2}{4}r\,
\delta(\ell-ur^3/12)\,dr
=12^{-1/3}u^{4/3}\ell^{-1/3}.
\tag{21}
\]

Thus the coefficient before evaluating the one-sided Gaussian moment is

\[
c_{d,L}=12^{-1/3}\int_{S^{d-1}}
p_G(0)p_{He}(0)F_L(e)\,
\mathbb E[(u_+)^{4/3}\mid G=0],d\omega(e).
\tag{22}
\]

For `u | G=0 ~ N(0,V_L(e))`,

\[
\mathbb E[(u_+)^{4/3}\mid G=0]
=2^{-1/3}\frac{\Gamma(7/6)}{\sqrt\pi}V_L(e)^{2/3},
\tag{23}
\]

which gives (1).

The same derivation without formal delta functions follows from (18): set
`r=ell^{1/3}t`, so `z=12/t^3`. Its radial coefficient is

\[
3\int_0^\infty t^{-2}(12/t^3)^2p_{u\mid G=0}(12/t^3)\,dt
=12^{-1/3}\int_0^\infty u^{4/3}p_{u\mid G=0}(u)\,du.
\tag{24}
\]

## 6. Counting, signs, height, and units

* Each geometric pair is directed by its roles: maximum first, saddle
  second. Its displacement is integrated on the full sphere. This counts
  that geometric pair once; there is **no extra factor one half**.
* Swapping the points fails the maximum-first condition. If one instead
  counted arbitrary ordered endpoints with both orientations/signs, one
  would divide that count by two and use `E |u|^{4/3}`. This equals (22),
  since `E(u_+)^{4/3}=E|u|^{4/3}/2`. Applying both reductions would be wrong.
* The one-positive-direction saddle refers to the Hessian of `f`. The
  superlevel merge therefore has negative Morse index `d-1` for `f`, or
  index one for `-f`.
* No density of the common height appears. That height is integrated out;
  adding a `p_f(0)` or fixing a field value would change the observable and
  the transverse regression.
* All spatial derivatives use physical coordinates. The Fourier wave
  numbers are `2 pi m/L`, not `m` or normalized torus coordinates.
* There is no additional `L^{-d}` multiplying (1). It has already canceled
  the base-point volume. Dimensional checking gives: `p_G p_He` has units
  length `3d`, `F` length `-4(d-1)`, and `V^{2/3}` length `-4`; hence (1)
  has units length `-d`, as required.
* A good local fold has an exterior point higher than its maximum (Section
  8), so its bar is finite and cannot be the essential surviving maximum.

## 7. Explicit global bridge requirements

The collision coefficient alone is not a proof of an ordinary persistence
coefficient. The following are the requirements used here:

**G1 — Morse barcode representation.** Almost surely the field is smooth,
Morse, with distinct critical heights and finitely many critical points on
the compact torus. Every finite ordinary H0 bar under the stated elder rule
has exactly one maximum and one binary merging index `d-1` saddle. Thus the
barcode counting measure is dominated by the counting measure of all
maximum/saddle pairs with positive height gap. This does not assert that
every such pair is paired.

**G2 — Fold isolation and elder support.** For configurations converging to
`He=0`, `A_e<0`, `u>0`, with controlled C4 derivatives, the two actual nearby
critical points constitute an isolated cap above the saddle level. The cap
cannot leave its neighborhood before the saddle, and the other saddle branch
contains a point above the cap's maximum. Then the local maximum is the
younger component and dies at this saddle. Mere Hessian signatures, or a
candidate cancellation path alone, are insufficient.

**G3 — Exhaustion at the density scale.** Noncolliding pairs must have a
bounded small-gap density, while the complete near-diagonal all-pair density
must admit an integrable bound after `r=ell^{1/3}t`. This bound must cover
small third jets, nearly singular transverse Hessians, higher-order jets,
and configurations outside any fixed good-fold cutoff. It is not enough to
compute a leading term on a preselected fold subevent.

**G4 — Kac–Rice/disintegration validity.** Two-point Kac–Rice with determinant
weights and Gaussian conditioning is valid off the diagonal, extends to the
diagonal by integrable bounds, and admits the local fold marks by continuous
approximation. A direct barcode-mark formula needs the corresponding global
measurable mark extension. Alternatively, the measure sandwich described in
Section 9 avoids relying on continuity of the global barcode mark.

The full-spectrum covariance supports G1 by smoothness, nonsingular spectral
jet laws and the standard nondegenerate-root result. Distinct heights also
follow by counting pairs marked with a height difference in a null set.
The following two sections supply the substantive content for G2 and G3.

## 8. Deterministic fold isolation argument (G2)

Fix a good compact jet set: `A_e <= -eta I`, `u>=eta`, and a C4 norm at most
`M`, with `eta>0`. Consider a sequence of functions satisfying the exact two
gradient constraints at `+/-r e/2`, with midpoint jets tending to this set.
The estimates here may be made uniform on such a set.

Write physical coordinates as `(t,y)` in directions `(e,e perpendicular)`.
Transverse negative definiteness and the implicit function theorem give a
unique transverse critical graph `y=y_r(t)` in a fixed small tube. The two
specified points lie on it, since their transverse gradients are zero.
The parameter version of the Morse lemma, or a direct completion of the
negative quadratic form, supplies tube coordinates `(t,w)` with

\[
f_r(t,w)=\phi_r(t)-|w|^2.\tag{25}
\]

At the midpoint, the exact gradient constraints imply `H(0)e=O(r^2)`;
also `G(0)=O(r^2)`, so `y_r(0)=O(r^2)`. Thus the graph's mixed Hessian
at its midpoint is `O(r^2)`, giving `y_r'(0)=O(r^2)` and
`phi_r'''(0)->u`. Choose a fixed small tube
so that `phi_r'''>eta/2` throughout it. The exact critical points give
`phi_r'(-r/2)=phi_r'(r/2)=0`. Strict convexity of `phi_r'` implies it is
positive outside those two roots and negative between them. Thus `phi_r`
has a maximum on the left and a minimum on the right, and no other critical
points in this tube.

For a fixed small `delta`, the left tube end has
`phi_r(-delta)<s`, while the right end has `phi_r(delta)>b`, for sufficiently
small `r`. These inequalities follow from the strictly positive cubic limit
and `b-s=O(r^3)`. Choose a fixed transverse wall radius whose squared size
exceeds `b-s`; on the left half of the tube its wall is below `s` by (25).
The plane `t=r/2` also has `f_r<=s`.

Consequently, for each `h in (s,b)`, the excursion component born at the
left maximum is confined within the left half-tube. No path can leave
through the left end, the transverse walls, or the saddle plane above `s`.
This is an isolation statement about the entire component, not just a
statement about a single gradient path.

At level `s`, this cap reaches the saddle and joins the right branch of
(25). That branch contains a point of height `>b`. In the global superlevel
set it therefore belongs to a component whose birth maximum has height
`>b`. The cap has experienced no earlier merge, and the older right
component survives under the given elder rule. The specified maximum dies
at the specified saddle in an ordinary finite H0 bar.

This reasoning is independent of distant critical points or possible
global loops. The isolated cap guarantees distinct components at the
saddle; the right point above `b` guarantees the elder comparison. A loop
birth at a saddle, or a saddle whose other branch is younger, does not
satisfy this good-fold conclusion.

For the conditioned Gaussian fields in (17), uniform nonsingularity of
`Cov(P_r,D_r,W_r)` and smooth derivative covariance imply conditional C4
tightness for fixed `z` in compact sets. The regression means are bounded
in C4 and linear in `z`; the residual covariance is bounded by the original
smooth field covariance. More explicitly, take an integer `k>d/2+5`.
The original Fourier decay gives finite expected squared Sobolev `H^k`
norm. Gaussian conditioning decreases each derivative's residual variance,
while the conditional mean functions have uniformly bounded `H^k` norm
for bounded `z`: their covariance difference quotients converge smoothly
to covariances with `(G,He,u)`, and the conditioning matrices are uniformly
invertible. This gives uniform conditional `H^k` moment bounds and, by
compact Sobolev embedding, tightness in C4. The same regression convergence
identifies the conditional limit. On good cutoffs, the jet limits and the preceding
deterministic argument make the true-pair indicator tend to one. Allowing
`eta->0` and `M->infinity` covers the limiting event `A_e<0,u>0`.

## 9. Gaussian domination and exhaustion (G3–G4)

For any two distinct torus points, the Gaussian vector
`(G_-,G_+,f_- -f_+)` is nondegenerate. If a linear combination had zero
variance, positivity of every Fourier mass would make a distribution
supported at the two distinct points, consisting of derivative evaluations
and value differences, have all Fourier coefficients zero. It would then
be the zero distribution, forcing every coefficient of that combination
to be zero. This also proves gradient-pair nondegeneracy.

Therefore, away from a fixed diagonal neighborhood, joint densities and
conditional Hessian moments are bounded uniformly for `ell` near zero.
The torus has finite volume, so that entire off-diagonal contribution is
`O(1)=o(ell^{-1/3})`. This bounds true persistence pairs there as well. It
does not deny the existence of short bars whose endpoints are far apart.

Near the diagonal, the covariance of `(P_r,D_r,W_r)` converges uniformly
over the sphere to the positive definite covariance of `(G,He,u)`. Thus,
for `z=12 ell/r^3` and sufficiently small `r`,

\[
p_{P_r,D_r,W_r}(0,0,z)\le C\exp(-c_0z^2).\tag{26}
\]

Under `D_r=0`, the endpoint columns `H_+e` and `H_-e` are `O(r)` in all
conditional Gaussian moments. Indeed `(H_+e-D_r)/r` and
`(H_-e-D_r)/r` are Gaussian linear combinations of third derivatives with
bounded covariance, by the integral form of Taylor's theorem. The remaining
Hessian columns have bounded covariance. Conditioning additionally on
`P_r=0,W_r=z` gives means bounded by `C|z|`. Hadamard's determinant
bound and Gaussian moment bounds consequently give

\[
\mathbb E[|\det H_-\det H_+|\mid P_r=D_r=0,W_r=z]
\le Cr^2(1+|z|)^{2d}.\tag{27}
\]

Use (18), physical polar measure, (26) and (27). The radial density integrand
of **all** ordered critical pairs is bounded by

\[
Cr^{-2}(1+|12\ell/r^3|)^{2d}
\exp\{-c_0(12\ell/r^3)^2\}.\tag{28}
\]

After `r=ell^{1/3}t`, multiplication by `ell^{1/3}` yields an
ell-independent integrable majorant

\[
Ct^{-2}(1+12/t^3)^{2d}\exp\{-c_0(12/t^3)^2\}.
\tag{29}
\]

It is integrable at zero by exponential decay and at infinity by `t^{-2}`.
This is the needed density-scale exhaustion. In particular no separate
assumption of a positive lower bound on `u` or on the transverse eigenvalues
is imposed in the final integral.

For fixed `t>0`, the conditional limiting third jet is `u=12/t^3>0`;
the endpoint determinant/signature limit is exactly (15) times
`1_{A_e<0}`. The transverse boundary has probability zero. Dominated
convergence with (29) yields (1) for the full maximum/saddle gap density.
It also controls the discarded tails and bad-jet sectors of any good-fold
approximation.

For the persistence observable, use upper and lower counting measures:
every finite H0 bar is among the maximum/saddle pairs (G1), while every
good isolated fold is a true finite H0 bar (Section 8). Kac–Rice for smooth
local cutoff marks, followed by bounded approximation, gives the lower
densities. Take increasingly broad good cutoffs; their coefficients tend to
(1) by the same domination. The all-pair upper density also has coefficient
(1). This proves the integrated statement and the essential density
asymptotic, with no assumption that a globally defined persistence mark is
continuous. If a direct global marked Kac–Rice representation is admitted,
the identical limit gives (2) for that representative.

The remaining formalization checks for an acceptance review are the
uniform tube version of Section 8, the conditional C4 tightness statement,
and the precise marked-measure approximation in this sandwich. They are
openly retained as proof verification obligations; neither a numerical
test nor agreement among agents would prove them.

## 10. Independent consistency checks

As `L->infinity`, the spectral coordinate law tends to `N(0,1)`, so
`a->1`, `b->3`, `c->15`, `kappa_4,kappa_6->0`. Then

\[
B(e)=I+2ee^T,\quad \det B(e)=3,\quad V(e)=6,
\quad A_e\overset d=\operatorname{GOE}_{d-1}+\sqrt{2/3}\,Z I.
\tag{30}
\]

Here the GOE convention is independent off-diagonal variance one and
diagonal variance two, and `Z~N(0,1)` is independent. The `2/3` scalar
variance comes from conditioning the longitudinal Hessian on zero; it
would be one without that constraint.

Writing `F_{d-1}^*=E[(det A)^2 1_{A<0}]` for (30), the continuum check is

\[
c_{d,\infty}
=\frac{|S^{d-1}|}{(2\pi)^d\sqrt3}\,
 (3/2)^{1/3}\frac{\Gamma(7/6)}{\sqrt\pi}\,F_{d-1}^*.
\tag{31}
\]

For `d=2`, `Var A=8/3` and `F_1^*=4/3`, hence

\[
c_{2,\infty}
=\frac{2}{3\pi\sqrt3}(3/2)^{1/3}
\frac{\Gamma(7/6)}{\sqrt\pi}.
\tag{32}
\]

These are consistency checks derived within this note, not values read
from project constants or earlier results. The accompanying standard-library
script checks the finite-spectrum tensor and regression identities by
independent direct lattice summation and checks the radial change of
variables numerically. It does not prove the global bridge.

import itertools
import math


def dot(x, y):
    return sum(a * b for a, b in zip(x, y))


def normalized(x):
    s = math.sqrt(dot(x, x))
    return tuple(v / s for v in x)


def inverse(matrix):
    n = len(matrix)
    rows = [list(row) + [float(i == j) for j in range(n)]
            for i, row in enumerate(matrix)]
    for j in range(n):
        p = max(range(j, n), key=lambda i: abs(rows[i][j]))
        rows[p], rows[j] = rows[j], rows[p]
        assert abs(rows[j][j]) > 1e-14
        pivot = rows[j][j]
        rows[j] = [v / pivot for v in rows[j]]
        for i in range(n):
            if i != j:
                factor = rows[i][j]
                rows[i] = [a - factor * b for a, b in zip(rows[i], rows[j])]
    return [row[n:] for row in rows]


def bilinear(x, matrix, y):
    return sum(x[i] * matrix[i][j] * y[j]
               for i in range(len(x)) for j in range(len(y)))

    basis = []
    for j in range(len(e)):
        v = [float(i == j) for i in range(len(e))]
        for axis in [e] + basis:

            v = [x - proj * y for x, y in zip(v, axis)]
        if dot(v, v) > 1e-14:
            basis.append(normalized(v))
        if len(basis) == len(e) - 1:
            break
    return basis

    assert count % 2 == 0

    even = math.fsum(fun(lo + i * step) for i in range(2, count, 2))
