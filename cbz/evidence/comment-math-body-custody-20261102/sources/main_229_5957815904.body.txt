## 2. Signed coordinate and exact weighted pushforward

For every c in R and psi>a, define

    q_c(psi) = 4(psi-2c) sqrt(psi+c).                   (3)

The root is real and positive because psi>|c|. Importantly this q is
signed: for c>0 it is negative on a<psi<2c. Equation (1) is equivalent to

    e = 1 iff q_c(psi)>=|R|.                           (4)

Both the sign test and the squared comparison in (1) are necessary for
this equivalence. Replacing q by |q| loses the sign test.

**Lemma 1 (pushforward and uniform Lipschitz constant).** The map q_c is
strictly increasing, with range (q_min,infinity), where

    q_min = -4 sqrt(2) c^(3/2)  if c>0;  q_min=0 otherwise.

The pushforward of w_c(psi)dpsi has density

    h_c(q) = (psi-c)(psi+c)^(3/2)/(6psi), psi=q_c^(-1)(q),

on that range. Extend h_c by zero for q<=q_min. This continuous extension
is nonnegative, nondecreasing, and globally 1/9-Lipschitz. On q>=0 it is
1/16-Lipschitz. Its value at zero is

    h_c(0) = (3c_+)^(3/2)/12.                          (5)

*Proof.* Direct differentiation gives

    q_c'(psi)=6psi/sqrt(psi+c)>0,
    w_c(psi)/q_c'(psi)=(psi-c)(psi+c)^(3/2)/(6psi).

Both endpoint limits and (5) follow from these expressions: at c>0 the
zero of q is psi=2c; at c<=0 its left endpoint is zero and h tends to zero.
When c=0, explicitly q=4psi^(3/2) and h=q/24 on q>0.

For x=c/psi in (-1,1), differentiation once more yields

    dh_c/dq = (3+2x+x^2+2x^3)/72 =: p(x)/72.           (6)

Here p(-1)=0, p(1)=8, and

    p'(x)=6(x+1/6)^2+11/6>0.

Thus 0<=dh/dq<=1/9. If q>=0, then x<=1/2 (automatically when c<=0),
and p(1/2)=9/2 gives dh/dq<=1/16. Integrating these derivative bounds,
and using h(q_min)=0 when crossing the zero extension, proves the
Lipschitz statements. No root derivative is left singular in this weighted
coordinate. QED.

**Theorem 2 (uniform weighted boundary tube).** For every c,R in R and
epsilon>0, let

    T_epsilon(c,R) = integral_{psi>|c|}
        (psi^2-c^2) 1{|q_c(psi)-|R||<=epsilon} dpsi.

Then

    T_epsilon(c,R) <= epsilon (3c_+)^(3/2)/6
                    + epsilon |R|/8 + 2epsilon^2/9.    (7)

*Proof.* By Lemma 1 this is the integral of h_c over an interval of length
2epsilon centered at |R| (the zero extension covers any part below
q_min). On that interval

    h_c(q)<=h_c(|R|)+epsilon/9
           <=h_c(0)+|R|/16+epsilon/9.

Multiply by 2epsilon and use (5). In particular (7) includes the strip
psi<2c and does not need a second, unweighted sign-boundary exception.
QED.

## 3. Gaussian weighting removes the apparent inverse-gamma singularity

**Corollary 3 (a bound uniform down to k=0).** Under the independent
Gaussian jets in (2), for every K>=0, 0<k<=K and 0<epsilon<=1,

    E[gamma^6 T_epsilon(c(k),R(k))]
        <= 500(1+12K)^2 epsilon.                      (8)

The bound applies to the algebraic classifier and, under the exact
Theorem A interface, to its model elder boundary. No positive lower
cutoff on k or |gamma| is used. The set gamma=0 is null; arbitrary values
there do not affect the expectation.

*Proof.* Write G=|gamma|. Gaussian integration by parts gives
E[G^4]=12, E[G^6]=120. We may use the elementary upper estimates

    E|B|<2, E|C|<3, E[G^3]<11,
    E|B|^(3/2)<=E[1+B^2]=3, E[G^3]<=1+E[G^4]=13.

The first three follow from Cauchy--Schwarz and variances 2,6 and
E[G^6]=120<121. Independence is used only when factoring the mixed
moments below. Since c_+<=1+12k|B|/G^2, the inequality
(x+y)^(3/2)<=sqrt(2)(x^(3/2)+y^(3/2)) gives

    E[G^6 (3c_+)^(3/2)/6]
       <= 2[120+39(12K)^(3/2)]
       <= 240+78(1+12K)^2.                            (9)

The factor 2 is a deliberate upper bound for sqrt(6)/2. Also,

    G^6 |R| <= 8G^6+144k|B|G^4+576k^2|C|G^3,

so E[G^6|R|]/8 <=120+432K+2376K^2. Finally, for epsilon<=1 the last
term of (7) contributes at most (80/3)epsilon. Adding all terms yields

    [1394/3+2304K+13608K^2]epsilon
        <=500(1+12K)^2 epsilon,

because the difference is
106/3+9696K+58392K^2>=0. All dominating functions have explicitly finite
Gaussian moments, so this is also a justification of integrability.
The constants are not optimized. QED.

**Conditional transfer corollary.** Let e_tilde be another measurable
binary decision on the same typed parameter space. If, outside null
sets, e_tilde can differ from (4) only where
|q_c(psi)-|R||<=epsilon, then its determinant-weighted symmetric
difference obeys (7), and its Gaussian weighted difference obeys (8).
In the normalization (2), the difference in the corresponding rejection
functions is at most (5/8)(1+12K)^2 epsilon. This follows from absolute
value bounded by the symmetric-difference integral; exp(-12k^2)<=1.

For a jet-dependent epsilon, (7) remains valid pointwise before
averaging. A usable field estimate must supply integrability of its
products with the displayed weights; a uniform epsilon is sufficient,
not necessary. A changed conditional law or a changed determinant
weight requires its own comparison and is not absorbed into this
decision-only corollary.

## 4. Negative result: squared margins really can lose a logarithm

Keeping the sign by restricting to psi>=2c does not by itself repair the
squared-equation margin. We give an analytic obstruction, not a sampled
numerical observation.

Fix c=1 and let p be the standard normal density. For 0<delta<1 define

    S_delta = integral_{|R|<=1} p(R)
       integral_{psi>=2, 0<=q_1(psi)<=1}
        w_1(psi) 1{|q_1(psi)^2-R^2|<=delta} dpsi dR.

**Proposition 4.** S_delta=Theta(delta log(1/delta)) as delta decreases
to zero. In particular S_delta is not O(delta).

*Proof.* On 0<=q<=1, Lemma 1 gives

    h_0 := h_1(0)=3sqrt(3)/12 >0,
    h_0<=h_1(q)<=h_0+1/16.

Set v=|R|, s=q+v, z=q-v. Then dq dv=(1/2)ds dz and q^2-v^2=sz.
The triangle q,v>=0, q+v<=A has |z|<=s<=A. For 0<delta<A^2, the
area of its intersection with |q^2-v^2|<=delta is exactly

    A_delta(A)=integral_0^A min(s,delta/s) ds
              =delta/2+delta log(A/sqrt(delta)).        (10)

The A=1 triangle lies inside the unit (q,v) square; that square lies
inside the A=2 triangle. Let p_min=p(1)>0 and p_max=p(0). Since p is
even and lies between these constants on [-1,1],

    2p_min h_0 A_delta(1)
       <= S_delta <=2p_max(h_0+1/16) A_delta(2).        (11)

Equation (10) proves the stated two-sided order. In particular the
lower bound divided by delta grows as p_min h_0 log(1/delta). QED.

The obstruction also occurs under the actual Gaussian jet averaging
in (2), not just under an auxiliary choice of p. Fix any k>0 and
restrict to 1<=gamma<=2, 1<=c<=2, |R|<=1, and 0<=q_c(psi)<=1.
The change from (gamma,c,R) to the original jets is

    B=(1-c)gamma^2/(12k),
    C=(R+4-12c)gamma^3/(576k^2),
    |Jacobian|=gamma^5/(6912k^3).

Its Gaussian density is continuous and strictly positive on this
compact box, hence has positive minimum m_k and finite maximum M_k.
Also 1<=gamma^6<=64 and h_1(0)<=h_c(q)<=h_2(0)+1/16 there. The same
triangle calculation, integrated over gamma and c (each interval has
length 1), bounds this restricted weighted squared tube between

    m_k h_1(0) delta[1+log(1/delta)]

and a finite k-dependent constant times delta[1+log(1/delta)]. Thus even
the full nonnegative Gaussian weighted squared tube cannot be O(delta)
for any fixed k>0. No uniform lower constant as k tends to zero is
claimed; m_k can become very small. Exceptional classifier curves have
zero volume and do not remove this positive-volume obstruction.

This is a counterexample to deducing a linear weighted-tube bound from
an absolute perturbation of g_c-R^2 alone. It is not a counterexample to
Conjecture 7, and it does not assert a logarithm in the actual field
asymptotic. Cancellation or a stronger perturbation metric could remove
the loss. An O(delta) perturbation of the squared equation does NOT
automatically yield an O(delta) perturbation of the signed coordinate.
