## 1. The theorem and its exact hypotheses

Let E be a finite-dimensional real normed vector space, let p be a point of E, and let f:E→R be twice continuously Fréchet differentiable on an open neighborhood O of p. Set a=f(p) and let

S_a = {q∈E : a≤f(q)}

carry the actual subspace topology inherited from E. The function may be defined on all E without being C² away from O. Suppose:

1. Df(p)=0.
2. There are actual linear subspaces Y,Z of E such that E=Y⊕Z.
3. The Hessian H=D²f(p) is strictly negative on Y∖{0} and strictly positive on Z∖{0}.

No orthogonality of Y and Z, either for the ambient inner product or for H, is assumed. No local path connectivity, path, ridge, Morse chart, supplied inverse certificate, critical-point finiteness, distinct critical heights, periodicity, or compactness is assumed. Symmetry of H follows from C² regularity.

**Critical-boundary theorem.** There exist positive r₀,s₀, a continuously differentiable map κ from the Z-ball B_Z(0,r₀) to Y with κ(0)=0, and actual ambient open sets

Ω_{r,s} = {p+y+z : z∈Z, ||z||<r, y∈Y, ||y−κ(z)||<s},
                0<r<r₀, 0<s<s₀,

contained in O, such that their actual subtype restrictions

N_{r,s} = {q∈S_a : q.val∈Ω_{r,s}}

are open, contain p as a point of S_a, and are path connected. They are cofinal among all neighborhoods of p in S_a. In particular,

U∈𝓝_{S_a}(p)
 iff ∃r,s, 0<r<r₀ ∧ 0<s<s₀ ∧ N_{r,s}⊆U.

The family is directed by simultaneous minima of the radii. Thus it is a genuine neighborhood filter basis, and also proves the literal C297-shaped conclusion

(𝓝 p).HasBasis (fun V => V∈𝓝 p ∧ IsPathConnected V) id

on the actual subtype S_a. The displayed formula is an interface description, not a claim that this paper has been encoded or checked in Lean.

Only C² regularity of f is required. The implicit map κ is asserted only C¹. The proof of C² regularity of the reduced height below uses the stationary envelope identity, rather than an unsupported second derivative of κ.

## 2. Actual complementary coordinates and the partial-gradient IFT

Equip Y and Z with their inherited norms. The linear map

T:Y×Z→E,     T(y,z)=y+z,

is a bijection because the subspaces are complementary. In finite dimensions T and its inverse are continuous linear maps. Its inverse consists of the continuous complementary projections P_Y,P_Z. We use the affine homeomorphism A(y,z)=p+T(y,z). No estimate in the proof treats T as an isometry.

On the open set A^{-1}(O), define g(y,z)=f(p+y+z). Then g is C², g(0,0)=a, Dg(0,0)=0, and its Hessian at (0,0) is the actual original Hessian applied to the included directions. Write

F(y,z)=D_y g(y,z)∈Y*,

where Y* is the continuous real dual of Y. Since g is C², F is C¹. Also F(0,0)=0. Its derivative in the y variable at (0,0) is

A_Y:Y→Y*,     (A_Y v)(u)=H(v,u),     v,u∈Y.

When Y has positive dimension, strict negative definiteness makes A_Y injective: A_Y v=0 implies H(v,v)=0, hence v=0. The finite-dimensional dual has the same dimension as Y, so A_Y is bijective. Its inverse is continuous. This derives the exact IFT inverse from the literal negative restriction; an inverse is not an additional hypothesis.

Assume for the moment that Y and Z have positive dimension. Apply the ordinary C¹ implicit function theorem to (z,y)↦F(y,z), with parameter z and unknown y. After shrinking neighborhoods, it gives an open neighborhood V of 0 in Z and a C¹ map κ:V→Y satisfying

κ(0)=0,                 D_y g(κ(z),z)=0 for every z∈V.

Shrink V if needed so that the whole graph lies in A^{-1}(O). This is one common neighborhood and one chosen function κ.

If Y={0}, instead define κ(z)=0 on a sufficiently small Z neighborhood; the stationarity assertion is vacuous and κ is smooth. If Z={0}, define κ(0)=0 on the zero space; its graph is just (0,0), and stationarity there follows from Df(p)=0. These cases require no appeal to a unit sphere or a nontrivial IFT. They also satisfy all equations used below. When a zero-space linear inverse appears as a formal bookkeeping object, it is the unique map on that zero space; the proof does not infer invertibility from an empty sphere.

## 3. Reduced C² regularity without assuming κ is C²

Define the reduced height on V by

h(z)=g(κ(z),z).

It is initially C¹, because g is C² and κ is C¹. For u∈Z the chain rule and stationarity give, at every z∈V,

Dh(z)[u]
 = D_y g(κ(z),z)[Dκ(z)u] + D_z g(κ(z),z)[u]
 = D_z g(κ(z),z)[u].

The map (y,z)↦D_z g(y,z) is C¹ with values in Z*. The graph map z↦(κ(z),z) is C¹. Their composition is therefore C¹. By the displayed equality this composition is the derivative map Dh, so Dh is C¹ and h is C². This is the stationary envelope argument; it uses no D²κ and no C³ hypothesis on g.

In particular, h(0)=a and Dh(0)=0.

Put L=Dκ(0):Z→Y. Differentiate the identity F(κ(z),z)=0 at zero. For every u∈Z and v∈Y it yields

H(Lu+u,v)=0.                                      (1)

Here Lu and u are their actual included vectors in E. If B:Z→Y* is defined by (Bu)(v)=H(u,v), then (1) says A_Y L+B=0 and hence L=−A_Y^{-1}B when the spaces are nonzero. There is no reason for L to vanish.

Differentiating the envelope identity once gives, for u,w∈Z,

D²h(0)[u,w] = H(Lu+u,w).

Equation (1), applied to v=Lw, permits the equivalent formula

D²h(0)[u,w] = H(Lu+u,Lw+w).                       (2)

Symmetry of H is available from C² regularity. Taking v=Lu in (1), and then expanding (2) on the diagonal, gives

H(Lu,Lu)+H(u,Lu)=0,
D²h(0)[u,u]
 = H(u,u)+2H(u,Lu)+H(Lu,Lu)
 = H(u,u)−H(Lu,Lu).                               (3)

For nonzero u∈Z the first term on the right is strictly positive. The second original quadratic value H(Lu,Lu) is nonpositive, including the case Lu=0. Thus D²h(0)[u,u]>0.

This proves positive definiteness on the actual positive complement even with nonzero cross terms. Equivalently the reduced Hessian is the Schur complement D−B* A_Y^{-1}B, where D is the Z-restriction of H and B* is the dual transpose, not an assumed ambient orthogonal projection. The proof of positivity is (3) from the literal negative/positive restrictions. It does not assume a positive Schur complement, an orthogonal split, a prior Morse normal form, or an unproved identification of the index.

If Y={0}, (3) reduces to the given positive restriction. If Z={0}, the positive-definiteness assertion has no nonzero vector and h is the constant a on a singleton; no minimum over an empty unit sphere is taken.

## 4. Uniform signs, a common valid product, and the ridge height

First obtain uniform fiber concavity on a fixed product neighborhood. If Y is nonzero, the continuous positive function v↦−H(v,v) has a positive minimum m_Y on the compact unit sphere of Y. Set α=m_Y/2>0. Continuity in operator norm of the y-direction Hessian of g gives positive product radii R and ρ for which

D²_{yy}g(y,z)[v,v] ≤ −α||v||²                     (4)

whenever ||y||<R, ||z||<ρ, v∈Y. These radii may be shrunk to put the whole product inside A^{-1}(O). If Y={0}, take α=1; (4) then reads 0≤0 for its only vector, and choose any positive R permitted by the product-neighborhood inclusion.

Next obtain a positive reduced-height Hessian on a Z-ball. If Z is nonzero, (3) and compactness of the Z unit sphere give a positive minimum m_Z of u↦D²h(0)[u,u] on that sphere. Put β=m_Z/2>0. Since h is C², shrink its ball so that

D²h(z)[u,u] ≥ β||u||²                            (5)

for all z in that ball and u∈Z. If Z={0}, take β=1; its inequality is 0≥0 and h(0)=a.

Choose one positive radius ρ₁ small enough that all of the following hold simultaneously for ||z||<ρ₁:

- κ is defined and C¹, and the stationarity identity holds;
- (4) holds for every ||y||<R at this z;
- (5) holds at z;
- ||κ(z)||<R/2.

The last condition follows from continuity at zero and κ(0)=0. The first three are restrictions of neighborhoods already obtained. All balls in Z used henceforth have center zero and lie inside this one ball, so every radial segment tz, 0≤t≤1, remains in the domain.

For each such z apply the one-dimensional C² formula to φ(t)=h(tz). Since h(0)=a and Dh(0)=0,

h(z)−a
 = ∫_0^1 (1−t)D²h(tz)[z,z]dt
 ≥ (β/2)||z||²
 ≥ 0.                                           (6)

Only the final inequality h(z)≥a is needed for the paths. For Z={0}, (6) is the identity h(0)=a.

For each fixed z in this ball, (4) implies that y↦g(y,z) is concave on the convex ball B_Y(0,R). Indeed, its restriction to any segment in that ball has nonpositive second derivative, which is the one-dimensional concavity criterion. In particular, for y₀,y₁ in that ball and 0≤t≤1,

g((1−t)y₀+ty₁,z)
 ≥ (1−t)g(y₀,z)+t g(y₁,z).                       (7)

This establishes concavity on the full segment, rather than inferring it from the Hessian only at the critical point.

## 5. One family of actual ambient open tubes

Set r₀=ρ₁ and s₀=R/2. For 0<r<r₀ and 0<s<s₀ use the tubes in the theorem:

Ω_{r,s}={p+y+z : ||z||<r, ||y−κ(z)||<s}.

Each point in the coordinate tube satisfies

||y|| ≤ ||κ(z)||+||y−κ(z)|| < R/2+R/2=R.

Thus Ω_{r,s} is contained in the fixed valid product and hence in O. The graph point (κ(z),z) and the entire fiber segment between (y,z) and (κ(z),z) lie in that same fixed product.

The tube is ambient open. On the open domain Y×B_Z(0,r₀), the shear map

(y,z)↦(y−κ(z),z)

is a homeomorphism, with inverse (v,z)↦(v+κ(z),z). The coordinate tube is its inverse image of the product of the open Y-ball of radius s and the open Z-ball of radius r. It is therefore open in that open domain and hence in Y×Z. The affine homeomorphism A carries it to Ω_{r,s}, which is open in E. This argument uses actual complementary coordinates and continuity; it does not replace the split by an orthogonal one.

Also p∈Ω_{r,s} because κ(0)=0. Consequently N_{r,s}=S_a∩Ω_{r,s}, interpreted in the actual subtype, is an open neighborhood of p in S_a.

## 6. Actual paths remain in the same tube and the same closed level

Fix q=p+y+z∈Ω_{r,s} with f(q)≥a. Its coordinates are unique. Let v=y−κ(z). Define the first leg, from q to its ridge point, by

γ₁(t)=p+κ(z)+(1−t)v+z,           0≤t≤1.

It is continuous, γ₁(0)=q, and γ₁(1)=p+κ(z)+z. Its z coordinate stays z, and its displacement from the ridge is (1−t)v, whose norm is at most ||v||<s. Therefore this entire leg is in Ω_{r,s}. By (7), with endpoints y and κ(z), and then by (6),

f(γ₁(t))
 = g((1−t)y+tκ(z),z)
 ≥ (1−t)g(y,z)+t g(κ(z),z)
 = (1−t)f(q)+t h(z)
 ≥ a.

All these points lie in the same actual closed superlevel S_a, with the fixed threshold a=f(p). No threshold is changed along the path.

Define the second leg, from the ridge point to p, by

γ₂(t)=p+κ((1−t)z)+(1−t)z,       0≤t≤1.

It is continuous because κ is continuous on its ball. Its endpoints are γ₂(0)=p+κ(z)+z and γ₂(1)=p. The Z norm is at most ||z||<r and the fiber displacement is zero<s, so it stays in the same Ω_{r,s}. Its height is h((1−t)z)≥a by (6), so it also stays in S_a.

Concatenate γ₁ and γ₂. Explicitly, use γ₁(2t) for 0≤t≤1/2 and γ₂(2t−1) for 1/2≤t≤1. The two formulas agree at t=1/2, so the pasting lemma on the two closed half intervals proves continuity. The concatenation has endpoints q,p and lies in N_{r,s}. As a map into E it has continuous range in S_a and in Ω_{r,s}; the induced map into the subtype N_{r,s} is continuous by the subspace universal property.

Thus every point of N_{r,s} can be joined to p inside N_{r,s}. Given two points q,q', concatenate the path q→p with the reverse of q'→p. It follows that the nonempty N_{r,s} is path connected.

The proof has never claimed that a straight ambient segment from q to p stays in S_a. Its first leg changes only the negative fiber and uses concavity; its second leg follows the implicit ridge and uses the reduced-height inequality.

## 7. Cofinality and the exact neighborhood filter basis

Let U be any neighborhood of p in the actual subtype S_a. By the definition of the subspace topology there is an ambient open neighborhood W of p such that S_a∩W⊆U.

The inverse image A^{-1}(W) is an open neighborhood of (0,0) in Y×Z. Hence there are ε_Y,ε_Z>0 with

B_Y(0,ε_Y)×B_Z(0,ε_Z) ⊆ A^{-1}(W).

Continuity of κ at zero provides δ>0 such that κ is defined and ||κ(z)||<ε_Y/2 whenever ||z||<δ. Choose positive r,s with

r<min(r₀,ε_Z,δ),            s<min(s₀,ε_Y/2).

These choices are independent of the point q. Every p+y+z in Ω_{r,s} satisfies ||z||<ε_Z and

||y||≤||κ(z)||+||y−κ(z)||<ε_Y/2+ε_Y/2=ε_Y.

Therefore Ω_{r,s}⊆W and N_{r,s}⊆U. This proves cofinality in every actual subtype neighborhood, rather than merely the existence of one connected neighborhood.

Conversely, any set U containing some N_{r,s} is a neighborhood of p because N_{r,s} is an open set containing p. This proves the asserted filter equivalence.

For two admissible pairs of radii, their coordinate definitions use the same κ and give

N_{min(r₁,r₂),min(s₁,s₂)}
 = N_{r₁,s₁} ∩ N_{r₂,s₂}.

Both minimum radii are positive and remain below r₀,s₀. Thus the family is directed and satisfies the usual filter-basis requirements explicitly.

Finally, to obtain the C297-shaped basis indexed by all path-connected neighborhoods, let U∈𝓝 p. Cofinality supplies N_{r,s}⊆U and N_{r,s}∈𝓝 p with IsPathConnected N_{r,s}. In the reverse direction, a set containing any neighborhood is a neighborhood. This is precisely the HasBasis statement given in Section 1.

## 8. Zero-dimensional cases

The preceding construction works with the stated conventions. To make its geometry and sphere arguments explicit:

- If Y={0} and Z is nonzero, κ=0 and h(z)=f(p+z). The negative-fiber inequality is vacuous with α=1. Equation (6) shows that a sufficiently small ambient Z-ball lies in S_a. Ω_{r,s} is that ball in actual E=Z, independent of s. The first path is constant and the second is radial.
- If Z={0} and Y is nonzero, κ(0)=0 and h=a. There is no positive unit sphere and β=1 is a vacuous bound. Taylor's one-dimensional formula along y, Df(p)=0 and (4) give g(y,0)≤a−(α/2)||y||² for sufficiently small y. Thus the tube's intersection with S_a is exactly {p}. It is an open neighborhood in the relative topology, even though {p} need not be open in E. The paths are constant on that intersection.
- If Y=Z={0}, complementarity forces E={0}. The ambient space, its relevant closed superlevel, every tube and every N_{r,s} are the singleton p. Choose any positive r₀,s₀, for example 1, and κ=0. All openness, path and cofinality assertions are immediate.

No compactness argument is applied to the empty unit sphere of a zero space, and no nontrivial inverse-function assertion is needed in these cases.

## 9. All-point Euclidean closed-superlevel LPC

**All-level corollary.** Let f:E→R be C² on E. Suppose every critical point p has actual complementary subspaces with the negative/positive Hessian restrictions in Section 1. Then for every real a, the actual closed-superlevel subtype {q∈E : a≤f(q)} is locally path connected.

For completeness, the three point cases are as follows. Fix x in that subtype.

**Interior point a<f(x).** Continuity gives an ambient open neighborhood where f>a. Arbitrarily small convex balls about x inside that neighborhood are contained in the superlevel, hence give open path-connected subtype neighborhoods.

**Regular boundary point a=f(x), Df(x)≠0.** Put ℓ=Df(x). Choose e∈E with ℓ(e)=1, and K=ker ℓ. The complementary projection π_K(v)=v−ℓ(v)e has values in K. The C¹ map

Φ(q)=(f(q),π_K(q−x))

has derivative at x given by v↦(ℓ(v),π_K(v)), a continuous linear isomorphism E→R×K with inverse (t,k)↦te+k. The ordinary C¹ inverse function theorem supplies an actual local chart with first coordinate exactly f. Restrict that chart to the superlevel and the halfspace H_a={(t,k):a≤t}. In the chart target, intersections of sufficiently small balls centered at Φ(x) with H_a are convex, relatively open in H_a, path connected, and cofinal among neighborhoods there. Their inverse images are the required open path-connected neighborhoods at x in the actual superlevel.

This is the conventional regular-point argument realized by the existing C296/C297 source route. It does not assume the desired chart as a public hypothesis: the nonzero derivative constructs it. For the project's stronger IsSmooth hypothesis, the exact existing C297 literal theorem already supplies this regular/interior conclusion.

**Critical boundary point a=f(x), Df(x)=0.** Use the assumed actual Hessian split at x and the critical-boundary theorem proved above.

These cases exhaust x because a≤f(x). At every point and inside every open neighborhood there is an open path-connected neighborhood. This is the definition/standard basis characterization of local path connectivity, proving the corollary. If a superlevel is empty, the assertion is vacuous.

For the exact project predicates, IsSmooth supplies all finite ContDiff and hence C². IsMorseDistinct supplies the needed HessianHasInertia at every critical point; its distinct-critical-height clause is not used. The finrank equalities identify the source's index but are not needed in the neighborhood argument beyond finite dimension. Thus the exact smooth Morse-distinct source hypotheses imply all-point Euclidean closed-superlevel LPC conventionally, without adding a full Morse chart or target LPC as a premise. Periodicity, compact quotient, critical finiteness and elder pairing remain outside this local proof.
