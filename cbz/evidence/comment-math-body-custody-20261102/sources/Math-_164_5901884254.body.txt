This is a research lead, not an adopted theorem for the conditioned Gaussian field. The displayed proposition divides a mixing bound by selected-process intensity. Its coupling convention needs care: the definition uses half-L1 beta, whereas equation2.6 writes 2beta. We retain its conservative displayed constant4 and make no paper-wide acceptance claim.

**Conditional inference.** Put delta=r^3. Suppose the proposition actually applies to stationary processes xi_r and zeta_r with the stated measurable restrictions and the locality used by the coupling proof (the selected anchors/tuples in B must be determined by xi restricted to B+D). Fix p>1. Suppose uniformly in r that E[zeta_r(B)]>=a delta>0 and E[zeta_r(B)^p]<=C delta. With beta_r the joint-restriction-law coefficient for B+D and B+A, its displayed bound implies

TV((xi_r)_A, (xi_r^{0,zeta_r})_A) <= (4 C^(1/p)/a) (beta_r/delta)^(1-1/p).

**Complete algebraic proof.** For each fixed r and every sufficiently large finite q, Proposition2.2 bounds TV by 4 (E zeta(B)^p)^(1/p)/(E zeta(B)) times beta_r^(1-1/p-1/q). Its constant is independent of q; taking q to infinity gives exponent1-1/p. Substituting the two assumed moment/intensity bounds gives 4 C^(1/p) delta^(1/p)/(a delta) beta_r^(1-1/p), the displayed expression. This takes q to infinity at fixed r; it interchanges no limit in r. At beta_r=0 the finite-q bound already vanishes. Thus beta_r=o(delta), with uniform a,C, is sufficient for this conditional estimate to vanish. It is not asserted necessary in every model.

**Full elementary falsifier for the shortcut beta=O(delta).** Let L~Bernoulli(delta), 0<delta<1, and Y=L. The joint masses at (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1) are (1-delta,0,0,delta); the product-of-marginals masses are ((1-delta)^2,delta(1-delta),delta(1-delta),delta^2). Each signed difference has magnitude delta(1-delta). Hence half their L1 sum, equivalently event-sup TV, is beta=2delta(1-delta)=O(delta), tending to zero. But Y conditional on L=1 is identically1, whose TV from Bernoulli(delta) is 1-delta, tending to1. Also E[L^p]=delta. This is a generic bits counterexample, not a Gaussian-field or stationary Palm-process realization.
