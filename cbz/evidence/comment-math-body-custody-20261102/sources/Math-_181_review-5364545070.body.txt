## Analytic verification

Write h(z)=z^7(z+1)^4 and

    a(rho,z)=(1+rho*z)^4(1+rho*(z+1))^7
             /[(1+rho)^5(1+rho*(2z+1))^9].

The conditional interface is precisely the probability density

    f(rho,z)=(165888/D) rho^12 h(z) a(rho,z),

on the open unit square, with V=1/rho and D>0. All subsequent probabilities below are in that already-formed law.

1. **Uniform Taylor expansion.** Every denominator is at least one on the closed square. The rational expression therefore extends smoothly to an open neighborhood of that compact square, and its second rho derivative has a finite uniform bound there. Also a(0,z)=1. Differentiating its logarithm gives

       d_rho log a(0,z)=4z+7(z+1)-5-9(2z+1)=-7(z+1).

   Thus d_rho a(0,z) has the same value, and Taylor's theorem gives

       a(rho,z)=1-7(z+1)rho+R(rho,z),
       |R(rho,z)|<=M rho^2,

   for a single finite M and every point of the closed square. In particular this is a uniform expansion, not merely a pointwise limit near rho=0.

2. **Exact integrals and mean.** The reference z normalizer follows by polynomial integration:

       H0=sum_{j=0}^4 binom(4,j)/(8+j)
         =(495+1760+2376+1440+330)/3960
         =6401/3960.

   Likewise

       integral_0^1 z^7(z+1)^5 dz
         =1/8+5/9+1+10/11+5/12+1/13
         =(1287+5720+10296+9360+4290+792)/10296
         =31745/10296.

   Consequently

       mu=(31745/10296)/(6401/3960)=158725/83213,

   since 3960/10296=5/13. The density 13y^12 is normalized because its integral is one, and directly

       E[Y]=13 integral_0^1 y^13 dy=13/14.

   Product density implies E[Y(Z+1)]=(13/14)mu=m. These identities are analytic evaluations; simulations or finite test cases are unnecessary.

3. **Second tail term.** Put epsilon=1/T for T>=1. Integrating the uniform expansion against rho^12 h(z) gives

       P(V>T)=(165888/D)[H0 epsilon^13/13
                 -7 H0 mu epsilon^14/14+O(epsilon^15)].

   The remainder is bounded in absolute value by (165888/D) M H0 epsilon^15/15, so the order is justified quantitatively. Factoring out the positive leading coefficient yields

       P(V>T)=C13 T^-13[1-7m/T+O(T^-2)],
       C13=165888 H0/(13D).

   The sign, exponent, normalization and coefficient in the proposal are all correct.

4. **Uniform normalized-density expansion.** Let p(y,z)=13y^12 h(z)/H0 be the reference product probability density. After rho=epsilon*y, with its Jacobian epsilon, the exact conditional density can equivalently be written

       g_T(y,z)=p(y,z) a(epsilon*y,z)/A_epsilon,
       A_epsilon=E_p[a(epsilon*Y,Z)].

   Uniform Taylor gives A_epsilon=1-7m epsilon+O(epsilon^2). For all sufficiently small epsilon this is bounded away from zero, and algebraic reciprocal expansion gives A_epsilon^-1=1+7m epsilon+O(epsilon^2). Multiplying the two uniform expansions proves

       g_T=p[1+7epsilon(m-y(z+1))+r_epsilon(y,z)],
       sup |r_epsilon|<=C epsilon^2.

   This is a uniform relative factor bound even where p vanishes on the boundary: it is obtained by factoring the exact density and needs no division by boundary values of p. The first correction integrates to zero by the mean identity in item 2.

5. **Sharp total variation and positivity.** With the convention dTV=(1/2) integral |g_T-p|, the elementary inequality ||u+v|-|u||<=|v| gives

       |dTV(G_T,p)-(7epsilon/2)E_p|m-Y(Z+1)||
         <=(1/2)E_p|r_epsilon|<=C epsilon^2/2.

   Therefore the claimed K/T+O(T^-2) expansion follows. Let X=Y(Z+1). Since 0<X<2 and m=E[X] lies in (0,2), E|X-m| is finite. It is strictly positive: the event Y<1/4 has positive probability 4^-13 and forces X<1/2, while m=(13/14)mu>13/14>1/2. In particular

       E|X-m|>=(m-1/2)4^-13>0.

   This establishes K>0 without relying on a numerical value or on an unproved nondegeneracy assertion. The O(T^-2) remainder then implies dTV/(1/T)->K>0, and hence the stated genuine Theta(T^-1) rate.

6. **Bounded-test statement.** The proved density expansion entails the precise signed identity

       E_{G_T}[F]-E_p[F]
         =(7/T)E_p[F(Y,Z)(m-Y(Z+1))]
           +O(||F||_infinity T^-2),

   with one remainder constant valid for every bounded measurable F. This verifies the mathematical expansion intended in the final paragraph. A wording clarification would replace “bounds the error ... by” with “gives the signed expectation expansion.” The signed first term alone is not an upper bound on an absolute error; for an absolute bound one uses (7/T)E_p[|F| |m-X|]+O(||F||_infinity T^-2). This does not alter the main proposition or its proof.
