**Compatibility test — deductions from the project law and the displayed literature hypotheses.**

1. As an algebraic baseline, a fixed area A and delta=kr^3 turn A^2 delta^2+A delta into A^2 k^2 r^6+Akr^3. Thus an O(r^3) second-moment rate already arises in the earlier paper's own admissible setting. Since (N)_2 <= N^2, the same upper bound controls a second factorial moment there. This observation alone supplies neither the particular torus/pin/weight transfer nor a lower bound or higher-q result.

2. Q_r and Q_r^W must not be interchanged. Under Q_r the field is Gaussian but has deterministic heights and gradients at the pins. Its gradient covariance at M and S is zero. Its mean is generally spatially varying. Under the Hessian/index tilt Q_r^W, one cannot simply invoke a theorem for a Gaussian field law. These are concrete obstacles, not a stationarity-only objection.

3. Removing M and S from the count does not itself produce a uniform nondegenerate family on the entire punctured domain. By smoothness the conditional gradient variance approaches zero as a witness approaches a forced pin. A compact exhaustion away from the pins may be useful, but passing to the full domain requires estimates uniform in both the exclusion radius and r. No such transfer is supplied by a citation alone. Nor is a planar theorem automatically a fixed-d theorem for all d>=2.

4. Pointwise recentering and variance normalization do not preserve this critical-point problem. If g=(f-m)/sigma, then

       grad g = (grad f-grad m)/sigma - (f-m) grad sigma/sigma^2.

   Away from zero-variance locations this identity already shows why the critical sets need not agree; at the pins sigma=0. A constant normalization of a suitable stationary baseline is a different operation. Similarly, a family convergence argument needs a limit satisfying the required nondegeneracy; the deterministic pin constraints prevent a direct global application here. This does not rule out a carefully proved off-pin or rescaled adaptation.

5. The quantity that must be bounded is E_(Q_r)[W_r (N)_q]/Z_r, with the full weight retained. The current C6 proof uses a global cap Psi and the bound (N)_q <= N Psi^(q-1), inserts that mark into Kac–Rice, and controls its moments under the additional witness regression (sections 4–6). Its regional penalty/degeneracy bookkeeping is the specific transfer work to compare with outside methods. The source's Z_r lower bound means a uniform O(r^5) numerator bound would suffice for an O(r^3) quotient; an unweighted O(r^3) estimate alone does not furnish it.

**Exact negative control for the weighting shortcut.** This is an abstract two-outcome probability model, not a Gaussian field, not the project's W_r, and not a counterexample to C6. For 0<r<1, let P(A)=r^3, N=2 1_A and W=1_A+r^2 1_(A^c). Then 0<W<=1, every unweighted count moment is 2^p r^3, and

    Z=E W=r^2(1+r-r^3) >= r^2,
    E[N(N-1)]=2r^3,
    E^W[N(N-1)]=2r/(1+r-r^3).

The last expression has order r, not r^3. Even a bounded weight, all unweighted moments, and a normalizer floor of order r^2 do not justify dropping the weight. The calculation is exact symbolic algebra; no simulation, numerical benchmark or project-runtime verification is represented by it.
