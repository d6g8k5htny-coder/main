## 2. Fixed model and the whole-field conditional kernel

Fix n=d>=2 and L>0, the actual smooth variance-one periodized Gaussian field on X=R^d/(L Z^d), and physical torus volume V=L^d. The covariance is P's normalized periodization, with every Fourier coefficient positive and Gaussian-decaying. We do not replace it by a nonperiodic or isotropic surrogate.

Let t=(x,y) lie in a compact domain D off the torus diagonal. Put E=C^2(X) and

    G_t phi=(grad phi(x),grad phi(y)),
    H_t phi=(phi(x),phi(y)).

The actual original coordinate value/gradient observations have positive covariance for distinct torus sites. P's distribution/jet argument, reconstructed in C229 at precisely this list, supplies that fact. This is not a claim for arbitrary pairwise different but linearly dependent directional functionals. In particular Sigma_G(t)>0 uniformly on each such compact D.

Writing F for the E-valued field, define

    B_t=Cov_E(F,G_tF) Sigma_G(t)^(-1),
    R_t=F-B_t G_tF,
    K(t,z)=Law(R_t+B_t z).                              (1)

The cross-covariance is a genuine E-valued covariance: Cauchy–Schwarz uses finite E-second moments supplied by the Fourier majorant. Finite families of continuous field functionals and the gradient vector are jointly Gaussian. Vanishing residual cross-covariance yields independence on every finite family; countable derivative evaluations generating Borel(E) extend it to the entire residual random element. Thus F=R_t+B_tG_tF proves the conditional integral identity, and (1) specifies its value at every z, including zero.

The evaluation (t,phi)↦G_t phi is jointly continuous for C^2 fields. Smooth covariance and the compact inverse bound make B_t continuous into the finite-rank E-valued maps. The common-sample map

    (t,z,F) ↦ F-B_tG_tF+B_tz

is jointly Borel, indeed continuous on the E-sample space. Its pushforward gives a jointly Borel kernel and weak continuity against bounded continuous functions. No value of an arbitrary almost-everywhere conditional version is chosen at a singleton.

W§4 constructs the same regression law in C^4. The continuous inclusion i:C^4→C^2 commutes with the finite observations, the Bochner covariance and its finite-dimensional inverse. ## 3. Marked measures: what the cited theorem provides

    Delta(t,phi)=|det Hess phi(x) det Hess phi(y)|.

## 4. Global elder mark and exact full-height disintegration

Define d_phi(x) by P's maximin over paths from x to a point with value strictly greater than phi(x), with supremum of an empty set equal to minus infinity. If d_phi(x)>h, one such path has positive clearance above h and its endpoint has positive clearance above phi(x). Compactness and polygonal approximation permit a rational polygon template, with a short initial segment depending continuously on x inside a chart. Conversely such a template is an admissible path. Countably many charts/templates give Borel strict-superlevel tests. Thus d is an extended-real Borel map and

    J(t,phi)=1{d_phi(x)=phi(y)}

is a bounded Borel mark. It can be defined on all E. Morse and distinct-critical-value hypotheses are required for its interpretation as the unique elder death partner; they are not an excuse to leave the Borel mark undefined elsewhere. The essential maximum has d=-infinity and contributes no finite partner. No local branch-adjacency or cap event is substituted for J.

Hessian index indicators are Borel. Put

    W(t,phi)=Delta(t,phi)
             1{index Hess phi(x)=n, index Hess phi(y)=n-1}.

This is the one determinant product from (2), now with ordered maximum/saddle roles.

For explicit disintegration after the zero-gradient condition, set

    V_t=H_t R_t, S_t=Cov(V_t)>0,

    Q_(t,z,h)=Law(B_tz+D_t(h-H_tB_tz)+R'_t).            (3)

## 7. Analytic controls and two nonblocking wording clarifications

These are mathematical negative controls, not software executions.

1. Modify an arbitrary regular conditional law at z=0 only. Since the gradient vector has a density, that change leaves its almost-everywhere conditional-law property intact but can change the proposed Kac–Rice integrand at zero. Equations(1),(3),(4) fix the canonical law at every target and forbid this substitution.
2. Modify a lifetime density at a sequence ell_j↓0 by setting its value there to ell_j^(-2). The expected measure is unchanged; the pointwise ell^(-1/3)+O(1) conclusion fails. This proves why the chosen-version convention above is necessary.
3. Replace the full-field mark by a local cap or branch-adjacency indicator. Equation(2) then counts a different selected measure. A later comparison implication does not make those marks identical.
4. Insert a second determinant after disintegrating heights. This yields E_Q(W²J) instead of E_Q(WJ). For a simple two-point law with equally probable W=1 or2 and J=1, the former is5/2 and the latter3/2: they are not the same normalized intensity.
5. Drop the height Jacobian r³, or divide ordered pairs by2, or normalize sphere area to1 without compensation. Each changes (7) and (8); merely reproducing a power-law exponent cannot detect every coefficient error.
6. Include x=y in the base compact domain. Repeated gradient observations make the covariance singular. The separated construction and subsequent exhaustion cannot be bypassed.
7. Extend the compact B,K radial bound directly to all marks. Compact boundedness supplies no integrable all-height majorant. The explicit C4 Gaussian-polynomial envelope is a separate necessary input.
8. Replace the Borel extension with a direct invocation of the weighted theorem on the elder equality indicator. Borel does not imply lower semicontinuous; the continuous-cylinder/finite-measure argument is necessary.
