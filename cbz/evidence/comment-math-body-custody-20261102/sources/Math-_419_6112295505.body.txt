## 2. Total data on arbitrary functions

For an arbitrary function g:E->R and point M in E define

    A_g(M)  :iff  for every y in E, g(y) <= g(M),

    C_g(M) = {h in R : there exist y in E and a continuous path
               gamma:[0,1]->E from M to y such that
               g(M) < g(y) and, for every t in [0,1], h < g(gamma(t))}.
                                                                  (1)

The path in (1) is precisely the mathematical datum represented by the
source's `Path M y`, with its compact `unitInterval` parameter. In particular
gamma(0)=M and gamma(1)=y. We use strict clearance at every parameter,
including the endpoints.

Define the real-valued function

    D_g(M) = sup C_g(M), if C_g(M) is nonempty;
             0,          if C_g(M) is empty.                       (2)

Every nonempty set in the first branch is bounded above, without any
regularity assumption on g: a witness for h in C_g(M), evaluated at t=0,
gives h<g(M). Therefore g(M) is an upper bound. The nonempty branch in (2)
is consequently the ordinary finite real least upper bound. Formula (2)
is a classical, possibly noncomputable total definition, and it does not
appeal to an undefined or infinite real supremum. An implementation may
use real `sSup` in the first branch; its boundedness and nonemptiness are
proved here rather than assumed from Lean's totalized notation.

Finally set

    E_g(M,S) :iff IsNondegMax d g M and IsIndexSaddle d g S
                 and not A_g(M) and D_g(M)=g(S).                    (3)

Here the maximum and saddle predicates are exactly the source definitions,
including their existing `fderiv` and Hessian conventions. Formula (3)
defines a total relation even on nonsmooth functions. We claim no geometric
meaning for it there. It is a definition of the interface's relation; its
agreement with actual persistence will come separately from C258.

If A_g(M) holds, a strictly higher endpoint cannot exist, so C_g(M) is
empty and D_g(M)=0. This real zero is a deliberately arbitrary convention
for an essential input. It is not the extended-real value minus infinity
mentioned informally in the source comment, and is not an essential-bar
death. The typed source law for `deathHeight_maximin` excludes precisely
these inputs. Moreover (3) is false at essential M regardless of that zero.

For arbitrary g, nonemptiness of C_g(M) implies not A_g(M), because a
witness includes a strictly higher endpoint. The converse will need
continuity; it is not silently imposed on all inputs in this total record.

## 3. The strict-clearance cut for a continuous nonessential input

Fix a continuous g and assume not A_g(M). Classical negation and the total
order of R give some y with g(M)<g(y). The straight segment from M to y is
a continuous path gamma. The continuous function t |-> g(gamma(t)) attains
a finite minimum m on the nonempty compact interval. At every t its value
is at least m, so m-1 has strict clearance along this path. Thus

    m-1 belongs to C_g(M); in particular C_g(M) is nonempty.        (4)

This lower witness is pathwise. Neither compactness of all of E nor a
global lower bound on g is required.

Two elementary facts identify the cut exactly. First, it is downward closed:
if c belongs to C_g(M) and h<c, the same endpoint and path have clearance
above h, so h belongs to C_g(M). Second, every member can be increased:
if h belongs to C_g(M), take its witnessing path gamma and its attained
minimum m. Because every value along this path is strictly above h, the
value at a minimizing parameter is strictly above h; hence m>h. The number

    h' = (h+m)/2

satisfies h<h'<m and therefore also belongs to C_g(M), with the same path.
This step uses attainment on a compact interval. A pointwise strict
inequality alone does not supply a uniform positive margin without that
continuity and compactness argument.

Write D=sup C_g(M), now in the nonempty bounded branch of (2). We prove

    for every real h, h<D  iff  h belongs to C_g(M).                (5)

If h<D and there were no c in C_g(M) with h<c, then h would be an upper
bound for C_g(M). The least-upper-bound property would give D<=h, a
contradiction. Choose such a c; downward closure gives h in C_g(M).
Conversely, if h belongs to C_g(M), choose h'>h in the same set as above.
Because D is an upper bound, h'<=D and hence h<D. This proves (5) in
both directions, including its strictness at the boundary.

In particular C_g(M)=(-infinity,D), D<=g(M), and D does not belong to
C_g(M). No path with clearance strictly above D exists. This does not
exclude a path staying at or above D, and it does not prove such a path
exists under mere continuity. Also D<g(M) is not asserted for an arbitrary
continuous nonglobal point. C256 provides the stronger separation and
closed-threshold attainment on its particular Morse-maximum locus.

## 4. Agreement with the source's supremum of path minima

For the same continuous nonessential input let

    B_g(M) = {min_(t in [0,1]) g(gamma(t)) :
              y in E, g(M)<g(y), gamma a continuous path M->y}.

Every displayed minimum exists by the same extreme-value argument. A
straight path proves that B_g(M) is nonempty, and evaluation at its initial
point bounds every member above by g(M). For any such path with minimum m,
it has strict clearance above h if and only if h<m. The reverse implication
is immediate from minimality; for the forward implication evaluate at a
minimizer. Consequently

    C_g(M) = union over m in B_g(M) of (-infinity,m).               (6)

Let b=sup B_g(M). If h belongs to C_g(M), (6) supplies m with h<m<=b.
Thus b is an upper bound for C_g(M), so D<=b. Conversely, every m in
B_g(M) satisfies m<=D: if D<m, the midpoint (D+m)/2 would, by (6), belong
to C_g(M) while exceeding its upper bound D. Therefore D bounds B_g(M)
from above and b<=D. We have proved

    D_g(M) = sup_(gamma(0)=M, g(gamma(1))>g(M))
                    min_(t in [0,1]) g(gamma(t)).                  (7)

This proves the source's maximin expression on the continuous nonessential
locus. It makes no assertion that the supremum in (7) is attained by one
path. Its essential empty-family case is handled by the explicit convention
in (2), not by interpreting an empty real supremum as minus infinity.

## 6. What is unique, and on exactly which locus

First fix continuous g and nonessential M independently of any interface.
If a real number a satisfies, for all h, h<a iff h belongs to C_g(M),
then a=D_g(M) by (5). To see uniqueness directly, if a<D choose their
midpoint h. It lies below D but not below a, contradicting the two cut
characterizations; if D<a use the same argument with the roles reversed.
Thus the continuous nonessential cut determines a unique finite real value.

Next let I and J be any two objects of the pinned interface for the same
d,L. On a smooth L-periodic g, their `essential_iff` laws imply, for every M,

    I.IsEssential(g,M) iff A_g(M) iff J.IsEssential(g,M).            (8)

If not A_g(M), both maximin clauses characterize their death values by
the same C_g(M). The uniqueness just proved gives

    I.deathHeight(g,M) = D_g(M) = J.deathHeight(g,M).                (9)

The smooth periodic restriction in (8)–(9) matters. Although D itself
has a characterization for every continuous nonessential g, an arbitrary
interface's axioms constrain its deathHeight only on smooth periodic g.
Therefore (9) is not asserted for arbitrary merely continuous g, or for
arbitrary nonsmooth or nonperiodic inputs.

If in addition g is on the source's distinct-Morse locus, the elder
relations also agree for every M,S. When A_g(M) holds, the nonessential
conjunct in each `elderPartner_iff` is false, so both relations are false.

identical. Hence

    I.ElderPartner(g,M,S) iff E_g(M,S)
                          iff J.ElderPartner(g,M,S).              (10)

The same conclusion holds for the source's `IsFiniteBar` predicates there,

## 2. Continuous nonessential input need not have a positive lifetime

Take d=1, g(x)=x, M=0 and older point y=1. For h<0 the straight segment
gamma(t)=t satisfies g(gamma(t))=t>=0>h. For h>=0 every proposed path fails
already at its initial point, since g(M)=0 is not strictly above h. Thus

    C(g,0)=(-infinity,0),       sup C(g,0)=0=g(M).

At the supremum there is no strict witness. There is a closed-level path at
level zero. Continuity and nonessentiality alone therefore yield neither a
strict birth/death gap nor a nondegenerate maximum. Here the derivative is
identically 1, so M is not critical. This example is deliberately
nonperiodic: it controls the continuity-only cut argument, not the torus
actual-bar theorem. A proof must not infer a finite bar merely from a
nonempty bounded strict connection cut.

## 3. Smooth periodic cut with an exact negative death level

Let L=2*pi and, on R, put

    p(x)=cos(2x)+cos(x),       M=pi.

Write u=cos(x). Then

    p(x)=2u^2+u-1=2(u+1/4)^2-9/8=(2u-1)(u+1).

Let alpha=arccos(-1/4), so 0<alpha<pi. The global minimum is -9/8,
attained at alpha and 2*pi-alpha modulo L. The point M has p(M)=0, while
p(0)=2>0. Since u is between -1 and 1, the factorization proves

    p(y)>0 iff cos(y)>1/2.

In particular every higher endpoint lies outside the interval
[alpha,2*pi-alpha] containing M: throughout that interval, u belongs to
[-1,-1/4] and p<=0. Every continuous real path from M to such an endpoint
must therefore cross alpha or 2*pi-alpha by the intermediate value theorem.
At that crossing its height is -9/8. This applies to every path, including
paths that reverse direction or travel across several periods.

Conversely the straight segment from pi to 0 stays at height at least -9/8
and reaches the older endpoint of height 2. Consequently

    C(p,pi)=(-infinity,-9/8),       sup C(p,pi)=-9/8.

For h<-9/8 that segment is a strict witness. For h>=-9/8 the mandatory
minimum crossing rules out every strict witness. At h=-9/8 the same segment
is a closed-level witness, with equality at the minimum. The exact gap is
0-(-9/8)=9/8. This separates strict-cut membership from attained closed
connection; replacing either strict inequality by a weak one changes the
statement at the endpoint.

The derivative is -sin(x)(4*cos(x)+1). The critical points modulo L are
0, pi, alpha, 2*pi-alpha, with second derivatives respectively
-5, -3, 15/4, 15/4. All are nondegenerate, but the two minima have the same
critical value -9/8. Thus the example is smooth, periodic, and Morse, while
it fails **distinct critical values**. It is an admissible exact control of
the interface's maximin clause. The interface's `elderPartner_iff` clause
does not apply to it. No choice of a unique elder death point or actual
bar/pair theorem is inferred from this example.

## 4. Dropping continuity changes the strict cut into a closed ray

Define an everywhere positive, discontinuous function on R by

    q(x)=1     if x<=0,
         x     if 0<x<1,
         2     if x>=1.

Set M=0, so q(M)=1. The higher endpoints are exactly y>=1. The segment from
0 to 1 has q>0 at every parameter value, and therefore witnesses 0 in
C(q,0). It also witnesses every h<0.

For any h>0, choose an integer n>=2 with 1/n<=h. Every continuous path from
0 to any higher endpoint y>=1 crosses the coordinate 1/n. At that crossing
q=1/n<=h, so the strict height condition fails. It follows exactly that

    C(q,0)=(-infinity,0],       sup C(q,0)=0.

The maximin equivalence at h=0 would assert the false statement
`0<0 iff true`. Along the displayed segment the values are all positive,
but their infimum is zero and is not attained. Compactness of the parameter
interval alone has not produced a strictly positive uniform clearance.
Continuity of the composed height is the missing hypothesis.

This does not contradict a total interface: its axioms do not require the
maximin equivalence on this discontinuous function. It does refute extending
that equivalence to all functions merely because the assigned supremum is
a real number and the cut is nonempty and bounded above.

## 5. Abstract cut control: lower closure alone is insufficient

The set A=(-infinity,0] is nonempty, bounded above, and downward closed.
Nevertheless h in A is not equivalent to h<sup A at h=0. Thus the abstract
step needed by the continuous construction must also exclude a largest
element, for example by proving the following upward extensibility:

    for each h in C, there is k in C with h<k.

For continuous g and a witnessing compact path, let m be the attained
minimum of g along that path. The strict witness gives h<m. The exact
midpoint k=(h+m)/2 has h<k<m and is witnessed by the same path. This is the
load-bearing step that fails in Section 4.

For clarity, the two directions of the resulting supremum argument differ.
If h is in C, extensibility supplies k>h in C, so sup C>=k>h. If h<sup C,
some k in C satisfies h<k; otherwise h would itself be an upper bound,
contradicting h<sup C. Downward closure then gives h in C. Neither direction
licenses a witness at h=sup C. The real-supremum argument additionally uses
nonemptiness and boundedness; it cannot be applied to an essential empty cut.

## 6. Essential real defaults are not finite death levels

Take g(x)=cos(x), L=2*pi, M=0. There is no higher endpoint, since g<=1=g(M).
Thus C(g,M) is empty. The maximin axiom has a nonessential hypothesis and
places no restriction on this essential `deathHeight` value. Assigning
0, 42, or -1 there does not represent a finite ordinary death and is not
a choice of a real negative infinity.

More generally, given a valid total interface, changing its death-height
values only at actual global maximizers preserves its source axioms if the
essential and elder predicates are kept fixed. On smooth periodic inputs,
`essential_iff` identifies those points as essential. The maximin axiom
then has no applicable premise. In `elderPartner_iff`, the right-hand side
is already false because it conjoins nonessentiality, whatever real value
is assigned. Hence there can be distinct total interfaces with different
essential death defaults; no global uniqueness theorem follows from the
source clauses.

The nonessential guard in the elder characterization is necessary. In the
cosine example, S=pi is a nondegenerate index-zero saddle in dimension one,
and M is a nondegenerate maximum at positive torus distance pi from S. If
one selects the allowed essential default deathHeight(g,M)=-1=g(S) and then
illegitimately omits the nonessential guard, that weakened formula accepts
(M,S) with positive gap 1-(-1)=2.

There is no corresponding finite ordinary H0 bar: on the circle the
superlevel is empty for h>1, a connected arc or a point for -1<h<=1, and
the whole connected circle for h<=-1. Its sole ordinary H0 class is
essential. The real default must therefore remain semantically separated
from an actual death, even when it numerically equals a saddle value.

## 7. Off-locus elder freedom and the precise role of finite-pair guards

The source constrains `ElderPartner g M S` only when g satisfies all three
hypotheses: smoothness, periodicity, and Morse distinctness. For any valid
total interface, changing this predicate at one function failing that joint
locus preserves all source axioms: the other two axioms do not mention the
elder predicate, and the elder axiom cannot be instantiated at that
function. This supplies distinct valid extensions once existence has been
constructed. It gives no new actual persistence interpretation there.

Use p from Section 3, which fails Morse distinctness. One allowed extension
can make its elder predicate true for every ordered pair; another can make
it false there, without changing death height or essentiality.

The candidate guard is substantively additional off this locus. For
M=pi/3 and S=pi/2, one has p(M)=0 and p(S)=-1, hence a positive height gap
1 and positive torus distance pi/6. But the derivatives are respectively
-3*sqrt(3)/2 and -1, so neither endpoint is critical and the candidate
predicate fails. If the off-locus elder predicate is true, dropping the
candidate guard admits these noncritical endpoints.

The positive-gap guard is separately additional. In dimension two set

    r(x,y)=p(x)+cos(y),       L=2*pi,
    M=(pi,0),                S=(0,pi).

Both have height 1. The Hessians are diagonal:

    Hess r(M)=diag(-3,-1),       Hess r(S)=diag(-5,1).

Their gradients vanish. M is a nondegenerate maximum and S has exactly
one negative direction, the required index d-1=1. Their torus distance is
pi*sqrt(2)>0: each coordinate displacement is an odd multiple of pi after
adding any lattice vector, and the minimum absolute displacement in each
coordinate is pi. They form a candidate pair, but their gap is zero.
Their equal heights at distinct critical positions explicitly violate
Morse distinctness. An off-locus elder extension declaring this pair true
is allowed by the interface. Dropping only the positive-gap condition
would admit it as a finite pair despite its zero lifetime.

Finally, these two guards do **not** themselves exclude every essential
birth off the Morse-distinct locus. In the one-dimensional p example, take
its global maximum M=0, of height 2, and S=alpha, of height -9/8. The
candidate predicate holds, the torus distance is alpha>0, and the gap is
25/8>0. If the unconstrained off-locus elder extension declares this pair
true, the source `IsFiniteBar` predicate is true although M is essential.
Thus even retaining both guards does not justify an actual-bar theorem
for arbitrary valid interfaces at arbitrary inputs. A particular canonical
extension may impose nonessentiality everywhere; that is a property of
that extension, not a uniqueness or universal consequence of the interface.

## 8. What these controls resolve, and what remains outside them

The examples enforce four separate boundaries: strict-cut versus closed
connection endpoints; continuity versus arbitrary total input; essential
real defaults versus finite death levels; and Morse-locus characterization
versus unconstrained elder extensions. They also distinguish a positive
gap/candidate conjunction from either conjunct alone.

No calculation here supplies Morse genericity, almost-sure finiteness,
measurability of the samplewise assignment, integrability of counts,
lifetime densities, stationarity, Gaussian laws, any full normalizer or
limit exchange, field-first sampling, all-small-bars converses, or regional
shrinking multiple-witness collision estimates. The C258 bar/count bridge
must retain its original hypotheses and explicit classical imports after
its given-interface premise is instantiated. It does not acquire a proof
off that locus from totality of the data.

All controls were derived by the displayed exact algebra and topology.
Model agreement, registration, or successful byte verification is not a
mathematical proof of C260. Distinct nonauthor review, formal realization,
hosted compilation, kernel checks, and scientific acceptance are separate
work; none is performed or claimed by this contributor file.
