### A. f1 is strictly increasing for EVERY admissible parameter set

The key identity is `s-beta(c-s)=(s-a)/w=:z>=0` for every s>=a. Exact differentiation gives

```
f1'(s)=8s [h²+4hs²+8s⁴-2 beta(c-s)s³]/(h+4s²)²
       =8s [h²+4hs²+6s⁴+2s³ z]/(h+4s²)² > 0.
```

Thus the example's increasing f1 branch extends to the ENTIRE feasible ray [a,infinity), not merely [a,c]. This is a sum-of-positive-terms proof, not a sampled derivative. Writing A=pu²+qv², its minimum is the explicit value

`f1(a)=4a²(A+a²)/(A+3a²)`,

attained by pδu+qδv+wδ0 at direction t=1/4. This branch minimum alone is not necessarily C.

### B. At most six candidate VALUES suffice for the exact sharp constant

Define

```
G(s)=s h(s)²-beta(c-s)[h(s)²+2h(s)s²+2s⁴].
f0'(s)=2G(s)/(h(s)+s²)².
G(a)=-2a³[h(a)+a²]<0,   G(c)=c g0²>0.
```

Neither endpoint minimizes f0. Its global minimum on the compact interval occurs at an interior zero of G. With d=g0+beta c², G is a genuine quintic: its coefficients, in ASCENDING powers of s, are

```
-beta*c*d²,
d*(4*beta²*c²+beta*d+d),
-2*beta*c*(2*beta²*c²+3*beta*d+3*d),
2*beta*(4*beta²*c²+4*beta*c²+beta*d+2*d),
-beta*c*(5*beta²+10*beta+2),
beta*(beta+1)*(beta+2).
```

The last coefficient is strictly positive. Let R be ALL distinct roots of G in (a,c): it is nonempty and has at most five elements. Then exactly

`C=min( {4a²(A+a²)/(A+3a²)} union {F0(r²,h(r)): r in R} ).`

This includes every stationary value, not only a guessed unique root. Local maxima in that finite set do no harm; a true global minimum is included. Attainment uses the unchanged source law mu_s and t=1/4 for the explicit candidate or t=0 for a winning stationary candidate. Ties are allowed. At P=1, use the source's fixed two-atom formula directly; beta and this quintic construction are NOT defined there.

### C. One direction crossing; useful sufficient regimes

```
F0(x,g)-F1(x,g)=(g-2x)(g+2x)²/[(g+x)(g+4x)],
(h(s)/s²)'=2[beta*c*(s-c)-g0]/s³<0  on [a,c].
```

The minimizing endpoint can therefore cross at most once as s increases, from t=1/4 to t=0. At equality all directions agree. If A<=3a² then f0(a)<=f1(a), while f0'(a)<0; hence a stationary t=0 value strictly wins globally. This covers the published p=q=1/4,u=1,v=4 example, without needing its special quartic merely to prove the f1 branch increases. If g0>=2c², then h(s)>=2s² on [a,c], so C=f1(a). These are sufficient, not exhaustive, regimes.

### D. A universal unique-stationary-root claim would be false

Use p=q=1/200,u=1/2,v=3/2. Then a=1/100,c=1,g0=1/400,beta=1/99. Exact rational signs are:

| s | G(s) |
|---|---|
|1/100|-1/40000000|
|11/1000|1208904173929/9702990000000000000|
|3/20|1703147/1552478400000|
|4/25|-100663/79860000000|
|999/1000|-135143246896219/9702990000000000000|
|1|1/160000|

The three disjoint intervals (1/100,11/1000), (3/20,4/25), (999/1000,1) each contain an interior zero by continuity. This proves AT LEAST THREE stationary points, not exactly three and not three global minima. #376's uniqueness proof is for different fixed inputs and is unaffected. Any general-purpose extension of its single-root bisection needs a separate all-root argument.

Minimal exact sign reproduction (standard library, no root-fitting):
