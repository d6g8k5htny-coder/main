## 2. Nonnegative integration and the two primitive identities

For y>=0, Tonelli and integration by parts give

 L(y)=E(Z-y)_+=integral_y^infinity(z-y)phi(z)dz
                  =phi(y)-y barPhi(y)>=0.

For x>=0, the (x-t)_+ factor lets the source's J be integrated over0<=t<=x;
the other positive part already enforces Z>x+t. Therefore

 h(x)=integral_0^x (x-t)L(x+t)dt
     =integral_x^{2x}(2x-y)L(y)dy.

The interchange is justified by nonnegativity, not a formal swap of signed
improper integrals. Gaussian moments make the result finite. At x=0 it is0.
Define

 A0(y)=[y phi(y)-(1+y^2)barPhi(y)]/2,
 A1(y)=[(y^2-1)phi(y)-y^3 barPhi(y)]/3.

Direct differentiation using phi'=-y phi and barPhi'=-phi gives A0'=L and
A1'=yL. Thus h=2x[A0(2x)-A0(x)]-[A1(2x)-A1(x)]. Expanding the four endpoint
coefficients gives exactly(3), including the signs and the factors2/3,4/3.
Formula(3) is an identity, not a recommendation to subtract nearly equal
binary64 numbers at small x. A computed negative value must not simply be
clipped to zero and advertised as a certified result.

An independent reconstruction uses the source's clipped polynomial:

 J(d,x)=0                                      if d<=x,
 J(d,x)=(-d^3+6xd^2-9x^2d+4x^3)/6             if x<d<2x,
 J(d,x)=x^2d/2-2x^3/3                         if d>=2x.     (5)

The expressions agree at both thresholds; x=0 gives0 throughout. Upper
standard-Gaussian moments obey T0(a)=barPhi(a), T1(a)=phi(a),
Tn(a)=a^{n-1}phi(a)+(n-1)T_(n-2)(a). Integration of(5) over[x,2x] and
[2x,infinity), using n<=3, yields the same four polynomial coefficients as(3).
The supplied diagnostics verify these TWO derivations as exact polynomial
identities, not merely equality on a finite sample of x values.

## 3. Scale, symmetry and the one-dimensional law

For s>0 the defining integral shows J(sd,su)=s^3 J(d,u). In particular

    E_D J(D,u)=sigma^3 h(u/sigma).

Write B=sigma Z/2 with standard Z independent of D. Its squared argument is
B^2/sigma=sigma Z^2/4, not sigma^2 Z^2/4 or sigma Z^2. Taking expectation and
folding the even Z density gives(2). The factor2 is this folding operation;
it is not permission to change the B variance or to double its full-line
density a second time. J is homogeneous in(d,u), not in(d,B).

For reference, setting x=sigma z^2/4 gives the equivalent expression

 Gamma=sqrt(2/pi) sigma^(5/2) integral_0^infinity
                         h(x)x^(-1/2)exp(-2x/sigma)dx.      (6)

This is not used by the checker. The z form(2) avoids that displayed endpoint
power. These scale formulas require sigma>0. No degenerate0/0 extension is
asserted; a zero-variance input is outside the source coefficient hypotheses.

## 4. Small-argument series and outward brackets

Since L(0)=phi(0), L'(0)=-1/2 and L''(y)=phi(y),

 L(y)=phi(0)-y/2+phi(0) integral_0^y (y-s)exp(-s^2/2)ds.

Insert this into(4) and integrate the exponential power series on compact
intervals. Uniform convergence of that series justifies termwise integration.
For all real x>=0 one obtains the convergent identity

 h(x)=phi(0)[x^2/2+sum_(n>=0)(-1)^n a_n x^(2n+4)]-x^3/3,
 a_n=[2^(2n+4)-(2n+5)]/
       [2^n n!(2n+1)(2n+2)(2n+3)(2n+4)].                  (7)

Indeed integral_0^1(1-t)(1+t)^k dt
=[2^(k+2)-(k+3)]/[(k+1)(k+2)] gives these coefficients with k=2n+2.
For example a0=11/24, a1=19/240. This is also independently checked by
binomial integration in the finite diagnostics.

For 0<=x<=1/2, every exponential argument in the nested integrals obeys
0<=s^2/2<=1/2. Its odd-degree Taylor sum is a lower bound and the preceding
even-degree sum is an upper bound. Both integrations have nonnegative
kernels, preserving these directions. Therefore, if

    A_N(x)=x^2/2+sum_(n=0)^N (-1)^n a_n x^(2n+4),

then for positive odd N,

    phi(0) A_N(x)-x^3/3 <= h(x)
                        <= phi(0) A_(N-1)(x)-x^3/3.       (8)

The bracket gap is phi(0) a_N x^(2N+4); one must additionally propagate any
interval uncertainty in phi(0). The proof uses positive integral operators,
not an assumed alternating-term test for a transformed series outside its
stated domain. The numerical helper enforces x∈[0,1/2], exact rational inputs,
and odd cutoffs. It uses opposite interval endpoints for signed products.
Its parent pi/exp/CDF routines are exact-hash pinned, not silently replaced
by floating approximations.

In particular h(x)/x^2 ->phi(0)/2 as x decreases to0. The integrand of(2),
including its prefactor, divided by z^4 tends to sigma^5/(32pi). The apparent
cancellation of(3) is therefore removable, not a singular coefficient.
Neither this limit nor(8) claims uniformly accurate direct subtraction in(3).

## 5. Positive bounds and a rigorous outer tail

The source's J satisfies0<=J(d,x)<=d_+^3/24. To verify the maximum, for fixed
d>0 write ell=min(x,d-x) on0<=x<=d. The value ell^2(3d-4ell)/6 increases
for0<=ell<=d/2, reaches d^3/24 at ell=d/2, and is0 outside the support.
Also L(y)<=phi(y), hence for x>=0

    0<=h(x)<=phi(0)/12,
    h(x)<=x^2 phi(x)/2.                                   (9)

The first uses E Z_+^3=2phi(0), by integration by parts. The second uses the
monotone phi on[x,2x] in(4). These make the one-dimensional integral finite.
For cutoff A>0, write Gamma_A for(2) integrated only over[0,A]. Then

 0<=Gamma-Gamma_A <= (sigma^3 phi(0)/6)barPhi(A)
                   <= sigma^3 exp(-A^2/2)/(12pi A).         (10)

The last inequality follows from barPhi(A)<=phi(A)/A, since z/A>=1 on
z>=A and integral_A^infinity z phi(z)dz=phi(A). The tail is omitted only
from a LOWER truncation; any certified upper truncation must add(10).

As an actually computed, separately bounded example: #362's exact parameter
box gives v<=9/4, so sigma<=3/2. Its unchanged outward exp/pi arithmetic yields

    (3/2)^3 exp(-32)/(96pi) < 3/(2*10^16)=1.5*10^-16.

Thus choosing A=8 bounds the omitted part of Gamma in this new representation
by1.5e-16. This is NOT a bound for Gamma itself, C, a finite-r probability,
