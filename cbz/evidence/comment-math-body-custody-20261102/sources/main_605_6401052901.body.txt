### 3. Why density alone cannot close the band

At the inner band edge (s=Br),
[
a(r,Br)=Br^3+B^4r^4asymp r^3,
]
so the candidate window factor is order one. More generally it has two regimes:

- (rll sll r^{2/3}): at best (O(r/s));
- (sgg r^{2/3}): at best (O(r^3/s^4)).

Therefore the common-height window supplies no uniform vanishing factor across all dyadic shells. Using Claude01Cz’s all-height shell power count merely as a **heuristic diagnostic**, multiplying (r^2s^3) by these factors still does not produce the proposed (r^7s^{-2}) shell numerator.

The missing estimate must instead be a **joint marked Kac–Rice bound**
[
int_{I_r} p(T=0,F(x)=y),
  mathbb E[W_r|det H_xdet H_y|mid T=0,F(x)=y],dy,
]
with the height constraint used inside the determinant/energy estimate. The reviewed single-witness source gives the relevant pattern in (I18)–(I21): critical height suppresses endpoint curvature before density and moments are combined. It does not prove the two-witness version.

The fixed-remote collision proof is also not a substitute. Its factor (min(1,r^3/delta^3)) controls the **height difference between two witnesses** at short-edge length (delta); the common witness height has an (O(1)) density only because the centre stays a fixed distance from the pins. That hypothesis fails in this band.

### 4. Smallest corrected next statement

A useful next theorem is therefore:

1. augment the exact two-scale divided observation vector by the corrected common-height row and prove its sharp Schur scale (a(r,s));
2. derive a two-witness analogue of (I18)–(I21), retaining endpoint and witness determinant factors under the common-height conditioning;
3. combine that result with the separately delivered covariance feasibility, C321’s owned factorial Kac–Rice/Borel interface, and a distinct axis/V22-K-01 treatment.

Until (2) supplies the additional marked gain, a marginal density result—even if (1) is proved—does not establish the band theorem.
