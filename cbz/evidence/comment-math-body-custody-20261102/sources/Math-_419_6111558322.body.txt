## 1. Exact theorem and imported facts

Let X be a nonempty compact, path-connected, Hausdorff, second-countable smooth
manifold **without boundary**, of dimension d >= 1. Let g : X -> R be smooth
(C-infinity) and Morse: every critical point has nondegenerate Hessian. Let M be
a local maximum, let b = g(M), and assume that M is nonglobal, meaning

    there exists y in X with g(y) > b.                         (NG)

Distinct critical values may additionally be assumed; they are not needed for
attainment or the threshold equivalence. A path always means a continuous map
gamma : [0,1] -> X with the stated endpoints. Define

    A = {a in R : there are y and gamma with
                  g(y) > b, gamma(0)=M, gamma(1)=y,
                  g(gamma(t)) > a for every t in [0,1]},
    delta = sup A,
    C(h,M) <=> there are y and gamma with
                  g(y) > b, gamma(0)=M, gamma(1)=y,
                  g(gamma(t)) >= h for every t in [0,1].

We prove that A is nonempty and bounded above, delta is a finite real number,

    min_X g <= delta < b,
    A = (-infinity, delta),
    for every real h, C(h,M) iff h <= delta.                  (E)

In particular the equality case has an actual path to a strictly higher point.
We also prove that delta is a critical value. With distinct critical values,
there is exactly one critical point S of X with g(S)=delta. We do **not** identify
its index, prove a label merger, or identify it as an actual persistence partner.
The C-infinity hypothesis matches the target `Field.IsSmooth` clause. This
packet makes no application to a merely C4 field; lowering regularity would
require checking the corresponding Morse-chart and flow hypotheses separately.

The following standard facts are imported as mathematical results; none is
claimed to have a Lean implementation in this packet.

1. **Morse local normal form.** At a nondegenerate critical point p there is a
   smooth chart centered at p in which
   g = g(p) - |u|^2 + |v|^2, with u in R^lambda and v in R^(d-lambda).
   At a local maximum lambda=d. At a regular point p, the submersion/coordinate
   theorem gives a chart in which g-g(p) is a coordinate. A primary expository
   proof of the Morse statement and isolation consequence is Brian Conrad's
   [Morse lemma handout, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.4](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/diffgeomPage/handouts/morselemma.pdf).
2. **Elementary compactness and topology.** Continuous real functions on compact
   spaces attain extrema; connected components are closed; nested nonempty
   compact connected subsets of a compact Hausdorff space have connected
   nonempty intersection. Manifolds are locally path connected. In a locally
   path-connected space, path components are open and coincide with connected
   components. We give the application-specific arguments below, including the
   closed-superlevel local path connectivity that is essential at the endpoint.
3. **Smooth flow facts, used only for criticality in Section 7.** Smooth
   manifolds admit smooth Riemannian metrics and smooth real cutoff functions;
   a smooth vector field on a compact boundaryless manifold has a smooth flow
   defined for all real times, with the usual chain rule along flow lines.
   The precise vector field and height estimate are supplied below. No Morse
   handle theorem or persistence-module theorem is imported for this lemma.

## 2. Finitely many critical points and strict separation from birth

The critical set is closed, since dg is continuous and its zero section is
closed. It is compact as a closed subset of X. The Morse local normal form
shows that each critical point has a neighborhood with no other critical
point. These neighborhoods cover the compact critical set, so a finite
subcover shows that the critical set is finite. In particular

    P_old = {P : P is a local maximum of g and g(P)>b}

is finite. It is nonempty: a global maximizer exists by compactness and its
height exceeds b by (NG). Because X has no boundary, every local maximum is a
critical point, and the Morse hypothesis makes it nondegenerate.

In a Morse chart at M, g=b-|z|^2. Choose r>0 so the closed Euclidean ball of
radius r lies inside the chart image. Let U be the image of its open ball
under the inverse chart. Its closure is the image of the closed ball and its
boundary is the image of the radius-r sphere. Every point in closure(U) has
height at most b; every point on boundary(U) has height

    c = b-r^2 < b.

Any path from M to a point of height greater than b must leave U and therefore
meet boundary(U), by continuity. Consequently its minimum height is at most c.
For every a in A a witness path has all heights strictly greater than a, so
a<c. Thus c is an upper bound for A, and once delta is defined as a real
supremum we have delta<=c<b. This gives a uniform gap below birth, not merely
the absence of a path above b.

## 3. The strict thresholds and the genuine maximin are finite

Let m=min_X g. By (NG) choose y with g(y)>b; by path connectivity choose a path
from M to y. Every a<m belongs to A. Thus A is nonempty and delta>=m. Section 2
bounds A above by c, so completeness of R yields a finite delta in [m,c].

If h<delta, h cannot be an upper bound of A. Choose a in A with h<a; the same
path witnesses h in A. Conversely, suppose h in A and gamma witnesses it.
The continuous function g composed with gamma has a minimum mu on [0,1].
Because all its values are greater than h, its attained minimum satisfies
mu>h. Put k=(h+mu)/2. The same path witnesses k in A, so h<k<=delta. Therefore

    h in A iff h<delta.                                     (S)

This also verifies the literal path-maximin interpretation. Let B be the set
of minima of all paths from M to points strictly higher than b. It is nonempty,
and m<=mu<=c for every mu in B. If mu in B then every a<mu lies in A, hence
mu<=delta. If a in A its witnessing path has minimum mu in B with a<mu.
These two comparisons prove sup B=delta. Finiteness is therefore a property
of the actual path problem, not a convention for an interface's real field.

## 4. Replacing a higher endpoint by an older local maximum

Fix a in A and a witnessing endpoint y. Let O={g>a} and let U be the connected
component of O containing M. The witness path puts y in U. Since O is an open
subset of a manifold, O is locally path connected, so U is open in O (hence
in X) and path connected.

The closure of U in X is compact. Choose P in that closure maximizing g there.
Then g(P)>=g(y)>b>a, so P belongs to O. A connected component is closed in O;
therefore closure_X(U) intersect O equals U, and P belongs to U. Since U is
open in X and P maximizes g on closure_X(U), P is a local maximum on X.
Thus P belongs to P_old. Path connectivity of U supplies a path from M to P
entirely in {g>a}.

This argument does not assert convergence of a gradient-ascent trajectory.
It uses compactness to attain the component-closure maximum, then the strict
height inequality to put that maximizer in the open component itself.

## 5. Every closed Morse superlevel is locally path connected

For any real h put F_h={g>=h}, with its subspace topology. Fix p in F_h.

If g(p)>h, arbitrarily small manifold chart neighborhoods of p lie inside
{g>h}; they may be chosen as images of Euclidean balls and are path connected.

If g(p)=h and p is regular, choose a regular-point coordinate chart with
g-h equal to its first coordinate. Intersecting sufficiently small coordinate
balls centered at p with F_h gives half-balls, which are convex and therefore
path connected. These form a neighborhood basis of p in F_h.

If g(p)=h and p is critical, use a Morse chart centered at p and write
g-h=-|u|^2+|v|^2. The intersection of F_h with each sufficiently small centered
coordinate ball corresponds to

    { (u,v) : |(u,v)|<r, |v|^2>=|u|^2 }.

This set is star-shaped about the origin: multiplication by s in [0,1]
preserves the inequality and the ball. Radial contraction gives a path from
every point to the origin. The intersections again form a neighborhood basis
of p in F_h. This includes definite critical points: the maximum case is
the singleton origin and the minimum case is the whole ball.

The cases exhaust F_h, proving local path connectivity. The cone argument is
only made at a critical point whose height equals h. It does not assert that
arbitrary intersections of Morse charts with superlevels at other heights
are star-shaped or contractible.

For clarity, the standard component consequence follows because a path
component in a locally path-connected space is open: the union of path-
connected neighborhoods of its points stays in that path component. Its
complement is a union of the other open path components. A connected component
cannot meet two such disjoint open pieces, so it is one path component.

## 6. Closed endpoint attainment and the equivalence for every h

For integers n>=1 put a_n=delta-1/n and F_n={g>=a_n}. By (S), a_n is in A.
By Section 4 choose P_n in P_old connected to M by a path in {g>a_n}.
Let K_n be the **connected component** of F_n containing M. It contains P_n.
Each F_n is closed in compact X, each K_n is closed in F_n, and hence K_n is
compact and connected. Moreover F_(n+1) is a subset of F_n, so the connected
set K_(n+1), which contains M, lies in K_n. Thus (K_n) is nested.

Since P_old is finite, one P in P_old occurs as P_n for infinitely many n.
For every fixed n choose k>=n with P_k=P; then P lies in K_k, a subset of K_n.
Consequently both M and this single P lie in

    K = intersection over n>=1 of K_n.

The nested compact-connected intersection theorem says that K is compact
and connected. One way to see the connectedness assertion here is to suppose
K has a separation. Its two compact parts have disjoint ambient open
neighborhoods in the compact Hausdorff space X. All sufficiently large K_n
lie in their union (otherwise compactness gives a point in K outside that
union); every K_n meets both parts. This separates a connected K_n, a
contradiction.

For x in K, g(x)>=delta-1/n for every n, and hence g(x)>=delta. Thus K is a
connected subset of F_delta containing M and P. They therefore lie in the
same connected component of F_delta. Section 5 makes that component path
connected. There is a path gamma_* from M to P in F_delta, and g(P)>b. This
proves C(delta,M).

The argument does **not** infer that K itself is path connected from its
compactness or connectedness. It obtains the path in the containing component
of the locally path-connected space F_delta. It also does not take an
unjustified limit of the varying paths, or assume that arbitrary varying
higher endpoints have a limit whose height remains strictly above b.

If h<=delta, gamma_* proves C(h,M). Conversely, if C(h,M), the same path
witnesses a in A for every a<h. Thus a<delta for every a<h, and h<=delta.
Equivalently, its path minimum belongs to B and is at least h, whereas every
member of B is at most delta. This proves (E) for all real h.

In fact gamma_* has minimum exactly delta: its minimum is at least delta,
and any strictly larger minimum would give an element of A greater than
delta. The supremum of the actual path minima is attained.

## 7. Criticality, with the flow input explicitly isolated

Suppose delta is not a critical value. There are finitely many critical
values by Section 2. Choose epsilon>0 so [delta-2 epsilon,delta+2 epsilon]
contains no critical value and delta+2 epsilon<b. Choose a smooth function
chi : R -> [0,1] supported in the open interval
(delta-2 epsilon,delta+2 epsilon) and equal to 1 on
[delta-epsilon,delta+epsilon]. With any smooth Riemannian metric define

    V = chi(g) grad(g) / |grad(g)|^2

on the preimage of that open interval, and V=0 outside. There are no zeros
of grad(g) in the closed band; the cutoff vanishes near its boundary. Thus
the extended V is a globally smooth vector field. By the imported flow
theorem its time-epsilon map Phi_epsilon is defined and continuous on X.

Along its trajectories,

    d/dt g(Phi_t(x)) = chi(g(Phi_t(x))) >= 0.

If g(x)>=delta, its height at time epsilon is at least delta+epsilon. Indeed
this is immediate by monotonicity when the initial height is already at least
delta+epsilon; otherwise the height increases at unit speed until it reaches
delta+epsilon, which occurs in at most epsilon time. Both M and P have height
greater than delta+2 epsilon, so V vanishes there and the flow fixes them.
Apply Phi_epsilon to gamma_* from Section 6. The resulting path has the same
endpoints and all heights at least delta+epsilon. It witnesses, for example,
delta+epsilon/2 in A, a contradiction. Therefore delta is a critical value.

If distinct critical points have distinct values, the critical point S with
g(S)=delta is unique on X. Its existence and uniqueness here carry no claim
about index or which elder label is killed. Those require additional analysis.

## 8. Quotient torus and the given strict interface

Take X=R^d/(L Z^d), L>0, d>=1, with its usual quotient smooth structure, and
write pi : R^d -> X. The standard lattice quotient/covering theorem supplies
a smooth boundaryless Hausdorff second-countable manifold, with pi a covering
map and local diffeomorphism. Its compactness follows also from the surjection
of the compact cube [0,L]^d; its path connectivity follows by projecting
straight line paths. A smooth L-periodic function g_tilde descends to a unique
smooth g satisfying g_tilde=g composed with pi. These facts, and covering-path
lifting, are imported standard quotient results, not proved Lean instances.

Here is the needed path compatibility after those imports. Fix a Euclidean
representative M_tilde of M. A path in R^d from M_tilde to y_tilde projects
to a path from M to pi(y_tilde), with exactly the same height at each time.
Conversely any path in X from M to y has a lift starting at M_tilde; its
endpoint is some representative y_tilde of y, and its heights are again
identical. Since the higher endpoint is existentially quantified, that
possibly different representative is allowed. This proves equivalence of
the lifted and quotient strict predicates at each h, and also of their
closed predicates, without selecting the half-open fundamental domain along
a path. Local diffeomorphism identifies criticality, local maxima, and Hessian
nondegeneracy. Distinctness of critical values is imposed on quotient points.

Now suppose a real number D is supplied by `I.deathHeight g_tilde M_tilde`
and the actual hypotheses of `deathHeight_maximin` hold, including its
`not IsEssential` hypothesis. The `essential_iff` clause and surjectivity of

    for every h in R, h<D iff h in A.

By (S), h<D iff h<delta for all real h. If D and delta differed, their midpoint
would contradict this equivalence. Hence D=delta. Therefore (E) becomes the
desired closed-level characterization with that given interface number.
This is a proved uniqueness adapter consuming the interface's strict clause;

## 1. Proposed compact Morse endpoint argument: checked steps and exact cautions

Let X be a compact connected smooth manifold **without boundary**, g a smooth Morse function, M a nonglobal local maximum, and b=g(M). Set

    E = {h in R : there exist y with g(y)>b and a path M -> y
                 whose entire image lies in {g>h}},
    delta = sup E.

The torus satisfies the boundaryless assumption. For a manifold with boundary, maxima of a component closure can occur on the boundary without being interior Morse critical points; boundary hypotheses cannot simply be omitted.

1. **Finite real delta.** A connected manifold is path connected. A higher point exists because M is nonglobal, so some path to a higher point exists; any h<min_X g belongs to E. Every h in E is <b because the initial endpoint is M. Thus E is nonempty and bounded above, and delta is finite.
2. **Strict gap below b.** The Morse maximum is strict and isolated. Choose a sufficiently small closed coordinate ball B about M on which M is the unique maximum and g<b away from M. Its boundary is compact; let a=max_(boundary B) g<b. Every higher endpoint is outside B, and every path from M to it meets the boundary. Hence E is contained in (-infinity,a), so delta<=a<b. This proves a strict gap; it does not require the supremum a to be attained by an admissible path.
3. **Every h<delta is admissible.** E is downward closed. By the defining property of its supremum, some t in E exceeds h, whence h is in E. This should be stated before choosing threshold paths.
4. **Replace the moving higher endpoint by a higher local maximum.** For h<delta, let U be the open component of {g>h} containing M and the chosen higher point y. Open subsets of a manifold are locally path connected, so U is path connected. The compact closure of U has a maximizer P, with g(P)>=g(y)>b>h. Because g(P)>h, a connected coordinate neighborhood of P inside {g>h} meets U; hence it lies in U and P belongs to U. P is consequently an interior local maximum of g. The closure maximization alone does not establish this last conclusion without the strict inequality g(P)>h and the neighborhood argument.
5. **Fix P using finiteness.** The critical set is closed; Morse critical points are isolated; compactness implies finitely many critical points, hence finitely many higher local maxima. Apply step 4 at h_k=delta-1/k. One higher maximum P occurs for an infinite, and therefore unbounded, set of k. For every n, choose such k>=n. The corresponding path lies in {g>delta-1/k}, hence in {g>=delta-1/n}. Therefore M and this single fixed P belong to the same component K_n of the latter closed set.
6. **Pass to a connected limit.** Each K_n is nonempty, compact, and connected. The nesting of the superlevel sets implies K_(n+1) is contained in K_n, since both components contain M. The nested intersection K is compact, connected, contains M and P, and lies in A={g>=delta}.
7. **Supply the missing path using Morse local geometry.** At a regular point of g=delta, the set A is locally a half-space. At a critical point of that level, Morse coordinates give

       g-delta = -||u||^2 + ||v||^2,
       A locally = {||u||<=||v||}.

   Intersections of this cone with sufficiently small balls centered at the critical point are star shaped about that point. Together with the regular-point case and interior points g>delta, this supplies a basis of path connected neighborhoods in A. Thus A is locally path connected, and its connected components are path connected. The connected set K lies in one such component, so M and P admit a path in A.

   **Caution:** it is not valid to infer that an arbitrary connected subset K of a locally path connected space is locally path connected. Use the component of A containing K. Alternatively, K actually equals the component of A containing M: that component is a connected subset of every {g>=delta-1/n}, hence is contained in every K_n, while connectedness of K gives the reverse inclusion.

These steps prove closed endpoint attainment with a fixed endpoint P satisfying g(P)>b. Distinct critical values are unnecessary for these seven steps; they matter later for the unique critical event and unambiguous elder label.

For C(h) defined using a path to some y with g(y)>b in {g>=h}, the endpoint path gives C(h) for all h<=delta. Conversely, C(h) with h>delta yields a strict path at any t with delta<t<h, contradicting t not in E. Thus C(h) iff h<=delta, even without restricting the statement to h<=b.

**Still separate:** proving delta is critical requires an explicit critical-free compact-band deformation/flow argument. If delta were regular, choose epsilon>0 with no critical points in [delta,delta+epsilon] and delta+epsilon<b. Raising the endpoint path into {g>=delta+epsilon}, while fixing M and P, would contradict delta=sup E. Identifying the critical point's index, establishing a merger rather than a nonmerging handle, and tracking its elder label require the further handle and component-label arguments. The endpoint proof alone does not discharge those steps.

## 2. Actual compact continuous counterexample: fixed endpoints

Let

    Q = [0,1] x [-1,1],
    G = {(x,sin(1/x)) : 0<x<=1},
    K = closure_Q G = G union ({0} x [-1,1]),
    a = (0,0),   p = (1,sin 1),
    f(q) = -dist(q,K).

The rectangle Q is compact and path connected and f is continuous. The graph G is connected as the continuous image of the interval (0,1]; therefore its closure K is compact and connected.

For every epsilon>0,

    N_epsilon(K) = {q in Q : dist(q,K)<epsilon}
                = union_(k in K) (Q intersect B(k,epsilon))

is path connected. Here is a proof that does not assume K is path connected. Each relative ball in this union is convex. Declare two centers equivalent if a finite chain of these balls, consecutive balls intersecting, links them. Each equivalence class of centers is relatively open in K: a center within epsilon of a given center has an intersecting ball. The complement is a union of such relatively open classes.
