## 3. Proposition and proof of the joint conditional interface

**Proposition.** Under Section2, for every s in T_hat there is a canonical conditional law of (X_hat(.),Z,V) given U_s=v, defined for every v in R^(2d), such that v_n->v implies joint weak convergence in

    C1_loc(T_hat,R^(2d)) x C_loc(R^d,R^N) x R^M.

**Proof.** Let L_s:E->R^(2d) be the continuous gradient-evaluation map, Sigma_s=Cov(U_s), and define the E-valued regression operator

    B_s v = Cov(f,U_s) Sigma_s^(-1) v,
    R_s = f - B_s U_s.

The covariance columns belong to E; in fact they are smooth deterministic functions. Smooth covariance and the finite E second moment justify these definitions. Gaussian regression gives Cov(R_s,U_s)=0. Every finite family of continuous linear functionals of R_s is jointly Gaussian with U_s, so zero cross-covariance implies independence. A countable determining family for the separable E-valued random element then gives independence of R_s and U_s as random elements.

    K(s,v,.) = law(R_s+B_s v)

    ||(R_s+B_s v_n)-(R_s+B_s v)||_E

pathwise. The three maps
f->X_hat(.), f->J2F, and f->L_V f

For the optional varying-pin assertion, on every compact subset of T_hat the entries of Sigma_s and Cov(f,U_s) vary continuously, Sigma_s^(-1) is continuous and bounded, and B_s varies continuously in operator norm into E.
