# Uniform covariance floor for one short critical-point edge

Additive analytic candidate, 2026-10-25. Exact scalar infinite periodic
Gaussian law, fixed L, all positive Fourier weights including the constant
mode. Inputs are the common smooth field, its finite C10 moments and positive
finite scalar-jet covariance from retained v19 `GAUSSIAN_DERIVATIONS.md`.
No sealed product, source acceptance, count identity or certificate changes.

## Geometry and observation convention

Let three scaled centers z1,z2,z3 have norm at most B and pairwise separation
at least delta>0. A fourth site is z*=zj+epsilon w, with |w|=1 and
0<epsilon<=epsilon_*. Choose epsilon_* below delta/4. Physical sites are
rRz, R in O(2). The ten observations consist of the six pin value/gradient
coordinates at two of the centers and the two witness gradients, with the
short edge either pin--witness or witness--witness. Replace the gradient at
z* by

    D_epsilon=[grad_R F(rRz*)-grad_R F(rRzj)]/epsilon.   (1)

Keep the gradient at zj among the other rows. Call the resulting ten-vector
Z. The two possibilities at epsilon=0 are pin6, grad_y, r H_pin w, and
pin6, grad_z, r H_z w. The target of (1) is zero for zero critical gradients.
The exact absolute raw-observation-to-Z Jacobian is epsilon^-2.

There are constants epsilon_*,r_*,c,C>0 depending only on B,delta,L such that

    c r^16 I <= Cov(Z) <= C I                         (2)

for 0<r<=r_* and 0<=epsilon<=epsilon_*. The endpoint epsilon=0 uses (1)'s
continuous extension rH_zj w. In particular no constant in (2) depends on the
short-edge length epsilon. Dividing by r epsilon instead of epsilon is a
different convention; the Taylor orders and Jacobians below use exactly (1).

## Degree-eight interpolation at the three distinct centers

For i!=j put

    ell_ij(z)=((z-zj) dot (zi-zj))/|zi-zj|^2,
    L_i(z)=product_(j!=i) ell_ij(z)^3.

L_i has degree six, equals one at zi, and vanishes through derivative order
two at the other two centers. For each multiindex alpha with |alpha|<=2,
let T_i^(2-|alpha|) denote Taylor truncation at zi and set

    H_i,alpha(z)=L_i(z) (z-zi)^alpha/alpha!
                   T_i^(2-|alpha|)(1/L_i)(z).

This degree-at-most-eight polynomial has jet D^beta H_i,alpha(zj)
=1_(i=j)1_(alpha=beta) for |beta|<=2. The identity follows because the
product with the truncated reciprocal equals (z-zi)^alpha/alpha! modulo
terms of total degree at least three. Its coefficients are rational
functions of separated center coordinates, bounded on the specified compact
family. Thus arbitrary full scalar second jets at the three centers admit
a degree-eight right inverse with a uniform coefficient-norm bound C_H.

Directional Hessian data H w=v can be prescribed with

    H=v w^T+w v^T-(v dot w) w w^T,

which is symmetric, has H w=v, and norm at most 3|v|. Prescribe unused jet
coordinates as zero. Hence the ten confluent rows at epsilon=0 have a
degree-eight polynomial right inverse bounded uniformly in the centers and
unit w. This is a scalar interpolation argument, preserving Hessian symmetry.

Let A_epsilon map the 45 scalar polynomial coefficients of degree <=8 to
the ten scaled rows: pin values, all retained scaled gradients, and
[grad p(z*)-grad p(zj)]/epsilon. Polynomial division or the integral formula
extends A_epsilon continuously to epsilon=0. Uniformly on bounded geometry,

    ||A_epsilon-A_0||<=C_A epsilon.

The lower singular-value floor supplied by the preceding right inverse
therefore persists after choosing epsilon_* small enough. Consequently
A_epsilon has a uniformly bounded right inverse H_epsilon for the entire
compact family 0<=epsilon<=epsilon_*. No four-distinct-site interpolation
constant with separation epsilon is used.

## Physical scaling and the correlated Taylor error

If Q(x)=p(x/r), its physical value rows equal the scaled values, while each
physical gradient row, including the difference row (1), is r^-1 times its
scaled polynomial row. For prescribed physical ten-data v, multiply its
eight derivative-type entries by r, apply H_epsilon, then rescale degree-d
coefficients by r^-d. For r<=1 this constructs a physical right inverse with
norm <=C r^-8. Embed it in the degree-nine polynomial coefficient space.

Let J9_R be all scalar physical Taylor coefficients through degree nine at
the origin in frame R. Their exact covariance has a uniform positive floor
m9>0 over O(2). If A_phys,9 maps degree-nine polynomials to the physical rows,
the right-inverse dual inequality gives, for every ten-row vector v,

    ||v^T A_phys,9 J9_R||L2 >=c r^8 |v|.              (3)

Let P9 be the degree-nine Taylor polynomial of the field. Its gradient
remainder is O(r^9 ||F||C10); its Hessian remainder is
O(r^8 ||F||C10) on the bounded physical domain. For the difference row,
apply the fundamental theorem of calculus along the short edge:

    [grad(F-P9)(r(zj+epsilon w))-grad(F-P9)(rzj)]/epsilon
       =r integral_0^1 Hess(F-P9)(r(zj+u epsilon w)) w du.

The extra geometric r proves an O(r^9 ||F||C10) bound uniformly down to
epsilon=0. Values have the smaller O(r^10 ||F||C10) remainder. Thus

    Z=A_phys,9 J9_R+R_r,   (E|R_r|^2)^(1/2)<=C r^9.   (4)

The error and jet need not be independent. Reverse triangle inequality in
L2 applied to (3),(4) gives sqrt(Var(v^T Z))>=cr^8|v|-Cr^9|v|.
Choose r_* to absorb the second term. This proves the lower bound in (2).
The upper bound follows from bounded value/gradient second moments and (1)'s
integral representation r integral H w. This proof does not make an invalid
Loewner comparison of a jet covariance and its correlated remainder.

## Density and energy interface

Write E for the full exact regression energy, invariant under the invertible
row transformation for epsilon>0. Then

    p_Z(target)<=C r^-80 exp(-E/2),
    p_T(target)<=C epsilon^-2 r^-80 exp(-E/2).

The actual six-pin density at compact b and positive compact k is bounded
below by c r^-5 using the retained pin rows. Hence the conditional four-
witness-gradient density is at most

    C epsilon^-2 r^-75 exp(-E/2).                     (5)

Retained v19's energy moment bound applies unchanged:
E[||F||Cq^p | T=target]<=C_pq(1+sqrt(E))^p. All actual regression means are
retained. Estimates (2),(5) are deliberately coarse but uniform in epsilon,
including axial/radial approach directions. Kac--Rice, normalization by the
endpoint weight, a global pair cover and full D5 remain separate.

## 1. Model, sources and constants

Fix an integer \(d\geq1\) and \(L>0\), once and for all. Let \(X=\mathbb R^d/(L\mathbb Z^d)\), \(\omega=2\pi/L\), and

\[
a_n=Z^{-1}e^{-\omega^2|n|^2/2},\qquad
Z=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z^d}e^{-\omega^2|j|^2/2}.
\tag{1}
\]

Thus every \(a_n>0\), \(a_{-n}=a_n\), and \(\sum_n a_n=1\). This is the exact infinite spectrum, including the random constant mode. There is no finite cutoff, sample-mean subtraction, variance renormalization, or substitution by an unperiodized field.

For identification with the image-sum covariance, integrate its periodization over a fundamental cube against \(e^{-i\omega n\cdot x}\). Translating the images tiles \(\mathbb R^d\), so the coefficient is

\[
\frac{(2\pi)^{d/2}}{L^d\sum_m e^{-|Lm|^2/2}}
 e^{-\omega^2|n|^2/2}.
\]

The Gaussian Fourier transform used here follows in one dimension from integration by parts: its derivative in frequency is minus frequency times itself, and its value at zero is \(\sqrt{2\pi}\); take products in \(d\) dimensions. Absolute summability identifies the continuous periodic series with the image sum by uniqueness of continuous periodic Fourier coefficients, and evaluating at zero gives (1). Everything below may instead take (1) as the definition, so that this Fourier-identification fact is not a hidden stochastic hypothesis.

Write

\[
\|f\|_{C^q}=\max_{|\alpha|\leq q}\sup_{x\in X}|D^\alpha f(x)|.
\]

All constants may depend on the fixed \(d,L,q,p\). On separated parameter sets they may also depend on the separation and on the named observation family. No estimate uniform as \(L\to\infty\), \(d\to\infty\), or separation tends to zero is inferred from a compactness argument.

## 2. An explicit common smooth version and all supremum moments

Let \(H\subset\mathbb Z^d\setminus\{0\}\) contain one representative of each pair \(\{n,-n\}\). On one probability space take independent real standard normals \(\xi_0,(\xi_n,\eta_n)_{n\in H}\), and define

\[
F(x)=\sqrt{a_0}\xi_0+
\sum_{n\in H}\sqrt{2a_n}
  [\xi_n\cos(\omega n\cdot x)+\eta_n\sin(\omega n\cdot x)].
\tag{2}
\]

Put

\[
A_q=\sqrt{a_0}+2\sum_{n\in H}\sqrt{2a_n}(1+\omega|n|)^q.
\tag{3}
\]

This definition includes the factor two for the two real coefficients. Since \(\sqrt{a_n}=Z^{-1/2}e^{-\pi^2|n|^2/L^2}\), a polynomial times this Gaussian lattice weight is summable. For example, for \(\beta=\pi^2/L^2\),

\[
(1+\omega r)^q e^{-\beta r^2}
 \leq C_{q,\omega,\beta}e^{-\beta r^2/2},\quad
C_{q,\omega,\beta}=\sup_{r\geq0}(1+\omega r)^q e^{-\beta r^2/2}<\infty,
\]

and the remaining lattice sum is bounded by the product of \(d\) convergent one-dimensional sums. Hence \(A_q<\infty\).

Every \(C^q\) norm of a finite partial sum is bounded by

\[
\sqrt{a_0}|\xi_0|+
\sum_{n\in H}\sqrt{2a_n}(1+\omega|n|)^q(|\xi_n|+|\eta_n|).
\tag{4}
\]

The infinite positive sum in (4) has finite expectation, so is finite almost surely. On that event the series of every derivative of order at most \(q\) converges absolutely uniformly. Intersect the probability-one events over integer \(q\geq0\). Uniform convergence of each derivative series and the fundamental theorem of calculus identify the limiting derivatives, yielding **one almost surely \(C^\infty\) version**. Define the field to be zero on the single exceptional null set.

For \(p\geq1\), Minkowski's inequality applied first to finite sums, then to tails, gives

\[
\left(\mathbb E\|F\|_{C^q}^{p}\right)^{1/p}
 \leq A_q g_p,\quad
 g_p=\left(\frac{2^{p/2}\Gamma((p+1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi}\right)^{1/p}.
\tag{5}
\]

The formula for \(g_p=\|N(0,1)\|_p\) is the Gaussian integral after \(t=x^2/2\). For \(0<p<1\), Jensen applied to the concave power gives \(\mathbb E\|F\|_{C^q}^p\leq(A_qg_1)^p\). The same estimates for omitted Fourier terms give convergence in \(L^p(C^q)\) for every finite \(p\geq1\), not merely pointwise smoothness.

The covariance of (2) is \(\sum_n a_ne^{i\omega n\cdot(x-y)}\). Its derivative series converge absolutely at every order. Thus all finite derivative covariance formulas below refer to the exact infinite law.

The Banach space \(C^q(X)\) is separable: map it isometrically into the finite product of (C(X)) spaces by all derivatives through order \(q\); its image is closed by the fundamental theorem of calculus, and (C(X)) is separable for compact metric (X). Finite partial sums are measurable \(C^q\)-valued random elements, so their norm limit is measurable. Every continuous linear functional of \(F\) is Gaussian, since its finite-partial-sum Gaussian laws converge in \(L^2\) and hence in distribution, with convergent means and variances. This constructs the needed Banach-valued Gaussian random element explicitly.

## 3. Finite jet nondegeneracy without a sampled covariance argument

**Lemma 1.** Any finite family of distinct physical derivative evaluation coordinates at finitely many distinct torus sites is linearly independent and has positive definite Gaussian covariance. At a single site, only distinct symmetric Hessian coordinates (indices i≤j) are used; statistical independence of those entries is not asserted, and \(D_{ij}=D_{ji}\) is not counted twice. An invertible change to an orthonormal frame preserves the assertion for an independent selected jet family.

**Proof.** Write a linear combination as \(\Lambda f=\sum_{j,\alpha}c_{j\alpha}D^\alpha f(x_j)\). If its variance under (2) vanishes, positivity of every mode weight and nonnegativity of the variance sum imply that it annihilates the constant, every sine, and every cosine. Thus it annihilates every trigonometric polynomial.

Choose disjoint coordinate balls around the finitely many sites. A smooth cutoff equal to one near one site and zero outside its ball, multiplied by \((x-x_j)^\alpha/\alpha!\), gives a smooth periodic function with that single jet coordinate one and all other selected coordinates zero. Such cutoffs can be formed from the smooth step built from \(e^{-1/t}\mathbf1_{t>0}\). Therefore a nonzero coefficient gives a smooth periodic (h) with \(\Lambda h\ne0\).

For completeness, a smooth periodic (h) is approximated in \(C^s\) by its Fourier partial sums, where (s) is the largest selected derivative order. Indeed integration by parts with \((1-\Delta)^k\) gives \(|\widehat h(n)|\leq C_k(1+|n|^2)^{-k}\). Choose \(2k>d+s\); the differentiated Fourier series is absolutely uniformly convergent through order (s). It equals (h) by continuous Fourier uniqueness, hence convergence is in \(C^s\). Continuity of the finite evaluation functional now gives \(\Lambda h=0\), a contradiction. This proves positive variance of every nonzero combination. \(\square\)

Consequently the pair-gradient vector \((\nabla F(x),\nabla F(y))\), and the full values-and-gradients vector at two distinct points, have positive definite covariance. On a compact separated domain

\[
D_\delta=\{(x,y)\in X^2:\operatorname{dist}(x,y)\geq\delta\},\quad\delta>0,
\]

their covariance matrices have common positive lower and finite upper eigenvalue bounds. Entries are continuous by the derivative covariance series; \(\lambda_{\min}\) is continuous because \(|\lambda_{\min}(A)-\lambda_{\min}(B)|\leq\|A-B\|_{op}\); compactness supplies the bounds. These constants may deteriorate as \(\delta\downarrow0\).

## 4. Whole-field regression, independence and the regular conditional kernel

Let (T) be a compact metric parameter space. For each \(\theta\in T\) let \(Z_\theta\in\mathbb R^m\) consist of finitely many linear combinations of derivative evaluations of \(F\), with continuous coefficients and sites and a fixed finite derivative-order bound. Integral averages of such evaluations with continuous parameters, as in §7, are also allowed. Assume explicitly that

\[
\Sigma_\theta=\operatorname{Cov}(Z_\theta)>0\quad\hbox{for every }\theta\in T.
\tag{6}
\]

Continuity and compactness then give \(\lambda_*I\preceq\Sigma_\theta\preceq\lambda^*I\) with \(0<\lambda_*\leq\lambda^*<\infty\). For the two-site physical pins on \(D_\delta\), Lemma 1 proves (6).

Define \(C^q(X)\)-valued rows

\[
H_\theta(x)=\operatorname{Cov}(F(x),Z_\theta),\quad
B_\theta=H_\theta\Sigma_\theta^{-1},\quad
R_\theta=F-B_\theta Z_\theta,\quad
F_{\theta,z}=R_\theta+B_\theta z.
\tag{7}
\]

Every derivative covariance series is uniformly absolutely summable. It follows that \(\theta\mapsto H_\theta\) is continuous in every finite \(C^q\) norm; \(\theta\mapsto\Sigma_\theta^{-1}\) is continuous and bounded. In particular \(B_\theta\) is \(C^q\)-continuous. The common smooth version makes \(\theta\mapsto Z_\theta(\omega)\) continuous for almost every sample; consequently \((\theta,z)\mapsto F_{\theta,z}(\omega)\) is continuous in \(C^q\). This is pathwise continuity of a common coupling. It does not incorrectly assume operator-norm continuity of highest-order point evaluations on a \(C^q\) unit ball.

For every finite derivative evaluation vector \(J(R_\theta)\), direct expansion gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(J(R_\theta),Z_\theta)=0.
\]

The joint vector is Gaussian; its characteristic function factors because its covariance is block diagonal. Hence that evaluation vector is independent of \(Z_\theta\).

This is independence of the **whole residual field**, not only of a chosen finite jet. Choose a countable dense set \(Q\subset X\). The maps \(f\mapsto D^\alpha f(x)\), \(x\in Q, |\alpha|\leq q\), generate the Borel sigma-algebra of \(C^q(X)\): the norm distance to any fixed (g) is the maximum of countable suprema of these coordinate differences, and separability expresses open sets as countable unions of balls. Finite-coordinate cylinder events form a generating algebra. A monotone-class argument therefore extends the finite-coordinate independence to \(R_\theta\) as a \(C^q\)-valued variable. Its residual covariance is

\[
K(x-y)-H_\theta(x)\Sigma_\theta^{-1}H_\theta(y)^T.
\]

For \(A\in\mathcal B(C^q(X))\), put

\[
\kappa(\theta,z,A)=\mathbb P\{F_{\theta,z}\in A\}.
\tag{8}
\]

The coupled map is jointly measurable (pathwise continuous in parameters and measurable in the sample), so (8) is a jointly measurable probability kernel. For bounded Borel \(\Phi\) and bounded Borel \(h\), residual independence and \(F=R_\theta+B_\theta Z_\theta\) give

\[
\mathbb E[\Phi(F)h(Z_\theta)]
=\int_{\mathbb R^m}h(z)\!\int\Phi(f)\kappa(\theta,z,df)
  \varphi_{\Sigma_\theta}(z)\,dz.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus (8) is a regular conditional law on the entire field space for each fixed parameter, and supplies a specified version at **every** target \(z\). Equation (9) is the conditional-law assertion; no conditioning on a positive-probability exact-pin event is used.

For bounded continuous \(\Phi(\theta,z,f)\), pathwise continuity and dominated convergence prove continuity of

\[
(\theta,z)\longmapsto\int\Phi(\theta,z,f)\kappa(\theta,z,df).
\tag{10}
\]

This includes bounded continuous cylinders of field derivatives through order \(q\), also at continuously varying sites. Hence the kernels are weakly continuous. Total-variation continuity is not claimed: different exact constraints can put the conditional laws on different affine subspaces.

Taking \(q=2\), \(T=D_\delta\), \(Z_{(x,y)}=(\nabla F(x),\nabla F(y))\), proves the concrete \(C^2\) kernel and conditional bounded-continuous expectation continuity required in the repaired §9 Gaussian interface. It does not by itself prove any Kac–Rice theorem or extend a continuous mark identity to a Borel elder mark.

## 5. Conditional moment bounds with an explicit regression-energy parameter

A useful stronger bound does not require a common covariance floor. Fix any finite nondegenerate observation vector (Z) and target \(z\); let

\[
E_Z(z)=z^T\Sigma^{-1}z.
\]

For any one standardized Fourier coefficient \(\zeta\) from (2), put \(c=\operatorname{Cov}(\zeta,Z)\). Positivity of the covariance of \((\zeta,Z)\), or the variance of \(\zeta-c\Sigma^{-1}Z\), gives

\[
0\leq c\Sigma^{-1}c^T\leq1,\quad
|c\Sigma^{-1}z|\leq\sqrt{E_Z(z)},\quad
\operatorname{Var}(\zeta-c\Sigma^{-1}Z)\leq1.
\tag{11}
\]

The middle inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz in the \(\Sigma^{-1}\) inner product. Therefore the conditional coefficient has \(L^p\) norm at most \(g_p+\sqrt{E_Z(z)}\), for \(p\geq1\). Conditional coefficients need not be independent.

Apply Minkowski to the absolutely summable weighted coefficient series. Its conditional mean series and residual series converge in \(L^p(C^q)\), by the same tail estimates as §2 and (11), and agree with (7). This yields the explicit bound

\[
\boxed{\quad
\bigl(\mathbb E\|F_{\theta,z}\|_{C^q}^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}
 \leq A_q\,[g_p+\sqrt{z^T\Sigma_\theta^{-1}z}],\qquad p\geq1.
\quad}
\tag{12}
\]

In particular every centered residual obeys \(\|\|R_\theta\|_{C^q}\|_p\leq A_qg_p\), uniformly over **all** nondegenerate finite observation families, even when the observation covariance floors shrink. This is a bound on each marginal expectation followed by a supremum over parameters. It does **not** assert a bound for \(\mathbb E\sup_\theta\|R_\theta\|^p\) over an arbitrary degenerating family.

On a compact family satisfying (6),

\[
\|\|F_{\theta,z}\|_{C^q}\|_p
 \leq A_q(g_p+\lambda_*^{-1/2}|z|)
 \leq C_{p,q,T}(1+|z|).
\tag{13}
\]

Thus every product of finitely many derivative evaluations, of total degree \(D\), has conditional absolute expectation bounded by \(C(1+|z|)^D\). Continuous functionals of polynomial growth also have continuous conditional expectations in (10): use a moment of order greater than their growth order for uniform integrability on compact target sets, then bounded truncation and (10). No critical-point count is automatically a polynomially bounded functional of a \(C^q\) norm.

For example, since \(\|H_f(x)\|_{op}\leq d\|f\|_{C^2}\),

\[
\mathbb E\{|\det H_F(x)\det H_F(y)|\mid Z_\theta=z\}
\leq d^{2d}A_2^{2d}
       (g_{2d}+\lambda_*^{-1/2}|z|)^{2d}.
\tag{14}
\]

The determinant product is a continuous polynomial-growth functional, so its conditional expectation times any bounded continuous cylinder weight is continuous on a compact separated pair domain. Bare Hessian-type indicators and the elder indicator need not be continuous; this proof does not assert their conditional expectations are continuous.

## 6. Unnormalized Gaussian envelopes and the fixed-far integral

On a compact nondegenerate family, the density satisfies

\[
\varphi_{\Sigma_\theta}(z)
 \leq (2\pi)^{-m/2}\lambda_*^{-m/2}
        e^{-|z|^2/(2\lambda^*)}.
\tag{15}
\]

Combining (13) with (15) gives an **unnormalized** envelope

\[
\varphi_{\Sigma_\theta}(z)
 \mathbb E[|P(JF)|\mid Z_\theta=z]
 \leq C(1+|z|)^D e^{-c|z|^2}
\tag{16}
\]

for a finite-jet polynomial \(P\) of degree \(D\) with bounded coefficients. Appending distinct symmetric-coordinate evaluations (one coordinate for each i≤j) \(A\) to the observation vector gives the same conclusion with \(z\) replaced by \((z,a)\), provided the **joint** enlarged covariance is uniformly nondegenerate. In particular its joint density is bounded by \(C e^{-c(|z|^2+\|a\|^2)}\). This avoids dividing by a small pin density.

For full physical values-and-gradients at a pair with distance at least \(\delta>0\), use target

\[
z=(b,0_d,b-\ell,0_d),\qquad 0<\ell\leq1.
\]

Then \(|z|^2\geq b^2\) and \(|z|\leq C(1+|b|)\), so (14)–(16) prove

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi_{\Sigma_\theta}(b,0_d,b-\ell,0_d)
\mathbb E[|\det H_F(x)\det H_F(y)|\mid Z_\theta=z],db\leq C_\delta,
\tag{17}
\]

uniformly in the separated pair and \(0<\ell\leq1\). Polynomial times a one-dimensional Gaussian is integrable, and the spatial domain has finite volume. Formula (17) is a completely derived Gaussian integrand bound. Identifying it as a bound for a candidate or elder density still requires the appropriate counting/Kac–Rice identity; that identity is not proved here.

## 7. Exact contact rows, their Peano kernels and a controlled contact extension

This section checks the source's actual parent (3.1), including its potentially ill-conditioned cancellation. Let \(R=(u,v_1,\ldots,v_{d-1})\in O(d)\), with midpoint zero, endpoints \(a=-ru/2,c=ru/2\). Put \(g(t)=F(tu)\). The four axial observations are exactly

\[
U_0=\frac{g(-r/2)+g(r/2)}2,\quad
U_1=\frac{g(r/2)-g(-r/2)}r,\quad
U_2=\frac{g'(r/2)-g'(-r/2)}r,
\]

\[
U_3=\frac6{r^2}\left[g'(-r/2)+g'(r/2)
           -2\frac{g(r/2)-g(-r/2)}r\right].
\tag{18}
\]

Twice integrating by parts, with \(w(s)=(s+r/2)(r/2-s)\), gives

\[
\int_{-r/2}^{r/2}w(s)g'''(s)\,ds
