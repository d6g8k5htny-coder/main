# A polynomial covariance floor for four separated scaled sites

Date: 2026-10-25. Scope: an auxiliary Gaussian/interpolation lemma for a collision-cover fallback. This note changes no v19/v20 artifact, theorem status, Rice identification, normalizer, or full D5 claim. Exponents below are deliberately nonoptimal.

## 1. Exact law, geometry, and statement

Use the exact centered real periodic Gaussian field on the fixed torus \(X=\mathbb R^2/(L\mathbb Z^2)\) from `/workspace/universal-law/v19/mathematics/GAUSSIAN_DERIVATIONS.md`, §§1–5. Its covariance is
\[
K(x-y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^2}a_n e^{i(2\pi/L)n\cdot(x-y)},\qquad
a_n=Z^{-1}e^{-(2\pi/L)^2|n|^2/2}>0.
\]
The constant Fourier mode is retained. The source proves a common smooth version, all finite global \(C^q\) moments, positivity of every independent finite derivative jet covariance, and whole-field Gaussian regression with an energy moment bound. The SHA-256 of the source bytes read for this note is
`946333fcb7985c4989d4f3030022fe35d06744d108860035633f39c98d4a6781`.

Fix \(B<\infty\) and \(\delta>0\). Let \(z_1=a=(-1/2,0),z_2=c=(1/2,0),z_3=\xi,z_4=\eta\) range over any compact family satisfying
\[
|z_i|\le B,\qquad |z_i-z_j|\ge\delta\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{1}
\]
All four pairwise separations are required, including witness–witness separation. Let \(R\in O(2)\), and evaluate at \(rRz_i\) modulo the torus. Write gradients in the frame \(R\), i.e. \(\partial_{R e_k}F\). Ordinary physical gradients differ by an orthogonal row transformation and have the same covariance eigenvalues and target norms. Let
\[
T_r=(F(ra),\nabla F(ra),F(rc),\nabla F(rc),\nabla F(r\xi),\nabla F(r\eta))\in\mathbb R^{10},
\tag{2}
\]
where (2) abbreviates the rotated sites and frame just specified.

**Lemma.** There exist \(r_0>0,c>0,C<\infty\), depending only on \(L,B,\delta\) and the fixed law, such that
\[
c r^{14} I_{10}\preceq\Sigma_r:=\operatorname{Cov}(T_r)\preceq C I_{10}
\quad(0<r\le r_0)
\tag{3}
\]
uniformly over (1) and \(R\). One may require \(r_0\le\min(1,L/(4B))\). No numerical value for \(c,r_0\), and no uniformity as \(\delta\downarrow0\) or \(L\) varies, is claimed. The exponent 14 also works for the full 12 values-and-gradients observations at the four sites.

## 2. Exact degree-seven scalar Hermite interpolation

For \(i\ne j\), set
\[
\ell_{ij}(z)=\frac{(z-z_j)\cdot(z_i-z_j)}{|z_i-z_j|^2},\qquad
L_i(z)=\prod_{j\ne i}\ell_{ij}(z)^2,
\]
\[
g_i=\nabla L_i(z_i)=2\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{z_i-z_j}{|z_i-z_j|^2},
\]
\[
H_{i0}(z)=[1-g_i\cdot(z-z_i)]L_i(z),\qquad
H_{ik}(z)=(z_k-z_{i,k})L_i(z),\quad k=1,2.
\tag{4}
\]
These are scalar polynomials of total degree at most seven. Since \(\ell_{ij}(z_i)=1\), \(L_i(z_i)=1\). At every other site \(z_j\), the factor \(\ell_{ij}^2\) makes both the value and all first derivatives vanish. At \(z_i\), the linear correction in \(H_{i0}\) cancels \(g_i\). Thus
\[
H_{i0}(z_j)=\mathbf1_{i=j},\quad \nabla H_{i0}(z_j)=0,
\]
\[
H_{ik}(z_j)=0,\quad \partial_l H_{ik}(z_j)=\mathbf1_{i=j}\mathbf1_{k=l}.
\tag{5}
\]
This proves exact interpolation for arbitrary full first-jet data at the four sites, with no generic-position assumption or noncollinearity requirement.

Here is a direct uniform coefficient bound. For a polynomial use the sum of absolute monomial coefficients \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{coef},1}\); it is submultiplicative. Put \(Q=(\sqrt2+B)/\delta\). Then
\[
\|\ell_{ij}\|_{\mathrm{coef},1}\le Q,\quad
\|L_i\|_{\mathrm{coef},1}\le Q^6,\quad |g_i|\le6/\delta.
\]
Consequently
\[
\|H_{i0}\|_{\mathrm{coef},1}\le[1+6(B+\sqrt2)/\delta]Q^6,
\qquad
\|H_{ik}\|_{\mathrm{coef},1}\le(1+B)Q^6.
\tag{6}
\]
All constants are uniform under (1), including collinear configurations. Let \(C_H\) be any resulting bound for the Euclidean operator norm of interpolation from 12 data coordinates to the 36 monomial coefficients of total degree at most seven.

## 3. A scaled right inverse for physical observation rows

Use local coordinates \(x\) with the field \(f_R(x)=F(Rx)\). For prescribed *physical* value/gradient data \(y=(y_{i0},y_{i1},y_{i2})_{i=1}^4\), define
\[
p_{r,y}(z)=\sum_i y_{i0}H_{i0}(z)+r\sum_{i,k}y_{ik}H_{ik}(z),\qquad
Q_{r,y}(x)=p_{r,y}(x/r).
\tag{7}
\]
The chain rule and (5) give \(Q_{r,y}(rz_i)=y_{i0}\) and \(\partial_kQ_{r,y}(rz_i)=y_{ik}\). For \(r\le1\), every coefficient of degree \(j\le7\) is multiplied by \(r^{-j}\le r^{-7}\), while scaling the input gradients by \(r\) has operator norm at most one. Therefore
\[
\|\operatorname{coef}Q_{r,y}\|_2\le C_H r^{-7}|y|.
\tag{8}
\]
For the 10 rows in (2), insert zero data for the two omitted witness values. This is an isometric embedding into the full 12-row data and leaves (8) unchanged.

Let \(A_{r,z}^{(8)}\) map the 45 monomial coefficients of a polynomial of total degree at most eight to its 10 physical observation rows at \(rz_i\). Embed the coefficients of (7) in that 45-dimensional space by zero degree-eight coefficients. This produces a linear right inverse \(H_{r,z}\) with
\[
A_{r,z}^{(8)}H_{r,z}=I_{10},\qquad
\|H_{r,z}\|_{op}\le C_Hr^{-7}.
\tag{9}
\]
For any \(v\in\mathbb R^{10}\),
\[
|v|^2=\langle (A_{r,z}^{(8)})^Tv,H_{r,z}v\rangle
\le |(A_{r,z}^{(8)})^Tv|C_Hr^{-7}|v|,
\]
so
\[
|(A_{r,z}^{(8)})^Tv|\ge C_H^{-1}r^7|v|.
\tag{10}
\]
The use of degree eight in the source Taylor jet does not degrade this estimate to \(r^8\): its right inverse is still supported on degrees at most seven.

## 4. Source-jet positivity and the correlated Taylor remainder

Let
\[
J_R=(D^\alpha f_R(0)/\alpha!)_{|\alpha|\le8},\qquad
P_{8,R}(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\le8}(J_R)_\alpha x^\alpha.
\]
The full order-eight jet covariance is positive definite by positivity of all Fourier weights and the finite-jet argument in source §3. This remains true after every frame change. Covariance entries depend continuously on \(R\); compactness of \(O(2)\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(J_R)\succeq\lambda_8 I_{45},\qquad \lambda_8>0.
\tag{11}
\]
There is no assumption that the jet coordinates are independent. From (10)–(11),
\[
\|v^TA_{r,z}^{(8)}J_R\|_{L^2}\ge \sqrt{\lambda_8}C_H^{-1}r^7|v|=:\gamma r^7|v|.
\tag{12}
\]

Taylor's integral remainder along the segment \(tx\), with \(|x|\le rB\), gives a value remainder bounded by \(C_Br^9\|F\|_{C^9}\). Differentiating the degree-eight polynomial gives the degree-seven Taylor polynomial of each first derivative, whose remainder is bounded by \(C_Br^8\|F\|_{C^9}\). Directional derivatives in a rotated frame introduce only finite dimension/order constants (for example a product of \(s\) unit directional derivatives is bounded by \(2^{s/2}\|F\|_{C^s}\)). The source's \(C^9\) second moment bound therefore gives the uniform vector bound
\[
T_r=A_{r,z}^{(8)}J_R+\varepsilon_{r,z,R},\qquad
\big(\mathbb E|\varepsilon_{r,z,R}|^2\big)^{1/2}\le C_Er^8.
\tag{13}
\]
All variables in (13) are centered. The remainder is generally correlated with the Taylor jet. We use the reverse triangle inequality in \(L^2\), **not** a Loewner comparison with Taylor covariance:
\[
\sqrt{v^T\Sigma_rv}=\|v^TT_r\|_2
\ge\gamma r^7|v|-C_Er^8|v|.
\tag{14}
\]
Choose \(r_0\le\gamma/(2C_E)\) if \(C_E>0\). Then (14) gives (3) with \(c=\gamma^2/4\). The upper bound follows from the uniform second moments of value and first-derivative evaluations. The same argument applies verbatim to all 12 rows.

This resolves the possible degree-seven remainder defect: with a degree-seven Taylor polynomial, the gradient remainder would only be \(O(r^7)\), so a lower bound could not be inferred from this argument. The extra Taylor degree makes the error smaller by an explicit factor \(r\).

## 5. Conditional fields, densities, and bounded physical targets

Define the exact regression energy \(E_r(t)=t^T\Sigma_r^{-1}t\). The specified whole-field conditional Gaussian kernel of source §4 exists for every \(r>0\) and every target \(t\), not merely almost every target. Source (12), applied at each parameter, and (3) give for every finite \(q\ge0,p\ge1\)
\[
\left(\mathbb E[\|F\|_{C^q}^p\mid T_r=t]\right)^{1/p}
\le A_q(g_p+\sqrt{E_r(t)})
\le A_q(g_p+c^{-1/2}r^{-7}|t|).
\tag{15}
\]
In particular, on \(|t|\le M\) the \(p\)-th moment is at most \(C_{p,q,M}r^{-7p}\). The conditional mean is bounded in \(C^q\) by \(A_q\sqrt{E_r(t)}\), and the centered conditional residual has \(C^q\) \(L^p\) norm at most \(A_qg_p\), uniformly in all the displayed parameters. These are suprema of individual conditional moments; no expectation of a supremum over configurations is asserted.

The exact 10-dimensional physical density satisfies
\[
\varphi_{T_r}(t)
=(2\pi)^{-5}(\det\Sigma_r)^{-1/2}e^{-E_r(t)/2}
\le C r^{-70}e^{-E_r(t)/2}.
\tag{16}
\]
For a finite-jet polynomial \(P\) of total degree \(D\), evaluated at arbitrary sites and with uniformly bounded coefficients,
\[
\varphi_{T_r}(t)\,\mathbb E[|P(JF)|\mid T_r=t]
\le C r^{-70}(1+E_r(t)^{D/2})e^{-E_r(t)/2}.
\tag{17}
\]
On bounded target sets this is also bounded by \(C_Mr^{-70-7D}e^{-E_r(t)/2}\). Keeping the energy form in (17) is often sharper.

If \(P_r\in\mathbb R^6\) denotes the endpoint values and gradients and \(G_r\in\mathbb R^4\) the two witness gradients, write \(T_r=(P_r,G_r)\). The conditional covariance \(S_r=\operatorname{Cov}(G_r\mid P_r)\) obeys \(S_r\succeq cr^{14}I_4\). Indeed, for every \(w\), its Schur-complement variational formula gives
\[
w^TS_rw=\min_u (u,w)^T\Sigma_r(u,w)\ge cr^{14}|w|^2.
\]
Thus its conditional density has the separate bound
\[
\varphi_{G_r\mid P_r=p}(g)\le Cr^{-28}
\exp\{-\tfrac12(g-m_r(p))^TS_r^{-1}(g-m_r(p))\}.
\tag{18}
\]
This is a Gaussian conditional-density statement; it does not divide an unnormalized integral by an unrelated selection normalizer.

For the raw physical pin \(p=(b,0,0,b-kr^3,0,0)\), compact \((b,k)\) and \(r\le1\) give bounded \(p\). Adding bounded witness-gradient targets gives bounded \(t\), so (15) applies directly. No uniform bounded conditional mean is claimed for the enlarged ten-row conditioning: \(r^{-7}\) growth is allowed. Any rare-target energy bound established separately may be inserted into (16)–(18) with its exact target and measure convention.

## 6. Optional finite higher-jet extension

For clarity, a polynomial bound for an **enlarged** joint density is not a consequence of (3) alone. It can, however, be proved by the same construction when the extra rows are distinct physical derivative coordinates at these same four sites, of order at most a fixed \(m\ge1\).

Put \(L_i^{(m)}=\prod_{j\ne i}\ell_{ij}^{m+1}\), of degree \(3(m+1)\). For multiindices \(|\alpha|\le m\), let \([1/L_i^{(m)}]_{\le m-|\alpha|,i}\) be the Taylor polynomial about \(z_i\) of that reciprocal, truncated at the indicated degree. It exists because \(L_i^{(m)}(z_i)=1\). Then
\[
H_{i\alpha}^{(m)}(z)=L_i^{(m)}(z)\frac{(z-z_i)^\alpha}{\alpha!}
 [1/L_i^{(m)}]_{\le m-|\alpha|,i}(z)
\tag{19}
\]
has degree at most \(d_m=4m+3\) and satisfies
\(D^\beta H_{i\alpha}^{(m)}(z_j)=\mathbf1_{i=j}\mathbf1_{\alpha=\beta}\) for all \(|\beta|\le m\). At other sites the order-\(m+1\) factor kills those derivatives; at its own site the reciprocal truncation leaves the desired monomial modulo terms of degree at least \(m+1\). Coefficients are uniformly bounded: the finite Taylor recursion for the reciprocal has constant denominator one, and all coefficients of \(L_i^{(m)}\), translated to \(z_i\), are uniformly bounded by (1).

Scale input derivative data by \(r^{|\alpha|}\) and the polynomial variable by \(1/r\). The right inverse has norm at most \(Cr^{-d_m}\). Use the source Taylor jet through degree \(D_m=d_m+m\). The largest-order observation remainder is \(O_{L^2}(r^{D_m+1-m})=O(r^{d_m+1})\), smaller than the Taylor standard-deviation floor \(cr^{d_m}\). Hence any selected \(n\) distinct rows from these jets have
\[
\lambda_{\min}\ge c_m r^{2d_m},\qquad
\varphi(t)\le C_mr^{-d_mn}e^{-E(t)/2},\qquad
\|\|F\|_{C^q}\|_{L^p(\,\cdot\mid t)}\le C(1+r^{-d_m}|t|).
\tag{20}
\]
For example, adding all three distinct symmetric Hessian coordinates at each site to the ten rows gives \(n=22,m=2,d_m=11\): a valid, nonoptimal covariance floor is \(cr^{22}\) and joint-density prefactor \(Cr^{-242}\). Symmetric Hessian coordinates are counted once; duplicate rows would invalidate positivity.
