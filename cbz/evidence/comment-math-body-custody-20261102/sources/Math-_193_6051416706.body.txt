## 1. Exact source law and one Banach-valued Gaussian field

Let T=ℝᵈ/(Lℤᵈ), ω_k=2πk/L,

\[
 Z=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}e^{-|\omega_k|^2/2},\qquad
 p_k=Z^{-1}e^{-|\omega_k|^2/2}.
\]

Choose a half-lattice H containing one representative of each nonzero pair {k,−k}. On one countable product standard-normal probability space, use independent ξ₀, ξ_k, η_k and

\[
 F(t)=\sqrt{p_0}\xi_0+
 \sum_{k\in H}\sqrt{2p_k}
 [\xi_k\cos(\omega_k\cdot t)+\eta_k\sin(\omega_k\cdot t)].
\]

The covariance is exactly Σ_k p_k exp(iω_k·(t−v)). Unfolding the Fourier coefficient of the periodized exp(−|t|²/2) gives (2π)^{d/2}L^{−d}exp(−|ω_k|²/2); its common factor cancels against evaluation at zero. Thus this series has precisely the requested K_L and variance one. The √(2p_k) amplitude is retained. No nonperiodic covariance, isotropy or additional field scaling is introduced.

For an integer m≥0 define

\[
 S_m=\sqrt{p_0}|\xi_0|+
 \sum_{k\in H}\sqrt{2p_k}(|\xi_k|+|\eta_k|)(1+|\omega_k|)^m.
\]

For every finite p≥1, Minkowski gives

\[
 \|S_m\|_{L^p}\le
 \sqrt{p_0}\|\xi_0\|_{L^p}+
 2\|\xi_0\|_{L^p}\sum_{k\in H}\sqrt{2p_k}(1+|\omega_k|)^m<\infty.
\]

The last sum is a polynomial times a Gaussian on the lattice. Tonelli and a countable intersection over m produce a single probability-one event on which all derivative series converge absolutely and uniformly, giving the same periodic C∞ sample. Finite partial sums converge in L^p(C^m), with tails bounded by the corresponding tails of S_m. The coordinate norm is max over |α|≤m of sup_T|∂^αF|; changing to tensor or rotated-frame operator norms costs constants depending only on d.

E=C⁴(T;ℝ) is separable: embed its derivatives into a finite product of separable C(T) spaces; its image has the compatibility relations of derivatives and is a metric subspace. Define the Fourier limit to be zero on its null exceptional set. It is a Borel E-valued random variable. For each continuous linear functional λ∈E*, λF_N is centered Gaussian and converges in L², so its variance and characteristic function converge to those of a centered Gaussian λF. Hence F is a centered Banach Gaussian random element. In particular E||F||_E^p<∞ for all finite p>0, using the preceding estimate and Jensen when p<1. This is a whole-field law, not just Gaussian evaluation marginals or a choice of incompatible derivative versions.

All constants below depend on this fixed d,L and the displayed norm. No uniformity in L, large dimension or field amplitude is asserted.

## 2. Exact cancellation and uniform observation-operator convergence

Put x=−re/2, y=re/2, e∈S^{d−1}, with r small enough for this segment to lie in an embedded torus chart. For a deterministic u define

\[
 \bar g_r(u)=\tfrac12[\nabla u(x)+\nabla u(y)],\quad
 J_r(u)=\frac{\nabla u(y)-\nabla u(x)}r,\quad
 W_r(u)=\frac{u(x)-u(y)+r e\cdot\bar g_r(u)}{r^3}.
\]

Let T_{r,e}u=(\bar g_r,J_r,W_r)∈ℝ^{2d+1}, and

\[
 T_{0,e}u=(\nabla u(0),D^2u(0)e,\partial_e^3u(0)/12).
\]

There is no unstable subtraction estimate in the last row. With ψ(t)=u(te), a=r/2, integration by parts twice gives the exact identity

\[
 a[\psi'(-a)+\psi'(a)]-\int_{-a}^a\psi'(t)\,dt
 =\tfrac12\int_{-a}^a(a^2-t^2)\psi'''(t)\,dt.
\]

Since u(x)−u(y)=−∫ψ', rescaling yields

\[
 \boxed{W_r(u)=\tfrac12\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}
       (1/4-t^2)\partial_e^3u(rte)\,dt.}
\]

The nonnegative kernel has mass 1/12. Thus the limit is **+∂_e³u(0)/12**, with the supplied convention D=u(x)−u(y). Reversing that convention without changing the cancellation would be incorrect. Also

\[
 J_r(u)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}D^2u(rte)e\,dt.
\]

These identities show sup_{r,e}||T_{r,e}||_{C^3→ℝ^{2d+1}}≤C. Symmetric Taylor estimates for the first two rows and the kernel estimate for the third give

\[
 \sup_e\|T_{r,e}-T_{0,e}\|_{C^4\to\mathbb R^{2d+1}}\le Cr.
 \tag{1}
\]

In particular U_{r,e}=T_{r,e}F tends to U_{0,e}=(g,He,A_e/12), uniformly over directions, in every finite L^p and on the same smooth-sample event. The first two rows permit better symmetric estimates, but no higher asymptotic term is used here. Uniform translations in the midpoint are also allowed, since torus translation preserves the norms and stationarity; the displayed coupling fixes midpoint zero as requested.

**Regularity correction.** C³ suffices for these exact integral identities and uniform-in-e convergence for each fixed C³ function, using the uniform modulus of continuity of its third derivative. It does not give operator-norm convergence on the C³ unit ball. For example, take e=e₁, r=L/(2πN), and u_r(t)=r³sin(t₁/r). For r≤1 its C³ norm is one. The third row minus its contact value equals

\[
 \tfrac12\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}(1/4-t^2)[1-\cos t]\,dt>0,
\]

independently of r. C⁴ is the first integer C^m space giving the uniform operator estimate (1); C^{3,α} would instead give order r^α. C⁴ control gives a leading pin identity D=r³A_e/12+O(r⁴||u||_{C^4}) under zero endpoint gradients. It does not by itself justify an O(r⁵) remainder in the frozen answer; such an estimate needs additional fifth-order control. The exact law does have all higher derivative moments, but the leading route here uses only C⁴.

## 3. Uniformly positive limiting covariance and C⁴ cross-covariance maps

Write Γ_{r,e}=E[U_{r,e}U_{r,e}^{\mathsf T}]. The contact vector has strictly positive covariance for each e. A real linear combination of its rows has Fourier symbol

\[
 P(\omega)=i\,a\cdot\omega-(b\cdot\omega)(e\cdot\omega)
          -\frac{ic}{12}(e\cdot\omega)^3.
\]

Its variance is Σ_k p_k|P(ω_k)|². Every p_k>0, so zero variance forces P to vanish on the full scaled integer lattice. A polynomial vanishing there is identically zero, by successively fixing all but one integer coordinate. Its distinct homogeneous degrees imply c=0 and a=0; the degree-two product then implies b=0 because e≠0 and the polynomial ring has no zero divisors. Thus no nonzero row combination vanishes. This also proves strict positivity of the cubic Schur complement after conditioning on g and He.

The entries of Γ_{0,e} are continuous in e, by their finite polynomial dependence on e and the absolutely summable spectral moments. Compactness of S^{d−1} therefore gives

\[
 0<\lambda_*\le\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_{0,e})
 \le\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma_{0,e})\le\Lambda_*<\infty.
\]

Equation (1) and E||F||_E²<∞ give sup_e||Γ_{r,e}−Γ_{0,e}||≤Cr. Reducing a positive r_* if necessary, all 0≤r≤r_* satisfy a common lower and upper covariance bound; their inverses are bounded uniformly and converge uniformly, since Γ_r^{−1}−Γ_0^{−1}=Γ_r^{−1}(Γ_0−Γ_r)Γ_0^{−1}. This is an existence bound for fixed d,L, not a numerical enclosure and not rotational invariance.

Now define the genuine Banach cross-covariance map

\[
 C_{r,e}:\mathbb R^{2d+1}\to E,\qquad
 C_{r,e}v=E[F\,(v\cdot U_{r,e})].
\]

This is a Bochner expectation: its integrability follows from E||F||_E² and the uniform bound on T. Cauchy–Schwarz gives ||C_r||≤C, and, more directly,

\[
 \sup_e\|C_{r,e}-C_{0,e}\|_{\mathbb R^{2d+1}\to E}
 \le E[\|F\|_E\sup_e\|(T_{r,e}-T_{0,e})F\|]
 \le Cr E\|F\|_E^2.
 \tag{2}
\]

Thus the cross-covariances converge as actual functions with four derivatives on the whole torus, not merely at fixed derivative-evaluation sites. T_{r,e}C_{r,e}=Γ_{r,e}, by applying the continuous linear operator inside the Bochner expectation.

For an independent sign check, the contact cross-covariance evaluated at t is

\[
 C_{0,e}(t)=
 [-\nabla K_L(t),\ D^2K_L(t)e,\ -\partial_e^3K_L(t)/12].
\]

The cubic cross-covariance has a minus sign because the observation differentiates the second covariance variable, whereas the observation itself tends to +A_e/12. Using a smooth-kernel calculation requires mixed derivatives of total order up to seven to place this contact correction in C⁴ and up to eight for a direct Lipschitz estimate. They exist for the exact spectrum. Equations (1)–(2) prove the same facts using only a C⁴-valued F with a finite second moment; they do not require a C⁸ sample assumption.

## 4. Canonical whole-field conditional laws and one residual coupling

Let B_{r,e}=C_{r,e}Γ_{r,e}^{−1}:ℝ^{2d+1}→E. The preceding estimates imply

\[
 \sup_{r,e}\|B_{r,e}\|\le C,\qquad
 \sup_e\|B_{r,e}-B_{0,e}\|\le Cr.
\]

On the original single Fourier probability space define

\[
 R_{r,e}=F-B_{r,e}U_{r,e},\qquad
 F_{r,e,u}=R_{r,e}+B_{r,e}u.
 \tag{3}
\]

For each fixed (r,e), the E-valued residual is jointly Gaussian with U_{r,e}. Every continuous linear functional of R has zero covariance with every coordinate of U, because C−BΓ=0. Gaussian characteristic functions therefore give independence for every finite family of residual evaluations and U. Derivative evaluations through order four on a countable dense subset of T generate the E-Borel sigma-algebra, so a monotone-class argument gives independence of the entire E-valued R and U. This independence is for each parameter; no independence of the entire collection of residuals from every observation at every other parameter is asserted.

Moreover T_{r,e}R_{r,e}=0 and T_{r,e}B_{r,e}=Id exactly. Hence

\[
 T_{r,e}F_{r,e,u}=u.
 \tag{4}
\]

F=R+B U, with R independent of U, proves that u↦Law(R+B u) is a regular conditional distribution of F given U. Explicitly, integrate any bounded Borel field test and bounded Borel observation test using independence; the result is the defining disintegration identity. The kernel is defined and weakly continuous for **every** u, not just almost every u. Its parameters are continuous in the same coupling, so it is also Borel in (r,e,u). This is the canonical continuous Gaussian regression version. It is not conditioning on a positive-probability event; the prescribed exact observations have probability zero.

Subtracting the two residuals in (3) and using the uniform operator bounds gives, on the one smooth-sample event,

\[
 \sup_e\|R_{r,e}-R_{0,e}\|_E\le Cr\|F\|_E.
\]

Therefore the residuals converge uniformly over e almost surely and in every finite L^p(E). For u_z=(0,0,z),

\[
 \sup_e\|F_{r,e,u_z}-F_{0,e,u_z}\|_E
 \le Cr(\|F\|_E+|z|).
 \tag{5}
\]

This is uniform over bounded z-sets, with the displayed quantitative growth for arbitrary z. It is not a claim of convergence uniformly over all unbounded z. If e varies towards e₀, continuity of T_{0,e}, Γ_{0,e} and C_{0,e} also gives the corresponding same-field C⁴ convergence to F_{0,e₀,u_z}. No finite-dimensional substitute for (5) is used.

At positive r the observation change is exactly invertible:

\[
 \nabla F(x)=\bar g-rJ/2,\quad
 \nabla F(y)=\bar g+rJ/2,\quad
 D=r^3W-r e\cdot\bar g.
\]

Consequently the canonical conditional law at endpoint gradients zero and D=ℓ is precisely (3) with u_z and z=ℓ/r³. Equation (4) gives those endpoint identities for every constructed sample. At contact it gives g=0, He=0 and A_e=12z exactly. For positive fixed z the cubic sign is positive. The contact field has a degenerate critical Hessian at zero, intentionally forced by He=0; it must not be called Morse at that point.

## 5. Supremum moments, determinant control and leading domination

From (3), ||R_{r,e}||_E≤C||F||_E and ||B_{r,e}u_z||_E≤C|z|. Thus for every finite p>0,

\[
 \sup_{0\le r\le r_*,e}
 E\|F_{r,e,u_z}\|_{C^4}^p\le C_p(1+|z|)^p.
 \tag{6}
\]

This bounds global derivative suprema through order four and all corresponding rotated-frame tensor norms, uniformly in direction. The same Fourier construction gives higher fixed derivative orders when required, with their own constants. Neither a random inverse Hessian moment nor a normalization by a rare selection event is used.

Under the exact finite-r pins, the endpoint gradients agree, so

\[
 \frac1r\int_{-r/2}^{r/2}D^2F(te)e\,dt=0.
\]

Subtracting this average from either endpoint column and using the third derivative gives ||D²F(x)e||,||D²F(y)e||≤Cr M₃, where M_j bounds the global order-j tensor derivative. In an orthonormal basis beginning with e, Hadamard's inequality therefore yields

\[
 |\det D^2F(x)\det D^2F(y)|
 \le Cr^2 M_3^2M_2^{2d-2}
 \le Cr^2\|F\|_{C^3}^{2d}.
\]

Together with (6), this proves the actual conditional bound

\[
 E[|\det D^2F(x)\det D^2F(y)|\mid
    \nabla F(x)=\nabla F(y)=0,D=\ell]
 \le Cr^2(1+|\ell/r^3|)^{2d},
 \tag{7}
\]

for the canonical conditional version. C³ controls this determinant step; C⁴ supplies the derivative supremum needed by the controlled-fold step.

The common positive covariance floor implies a uniformly positive Schur-complement variance for W given (\bar g,J)=0, and a uniformly bounded upper variance. Its centered Gaussian density is ≤C exp(−cz²). Changing D=r³W gives a lifetime density ≤Cr^{−3}exp(−c(ℓ/r³)²). The endpoint-gradient transformation has absolute determinant r^d, so its zero density is bounded by Cr^{−d}. Including radial volume r^{d−1} and (7) gives the leading-only unnormalized candidate majorant

\[
 Cr^{-2}(1+|\ell/r^3|)^{2d}e^{-c(\ell/r^3)^2}.
\]

With r=ℓ^{1/3}u and the prescribed ℓ^{1/3} prefactor, the resulting integrable bound is

\[
 Cu^{-2}(1+u^{-3})^{2d}e^{-cu^{-6}}.
\]

It decays like u^{−2} at infinity and faster than every power at zero. It uses the entire conditional field and integrates the unobserved birth rather than imposing a bounded birth window. This verifies the uniform regression/moment part of the frozen answer's domination route; existence of the particular marked lifetime-density version and its actual-bar interpretation remain separate requirements.
