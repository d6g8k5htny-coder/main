### 1. Lemma L: affine fields, polynomial determinants, and the correct Lipschitz loss

The target moves in **three** observation rows:
`delta v_r=(-r³/2,-r²,0,12,0,...)`.
The exact Gaussian regression (R3) therefore gives
`F_r^(k)=F_r^(0)+k omega_r`, `omega_r=C_r Sigma_r^-1 delta v_r`,
with a deterministic uniformly C⁷-bounded shift by (R2). All `0<=k'<=1` share a single `T0` with `||T0||p<=C_p(1+|b|)`. There is no claim that the actual transverse Hessian is independent of k or that its determinant is affine in k. The **matrix** path is affine; each determinant is a polynomial of degree at most d.

The scaled matrix derivative is uniformly bounded: the pinned shift obeys `alpha_M=-6+O(r)`, `alpha_S=6+O(r)`, bounded transverse blocks and bounded beta, so the apparently singular scaling introduces no 1/r derivative loss. Multilinearity gives `|D_i'|<=C T0^(d-1)`.

The filtered product w is continuous. On each interval between roots of `p=D_M D_S`, inertia is constant and w is either `-p` or zero. At each root its determinant weight vanishes. If p is identically zero, w is identically zero. Thus w is absolutely continuous and
`|w(k)-w(0)|<=integral_0^k |p'(t)|1_typed(t) dt`.
An unweighted type indicator would not satisfy this argument.

The common-shift sign-window expansion remains valid with the **actual midpoint transverse block of F_r^(k')** in place of the coupled contact block. This follows directly from the pinned Taylor identities:
`alpha_M,S=∓6k'+r f4/12+O(r²T0)`,
`A_M,S=A_mid∓rB/2+O(r²T0)`,
`beta_M,S=∓gamma/2+O(rT0)`.
The transverse gradient pins give the needed center mixed derivative `O(r²T0)). Consequently
`D_M,S=∓6k' Delta+rY+O(r²T0^N)`
with the same Y, and opposite determinant signs imply
`|D_i|<=C(k'+r²)T0^N`.
Combining this with the derivative bound, then integrating and taking moments, gives `C(k²+kr²)(1+|b|)^N`. No independence or eigenvalue-density estimate is used in this step.

For the pin-density factor, differentiating the uniformly nonsingular Gaussian along the affine target gives `|pi(k)-pi(0)|<=Ck(1+|b|)exp(-cb²)` on `0<=k<=1`. With (W.1), the extra terms `k³` and `kr⁴` are absorbed into `k²+kr²` because `k<=1,r<=r0*<=1`. This proves (7.1).

**A useful exact guard for this argument:** locally take
`f_k(x,y)=2k(x-r)(x+r/2)²+x(x²-r²/4)²-y²/2`.
At M=-r/2, S=r/2 it has the prescribed values and zero gradients. For k>=0, `det K_M=6k+r²`, `det K_S=-6k-r²`, and
`w(k)-w(0)=36k²+12kr²`.
Thus a purported pathwise O(k²) replacement would be false as k/r² tends to zero. This is a local deterministic control of the proof method, not a counterexample to the stated Gaussian lemma or a claim about necessity after Gaussian averaging.
