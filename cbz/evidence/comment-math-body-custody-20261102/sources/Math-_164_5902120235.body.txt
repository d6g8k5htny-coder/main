## 3. Fourier normalization, reality, and covariance

Write `k_n=2*pi*n/L`, `w_n=exp(-|k_n|^2/2)`, and `S=sum_n w_n`. The Gaussian lattice sum is positive and finite, so `a_n=w_n/S` is positive, symmetric in n, and sums to one.

Here is a complete choice of the standard real Gaussian coordinates implicit in the source. Choose one representative from each pair `{n,-n}`, n nonzero. For independent standard real Gaussians Z0 and X_n,Y_n, set

`c_0=sqrt(a_0)*Z0`,

`c_n=sqrt(a_n/2)*(X_n+iY_n)`, `c_{-n}=conjugate(c_n)`.

The real and imaginary coordinates of each nonzero representative have equal variance a_n/2. Thus `E|c_n|^2=a_n` and `E c_n^2=0`. This is the usual circular conjugate-pair construction; equal variances matter. The statement should not be read as claiming that every arbitrary Gaussian pair with only the same total second moment has the specified stationary covariance.

A conjugate pair contributes

`sqrt(2*a_n)*(X_n*cos(k_n.x)-Y_n*sin(k_n.x))`,

which is real. Its covariance at x,y is `2*a_n*cos(k_n.(x-y))`. Including the zero mode and all pairs gives

`E[F(x)F(y)] = sum_n a_n*exp(i*k_n.(x-y))`,

and variance `a_0+2*sum_representatives a_n=sum_n a_n=1`. Finite partial sums are centered Gaussian. Their L2 limits have this covariance and Gaussian finite-dimensional laws. The almost-sure uniform convergence established below supplies the continuous version.

The coefficients also have the asserted periodized Gaussian normalization. For

`P_L(t)=sum_{m in Z^2} exp(-|t+Lm|^2/2)`,

the nth torus Fourier coefficient is obtained by integrating over a fundamental square and translating its summands:

`L^(-2) integral_R2 exp(-|x|^2/2) exp(-i*k_n.x) dx = (2*pi/L^2) exp(-|k_n|^2/2)`.

Absolute Gaussian integrability justifies the translation/sum interchange. The one-dimensional Gaussian transform follows, for example, by integration by parts: its derivative in frequency equals minus frequency times the transform, with value sqrt(2*pi) at zero. Taking the product yields the displayed two-dimensional coefficient. Consequently `P_L(0)=(2*pi/L^2)*S`, and the covariance `P_L(t)/P_L(0)` has exactly coefficients a_n. There is no missing factor of two, L, or pair multiplicity.

## 4. One common event of normal convergence and entire extension

For each positive integer R, Cauchy–Schwarz gives `E|c_n| <= sqrt(a_n)`. A useful explicit domination is

`-t^2/4+R*t <= -t^2/8+2*R^2` for t>=0,

since the difference is `(t-4R)^2/8`. It follows that

`sum_n sqrt(a_n)*exp(R*|k_n|) <= S^(-1/2)*exp(2*R^2)*sum_n exp(-|k_n|^2/8) < infinity`.

Tonelli therefore gives finite expectation for the nonnegative random sum `S_R=sum_n |c_n|exp(R|k_n|)`. A nonnegative random variable with finite expectation is finite almost surely. Intersecting these probability-one events over integer R gives **one probability-one event** on which every S_R is finite.

For a complex compact set K in C2, choose integer R with `|Im z|<=R` on K. Then

`sup_(z in K) |c_n exp(i k_n.z)| <= |c_n|exp(R|k_n|)`.

The series is absolutely and uniformly convergent on K on the common event. Each summand is entire, and locally normal convergence gives an entire function. Equivalently, derivatives may be justified directly: each polynomial factor `|k_n|^m` is bounded by a constant times `exp(|k_n|)`, so S_(R+1) dominates the derivative series on K. This also verifies the smoothness used by the finite-difference step without a formal exchange of unbounded series.

Every summand is L-periodic in each real coordinate, so the limit descends to the real torus. Realness follows from the conjugate pairing and absolute convergence. Exceptional outcomes can be assigned the zero continuous function, giving a `C(T)`-valued measurable version as the uniform limit of measurable finite sums. Another continuous modification is indistinguishable: equality at a countable dense set holds on one probability-one event, and continuity extends it to all of T. If versions are initially realized on different spaces, the same countable evaluation law determines their `C(T)` laws; a special coupling is not needed for the distributional conclusion.

## 5. Explicit countable measurable reconstruction

Fix deterministic U. Choose a deterministic lift u of a point in U and a coordinate ball of radius rho around u contained in the lifted set, with rho smaller than the injectivity radius. For a multi-index alpha=(alpha1,alpha2), write `|alpha|_1=alpha1+alpha2` and choose, for example,

`h_(alpha,j)=rho/[2*j*(1+|alpha|_1)]`, j>=1.

Every required point `u+h_(alpha,j)*k`, `0<=k_i<=alpha_i`, lies in that ball because its distance is at most `h_(alpha,j)*|alpha|_1<rho`. The mixed forward-difference quotient

`D_(alpha,j)(s)=h_(alpha,j)^(-|alpha|_1) sum_(0<=k_i<=alpha_i) (-1)^(|alpha|_1-|k|_1) binom(alpha1,k1) binom(alpha2,k2) s(u+h_(alpha,j)k)`

is a finite linear combination of evaluations and hence is Borel measurable on `C(U)`. The case alpha=0 simply evaluates at u. Repeated applications of the fundamental theorem of calculus express the nonzero-order quotient as the average of the continuous derivative `partial^alpha F` over a shrinking product cube. Therefore it converges to `partial^alpha F(u)` on every entire sample path.

For arbitrary input s, the set on which the real sequence D_(alpha,j)(s) converges finitely is Borel: it is its Cauchy set, written using countable unions/intersections and inequalities between measurable functions. On that set use its limit; elsewhere assign zero. This defines a real-valued Borel function d_alpha(s). There are countably many alpha, and on the common entire-path event every d_alpha simultaneously equals the actual derivative. No assertion about convergence for all arbitrary continuous inputs is needed.

For a fixed target lift v, form the finite total-degree Taylor polynomials

`T_M(s;v)=sum_(|alpha|_1<=M) d_alpha(s)*(v-u)^alpha/alpha!`.

They are Borel functions. An entire function of two complex variables has an absolutely convergent Taylor expansion about u at every v. To see the relevant convergence explicitly, choose a polydisc with radii R1,R2 strictly larger than `|v_i-u_i|`. Cauchy's coefficient bound gives an absolutely summable geometric majorant with ratios `|v_i-u_i|/Ri<1`; hence total-degree partial sums converge to F(v). This uses the established **entire** extension, rather than assuming that a merely local real-analytic Taylor radius reaches an arbitrary point.

Define g_(U,v)(s) as the finite limit of T_M(s;v) when it exists and zero otherwise. The same Cauchy-set construction makes g_(U,v) Borel. It equals F(v) almost surely. Choosing a deterministic countable dense subset of T and performing the construction at all its points gives a countable family of decoders that simultaneously recover those global evaluations. Since countably many dense evaluations generate the Borel sigma-field of `C(T)`, the full continuous random field is determined by the restriction modulo null events. The stronger existence of a globally well-behaved decoder on every nonanalytic input is not used or claimed.

The identical construction applies to V. Periodicity makes the chosen lifts immaterial on true field samples; open sets that wrap around the torus cause no problem because the initial ball is local and the extension is global.

## 6. The separating event and the TV convention

Fix deterministic x0 in T and the two Borel decoders `g_U=g_(U,x0)` and `g_V=g_(V,x0)`. Let `X=F(x0)`. The preceding steps give `g_U(F|U)=X=g_V(F|V)` almost surely, and normalization gives `X~N(0,1)`.

The set `E={(s,t):g_U(s)=g_V(t)}` is measurable because the diagonal in R2 is closed and both decoders are Borel. Under the actual joint law, E has probability one. Under the product of the restriction marginals, the two decoded variables are independent, and each still has the same non-atomic N(0,1) law. Its diagonal probability is

`integral P(X=x) Law(X)(dx)=0`.

Therefore the supremum event difference is at least one; for probability laws it is at most one. The claimed value is exactly one, and these two laws are mutually singular. The usual absolutely-regular/beta coefficient for these random elements, under the stated half-variation convention, has the same value. There is no factor-of-two ambiguity left by this convention.

## 7. Exact analytic negative controls and boundaries

These controls are mathematical counterexamples or exact identities, **not numerical experiments**. Their derivations are also recorded in `NEGATIVE_CONTROLS.md`.

1. **Pair normalization must be respected.** Taking `c_n=sqrt(a_n)*(X+iY)` instead of `sqrt(a_n/2)*(X+iY)` doubles the covariance contribution of that conjugate pair. For a_n=1/8, the correct pair variance is 1/4 and the incorrect one is 1/2. This rejects a missing circular-pair factor without testing an infinite series numerically.
2. **Covariance information at sampled points is insufficient.** The analytic periodic field `G(x)=A*cos(2*pi*x1/L)+B*sin(2*pi*x1/L)` has independent values at x1=0 and L/4, with covariance exactly zero, but either nonempty open restriction reconstructs the whole field and hence a common non-atomic variable. Its full-restriction joint-versus-product TV is one. Zero pair covariance cannot be substituted for control of the observation sigma-fields.
3. **Finite observations of this BF field are not covered by TV=1.** At any finite set of pairwise distinct torus points, the BF covariance matrix is strictly positive definite because all spectral coefficients are positive. A zero quadratic form would annihilate every Fourier character; finite trigonometric polynomials separating those distinct points then force all coefficients in the linear combination to vanish. Thus for disjoint finite collections of distinct points, both the joint Gaussian law and the product marginal law have strictly positive densities on the same Euclidean space. Their densities have positive overlap, so their TV is strictly less than one. This directly rejects extending the continuum statement to all finite samples of the same field.
4. **A smaller observation sigma-field can lose maximal dependence.** For one shared standard Gaussian X, the pair `(sign X,sign X)` has joint masses 1/2 on the two equal-sign outcomes, while the product has mass 1/4 on all four. Its TV is exactly 1/2, although `(X,X)` versus independent copies has TV one. Data processing gives an upper bound, not preservation of equality. Accordingly the theorem does not decide the critical-point-process sigma-field.
5. **Noise changes the observation problem.** For `A=X+Z1`, `B=X+Z2` with independent standard Gaussians, the joint covariance is `[[2,1],[1,2]]`, determinant three, whereas the product covariance is diagonal. Both laws have positive densities, so TV is less than one. This finite noisy example refutes an automatic transfer of the exact-observation decoder. It does not assert a result for any unspecified continuum noise model.
6. **Smoothness alone is insufficient.** Independent Gaussian amplitudes multiplying smooth nonanalytic bump functions supported in disjoint sets give independent nontrivial open restrictions and TV zero. A bump may vanish with every derivative near U yet be nonzero elsewhere. The entire/analytic hypothesis is doing essential work.
7. **Truncating Fourier modes does not by itself fix exact open observations.** Any nonzero finite Gaussian trigonometric truncation is still entire and periodic. An open restriction recovers its global evaluations; choosing one with positive variance repeats the separating-event argument. The usual centered truncations retain at least the random constant mode, so a non-atomic shared variable exists. This says nothing about observing only finitely many values of that truncation.

The theorem does not establish maximal dependence, mixing, or failure of mixing for critical-point-process restrictions. It also does not supply a result for finite jets, specified noisy-field observations, random observation sets, or a relative-rate coupling. It does not refute gamma-LOC, any Chenavier–Otto hypothesis for a point process, or typed-Palm conclusions. Deliberately independent field replicas used in Math164 are unaffected.
