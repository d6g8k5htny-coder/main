## Derivative supremum: averaging and the entire-field envelope

The pin identities give four bounds for all points of D:

    |f_xxx| <= 12k+r Delta1,       Delta1=(5/2)A40+2A31,
    |f_xxy| <= 2|v|+r Delta2,      Delta2=(5/2)A31+2A22,
    |f_xyy| <= |om|+r Delta3,      Delta3=(5/2)A22+2A13,
    |f_yyy| <= |Y|+r Delta4,       Delta4=2A13+2A04,

with `Aac=sup_D |d_x^a d_y^c f|`, `a+c=4`.

For `h=r/2`, the preconditioned axial pin U3 equals the average of `f_xxx(t,0)` with nonnegative Peano kernel `3(h^2-t^2)/(4h^3)`, of total mass one, and U3=12k. For `q(t)=f_y(t,0)`, the pins give q(±h)=0; integration by parts gives `-2v=int_(−h)^h 2(h-t)q''(t)/r^2 dt`, another average of mass one. The omega quotient is the average of `f_xyy(t,0)` on the same interval, while Y is `f_yyy(0,0)`. Along segments inside convex D, the axial distance from a point of that averaging interval is at most `5r/2`, and the transverse distance at most `2r`; the Y case has both distances at most `2r`. This proves every coefficient in Delta. The controls verify the two nontrivial kernel identities exactly on monomials; the written integration identities apply to arbitrary C4 functions.

The field has an entire modification. For the torus, its Fourier coefficients decay as Gaussian functions of the mode index; the expected sum of absolute Gaussian coefficients times their complex-compact growth is finite. Tonelli therefore gives almost-sure uniform convergence on each complex compact, and the finite regression correction is entire. For the reference kernel, the feature series `exp(-z·z/2) sum_alpha xi_alpha z^alpha/sqrt(alpha!)` has the correct covariance and the same absolute-compact convergence property. Conditioning adds a finite entire regression function in either case. Consequently

    A_alpha <= |X_alpha| + T_alpha,
    T_alpha=sum_(beta!=0) |d^(alpha+beta)f(0)| (2r)^|beta|/beta!.

Let `s_gamma` be `sd0_bound`: its square bounds unconditioned derivative variance by the reference double-factorial product plus `(2gx)!(2gy)!M1`. Division by the physical Theta can only lower that upper bound because Theta>=1. Gaussian regression decreases variance and bounds the magnitude of the conditional mean by `s_gamma sqrt(E)`, by Cauchy–Schwarz in the pin inverse-covariance inner product. Therefore Minkowski gives

    ||T_alpha||_p <= S_alpha(r1)(sqrt(E_hi)+||N(0,1)||_p).

`S_alpha` explicitly sums all multi-indices of orders 1 through 24. For its omitted tail, the elementary central-binomial bound yields

    s_gamma <= 2^|gamma| gamma! sqrt(1+M1)
            <= 2^(|gamma|+1) gamma!,

using the retained `M1(10)<1`. With `|alpha|=4`, `(alpha+beta)!/beta! <= (n+4)^4 <= (n+4)^8` at `|beta|=n`, and there are n+1 such two-dimensional multi-indices. The code deliberately uses the larger eighth power. For n>=25, the consecutive ratio of `(n+1)(n+4)^8 q^n` is at most `2q`; this follows from the decreasing rational ratio and its value at n=25. Here `q=4r1<=1/16`. Its geometric sum is exactly bounded by

    2^(|alpha|+1) (NB+2)(NB+5)^8 (4r1)^(NB+1)/(1-8r1), NB=24.

This proves `taylor_tail_coefficient` and its endpoint use, including convergence and finite Lp moments. It is not a replacement of the spatial supremum by a midpoint or grid maximum.

Regress every jet and target on w: `J=J_g+beta_J(w-mu_w)`. The whole countable residual Gaussian jet family is independent of w, has the same means and smaller variances. Its regression coefficient obeys `|beta_J|<=s_gamma/sigma_w`. Thus

This is code `B(n)`. `nA` bounds `||A_alpha^g||_p` by the residual order-four Gaussian norm plus its Taylor-tail norm; `nDelta` applies the averaging coefficients. No independence between the residual derivatives is assumed for these Minkowski bounds.

## Quadratic near/far cover

On typed support, `|w-mu_w|<=lambda+|mu_w|`. The preceding bounds give

    M3 <= max_i (y_i'+beta_bar_i lambda),

where `y'` is residual-field measurable and nonnegative. The four `beta_bar_i` are exactly `bbars`: the residual-regression coefficients of `2v,om,Y`, plus r times the corresponding B-alpha combination. Replacing r by r1 and taking interval absolute upper bounds only increases them. Similarly the `|mu_w|` used in y' is bounded by the box maximum.

Put `a=4/(3k)` and `x_i=a r beta_bar_i y_i'`. If cap failure `lambda<=a r M3^2` occurs, then one component satisfies `lambda<=a r(y_i'+beta_bar_i lambda)^2`. For x<=1/4 its roots obey

    lambda_minus <= a r y'^2/(1-3x),
    lambda_plus  >= 1/(4 a r beta_bar^2).

For beta_bar=0 only the small branch exists. These inequalities follow from the quadratic formula and `sqrt(1-4x)>=1-4x`; a direct alternative checks the quadratic signs at the proposed intermediate endpoints.

With actual x0=1/64, let `kappa=3/(1-3x0)` and `Fk=1+kappa x0`. On `0<=x<=x0`, `1/(1-3x)<=1+kappa x`. Hence typed cap failure is covered by four events:

- N: a small branch, `0<lambda<=rU`, with all x_i<=x0, and `U=max_i a y_i'^2(1+kappa x_i)`.
- F: `lambda>=1/(4a r beta_bar^2)`, with beta_bar the maximum of the four coefficients.
- X: some x_i>x0.
- A: `rM4>3k/10`.

The entire near-window endpoint U and the x_i restrictions depend only on the residual field, independent of lambda. If a beta_bar is zero, its X event is empty and it creates no far branch; the code's zero-coefficient branches implement this correctly.

For the main-term estimates it is convenient to set U to zero outside the residual event `{all x_i<=x0}`. Every near-event integrand is unaffected, and all ensuing moments admit unconditional nonnegative majorants. This makes precise the implementation comments “on the event” and avoids treating a restricted bound as a global one.

The retained incomplete-moment enclosures carry the subtraction in an interval before selecting the upper end. Correlation lost there only enlarges it. On typed support, `P1<=|c1|lambda` and

    P2<=|c2|(lambda+r|om|)+r e.

    E[W 1_N] <= r^5 pbar E[|c1 c2|(U^3/3+bar-om U^2/2)+|c1|bar-e U^2/2].

The density also satisfies `p(lambda)<=p0+lambda Lp`, giving the extra r times the same terms with powers U^4/4 and U^3/3. The endpoint enclosures are valid:

    pbar <=code phi(max(0,mu_w.lo)/sw_hi)/sw_lo,
    p(0) <=code phi(m0/sw_hi)/sw_lo,

Here m0 is the smallest |mu_w| permitted by its interval, or zero if it straddles zero. Density monotonicity in its nonnegative argument explains the upper standard-deviation endpoint inside phi and the lower endpoint in the denominator. The rational `0.24198` bound is also checked using the retained phi enclosure.

## Normalizer floor and its direction

Let `G0={r|T|<=3k/2,r^2|tau|<=3k/2}` and `D0=(2r|T|+r^2|tau|)/(6k)`. On G0,

    c1>=9k/2,  3k<=c2<=9k,  D0<=3/4,
    c1 c2>=36k^2(1-D0).

Writing lambda-plus for max(lambda,0), the typed product obeys the pointwise lower bound

    W/r^2 >= 1_G0 {36k^2[lambda-plus^2-r om-plus lambda-plus-D0 lambda-plus^2]
                     -r c2 v^2 lambda-plus}.

If lambda<=r om-plus the displayed right side is nonpositive. Otherwise, use `c2(c1 lambda-rv^2)_+(lambda-r om-plus)_+` below the typed product, expand, and apply the positive coefficient floor; the dropped cross correction is nonnegative. This proves the sign of every subtraction. In particular the lower bound does not replace the typed indicator by the full positive-lambda cone without a correction.

After expectation and division by `36k^2`, Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the removed `lambda^2 1_G0c` by `||lambda||4^2 sqrt(q_bad)`. Hölder bounds the D0 term. Since c2<=9k, the v term could use `1/(4k)`; the code uses the larger `1/(3k)` as safe slack. The regression bounds are

    E[|om|lambda-plus] <= E|om_g| E lambda-plus
                +|bw_om|[|mu_w| ||lambda||1+||lambda||2^2],

    E[v^2 lambda-plus] <= 2 E[v_g^2] E lambda-plus
                +2 |bw_v|^2 (sw_hi ||Z||4)^2 ||lambda||2.

Independence is used only for the residual factor and lambda in the first terms; the latter terms use Cauchy–Schwarz. `Eom_g`, `Evg2`, `Elp` and `lam_n` are the corresponding upper bounds. `El2=elam2_pos(-mu_w.hi,sw_lo).lo` is a lower bound because the positive-part second moment increases in mean and standard deviation. `q_bad` is a Gaussian-tail union bound for G0c.

It follows that `zt=El2-corr` satisfies `Z_r>=36k^2 r^2 zt` over the whole box/band. The guard `zt>0` is essential. Dividing either near numerator bound by this positive floor yields the two candidates for `main`; taking their minimum is valid. No positivity of the signed pointwise expression itself is required.

## Rare events and final band

For q in `{2,4,8,16}`, the typed-product upper bound and Hölder give `||W||q<=r^2 wnorm(q)`. Each coefficient/product factor is bounded in L(4q), so multiplying the two L(2q) polynomial bounds gives exactly code `g1*g2`. These use full-Q Gaussian norms, unlike the residual norms of the near branch.

The three rare probabilities are bounded independently:

- A: `M4<=max_alpha(|X_alpha|+T_alpha)`. At `t=3klo/(10r1)`, split at 7t/8 and t/8. The five Gaussian tails plus order-32 Markov bounds are `qA2`.
- F: the quadratic cover threshold is at least `1/(4 a_hi r1 bbar^2)`. A one-sided Gaussian upper bound with `|mu_w|` and `sw_hi` gives `qfar`.
- X: a component requires `y_i'>tx=x0/(a_hi r1 bbars_i)`. For i=1, its deterministic baseline is at most `12khi+bbars1 |mu_w|`; if the threshold does not exceed that baseline, probability one is safe, otherwise Markov bounds the residual r Delta term. For i>=2, split the positive sum at tx/2 into a half-normal Gaussian tail and a delta Markov tail. This is `qx`.

Cap each event bound at one. Then Holder gives

    E[W 1_(A union F union X)] <= r^2 min_q wnorm(q) sum_E P_bound(E)^(1-1/q).

`pow_tail` encloses each fractional power by raising an upward-rounded nonnegative input to q−1 and taking an outward q-th root. Its contract only needs the actual nonnegative inputs and power-of-two q. Minima across q remain bounds. On a positive-lower-end band, divide by `36klo^2 zt r0^3`; monotonic endpoint use ensures this bounds the probability divided by actual r^3.

For the final band `[0,R]`, keep every mean/variance, regression, S, moment and weight bound frozen at its whole-band value. Reconsider the threshold inequalities at r=R/2^j:

- Every Gaussian threshold argument grows by at least 2 per halving. A threshold of the form `(c/r-m)/s` does so because m>=0; the X Gaussian thresholds are exactly proportional to 1/r. The far threshold is at least proportional to 1/r with frozen bbar.
- If x>=3, shifting the Gaussian-tail integral gives `Phibar(2x)<=exp(-3x^2/2)Phibar(x)<Phibar(x)/64`. The last strict inequality already follows from `1+27/2+(27/2)^2/2>64`. This proof uses the true Gaussian tail before enclosing it, so no scaling identity for the piecewise numerical tail wrapper or its rounding is assumed.
- Every Markov piece drops at least as `2^-32` per halving. For X's first component, `tx-cb` at least doubles once positive; using the frozen R Delta norm is already enough, and using r Delta only improves the decay.

The code requires all initial Gaussian arguments >=3 and each uncapped sum `qA2,qfar,qx<1`. These conditions rule out a cap-at-one obstruction or a unit-probability first-component X branch. Therefore each probability sum drops by at least 64 per halving. After exponent `1-1/q`, q>=2, it drops by at least 8. Choose the q attaining the minimum at R and keep it fixed. For the dyadic interval `[R/2^(j+1),R/2^j]`, its radius-cube divisor increases the bound by 8^j, cancelled by that probability decrease. The uniform bound is thus the initial top-subband estimate using divisor `(R/2)^3`, precisely `rdiv=R/2`. The near term already used only whole-band constants and needs no radius-cube division.

This proves the final-band extension to every positive r, including arbitrarily small r, without building infinitely many laws or asserting that numerical tail bounds themselves scale exactly.
