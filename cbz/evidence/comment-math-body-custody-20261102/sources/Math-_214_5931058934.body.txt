### 1. Interior density: the scaling really yields N(N+5)/2

Apply Taylor to g = f′ through degree N+2. Use its evaluations at −τ, τ and τξ_i, and the integral of g over [−τ,τ]. Divide the integral row by τ. The resulting vector has the form

    B diag(1,τ,…,τ^(N+2)) b + O_L²(τ^(N+3)),

where b consists of a₁,…,a_(N+3), with factorial factors absorbed, and B is the fixed evaluation/integration matrix.

If B annihilates a polynomial P of degree ≤ N+2, the evaluation rows force

    P(x) = c(x²−1) ∏_i(x−ξ_i).

The stated nonzero integral forces c = 0, proving invertibility. After multiplying by B⁻¹ and the inverse diagonal, the remainder is O_L²(τ), so the rescaled covariance converges to a positive-definite jet covariance under (H). Restoring the integral-row factor gives the full covariance determinant of order τ^((N+3)(N+2)+2). The pin determinant has order τ⁸. Their quotient is the conditional determinant of order τ^(N(N+5)), hence the claimed density bound.

For N = 2, ξ₁ = −1/2 and ξ₂ = 1/2 are admissible: the integral of (x²−1)(x²−1/4) on [−1,1] is exactly 1/15. The density bound is therefore Cτ⁻⁷.

### 2. Thin band integral, with every cutoff factor retained

Lemma 4.1 follows by subtracting the band midpoint from the degree-(k+1) Taylor polynomial, applying the scaled Markov inequality, and estimating the differentiated Taylor remainder. No probabilistic independence is used.

Fix δ ∈ (0,1/10], t_* = (12h)^(1/(5−δ)), t₁ = h^(1/10), and take a sufficiently small fixed analytic cutoff t₀. Changing between this and a larger fixed cutoff contributes only a fixed-separation far estimate already reviewed.

On t ≥ t_*, α = 12h/t³ is bounded. Gaussian regression therefore gives uniform sub-Gaussian tails for K = sup|f^(10)| and for sup|f″| under Q. Truncate both at R = C√log(1/h), with exceptional weighted contribution O(h^M) for any chosen M after increasing C. The conditional vector (a₄,…,a₉) has uniformly nondegenerate covariance as t → 0; every subvector consequently has a uniformly bounded density.

For t ≤ t₁, Lemma 4.1 gives

    |H_±| ≤ C hR t⁻²,
    |a_j| ≤ C hR t^(−j),  j = 4,…,9.

Projecting to whichever coordinates have small intervals gives the rectangle probability bound. Since min(1,Rx) ≤ R min(1,x), the good-event kernel is at most

    C R⁸ h² t⁻⁸ ∏_[j=4,…,9] min(1,h t^(−j)).

This handles correlation between K and the jets by deterministic truncation; their probabilities are never multiplied as though independent.

On [t_*,h^(1/5)], only j = 4 is active, giving integral at most

    C R⁸ h³ t_*⁻¹¹ = C R⁸ h^(3−11/(5−δ)).

The exponent is at least 37/49 > 3/4. On [h^(1/m),h^(1/(m+1))], m = 5,…,9, the integral exponent is

    (m−3)/2 − 1/m = 4/5, 4/3, 13/7, 19/8, 26/9,

respectively. All logarithmic factors are absorbed with room to spare.

For t ≥ t₁, h ≤ t¹⁰ and the Hessian product is at most Ct¹⁶R². The two chosen interior derivative values are at most C√(hR) by finite-interval interpolation; the optimizing increment fits inside the band since √(h/R)/t → 0 uniformly for t ≥ h^(1/10). The interior density bound gives probability at most ChR t⁻⁷. Including the pin factor t⁻⁴, the kernel is at most

    C hR³ t⁵,

whose integral is O(h log(1/h)^(3/2)) = o(h^(3/4)).

Thus the claimed thin integral is O(h^(3/4)). **Expository clarification:** the literal √log cutoffs stated in the candidate naturally give O(h log(1/h)^(3/2)) in this last step, rather than the printed O(h log(1/h)); the weaker explicit bound above is fully sufficient for Lemma 4.3 and the theorem's target remainder. ### 3. Super-polynomial exceptional events

The Rolle input of Lemma 3.1 is valid: four zeros of f′ give two zeros of f‴ and a zero of f⁗; integrating the f^(5) bound twice yields the displayed O(t²K₅) estimate for α. For a typed pair, non-adjacency supplies the two extra zeros. The conditioned field is Morse off the pins by the same finite-point conditional nondegeneracy argument; Hessian degeneracy at the pins has zero conditional probability.

On t ≤ t_*, α/t² ≥ t^(−δ). A relevant derivative supremum has conditional mean at most C(1+α) and a uniform centered Gaussian tail. Its threshold cα/t² eventually dominates that mean. Therefore

    P_Q(K₅ ≥ cα/t²) ≤ C exp(−cα²/t⁴).

The same estimate holds for K^o when its large-width threshold is cα/t². Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and the direct near-pair moment bound from the far review control the Hessian weight. Since

    α²/t⁴ ≥ c h^(−2δ/(5−δ))  on t ≤ t_*,

these integrated exceptional terms are O(h^N) for every fixed N. This establishes the near adjacency correction without an unjustified unconditional-tail substitution.

### 4. Critical classification error: keep the odd part untruncated

Here I consume Corollary 3.4's deterministic implication and verify its probabilistic integration.

Write s = m/√v₁, so s is comparable to α/t. Its uncertain window has component widths, up to fixed constants,

    w_o = t²K^o/α,    w_e = t³K^e/α,    w₀ = t².

The parity split is exact under Q: E₁ is even and K^o is an odd-process functional. Hence they are independent. The even process is conditioned on U₂ = 0 and therefore has zero conditional mean, independent of α; K^e has uniform sub-Gaussian tails. Also E_Q K^o ≤ C(1+α).

Split a window of width w_o+w_e+w₀ into the union of three windows with tripled widths. Widths larger than a sufficiently small fixed constant are exceptional. For the odd width, the preceding Gaussian estimate handles that exception; on the remaining range the standard-normal interval lies a fixed positive fraction of s away from zero. Conditioning on K^o gives

    P(odd window) ≤ C (t²/α) E_Q K^o · s exp(−cs²)
                  ≤ C t(1+α) exp(−cs²),

plus a super-polynomial exceptional contribution. The deterministic t² width gives Ct²s exp(−cs²), absorbed by the same bound.

For the correlated even term, truncate only K^e at R = C√log(1/h). The exceptional probability is O(h^M) uniformly. On the good event,

    w_e ≤ C t^(1+δ)R → 0

uniformly for t ≤ t_*, and enlarging to a deterministic interval gives

    P(even window) ≤ C (t³R/α) s exp(−cs²)
                  ≤ C t²R exp(−cs²),

plus O(h^M). No independence between E₁ and K^e is assumed.

Consequently the classification event has probability bounded by

    C[t(1+α)+t²R] exp(−cα²/t²) + O(h^M)
      + C exp(−cα²/t⁴).

### 5. Integrate the Hessian weight without a correlation gap

For the typed product W, use

    W ≤ 2m² + 2E₂².

Discard the event from the E₂² term entirely. Its contribution is already small:

    ∫₀^t_* (12/t⁴)p_t(α) E_Q E₂² dt
      ≤ C∫₀^t_* t² dt
      = O(h^(3/(5−δ))) = o(h^(1/2)).

This removes any need to claim independence between E₂ and the odd remainder. For the m² term, the pin-density bound and m ≍ tα yield integrands bounded by

    C exp(−c₀α²)(1+α) α²/t · exp(−cα²/t²)
    C R exp(−c₀α²) α² · exp(−cα²/t²).

The first integrates to O(h^(1/2)), the second to O(h^(3/4)√log(1/h)): substitute α = 12h/t³ and t = h^(1/4)r. Equivalently, these reduce to h²∫t⁻⁷exp(−ch²/t⁸)dt and h²R∫t⁻⁶exp(−ch²/t⁸)dt. Both improper integrals are finite at both ends. The O(h^M) event contributes O(h^(M−1/3)); the large-width exception was handled above. The resulting complete classification error is O(h^(1/2)).
