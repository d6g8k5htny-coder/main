### Exact reduction, with the hypotheses exposed

Use Y=X² and let ν be its law. For a nonnegative weight w define the two-by-two Gram matrix

    Q_w(ν) = ∫ w(y) [[1,y],[y,y²]] dν(y) = [[α,β],[β,γ]].

Assuming its entries are finite and α>0, elementary expansion gives

    Φ(Q_w) := γ−β²/α = inf_c ∫ w(y)(y−c)² dν(y).

This is the scalar instance of the cited variational Schur formula. The following comparison is derived here explicitly to avoid attributing an uninspected theorem to an earlier paper. If ν≥ν₀ as nonnegative measures, then for EVERY c the full residual integral dominates the retained one; taking infima preserves that inequality. Equivalently Q_w(ν)−Q_w(ν₀) is positive semidefinite. **Do not renormalize ν₀ to probability**, which would change the coefficient.

Let ν₀=pδ_u+qδ_v with p,q>0, u,v>0, u≠v. For the project, ν is a probability law with finite third Y-moment, and ν≥ν₀ follows from the squared-radius mass bounds. Individual atoms X=a,b imply the special case u=a²,v=b², but the converse with the same single-atom masses is not assumed.

| Choice | Full-law residual | Retained two-mass residual |
|---|---|---|
| w(y)=1 | E[Y²]−E[Y]² = g | `pq(u−v)²/(p+q)` |
| w(y)=y | E[Y³]−E[Y²]²/E[Y] = Δ | `(pu)(qv)(u−v)²/(pu+qv)` |

Also E[Y]≥pu+qv by retaining nonnegative contributions. These are exactly the three common floors after the existing notation crosswalk. The Δ row is weighted least squares, or projection of X³ onto the span of X in L²; it is not a claim that the scalar matrix has already been identified with a Gaussian/Palm conditional covariance.

**Normalization and strictness survive the reduction.** For a finite measure of mass M>0, the first Schur residual is `∫Y²−(∫Y)²/M`, not the probability-only expression. The second row needs E[Y]>0 and suitable integrability, not mass one. A zero radius removes a weight from the Δ row, and coincident squared radii remove its separation. Positive pointwise floors need not stay uniformly positive as masses/radii vary. The notes' different gap-optimal and Δ-optimal remainder placements give separate sharpness, not a simultaneous extremizer claim.

### What the upper-moment-free directional argument adds

In #328 §5 let x=s², with s=E[X²]>0 and g>0. For direction θ∈[0,1/4], the source gives

    D0=x(g+x), D1=g(g+4x)/4,
    D(θ)=(1−4θ)D0+4θD1, N=xg(g+2x), S(θ)=N/D(θ).

Because N is positive and independent of θ while D is positive affine, `S(θ)≥min(N/D0,N/D1)`. The source's explicit simplifications yield `N/D0≥g` and `N/D1≥2x`, hence `S(θ)≥min(g0,2s0²)`. This explains why no upper moment input is needed: it does NOT substitute independent lower bounds into an uncontrolled quotient denominator.
