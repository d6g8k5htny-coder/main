## GW2.1 Gaussian interpolation with target dependence

Let mu_t=N(m_t,Sigma_t), 0<=t<=1, with cI<=Sigma_t<=CI, |m_t|<=CP and |dot m_t|+||dot Sigma_t||<=C epsilon P. Here P>=1 is the deterministic target envelope (application: 1+|b|+k). Differentiating the exact density gives

|partial_t p_t(y)| <= C epsilon P [1+|y-m_t|^2] p_t(y).                 (GW2.1)

No centered exp(-c|y|^2) envelope uniform in b is asserted.

Write y=(X,z). For any measurable interval I(X) of length <=C0 kappa, with arbitrary center, and any test function satisfying

|h_kappa(X,z)| <= M_kappa (1+|X|)^M 1{z in I(X)},

one has

|E_mu1 h_kappa - E_mu0 h_kappa| <= C epsilon kappa M_kappa P^N.       (GW2.2)

Proof: integrate GW2.1 in t. Uniform nondegeneracy plus completing the square gives a bound on sup_z of polynomial times p_t(X,z) by C P^N times a polynomial-Gaussian envelope in X-m_{t,X}. This is independent of the center of I(X). The z integral costs only its length O(kappa), and the X moment is finite.

The support-only counterexample from review5960011145 is explicitly excluded: width kappa does not imply M_kappa=kappa^2.

## GW2.2 Actual #240 N3 surrogate

#240 has
G_r=(w0+12kP1) 1{A<0, |z|<24kappa+36rQ1, r|Q1|<=c_e kappa}.

Its conditional f4-window has length O(kappa). Also |w0|<=36kappa^2 Delta^2 and, on the cutoff,

Thus M_kappa=C kappa^2(1+||A||)^N.

|E_Q G_r - E_{v0(b,k)} G_r| <= C r^2 kappa^3 P^N.                    (GW2.3)

This sharpens N3 for this explicitly localized surrogate only.

## GW2.3 N6 pin factor

The exact source bound is |pi_r-pi_0|<=Cr^2P^2 exp[-c(b^2+k^2)]. The rejected contact-window expectation is O(kappa^3 P^N) (amplitude kappa^2 times width kappa), hence

N6 rejected contribution <= C r^2 kappa^3 P^(N+2) exp[-c(b^2+k^2)].  (GW2.4)

More generally, if a separately proved finite-r localized mark has amplitude <=C(kappa^2+r^2)(1+|X|)^M and window length O(kappa), GW2.2 gives Cr^2 kappa(kappa^2+r^2)P^N <= Cr^2 kappa P^N. Thus the bridge needs localization plus a controlled amplitude; it does not need a universal kappa^2 amplitude.
