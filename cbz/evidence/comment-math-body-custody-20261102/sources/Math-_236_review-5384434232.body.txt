## 2. Whole-field coupling and contact jet — ACCEPT

References: NOTE lines 113–149; P §§2–3; R §2, especially (R2)–(R3).

There are `4 + 2(d-1) = 2d+2` rows. For `a=-r/2`, `c=r/2`, the first four rows
act on `(f(a), f_x(a), f(c), f_x(c))`, and each transverse block acts on two
first derivatives. The axial determinant has absolute value `12/r^4`; each
transverse block has determinant `1/r`. Thus the product is
`12 r^(-(d+3))`. Direct substitution of the pinned heights and zero gradients
gives precisely `(b-kr^3/2, -kr^2, 0, 12k, 0, ..., 0)`.

The apparent cancellation in the fourth row is controlled deterministically.
For a smooth scalar restriction to the axis, two integrations by parts give

```
U3(f) = (6/r^3) integral_a^c (t-a)(c-t) f_xxx(t,0) dt.
```

The weight is nonnegative and has integral one after multiplication by
`6/r^3`, because `integral_a^c (t-a)(c-t) dt = r^3/6`.
Consequently this row converges to `f_xxx(0)` for every smooth realization,
and is bounded by the axial third-derivative supremum. All other rows are
averages or first difference quotients with the stated derivative limits.
In particular, for a fixed frame and a small embedded radius band,
`|U_r(f)| <= C_d ||f||_(C^3)` uniformly in `r`.

P §2 supplies a smooth Gaussian version with rapidly summable positive Fourier
weights. The same average representations applied to the smooth covariance
kernel show that `C_r -> C_0` in global `C^4`, and `Sigma_r -> Sigma_0`.
The contact rows are independent differential functionals, so the retained
positive-spectrum argument gives `Sigma_0 > 0`. Hence inverse matrices are
bounded for all sufficiently small `r` and converge to `Sigma_0^-1`.

The common-sample construction

```
F_r = F + C_r Sigma_r^-1 (v_r - U_r(F))
```

has the correct conditional Gaussian mean and residual covariance. Moreover,
`U_r(C_r) = Sigma_r`, so it satisfies all the transformed observations exactly;

## 4. Conditional remote genericity — ACCEPT

References: NOTE lines 241–278; retained P §2 and the mesh argument in §8.

For each remote observation vector `Z`, a singular conditional covariance
would mean that a nonzero scalar combination of its random entries lies in
the linear span of `U_0` almost surely. Equivalently, after adding an origin
contact distribution, the resulting distribution has zero Gaussian variance.
Positive Fourier spectrum makes it the zero distribution. Since the remote
supports are separated from the origin, the distributions at those remote
supports must vanish separately.

For `Z_M(x,v)`, the order-two symbol of a combination of `H(x)v` is
`(beta . xi)(v . xi)`. Since `v != 0`, the integral-domain property of the
polynomial ring forces `beta=0`. The remaining first-order gradient symbol
then forces its coefficients to vanish. An independent explicit version of
the same fact is that, for any nonzero `v` and any `w`, the symmetric matrix

```
H = (w v^T + v w^T)/|v|^2 - (w.v) v v^T/|v|^4
```

satisfies `H v = w`. Thus the gradient plus Hessian-vector entries contribute
`2d` independent residual functionals.

For `Z_V(x,z)`, the value-difference coefficient appears as opposite
zeroth-order coefficients at two distinct supports. The zeroth- and
first-order coefficients must vanish at each support separately. This yields
`2d+1` independent residual functionals. For `Z_b(x)`, the same one-support
argument yields `d+1`; the fixed `b` shifts only the mean. Conditioning on
the contact cubic derivative and all other contact rows cannot create a
remote deterministic relation because their support remains at the origin.

For zero exclusion, choose a compact parameter chart with positive distance
from the forbidden supports (and, for `Z_V`, from the diagonal). Conditional
covariance depends continuously on the parameters and is positive definite
pointwise. Its determinant therefore has a positive lower bound on that
compact chart. The Gaussian density is bounded by
`(2 pi)^(-p/2) det(Sigma_cond)^(-1/2)`, independently of its mean.

On a realization with chart `C^1` norm at most `N`, a zero forces some point of
an epsilon net of cardinality at most `C epsilon^(-q)` to have norm at most
`C N epsilon`. The union bound and density bound give
`C' N^p epsilon^(p-q)`. The parameter/range dimensions are exactly

| Map | Parameter dimension q | Range dimension p | p-q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `Z_M` | `d+(d-1)=2d-1` | `2d` | 1 |
| `Z_V` | `2d` | `2d+1` | 1 |
| `Z_b` | `d` | `d+1` | 1 |

Fix `N`, let epsilon tend to zero, then take the countable union over `N`.
The random `C^1` norm is finite by smoothness. Compact chart subdomains can
be chosen inside slightly larger charts to avoid boundary issues. Countably
many such charts exhaust the punctured torus and the distinct-point domain;
finitely many suffice in the sphere direction. Thus (N10) follows.
