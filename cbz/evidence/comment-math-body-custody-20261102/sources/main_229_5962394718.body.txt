## 2. A direct two-coordinate Gaussian envelope

**Theorem CE.** Under G1, uniformly in r,kappa in the above range,

    0<=E hhat_(r,kappa)
       <=C kappa^3 min(1,kappa/r) (1+|b|+|k|)^N.      (2)

The same holds throughout covariance/mean interpolation between the G3 laws
and the contact laws, with their uniform nondegeneracy and moment bounds.
Neither G2 parity nor independence of W and f5 is needed for (2).

*Proof.* Condition on Y. The residual pair (W,f5) is a genuine two-dimensional
Gaussian. Its covariance, each scalar conditional variance, and each scalar
marginal variance are bounded above/below uniformly: the Schur complement of
a covariance >=cI is >=cI, since its quadratic form minimizes the joint
quadratic form over the conditioned coordinates. In particular the conditional
joint density and conditional scalar densities have bounded suprema independent
of their means. No bound on h(Y) or q(Y) is needed.

On the clip, 23<=a<=25, 71<b<73, and the weight is at most37kappa^2 Delta^2.
For every f5 the allowed W section has length at most146kappa. Integrating
first in W using its conditional density given Y,f5 and then integrating the
entire f5 marginal proves C kappa^3 Delta^2.

The clip also restricts f5 to an interval of length

    120 * 2tau/(36lambda)=20tau/(3lambda).             (3)

The two-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the allowed set is therefore at most
146kappa*20tau/(3lambda), even though its W section depends on f5. Use the
joint density supremum to obtain C kappa^3 Delta^2/lambda. Take the minimum
and integrate Delta^2 over Y. It is an ordinary polynomial Gaussian moment.
This proves (2), with no unweighted inverse-Hessian moment. QED.

**Scored version.** If two such genuine Gaussian laws have mean difference
<=epsilon P and covariance difference<=epsilon, P=1+|b|+|k|, then

    |E_1 hhat-E_0 hhat|
      <=C epsilon kappa^3 min(1,1/lambda) P^N.         (4)

Indeed, interpolate their means and covariances. The derivative of the joint
density is bounded by C epsilon P(1+|x-m_t|^2)p_t(x). Condition on Y. Gaussian
completion of squares bounds the residual-coordinate polynomial times density
by a polynomial in P+|Y| times the marginal Y density. For the 1/lambda bound
take the supremum over both residual coordinates and use the area in (3).
For the bound without 1/lambda take the W supremum, then integrate the f5
Gaussian moments. Finally integrate the polynomial in Y. This is a density
derivative argument on (Y,W,f5), not on the nonlinear variables (Y,z,Q).

## 4. The weighted Q density and a uniform crossover approximation

Under the fixed contact zero-gap G1–G2 law, conditioning on Y preserves
independence of W and f5. This follows from the block-diagonal even/odd
Gaussian covariance: Y conditions separately on coordinates in the two blocks.
It would not follow from G1 alone.

Let p_W(.|Y) and p_Q(.|Y) denote the corresponding conditional scalar
densities. The second is Gaussian, with variance
Var(f5|Y)/120^2 bounded away from zero. Its conditional mean may contain the
singular function h(Y); the uniform density derivative bounds are independent
of that mean. Define a finite weighted density on the real line by

    g(t)=E_Y[1{A<0} Delta^2 p_W(3q|Y) p_Q(t|Y)].       (9)

**Lemma.** g is positive everywhere, even, smooth with each fixed derivative
bounded by C_j(1+|b|)^N, and integrable. Moreover

    int g(t)dt=E[1{A<0}Delta^2 p_W(3q|Y)]<infinity.   (10)

*Proof.* All conditional Gaussian density derivatives have uniform bounded
suprema. They are dominated by C_j Delta^2 after the factor p_W is included,
so differentiation under expectation is justified by ordinary Gaussian moments.
Tonelli gives (10). Positive definiteness gives positive probability for A<0,
and both conditional densities are positive there; Delta^2>0. Under simultaneous
odd sign reversal, q and p_W(3q|Y) are unchanged while Q changes sign. In (9)
this makes g even. QED.

For lambda>=0 define

    H_tau(lambda)=int_R g(t) F(36lambda t)
                              1{|36lambda t|<=tau}dt,
    H_tau(0)=1792 int_R g(t)dt.                        (11)

**Theorem CX.** Uniformly for all lambda=r/kappa>0 in the permitted range,

    E_(0,0) hhat_(r,kappa)
      =kappa^3 H_tau(lambda)
          +O(kappa^5 min(1,1/lambda)(1+|b|)^N).       (12)

Consequently along every sequence kappa->0 and r/kappa->lambda_*<infinity,
the kappa^-3 normalized mass converges to H_tau(lambda_*).

*Proof.* Conditional on Y,f5, put z=kappa v in (1). The resulting integral,
after division by kappa^3 Delta^2, is

    (1/144) int_(a(u)<|v|<b(u))
             (b(u)^2-v^2)p_W(3q+kappa v|Y)dv.         (13)

Its domain and weight are symmetric in v. Taylor's theorem for p_W cancels
the first-order term exactly; the remainder is <=C kappa^2 because |v|<=73,
the weight integral is uniformly bounded and the second derivative of a
nondegenerate Gaussian density has uniformly bounded supremum. Thus (13)
equals F(u)p_W(3q|Y)+O(kappa^2), uniformly on the clip and in its arbitrary center.
Integrate over f5. Its conditional clip probability is bounded by
min(1,C_tau/lambda). Integrate Delta^2 over Y to prove (12).

For positive finite lambda_*, dominated convergence in (11) applies off the
two clip boundaries, which have g-measure zero. At lambda_*=0 use the uniform
bound on F and integrability (10). This proves continuity and the claimed
sequential limit. No joint Gaussian assertion about (q,Q,z) was used. QED.

## 5. Strict deficit and the large-shift coefficient

**Theorem CL.** H_tau is positive, continuous and strictly decreasing on
[0,infinity). For every lambda>0, H_tau(lambda)<H_tau(0). As lambda->infinity,

    H_tau(lambda)=C_tau(b)/lambda+O(lambda^-3(1+|b|)^N),
    C_tau(b)=I_tau g(0)/36>0.                         (14)

Equivalently C_tau=(10/3)I_tau E[1{A<0}Delta^2
p_(W,f5|Y)(3q,-120h(Y))]. The factor120 is the density Jacobian in Q=f5/120+h;
the joint density factorizes here only because G2 survives conditioning on Y.

Therefore, in every joint limit kappa->0 and r/kappa->infinity,

    (r/kappa) kappa^-3 E_(0,0)hhat_(r,kappa)
                                  -> C_tau(b).       (15)

*Proof.* Evenness of g lets us replace F on the clip by Fbar(36lambda|t|).
Equation(7) makes this test nonincreasing as lambda grows, with strict decrease
on any positive-length t interval on which both arguments are inside the clip.
Such an interval exists for any two finite distinct lambdas, and g is strictly
positive there. Positivity and strict monotonicity follow, including comparison
with lambda=0. This is a limiting contact-profile assertion; it does not assert
monotonicity or a sign for every finite-kappa or actual-field correction.

For lambda>0 change variables u=36lambda t in (11):

    H_tau(lambda)=(1/(36lambda))
                     int_(-tau)^tau F(u)g(u/(36lambda))du. (16)

Since g'(0)=0 and g'' is bounded, Taylor's theorem gives (14). Equation(12)
then gives (15), since its normalized error after multiplying by lambda is
O(kappa^2). These limits impose no order between kappa->0 and lambda->infinity
beyond r<=1. This is a fixed contact-law limit. Identifying a varying finite-r
law with that coefficient additionally requires r->0 and suitable transport;
the separate integrated argument below supplies it in its stated regime. QED.

This also quantifies why relative clipping loss cannot be dismissed: the
retained fraction H_tau(lambda)/H_tau(0) tends to zero. The corresponding
reference-profile loss tends to100 percent as lambda grows. It is a statement
about this artificial clip relative to h0, not a probability estimate for the
actual field's rejected event.

As a consistency check, CS Lemma2 gives int t^2 g(t)dt=M<infinity. The global
clipping Taylor remainder bound from CS and (6), together with evenness, imply

    H_tau(lambda)=H_tau(0)-288 M lambda^2+o(lambda^2)
                                             (lambda->0). (17)

This follows by dominated convergence with majorant C t^2g(t); on every fixed
t the clip is eventually inactive, and (36^2/2)F''(0)=-288. It agrees with QC.
Equation(12) alone does not replace QC's stronger joint small-r/kappa statement
after subtraction and division by r^2 kappa, since its separate kappa^5 error
need not be negligible under that normalization.

## 6. A sharp radial tail for the clipped surrogate

Fix 0<r0<=1 and 0<ell<r0^5. The sector kappa=ell/r^4<r is exactly
r>ell^(1/5). From (2), for any of the uniform G1 laws with |k|<=1,

    int_(ell^(1/5))^r0 E hhat_(r,ell/r^4)dr
       <=C(1+|b|)^N ell^4 int_(ell^(1/5))^r0 r^-17 dr
       <=(C/16)(1+|b|)^N ell^(4/5).                  (18)

At the fixed contact zero-gap law this has a positive exact coefficient:

    int_(ell^(1/5))^r0 E_(0,0)hhat_(r,ell/r^4)dr
      =J_tau(b) ell^(4/5)+O_b(ell^(6/5)),
    J_tau(b)=int_1^infinity x^-12 H_tau(x^5)dx,
                     0<J_tau(b)<infinity.            (19)

To prove it, set r=ell^(1/5)x in (12). Then kappa=ell^(1/5)x^-4 and
lambda=x^5. The main term is ell^(4/5)x^-12 H_tau(x^5)dx. The envelope
H_tau(x^5)<=C x^-5 makes the integral at infinity convergent. Its omitted tail
beyond x=r0 ell^-1/5 costs O_b,r0(ell^4). The error term in (12) integrates as
O(ell^(6/5) int_1^infinity x^-25 dx). This proves (19), with r0 fixed first.

Under G2–G3, including the stated pin-density bounds, the genuine finite-jet
radial surrogate Khat=12pi_r(b,k)E_(r,k)hhat with k=kappa r satisfies

    int_(ell^(1/5))^r0 Khat_(r,ell/r^4)dr
       =12pi_0(b,0)J_tau(b)ell^(4/5)
          +O(ell^(6/5)(1+|b|)^N exp(-c b^2)).         (20)

For completeness, mean shift from mu_(0,0) to mu_(0,k) is O(|k|), while the
G3 change is O(r^2 P). Apply (4) with epsilon=|k|+r^2. In this radial sector,

    k=ell^(2/5)x^-3, r^2=ell^(2/5)x^2,
    kappa^3/lambda=ell^(3/5)x^-17, dr=ell^(1/5)dx.

The law-transport errors are therefore bounded by ell^(6/5) times integrable
powers x^-20+x^-15. The pin-factor error follows from G3 and the zero-gap
formula for pi_0: |pi_r(b,k)-pi_0(b,0)|<=C(r^2+k^2)P^N exp(-c b^2).
Combine this with (2). All remaining birth polynomials are integrable against
the displayed Gaussian envelope. The same conclusions may be integrated over
a compact frame family with uniform G1–G3 constants. No actual-field decision
is introduced by this transport.
