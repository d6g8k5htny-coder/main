## 0. What is and is not proved

This note proves a uniform estimate for an explicitly defined, CLIPPED finite-jet
surrogate from Math-#240, not for the actual field's elder decision. Keeping the
small window in the calculation gives an error O(r^2 kappa), uniformly for
0<kappa<=1, even when kappa<r. We also identify a strictly negative quadratic
coefficient of this surrogate: -288 times a positive Gaussian moment. Neither
statement assumes that the clipping discards negligible actual-field mass.

## 1. Gaussian and algebraic setting

Fix d>=2, m=d-1 and a model/frame. Under the contact Gaussian law mu_(0,k),
let A be an m by m symmetric matrix, gamma and eta vectors, B a symmetric matrix,
f5 a scalar, and W=f4 a scalar. The remaining free jets may be included in X,
where X denotes ALL free coordinates except W. On A<0 put

    Delta=det A, lambda=lambda_min(-A), q=gamma^T A^(-1)gamma<=0,
    Q=f5/120-eta^T A^(-1)gamma/12
                       +gamma^T A^(-1)B A^(-1)gamma/8.             (1)

On the null set det A=0 set the following expressions to zero. We use these
explicit source hypotheses, stated so the scope is testable:

(G1) The joint Gaussian covariance of (X,W) has eigenvalues in a fixed [c,C],
0<c<C. Its mean has size at most C P, P=1+|b|+|k|, and all polynomial moments
are correspondingly bounded. Conditional W given X has variance in [c,C] and
an affine mean a(X) with |a(X)|<=C(P+|X|).

(G2) Under mu_(0,k), even jets E (including A,eta,W) and odd jets O (including
B,gamma,f5) are independent. The even law does not depend on k; the odd covariance
is fixed and its mean is k v for a fixed vector v. Thus W|X depends on the even
coordinates only. At k=0 the odd law is invariant under O->-O.

(G3) An optional finite-r Gaussian law mu_(r,k) on the SAME free coordinates has
mean/covariance differences from mu_(0,k) bounded by C r^2 P and C r^2. All
covariance eigenvalues remain in [c,C]. The source pin densities obey

    |pi_r(b,k)-pi_0(b,k)|<=C r^2 P^2 exp[-c(b^2+k^2)],
    pi_0(b,k)=pi_0(b,0)exp[-a' k^2],   pi_0(b,0)<=C exp(-c b^2),  (2)

with a' positive and bounded on the compact frame family. Constants in this
note may depend on the fixed d,L and these bounds; no dimension/L-uniform or
numerically calibrated constants are claimed.

Put z=W-3q. This is a shifted integration variable, NOT a jointly Gaussian
coordinate with X. Write p_X(z) for the conditional density of W at 3q+z.
Fix a clipping constant 0<tau<=1; [N] uses tau=36/200=9/50. For 0<kappa<=1 set

    delta=36rQ,
    a_s=24kappa+s,          b_s=(5184kappa^2+48kappa s)^(1/2),
    w_s(z)=Delta^2(b_s^2-z^2)/144.                           (3)

For |s|<=kappa, 23kappa<=a_s<=25kappa and
71kappa<b_s<73kappa. More importantly,

    b_s^2-a_s^2=4608kappa^2-s^2>0.                          (4)

Define the surrogate, setting it to zero outside the clip WITHOUT evaluating
an invalid square root there:

    hhat_(r,kappa)(X,W)=1{A<0, |delta|<=tau kappa}
                           w_delta(z)1{a_delta<|z|<b_delta}. (5)

It is nonnegative and equals the difference between [N]'s clipped first-order
candidate positive-part surrogate and clipped first-order elder surrogate.
On the clip it has amplitude <=37kappa^2 Delta^2 and total conditional W-support
length <=146kappa. Both claims, not support alone, will be used.

For A<0 define the conditional integral

    J_kappa(s;X)=int_(a_s<|z|<b_s) w_s(z)p_X(z) dz,
    Jhat_kappa(delta;X)=J_kappa(delta;X)1{|delta|<=tau kappa}. (6)

At s=0 this is the original rejected cusp annulus. The unnormalized reference
expectation below always includes 1{A<0}; we write that explicitly when helpful.

## 2. Exact moving-boundary derivatives and clipping, including large shifts

**Lemma 1.** Write S(x)=p_X(x)+p_X(-x), D(x)=p_X'(x)-p_X'(-x), and
L_kappa(X)=sup_(|z|<=73kappa)(p_X(z)+|p_X'(z)|). For |s|<=kappa,

    |J_kappa''(s;X)|<=66 Delta^2 kappa L_kappa(X).            (7)

At zero,

    J_kappa'(0)= (kappa Delta^2/3) P(24kappa<|z|<72kappa|X)
                        -32kappa^2 Delta^2 S(24kappa),       (8)
    |J_kappa(0)|<=1792 Delta^2 kappa^3 L_kappa,
    |J_kappa'(0)|<=96 Delta^2 kappa^2 L_kappa.               (9)

For EVERY real delta, including |delta|>tau kappa,

 |Jhat_kappa(delta)-J_kappa(0)-delta J_kappa'(0)|
        <=C_tau Delta^2 kappa delta^2 L_kappa,               (10)
 C_tau=50+1792/tau^2+96/tau.

*Proof.* Write J=C-I, where C integrates w_s over |z|<b_s and I over |z|<a_s.
Since w_s vanishes at the outer endpoints and b_s'=24kappa/b_s,

    C''(s)=8kappa^2 Delta^2 S(b_s)/b_s.

The weight at an inner endpoint is Delta^2(32kappa^2-s^2/144), so Leibniz'
rule, retaining both moving endpoints, gives

 I''(s)=Delta^2(kappa/3-s/72)S(a_s)
                 +Delta^2(32kappa^2-s^2/144)D(a_s).         (11)

For kappa<=1 the absolute bounds on these terms sum to
(16/71+25/36+64)Delta^2 kappa L_kappa<66 Delta^2 kappa L_kappa.
The first derivative at zero gives (8). The interval has length96kappa,
and its weight integral is exactly1792 Delta^2 kappa^3, proving (9).

If |delta|<=tau kappa, Taylor's integral remainder and (7) prove (10), with
33 in place of C_tau. If |delta|>tau kappa, Jhat=0 and (9) applies, using
kappa^3<=kappa delta^2/tau^2 and |delta|kappa^2<=kappa delta^2/tau.
This proves (10) without any assumption r<<kappa or a tail bound for Q. QED.

The clip is essential to the object being proved: without it, a_s may exceed
b_s or b_s^2 can be negative. Bounding the DISCARDED SURROGATE Taylor remainder
is not permission to discard the corresponding actual field realizations.

## 3. Integrability without an unweighted inverse-Hessian moment

**Lemma 2.** Uniformly for 0<kappa<=1 and |k|<=1,

    E_(0,k)[1{A<0} Delta^2 Q^2 L_kappa(X)]<=C P^N.         (12)

The same assertion holds with any extra fixed polynomial factor in the jets.

*Proof.* On A<0, Gamma=|A^(-1)gamma| satisfies Gamma^2<=|q|/lambda, and
Delta^2<=lambda^2||A||^(2m-2). Squaring (1), with a fixed numerical factor,
gives

 Delta^2 Q^2<=C||A||^(2m-2)
       [f5^2 lambda^2+|eta|^2 |q|lambda+||B||^2 q^2].       (13)

For m=1 the displayed norm power is zero. In p_X(z) put
v=3q+z-a(X). Since |z|<=73, powers of q are bounded by powers of
|v|+|a(X)|+73. The Gaussian density and its first derivative absorb the v
powers, with constants uniform in the conditional variance in [c,C]. Therefore

    (1+|q|)^j L_kappa(X)<=C_j(1+|a(X)|)^j,   j=0,1,2,     (14)

with harmless additive constants absorbed. Combining (13)-(14), using
lambda<=||A||, leaves a polynomial in the ordinary Gaussian jets. Its expectation
is at most C P^N by G1. Extra fixed polynomial factors are handled identically.
No naked inverse-lambda moment, matrix isotropy or independence of A and its
complement has been used. QED.

A useful polynomial identity is

 Delta^2 Q=Delta^2 f5/120-Delta eta^T adj(A)gamma/12
                         +gamma^T adj(A)B adj(A)gamma/8.   (15)

It remains meaningful at singular A and will control the linear term.

## 4. Uniform surrogate theorem

Let J0_kappa(X)=J_kappa(0;X), and h0_kappa the corresponding joint annulus test
function. The conditional density p_X used here is independent of k given the
even coordinates; (6) is therefore a fixed function of X as the odd mean varies.

**Theorem CS.** Under G1-G2, for 0<r<=1, 0<kappa<=1 and |k|<=1,

 |E_(0,k) hhat_(r,kappa)-E_(0,0) h0_kappa|
  <=C P^N[r^2 kappa+r|k|kappa^2+k^2 kappa^3].             (16)

In particular at k=kappa r this is O(r^2 kappa P^N), with no lower bound on
kappa/r. Under G3 define the finite-jet radial surrogate

    Khat_(r,kappa)=12pi_r(b,k) E_(r,k) hhat_(r,kappa),
    Acal_rej(b,kappa,u)=12pi_0(b,0) E_(0,0) h0_kappa.

Then, for k=kappa r and r in the source's allowed range,

 |Khat_(r,kappa)-Acal_rej(b,kappa,u)|
       <=C r^2 kappa (1+|b|)^N exp(-c b^2).                (17)

*Proof of (16).* Substitute delta=36rQ in (10) and use (12):

 E_(0,k)hhat=E_(0,k)h0+36r E_(0,k)[1{A<0}Q J_kappa'(0)]
                                      +O(r^2 kappa P^N). (18)

The factor J_kappa'(0)/Delta^2 depends on the odd jets only through q, and is
even under O->-O. Q is odd. At k=0 their product therefore has mean zero.
Equations (8)-(9), the uniform conditional density bound and (15) give

    |Q J_kappa'(0)|<=C kappa^2 |Delta^2 Q|,
    |E_(0,k)Q J_kappa'(0)|<=C|k|kappa^2 P^N.               (19)

The second inequality follows by differentiating the shifted odd Gaussian
density along its mean segment; its derivative is a linear score times that
density. The test function need not be differentiable in the jets. All resulting
polynomial moments are finite. This argument preserves the kappa^2 factor.

Similarly J0 is even in the odd jets, and 0<=J0<=C kappa^3 Delta^2. The first
odd-mean derivative at zero vanishes; differentiating the Gaussian density
twice supplies

    |E_(0,k)h0-E_(0,0)h0|<=C k^2 kappa^3 P^N.             (20)

Equations (18)-(20) prove (16). Since k=kappa r and kappa<=1, both mean-shift
terms are bounded by r^2 kappa.

*Gaussian law and pin transport for (17).* Here the genuinely Gaussian variables
are (X,W), not (X,z). On the clip, (5) has amplitude <=37kappa^2 Delta^2 and
conditional W-support length <=146kappa, with an arbitrary X-dependent center.
Interpolate the means and covariances in G3. The density derivative is bounded
by C r^2 P(1+|y-m_t|^2)p_t(y). Complete the Gaussian square in W given X and take
the supremum in W of this polynomial times density BEFORE applying the support
length. The remaining X integral is a polynomial Gaussian moment. Thus

    |E_(r,k)hhat-E_(0,k)hhat|<=C r^2 kappa^3 P^N.          (21)

No growth condition on 3q(X) is required by this proof. The same support/amplitude
estimate gives E_(r,k)hhat<=C kappa^3 P^N. Apply (2) to obtain a pin-factor error
C r^2 kappa^3 P^(N+2)exp[-c(b^2+k^2)]. Finally, replacing pi_0(b,k) by pi_0(b,0)
costs C k^2 kappa^3 P^N exp(-c b^2). Together with (16) these prove (17).
A centered Gaussian envelope uniform over unbounded b is never asserted. QED.

## 5. A nonzero quadratic coefficient: why r^2 kappa is a real scale

Fix b,u and the contact law at gap zero. Define

    M=E_(0,0)[1{A<0} Delta^2 Q^2 p_(W|X)(3q)].

Under G1-G2, 0<M<infinity. Finiteness follows from Lemma2. Positivity follows
because A<0 is an open set of positive Gaussian measure, the conditional density
of W is everywhere positive, and Q is affine in f5 with coefficient1/120.
The nondegenerate joint Gaussian law makes its conditional zero set null.

**Theorem QC.** As kappa->0 and r/kappa->0, in any joint sequence,

 [E_(0,0)hhat_(r,kappa)-E_(0,0)h0_kappa]/(r^2 kappa)
                             -> -288 M < 0.               (22)

*Proof.* From (11), at s=0,

 J_kappa''(0)=Delta^2[(kappa/9)S(72kappa)
             -(kappa/3)S(24kappa)-32kappa^2 D(24kappa)].   (23)

For fixed X and any |s|/kappa->0 the exact formula (11) yields
J_kappa''(s)/kappa -> -(4/9)Delta^2 p_X(0). Also delta/kappa=36(r/kappa)Q->0,
so the clip is eventually satisfied for this X. Taylor's integral remainder gives

 [Jhat(delta)-J0-delta J'(0)]/(r^2 kappa)
   =36^2 Q^2 int_0^1 (1-t)J_kappa''(t delta)/kappa dt
   -> -288 Delta^2 Q^2 p_X(0).

By (10) its absolute value is bounded by a constant times
Delta^2 Q^2 sup_(|z|<=73)(p_X+|p_X'|), integrable by Lemma2. Dominated convergence
therefore includes the random tail where clipping is active. The linear term
has exactly zero expectation by odd parity. This proves (22). QED.

For a locally constant density with Delta=1, the exact expansion already has
J_kappa(0)=1792kappa^3, J_kappa'(0)=-32kappa^2 and
J_kappa''(0)=-(4/9)kappa. The factor -288 is (36^2/2)(-4/9), not -576.
The executable diagnostic uses a genuine positive density profile exp(-z^2/2),
symmetric shifts Q=+/-1 and r=kappa^2, with rational polynomial enclosures of the
entire Gaussian integral. It approaches -288. It is a scalar diagnostic, not the
Gaussian-jet expectation M or a field simulation.

This is ONLY the quadratic correction caused by the first-order surrogate's
moving boundaries and matched weight. The actual field has additional second-order
jet, law and geometric-error terms. They may add to or cancel this coefficient.
No coefficient or sign of the full field's next lifetime-density term follows.

## 6. Relation to the limiting annulus and the intermediate research target

Changing variables z=kappa s in J0 gives the fixed symmetric annulus
D={24<|s|<72}:

 J0=(Delta^2 kappa^3/144) int_D(5184-s^2)p_(W|X)(3q+kappa s)ds.

For a C4 density, Taylor's theorem and symmetry give, conditionally,

 J0=1792 Delta^2 p_(W|X)(3q)kappa^3
       +(8183808/5)Delta^2 p_(W|X)''(3q)kappa^5+O_X(kappa^7). (24)

There is no kappa^4 or kappa^6 term. The absolute conditional remainder is bounded
by (11455561728/35)Delta^2 kappa^7 times the supremum of the fourth derivative
on |W-3q|<=72kappa. This precise remainder, not a bare formal series, is checked
against independent polynomial integrals in the companion tests.

 int_(ell^(1/4))^r0 r^2 kappa dr
       =ell[(ell^(-1/4))-1/r0]=O(ell^(3/4)).               (25)
