## Exact reference law and endpoints

For `S_e(q)=q^-11[1+e(q^-2-1)]`, direct differentiation gives

`p_e(q)=-S'_e(q)=q^-12[11(1-e)+13e q^-2]`.

For `-11/2<e<1`, this is strictly positive at every q in `[1,infinity)`. If e<0 its bracket is smallest at q=1 and equals 11+2e>0. If e>=0 the bracket is bounded below by 11(1-e)>0. Also `S_e(1)=1`, `S_e(infinity)=0`, and integrating the two density terms gives `(1-e)+e=1`. Hence it is exactly a probability survival function, not a truncated formal expansion.

The stated interval is sufficient and sound. At e=1 the density is still positive for every finite q and the law is Pareto13, so that endpoint could also be admitted; its exclusion does not make the stated proposition false. At e=-11/2 the density vanishes at q=1 but is positive for q>1. That endpoint would not meet the proposal's strictly positive density on the closed domain. Outside these endpoint bounds positivity fails either near q=1 or in the far tail. With fixed finite c, or a bounded deterministic function c_hat(t), the stated open interval holds for all sufficiently large t; it is not asserted for every small t.

## Exact pushforward and the moving-map issue

`T_e(q)=S_e(q)^(-1/11)` is continuous, satisfies T_e(1)=1, tends to infinity, and has derivative `p_e(q) S_e(q)^(-12/11)/11>0`. It is therefore an increasing bijection of `[1,infinity)` onto itself, with a measurable inverse. If Q_e has the reference law, then for x>=1,

`P(T_e(Q_e)>x)=S_e(T_e^-1(x))=x^-11`.

Thus its pushforward is exactly Pareto11. For any two probability laws mu and nu on this domain, a measurable bijection with a measurable inverse preserves TV: inverse images identify all measurable events on either space. In particular, for each fixed t,

`dTV(T_e#Law(R/t),Pareto11)=dTV(Law(R/t),reference_e)`.

The equality is exact even though e and the map vary with t. There is no appeal to a general weak-convergence principle for moving maps, and no extension to spatial coordinates. Taking e=c/t^2 makes the reference density equal to the displayed first two terms of p_t, so the right-hand side is O(t^-4).

## Calibration error and sharp coefficient

For a bounded deterministic calibration function c_hat(t), the exact reference density at e_hat=c_hat/t^2 yields

`p_t-p_e_hat=(c-c_hat)t^-2 h+r_t`.

The reverse triangle inequality for L1 shows

`|dTV(Law(R/t),reference_e_hat) - |c-c_hat|t^-2 ||h||_1/2| <= ||r_t||_1/2 = O(t^-4)`.

This bound is uniform over bounded calibration choices; indeed the remainder is the same actual-law remainder, independent of c_hat. The sole sign change of h is at q0=sqrt(13/11). Its total integral is zero, and its positive integral is

`integral_1^q0 h(q)dq=q0^-11-q0^-13=(2/13)(11/13)^(11/2)=kappa`.

Consequently `||h||_1/2=kappa`. Combining this identity with exact TV invariance proves precisely the proposal's formula

`dTV(Law(T_e_hat(R/t)),Pareto11)=kappa |c-c_hat|t^-2+O(t^-4)`.

The sufficient condition c_hat=c+O(t^-2) is correct. A fixed nonzero coefficient error gives a genuine order-t^-2 term. The fourth-order conclusion is an upper bound only: the parent neither supplies nor proves nonvanishing of the remaining coefficient. No Theta(t^-4) claim follows.

Here c_hat(t) is a deterministic mathematical or numerical calibration value, as in the proposal's use of a bounded function of t. If instead one makes c_hat a random estimator coupled to the sampled Q, the displayed unconditional transformed-law identity is not automatically applicable: the same fixed reference/map comparison is then unavailable without an additional joint or conditional argument. The proposal explicitly declines an automatic empirical benefit; this review likewise makes no data-dependent-estimator claim. This boundary does not require amendment of the deterministic corollary.
