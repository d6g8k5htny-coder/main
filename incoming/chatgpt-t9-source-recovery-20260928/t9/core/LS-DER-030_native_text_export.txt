EXPLICIT FIXED-WINDOW COEFFICIENT AND TORUS-AREA NORMALIZATION THEOREM


1. SETTING AND MEASURE CONVENTIONS


Let T_L^2 be the flat side-L torus equipped with ordinary Lebesgue area dx, and let


    A_L=area(T_L^2)=L^2.


For the current program L=24, hence


    A_L=576.                                                   (1)


Let B_*=[23/20,5/4]. For an ordered maximum-saddle pair in the short-distance chart, write


    x=(M+S)/2,
    h=S-M=r t_theta,
    theta in [0,2pi),
    ell=f(M)-f(S)=kappa r^3/6,
    kappa>0.                                                   (2)


The directed maximum-to-saddle convention fixes one theta for each ordered typed pair. No factor 1/2 is introduced.


LS-DER-021 explicitly states that its contact density J_r(theta,b,kappa) is integrated per unit torus area in the base point x and is a density with respect to


    r dr dtheta db dkappa.                                    (3)


Let p_r be the exact elder-selection probability under the identical all-typed Palm law, and set


    K_r(theta,b,kappa)=J_r(theta,b,kappa)p_r(theta,b,kappa).   (4)


Thus K_r is also a selected intensity per unit base-point area. This distinction controls whether the final lifetime density is specific, meaning per unit torus area, or total, meaning integrated over the entire torus.


Define


    bar_nu_{B_*}(ell)
      = expected selected lifetime count per unit torus area per unit ell,


and


    nu_{B_*}^{tot}(ell)
      = expected selected lifetime count on the whole torus per unit ell. (5)


Stationarity gives the exact relation


    nu_{B_*}^{tot}(ell)=A_L bar_nu_{B_*}(ell).                (6)


If a source instead uses normalized Haar area with total mass one, then its density is numerically bar_nu and every dx or dx dy integral must use that normalized measure consistently. The current sources do not declare normalized Haar measure, while LS-DER-021 expressly says per unit torus area. Therefore ordinary area plus (5)-(6) is the controlling explicit convention for this companion.


2. GEOMETRIC COORDINATE JACOBIAN


On the local injectivity chart, the linear change of variables


    M=x-h/2,
    S=x+h/2                                                     (7)


has absolute four-dimensional Jacobian one:


    dM dS=dx dh.                                                (8)


In directed polar coordinates h=r t_theta,


    dh=r dr dtheta,                                             (9)


with theta in [0,2pi). Because maximum and saddle types order the pair, theta+pi describes the reversed saddle-to-maximum displacement and is not a second atom of the ordered process.


The change from critical values (b,d) to (b,kappa), with


    d=b-kappa r^3/6,                                           (10)


has absolute Jacobian


    |partial d/partial kappa|=r^3/6.                           (11)


That factor is already included in the definition


    J_r=(r^3/6)p_X Z_r.                                       (12)


It must not be inserted again in the lifetime pushforward.


3. LIFETIME PUSHFORWARD


At fixed kappa>0,


    r=(6ell/kappa)^(1/3),                                     (13)


and


    d ell/dr=kappa r^2/2.                                     (14)


Therefore


    r dr
      =[r/(d ell/dr)] d ell
      =[2/(kappa r)] d ell
      =(6^(2/3)/3) kappa^(-2/3) ell^(-1/3)d ell.              (15)


The two forms of the numerical constant agree because


    2/6^(1/3)=6^(2/3)/3.                                     (16)


Set


    alpha=6^(2/3)/3.                                          (17)


For the full-kappa near-diagonal component with local cutoff r_c, define


    r_ell(kappa)=(6ell/kappa)^(1/3).                          (18)


The exact specific lifetime density is


    bar_nu_near(ell)
      =alpha ell^(-1/3)
        integral_{theta in S^1}
        integral_{b in B_*}
        integral_0^infinity
          1{r_ell(kappa)<=r_c}
          K_{r_ell(kappa)}(theta,b,kappa)
          kappa^(-2/3)
          dkappa db dtheta.                                   (19)


The exact total near density is


    nu_near^{tot}(ell)=A_L bar_nu_near(ell).                  (20)


There is no additional factor from x, theta, unordered-pair symmetrization, branch incidence, or persistence multiplicity. The x integral supplies exactly A_L for total counts and supplies one for specific counts.


4. LIMIT COEFFICIENT


The current frozen chain supplies, for each fixed kappa>0 and locally uniformly in theta,b,kappa,


    K_r(theta,b,kappa)->J_0(theta,b,kappa),                   (21)


where


    J_0(theta,b,kappa)
      =(1/6)p_0(theta,b,kappa)
        kappa^2 E[Q_{theta,b,kappa}^2
                    1{Q_{theta,b,kappa}<0}].                  (22)


It also supplies the global majorant


    0<=kappa^(-2/3)K_r(theta,b,kappa)
      <=C kappa^(-2/3)(1+kappa^4)e^{-c kappa^2}.              (23)


The right side is integrable on (0,infinity): it is O(kappa^(-2/3)) at zero and Gaussian-decaying at infinity. For every fixed kappa>0, the cutoff in (19) tends to one as ell decreases to zero. Dominated convergence therefore gives


    bar_nu_near(ell)
      =c_{B_*}^{spec} ell^(-1/3)(1+o(1)),                     (24)


with the explicit specific coefficient


    c_{B_*}^{spec}
      =alpha
        integral_{S^1}
        integral_{B_*}
        integral_0^infinity
          J_0(theta,b,kappa) kappa^(-2/3)
          dkappa db dtheta.                                   (25)


For ordinary torus area, the explicit total coefficient is


    C_{B_*}^{tot}
      =A_L c_{B_*}^{spec}                                    (26)


and, at L=24,


    C_{B_*}^{tot}
      =576 (6^(2/3)/3)
        integral_{S^1}
        integral_{23/20}^{5/4}
        integral_0^infinity
          J_0(theta,b,kappa) kappa^(-2/3)
          dkappa db dtheta.                                   (27)


Thus


    nu_near^{tot}(ell)
      =C_{B_*}^{tot} ell^(-1/3)(1+o(1)).                      (28)


5. FINITENESS AND POSITIVITY


Finiteness follows from (23):


    c_{B_*}^{spec}
      <=alpha |S^1| |B_*|
        integral_0^infinity
          C kappa^(-2/3)(1+kappa^4)e^{-c kappa^2}dkappa
      <infinity.                                               (29)


No vanishing of J_0 at kappa=0 is required. The exponent -2/3 is integrable.


Strict positivity follows by restricting the integral to the compact window


    K_*=[3/4,5/4].                                             (30)


LS-DER-021 and LS-DER-025 assert a continuous positive lower bound for J_0 on S^1 x B_* x K_*. Hence


    c_{B_*}^{spec}
      >=alpha
        integral_{S^1 x B_* x K_*}
          J_0(theta,b,kappa)kappa^(-2/3)
          dtheta db dkappa
      >0.                                                      (31)


Since A_L=576>0,


    0<C_{B_*}^{tot}<infinity.                                 (32)


6. FAR COMPONENT UNDER THE TWO CONVENTIONS


LS-DER-027 defines the far selected density using ordinary-looking Cartesian integration:


    nu_far^{written}(ell)
      =integral_{b in B_*}
        integral_{F_{r_c}}
          rho_sel(x,y;b,ell) dx dy db.                         (33)


With ordinary Lebesgue area, (33) is a total torus density. The corresponding specific density is


    bar_nu_far(ell)
      =(1/A_L)
        integral_{b in B_*}
        integral_{F_{r_c}}
          rho_sel(x,y;b,ell) dx dy db.                         (34)


By stationarity it may equivalently be written by fixing one base point and integrating the displacement variable over the torus. The boundedness proof survives either convention:


    nu_far^{tot}(ell)=O(1),
    bar_nu_far(ell)=O(1).                                     (35)


The normalization changes the finite constant, not the lower-order conclusion.


7. CORRECTED RECOMBINATION


Under the specific convention,


    bar_nu_{B_*}(ell)
      =bar_nu_near(ell)+bar_nu_far(ell)
      =c_{B_*}^{spec}ell^(-1/3)(1+o(1)).                      (36)


Under the total convention,


    nu_{B_*}^{tot}(ell)
      =nu_near^{tot}(ell)+nu_far^{tot}(ell)
      =C_{B_*}^{tot}ell^(-1/3)(1+o(1)),                       (37)


where C_{B_*}^{tot}=A_L c_{B_*}^{spec}.


The exponent -1/3, finiteness, positivity, and lower-order far complement are unchanged. What changes is the explicit coefficient and the labeling of the lifetime density.
