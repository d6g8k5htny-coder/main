## 2. Deterministic theorem, including varying parameters

Let X be a fixed square flat torus of side L. Consider smooth functions
f_j,f_0 on X such that f_j->f_0 in global C4. Let r_j>0 tend to 0 and suppose

    x_j -> x_0,
    (u_j,v_j) -> (u_0,v_0) in O(2),
    b_j -> b_0,  k_j -> k_0>0.

Assume the exact finite pins

    M_j=x_j-r_j u_j/2, S_j=x_j+r_j u_j/2,
    f_j(M_j)=b_j, f_j(S_j)=d_j=b_j-k_j r_j^3,
    grad f_j(M_j)=grad f_j(S_j)=0.                         (1)

Assume the actual limit contact has the physical derivative data

    (f_0,D_u f_0,D_u^2 f_0,D_u^3 f_0,D_v f_0,D_u D_v f_0)(x_0)
       =(b_0,0,0,12k_0,0,0),
    A_0=D_v^2 f_0(x_0)<0,                                (2)

in the limit frame. Assume its critical set is finite, every other critical
point is Morse, and their critical values are pairwise distinct and different
from b_0. CG supplies these conditions almost surely under its contact law;
here they are deterministic hypotheses.

For all sufficiently large j, f_j is Morse with distinct critical values.
Its only critical points in a fixed-size neighborhood of contact are the
exact M_j,S_j, respectively a maximum and an index 1 saddle. The component
born at M_j stays enclosed until d_j. There it merges through S_j with a
component having birth height>b_j. Thus (M_j,S_j) is the actual finite H0
superlevel elder pair, of exact lifetime k_j r_j^3.

For a fixed-parameter family f_r->f_0, the sequence theorem implies the result
for every sufficiently small r: a contrary failing sequence contradicts it.
The threshold can depend on the whole realization, its transverse pivot,
derivative modulus and global critical height/gradient gaps. A deterministic
ensemble-wide threshold is not asserted.

## 3. Actual transverse reduction

In the local charts put

    g_j(s,t)=f_j(x_j+u_j s+v_j t).

On one fixed embedded rectangle Q=[-delta,delta]x[-tau,tau], these converge
in C4 to g_0. This is a local chart operation, not rotational invariance of
the square-torus law. A_0<0 allows a small rectangle and a>0 such that

    g_0,tt<=-2a, eventually g_j,tt<=-a on Q.                 (3)

Subscript commas below denote partial derivatives. The transverse implicit
function theorem gives h_0 with g_0,t(s,h_0(s))=0, h_0(0)=h_0'(0)=0.
Shrink delta until |h_0|<tau/4. Strict concavity gives opposite strict signs
for the transverse derivatives at t=±tau/2, uniformly in s. C4 convergence
preserves them, so every s has exactly one root h_j(s) in(-tau/2,tau/2).
Implicit derivative formulas with the denominator bounded away from 0 give
h_j->h_0 in C3.

Define the actual reduced ridge functions

    phi_j(s)=g_j(s,h_j(s)), phi_j'=g_j,s(s,h_j(s)).          (4)

Their third derivatives converge uniformly. The contact pins give

    phi_0(0)=b_0, phi_0'(0)=phi_0''(0)=0,
    phi_0'''(0)=12k_0.

For the last identity, h_0'(0)=0 and g_0,st(0,0)=0 kill the additional
mixed and h_0'' terms. Shrinking again gives c>0 with

    phi_0'''>=2c, eventually phi_j'''>=c>0.               (5)

This derivative sign, rather than convergence alone, controls new critical
points near the degenerate contact.

## 4. Exactly two local critical points and their types

The exact pins and transverse uniqueness imply h_j(±r_j/2)=0 and
phi_j'(±r_j/2)=0. By (5), p_j=phi_j' is strictly convex. With those two
zeros it is negative between them and positive outside them in the interval.
Strict monotonicity of secant slopes proves the outside signs as well as
excluding a third zero. Further, p_j'(-r_j/2)<0 and p_j'(r_j/2)>0;
integration of p_j''>=c bounds both magnitudes below by c r_j/2.

At a ridge critical point the actual Hessian Schur pivot is

    phi_j''=g_j,ss-g_j,st^2/g_j,tt.                        (6)

Since g_j,tt<0, the left negative pivot makes the Hessian negative definite;
the right positive pivot makes it index 1. Any critical point in Q must be
on the transverse ridge and a zero of phi_j'. Thus M_j,S_j are all the
local critical points, with the required actual types.

The limit reduced derivative has a double zero at0 and positive second
derivative. It is positive elsewhere locally. Hence phi_0 is strictly
increasing and, with s_+=delta/2,

    phi_0(-delta)<b_0<phi_0(s_+).                          (7)

These are fixed realization-dependent height gaps; a quadratic lower bound
on phi_0' from (5) gives cubic gaps if desired.

## 6. Exact enclosure of the newborn component

Let Q_j^-=[-delta,r_j/2]x[-tau,tau] in the local chart. Its left boundary
has g_j<=phi_j(-delta), below b_j by a fixed gap from (7). On its right,

    g_j(r_j/2,t)<=phi_j(r_j/2)=d_j,

with equality only at t=0. Transverse concavity gives

    g_j(s,t)<=phi_j(s)-(a/2)[t-h_j(s)]^2.

On s<=r_j/2 the reduced signs imply phi_j<=b_j. Since |h_j|<tau/2,
the top/bottom values are at most b_j-a tau^2/8. For large j the fixed
left and transverse gaps exceed k_j r_j^3. Consequently the left/top/bottom
boundaries lie strictly below d_j, and the whole boundary has values<=d_j.

For d_j<c<b_j put

    C_j(c)={z in Q_j^-: g_j(z)>=c}.                         (8)

Its s projection is one closed interval around the maximum: phi_j increases
to b_j from the left and decreases to d_j on the right. Each vertical fiber
is one interval containing the ridge. A point joins vertically to the ridge,
and the connecting ridge segment stays above c; thus C_j(c) is path connected.
It is nonempty and avoids the rectangle boundary. Its global superlevel
intersection is open and closed in X_c={f_j>=c}, because the boundary misses
X_c. So it is an actual global component. An exterior route cannot join
it to an older component before d_j.

At c=b_j this region contains only M_j, and at c>b_j it is empty. The
component is actually born at b_j and contains no higher-birth maximum.
This proves enclosure for the actual component, rather than for a restricted
local candidate picture.

## 7. The other saddle branch is older

The fixed limit point p_+=x_0+u_0 s_++v_0 h_0(s_+) has f_0(p_+)>b_0.
A small fixed ball about it has values strictly above b_0. For large j its
f_j values are strictly above b_j, and the moving ridge endpoint

    p_j^+=x_j+u_j s_++v_j h_j(s_+)

lies in that ball. The ridge from S_j to p_j^+ has phi_j>=d_j, increasing
strictly after S. It continues within the high ball to fixed p_+. Thus the
actual right branch reaches a point with height>b_j.

For c in(d_j,b_j) its component differs from the enclosed component(8).
At c=d_j the complete ridge from M_j through S_j to p_j^+ lies in the
closed superlevel: it decreases from b_j to d_j then increases. This joins
the two actual components through S_j. The newborn component first meets
the exterior there; the other three rectangle sides remain below d_j.

The nonsingular saddle is the only critical point at that height. Its local
Morse form has two upper sectors, belonging to the two different components
just proved. Its index 1 attachment therefore merges them, instead of making
a loop inside one component. The component on the right was born at height
at least f_j(p_+)>b_j and is strictly older for descending superlevels.

## 8. Actual finite elder bar and endpoint convention

H0 over any field has one generator per component. At the merger the two
generators map to the same generator. Since the older component already
exists at b_j, replace the new generator by its difference from a continued
older generator. This difference is born at b_j, survives on(d_j,b_j],
and becomes 0 at d_j, while the older generator survives. This is the
component-level elementary basis proof of the elder convention and identifies
the actual finite bar killed by S_j.

With increasing parameter t=-c and closed superlevels {f_j>=c}, its interval
is[-b_j,-d_j); in height coordinates it is(d_j,b_j]. Its exact lifetime is

    ell_j=b_j-d_j=k_j r_j^3.                               (9)

The older component may represent several earlier mergers but still has birth
above b_j. The newborn maximum is not the essential global maximum. No
