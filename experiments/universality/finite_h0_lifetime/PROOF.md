## 2. Fixed ideal law, observable, and precise theorem

Fix an integer K>=2, L>0 and omega=2pi/L. On X=R^2/(L Z^2), with area V=L^2,
use the ideal real Gaussian field

    S_K={-K,...,K}^2,
    q_n>0, q_n=q_-n, sum q_n=1,
    F(z)=sqrt(q_0) C_0
       +sum_pairs sqrt(2q_n)
         [C_n cos(omega n.z)+S_n sin(omega n.z)],             (1)

where the coefficient coordinates are independent standard real Gaussians
and one representative of each nonzero pair {n,-n} is used. The coefficient
vector xi has dimension N=(2K+1)^2. The field is smooth, stationary and
variance one. It need not be rotationally isotropic.

Use superlevel H0 over any fixed coefficient field, with increasing filtration
parameter t=-c and sets {F>=c}. A finite elder bar born at a local maximum p
and dying at a component-merging index-one saddle q has height lifetime

    ell=F(p)-F(q)>0.

Its interval in increasing parameter is [-F(p),-F(q)); endpoint conventions
do not alter its lifetime. The one essential class is excluded. For a Borel
I subset of (0,infinity), let N_bar(I) count all such finite bars and put

    mu_bar(I)=E N_bar(I)/V.

The observable can be assigned value zero on the unconditional null set of
non-Morse or equal-critical-value fields. Section 4 gives a Borel version
which equals the actual finite-bar count almost surely.

**Composition theorem.** Consuming the analytic elder-transfer result of ET
for the same fixed law (1), mu_bar has a finite nonnegative Borel density
representative nu_bar on ell>0, specified by the marked kernel below, with

    nu_bar(ell) ~ C_loc ell^(-1/3), ell decreasing to zero,    (2)
    E N_bar((0,t])/V ~ (3/2) C_loc t^(2/3).                  (3)

The density coefficient is exactly C's finite positive candidate coefficient:

    C_loc=Gamma(7/6)/(24^(1/3) sqrt(pi))
       *integral_{S^1} p_G(0) p_Vu(0) tau_u^(4/3)
                                     [s_u^2/2] d sigma(u), (4)

where G=(F_u,F_v), V_u=(F_uu,F_uv), T_u=F_uuu, A_u=F_vv,
tau_u^2=Var(T_u|G=0), and s_u^2=Var(A_u|V_u=0). Every derivative and
covariance is from (1). Angular measure is unnormalized arclength of mass
2pi. The numeral 24 in (4) is the Jacobian/gamma factor, not the side L.
Whole-torus expected counts have coefficient V C_loc; at side24, V=576.

Equation (2) concerns the particular finite Borel representative constructed
in Section 5. An arbitrary Radon-Nikodym representative can be changed on a
null set and need not retain a pointwise asymptotic. No continuity of nu_bar
is proved, and no O(1) near-term remainder is claimed. Equation (3) is an
intrinsic statement about the expected measure and follows by integration,
not by differentiating a cumulative asymptotic.

## 3. A Borel elder-death threshold from open superlevels

Let D be a countable dense subset of X. Fix a countable basis of open metric
balls with centers in D and rational radii below one eighth of the torus
injectivity radius. Their closures are compact, and the balls are path
connected. This basis is fixed independently of F.

For a real level lambda, define C_lambda(p,z;xi) to mean that p,z belong to
the same path component of the open set {F_xi>lambda}. It has the following
countable description: there is a finite chain of basis balls B_1,...,B_m
such that

    p in B_1, z in B_m,
    B_i intersects B_(i+1),
    min_{closure B_i} F_xi > lambda for every i.             (5)

Each chain condition is Borel jointly in (lambda,p,z,xi). The minimum on
each fixed compact ball is continuous in xi, because coefficient convergence
implies uniform field convergence. The union over finite chains is countable.
Conversely, a path in {F>lambda} has compact image and can be covered by
finitely many basis balls with closures inside that open set; a chain along
the path gives (5). A chain gives a path because its balls are path connected
with consecutive nonempty intersections. Thus (5) is the exact connectivity
relation, not an approximation by a fixed finite mesh.

For every p define the extended-real threshold

    d_xi(p)=sup{lambda in Q:
        lambda<F_xi(p),
        there is z in D with F_xi(z)>F_xi(p),
        C_lambda(p,z;xi)},                                  (6)

with supremum of the empty set equal to -infinity. This is a Borel function
of (p,xi), by countability of the levels and dense points. It never equals
+infinity because qualifying levels are below F_xi(p).

The dense-point condition is equivalent to connecting to any point of height
strictly greater than F(p): strict inequality supplies an open neighborhood
containing a dense point, and a short extension of the path stays in the open
superlevel. Consequently (6) is intrinsic, despite the chosen basis. In
particular it is equivariant under torus translations.

If no point has greater height, d=-infinity. If some point has greater height,
every sufficiently low level qualifies, since X is connected and lambda can
be taken below min F. Therefore d is finite in that case. For a nondegenerate
local maximum, a small enclosing boundary has height strictly below F(p),
which makes d<F(p). These statements will exclude the essential maximum and
give strictly positive finite lifetimes.

## 4. Morse components, actual H0 once-counting, and selector

Let G be the coefficient set on which F is Morse and its critical values are
pairwise distinct. C Sections 4 and 6 prove that G has unconditional Gaussian
probability one, using the single-point coefficient incidence/Sard argument
before the pair-counting formula, and then the tie mark in that formula.
The critical set is finite. G is open: finitely many implicit-function
critical branches persist, the gradient has a positive lower bound on their
compact complement, and the finitely many nonzero critical-height differences
persist. It is therefore Borel. No static conditional genericity at every
six-pin target is inferred from this unconditional conclusion.

For completeness, the required component changes are deterministic. At any
nondegenerate critical point choose a real normalizer P with P^T H P=2J,
J a diagonal sign matrix. The integral Taylor matrix M(y) satisfies M(0)=J.
Near J, with Z=JM, the explicit two-dimensional branch

    R(M)=[Z+sqrt(det Z) I]/sqrt(tr Z+2sqrt(det Z))

is smooth, has R(J)=I and R(M)^T J R(M)=M, by Cayley-Hamilton and
J-self-adjointness. The same algebra also applies to J=I. Then
e(y)=R(M(y))y has De(0)=I, and the inverse function theorem gives the local
quadratic normal form. This is a pointwise local argument, not the uniform
derivative-window theorem and not a chart at the degenerate contact.

On a compact level slab containing no critical point, the vector field
grad F/||grad F||^2 changes F at unit speed. Smooth cutoffs outside a slightly
larger critical-free slab give a global smooth vector field; its flow, stopped
at the target regular level, deforms one superlevel onto the other. Hence
component inclusion maps are unchanged between consecutive critical values.
At one isolated critical level the local quadratic forms give exactly these
superlevel H0 possibilities:

* A maximum creates one new component. A sufficiently small upper-level disk
  is enclosed by a strictly lower boundary.
* An index-one saddle connects its two upper local sectors. If they belong
  to different global components, precisely those two components merge.
  If they already belong to one component, H0 does not change.
* A minimum fills a disk. Its upper local annulus is connected, so it does
  not merge distinct components or create a new H0 component.

Outside the critical neighborhood the critical-free flow supplies the same
gluing throughout a sufficiently short level band. The preceding local
descriptions therefore determine the global component changes. There is one
critical point at each critical value on G.

Descend through this finite sequence of events. Label each component by the
highest-birth maximum it contains. At a merger retain the older, higher label
and kill the younger label. In H0 the two component generators have the same
image after merging; subtracting the older generator from the younger gives
the generator killed there. Induction over the finite event sequence gives
the corresponding interval summands, including their birth and death levels.
This is an elementary component/basis proof of the elder rule for actual H0.

A component contains a point of height greater than the birth F(p) precisely
when it contains a maximum with greater birth height: maximize F on the
component closure; because the given point is strictly above the regular
level, that maximum is attained in its interior and is critical. Thus for a
maximum p, (6) is exactly the first merger level at which its label dies.
Open superlevels connect just below that level; their rational supremum is
the exact critical height. The unique global maximum has d=-infinity and
supports the essential class. Every other maximum has one finite death at
one index-one saddle. At most one younger component dies at each saddle.

Define the everywhere Borel selector

    S(p,q,xi)=1{xi in G}
       1{grad F(p)=grad F(q)=0}
       1{H_p<0} 1{det H_q<0}
       1{d_xi(p) is finite and d_xi(p)=F(q)<F(p)}.           (7)

All factors are Borel by (5)-(6) and continuous evaluation of finite fields.
On G, S equals one exactly for the unique ordered maximum/saddle pair of a
finite H0 elder bar. It is zero for the essential maximum and for saddles
which only create a loop. Consequently, almost surely and for every positive
lifetime Borel set I,

    N_bar(I)=sum_{p!=q, gradF(p)=gradF(q)=0}
                  S(p,q,xi) 1{F(p)-F(q) in I}.             (8)

The sum is finite. This is the required exact once-counting; every finite bar
appears once, and no other pair is counted. Countable local coefficient
neighborhoods with finite smooth critical branches make (8) a Borel random
variable. Assign it zero outside G.

## 5. Selected all-Borel area formula and height disintegration

    G_t=(gradF(p),gradF(q)),

    E sum_{G_t=0} w(t,xi)
      =integral_T p_Gt(0) E[Delta_t w(t,xi)|G_t=0] dt.      (9)

The four-gradient rank is supplied by the original two-site first jets.
There is no arbitrary Borel-mark continuity premise. Apply (9) with
`w=tau_t S(t,xi) 1{F(p)-F(q) in I}`. Indicators already implied by S can
be repeated without changing the value. Delta occurs exactly once, yielding

    K_t^sel(b,s)=p_Ot(b,0,s,0)
                    E_{Q^t_(b,s)}[W_t S(t,xi)].            (10)

This is jointly nonnegative Borel in (t,b,s). Indeed the observation matrix,

C already proves finiteness of its displayed candidate density at every

The unconditional null exceptional set G^c need not be null in every fixed
